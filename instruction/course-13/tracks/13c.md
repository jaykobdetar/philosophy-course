# C13C — Social knowledge and justice

Use with **all eight shared modules**, **160h total**. Prerequisites: Course 11B and Course 12D. Exact C1–C5 readings/editions/URLs are in [sources](../sources.md). Guiding question: when does a pattern of credibility, interpretation or exclusion constitute an epistemic wrong, and what follows for institutions? Outcomes: distinguish interpersonal and structural mechanisms; compare diagnoses without collapsing them; connect a remedy to a specified wrong; separate philosophical from empirical claims; assess evidence and source limitations.

## Specialist teaching: specify the mechanism

Begin with the difference between a false belief, an understandable information gap and a wrong connected to social power. A listener may rationally withhold belief because evidence is weak. That is not automatically an injustice. Conversely, a reliable statement may be discounted through prejudice, and a procedure can systematically exclude knowledge even when no individual consciously intends the result. Explain the mechanism connecting the social relation to the epistemic harm.

Fricker’s assigned chapters distinguish testimonial and hermeneutical injustice: the former concerns a person’s treatment as a source of knowledge; the latter concerns resources for making social experience intelligible. Do not use the second label for every unfamiliar word or every disagreement. Ask whose contribution to shared interpretation is marginalized and how that bears on the difficulty. The publisher abstract and chapter identities were checked; fine-grained applications require the full chapters, which remain access-dependent in this packet.

Mills’s title names an inquiry into socially patterned ignorance. Read C1 for the mechanisms actually proposed, not for a license to infer an individual’s beliefs from racial identity. Ask what selection, memory, concepts, testimony or institutional arrangements do in the argument, and which claims require historical or empirical evidence. A structural explanation can concern incentives and inherited practices; it need not reduce every act to deliberate deception. These are questions for the chapter, whose exact passages remain uninspected here.

Young supplies a framework for distinct forms of oppression. Revisit the chapter before deciding whether a case concerns exploitation, marginalization, powerlessness, cultural imperialism or violence; a list of labels is not an explanation. Anderson’s equality argument raises the issue of relations among people and the standing institutions should secure. A resource transfer and a relation of equal standing can come apart. Explain the criterion connecting the proposed institutional change to the identified problem rather than treating “equality” as a verdict.

Dotson’s publisher abstract identifies hearers’ failures to meet speakers’ vulnerabilities through pernicious ignorance as a focus of epistemic violence. The exact conditions and distinctions within the full article must be read before certifying an application. Do not turn any interrupted conversation into the theory’s central case. When reading, distinguish failure to recognize someone as a knower from a speaker’s limiting what they say in an unsafe or unreceptive setting, checking Dotson’s own terminology and conditions. This orientation is provisional until pp.236–257 are inspected; no full-text claim is inferred from the abstract alone.

A remedy needs a causal and normative argument. An anonymous reporting channel may reduce identity cues but fail to supply missing interpretive resources, alter decision authority or protect against retaliation. A training session may improve some conduct without changing the structure that produced the problem. Ask what must be true for this remedy to address this diagnosis, and what costs or new exclusions it creates. Avoid hypothetical allegations about identifiable people; all supplied cases are invented.

## Original worked case and practice

**C-P: The neighborhood archive.** A fictional archive accepts only typed submissions in a majority language. Residents who preserve local history orally cannot upload recordings. Staff believe the same rule for everyone is fair. A grant funds one day of translation, but the submission format and selection committee remain unchanged.

Worked analysis: the policy could exclude knowledge through access design even without a proved hostile intention. Translation funding addresses one barrier but not recording or selection. A claim of testimonial prejudice would require more about how speakers’ credibility is judged; absence from the archive is not alone proof of that mechanism. A structural diagnosis may fit the stipulated procedure, but using a named author requires the exact chapter’s criteria. The case supports a conditional institutional argument; it is not empirical evidence about real archives.

C-P1: 400 words distinguishing two plausible mechanisms and evidence needed to choose between them. C-P2: a two-column diagnosis/remedy table; include a proposed change, mechanism, expected effect, risk and one missing empirical premise. C-P3: 250 words explaining why a shared diagnosis of exclusion does not make C1–C5 interchangeable theories.

## Independent changed case M2-I-C

A fictional review board accepts recordings and translations. Its members understand a group’s account of a recurring harm, but discount the speakers because they assume that group exaggerates. An analyst says the sole problem is absence of a shared concept. In 250–350 words evaluate the diagnosis with C2 chapters 1/7 and a precise C5 claim if accessible. State a remedy linked to the alleged mechanism and a fact that could change the analysis. Criteria: distinguish intelligibility from credibility, avoid assuming every framework applies, identify evidence limits. Unavailable C5 detail remains unresolved rather than invented.

Repair: if every harm receives the same label, construct a case in which the label would not apply and explain the missing condition. If a remedy is merely an aspiration, specify a procedure, mechanism and failure condition. Retrieve the diagnosis/remedy relation at M4/M6; reserve M8’s distinct delayed task.

# C13B — Comparative moral cultivation

Use with **all eight shared modules**, **160h total**. Prerequisites: Course 11A, Course 6 and any completed Course 12 option. Exact B1–B5 readings and legal access routes are in [sources](../sources.md). Guiding question: how do dispositions, deliberate effort, habits and social practices contribute to ethical cultivation? Outcomes: reconstruct each author on its own terms; distinguish dimensions of agency; establish the actual Van Norden/Soles disagreement; test a precise comparison and its limits; justify a translation-sensitive claim without claiming philological expertise.

## Specialist teaching: compare a problem before equating vocabulary

Three distinctions make a cultivation argument clearer. First, a capacity to develop is not an already developed disposition. Second, acting voluntarily is not identical to acting from a settled excellent character. Third, a practice’s social origin does not settle whether an agent can reflectively endorse it. These are analytic tools for asking questions; they must not be attributed wholesale to every assigned author.

B1 III.1–5 gives the voluntary/involuntary, choice, deliberation and responsibility context for agency. Read the qualifications carefully: neither external influence nor ignorance is automatically a complete excuse. Ask what kind of ignorance matters, how particular circumstances affect action, and how choice relates to what is within our power. X.6–9 changes the explanatory setting toward happiness, activity, education and legislation. Do not silently treat Aristotle’s complete account as equivalent to habit formation or as a modern theory of autonomous choice. These are reading orientations; exact Irwin passages must be inspected before close attribution is assessed.

For Mengzi, follow each analogy and dialogue before extracting a definition of nature. In 6A1–10 ask how the examples bear on development, vulnerability, discrimination and moral motivation. In 2A2 track the relation between sustained cultivation and attempts to force its result. In 7A1–21 attend to what is being extended, preserved or realized rather than assuming one modern psychological model. A claim that people have morally relevant beginnings need not mean all actual conduct is good, nor that education is dispensable. Those stronger claims require textual evidence.

Read Xunzi 19, 22 and 23 together. Ritual is not merely an arbitrary list of rules: ask how the account links human desires, conflict, ordering and cultivated conduct. Chapter 22 matters because an agency interpretation must account for naming, the mind’s discriminations, approval and desire; reducing the dispute to a slogan from chapter 23 would bypass that evidence. Do not read “nature” and “deliberate effort” as contemporary genetic/environmental variables without argument. Their conceptual roles must be recovered from this translation and its passages.

The modern debate is a required research problem, not a supplied answer. For B4 and B5 separately identify: what they claim the primary disagreement is; which passages they invoke; what they think the opponent’s reading gets wrong; and what consequence follows for agency. Then place their claims side by side. Do not invent a convenient opposition such as “Van Norden sees disagreement, Soles sees none.” The curriculum explicitly warns against the latter claim. Their full arguments were not available during this authoring, so the exact scholarly contrast remains blocked until read. The lesson teaches how to establish it and supplies all tasks; it cannot certify a conclusion from unseen pages.

## Original worked case and practice

**B-P: The apprentice mediator.** A fictional apprentice initially follows a ritualized speaking order. She later notices an anxious participant before her turn and proposes a modified order. Her teacher approves the modification because it serves the practice’s stated purpose. The case stipulates learned skill and reflective adjustment, but not moral excellence.

Worked analysis: the case refutes neither the importance of spontaneous concern nor the importance of training. Both a development account and an account emphasizing deliberate practices could accommodate it under different explanations. The useful comparison asks what each account says training is developing or transforming, and why the modification is justified. A shared verdict would not establish a shared account of agency. Aristotle enters only after each Chinese text is reconstructed in its own intellectual setting; he is not a neutral measure of whether it counts as philosophy.

B-P1: 400 words comparing two explanations of the apprentice’s change; cite Mengzi and Xunzi and state one comparison limit. B-P2: a translation note on one short 6A passage or Xunzi 22 claim: identify the exact wording in the retained translation and either a lawful second identified rendering or the translator’s explicit note. Explain a philosophical consequence of the choice; do not claim to verify Chinese fidelity. B-P3: 300 words distinguishing voluntary action, developed character and reflective modification in B1 and the case. Show where the comparison stops.

## Independent changed case M2-I-B

A fictional child reacts sympathetically to familiar people but ignores strangers. Years of practice extend her concern to strangers. An observer concludes that the improvement proves she had no morally relevant initial dispositions. In 250–350 words evaluate that inference with B2 6A1–10 and B3 23, bringing in chapter 22 where relevant. Identify a disagreement that could remain even if both accounts expect improvement. Criteria: distinguish development from absence, preserve the texts’ different explanations, cite real passages and mark a limit. No live help or worked-case copying.

Repair: when the comparison produces slogans, reconstruct one argument per author without mentioning the other. When a translation note becomes an unsupported claim about Chinese, reduce it to a documented consequence of identified renderings. If B4/B5 access is unresolved, keep R4 and its assessment criterion open; do not invent agreement or opposition. Retrieve the strongest comparison limit at M4/M6; complete the genuinely delayed case in M8. Next: start M1 with the retained translations and source diary.

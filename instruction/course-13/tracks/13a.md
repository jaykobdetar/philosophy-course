## Specialist teaching: the inference is the object of inquiry

Turing’s opening replaces a broad question with a specified game. That move makes an inquiry more tractable; it does not by itself define every mental property. When interpreting an operational test, separate the task, the evidence a result supplies and the philosophical conclusion somebody draws from it. If successful performance is evidence of understanding, that is a further inference whose strength needs defense. A claim about understanding is also distinct from a claim about subjective experience.

Searle’s thought experiment asks whether implementing a program is sufficient for understanding. The central contrast is between producing appropriate symbols and understanding their meaning. The systems reply shifts the bearer of understanding from the person following instructions to the organized system. Searle’s internalization reply argues that incorporating the rule resources does not change the individual’s lack of understanding. To evaluate the exchange, identify the contested relation between the individual, the whole system and the property. Do not answer a systems claim merely by repeating a fact about one component; equally, saying “emergence” supplies no argument about which organization matters. These points are supported by inspected pp.417–419; the complete main article remains assigned.

Use A3 for its discussion of language-model development, risks and meaning, once the full article is available. Its subject is not a complete theory of consciousness. Ask which assumptions about communicative context support its argument and which harms could occur regardless of a system’s experience. Exact attribution and the argument’s section boundary remain source-dependent here. A philosophical distinction between harmful output and experienced suffering does not deny either kind of concern.

Chalmers distinguishes subjective experience from intelligent behavior and examines proposed evidence both for and against consciousness. His essay is a dated argument, not an empirical verdict on future systems. Butlin et al. adopt computational functionalism as a working hypothesis and develop a provisional indicator approach. Their v3 explicitly cautions that satisfying indicators does not establish consciousness. Compare what a proposed indicator would support under stated assumptions with what its absence would establish. Choose **recurrent processing versus global workspace** for the default close contrast; other taught contrasts require the same two-source reconstruction. These source summaries are orientation; use the full assigned readings for the detailed reconstruction.

Keep an inference ledger with five columns: observed/stipulated feature; proposed mental property; connecting premise; support; defeating evidence. “It says it is conscious” belongs in the first column. Whether its reports track experience rather than a learned conversational pattern is a question for the connecting premise. “It has a module called workspace” is likewise a label until a functional account is supplied. A necessary condition, sufficient condition, defeasible indicator and ethical precaution have different logical roles.

## Original worked case and coached practice

**Practice A-P: The archive guide.** A fictional museum guide answers questions accurately from a fixed text collection. In a new exhibition it repeats an outdated location. Updating its documents fixes the answer. It also says, “I remember being confused,” though no evidence about internal processing beyond this description is supplied.

Worked analysis: accurate answers establish some task performance; sensitivity to documents shows one dependency of that performance. Neither observation alone settles whether the guide understands the exhibits or experiences confusion. The self-report could arise under competing explanations. A useful question is whether the evidence discriminates those explanations, not whether one finds the guide charming. This example is deliberately underdetermined; do not invent architecture to settle it.

Practice output A-P1: 350 words separating performance, understanding and experience, with one missing premise for each disputed inference. A-P2: one-page contrast of two A5 theories, listing what counts as a candidate indicator, what further assumptions are needed and a limitation. A-P3: 200-word distinction between harms to users and possible harms to a conscious system. Cite A3/A4/A5 only for claims actually found in them.

## Independent changed case M2-I-A

A fictional planner has limited shared storage accessible by several task modules. It cannot produce language or self-reports. An analyst concludes that it is conscious because it has “a global workspace”; another concludes it is not conscious because it cannot converse. In 250–350 words assess both inferences using A1 and the chosen A5 sections, identifying exactly which further premises are needed. Do not equate a software label with the report’s functional criteria. Criteria: distinguish evidence type, attribute the theoretical condition carefully, preserve uncertainty where warranted. Task-specific anchors are withheld in examiner notes.

Repair: if all mental terms collapse into “intelligence,” construct three pairs where one property is stipulated and another left open; then cite the passage that distinguishes them. If a proposed theory comparison turns into engineering speculation, return to A5’s definitions and the selected contrast. Delayed retrieval: at M4 and M6 recall the connecting premise without notes; those short checks do not replace M8’s delayed case. Next: begin M1, preserving the original source and assistance record.

# Practice exemplars — compare reasoning, not prose polish

These are original teaching examples, not answers to independent assessment cases. Use during allocated M2/M4/M6 practice/feedback hours. Actual author claims still require exact sources; do not copy this prose into assessed work.

## Comparison response ladder

**Question:** In a fictional workshop, one teacher says training develops an existing capacity to notice others’ needs. Another says training reorganizes initially conflicting impulses into a stable practice. Both favor repeated participation in shared activities. Does that shared recommendation establish agreement about cultivation?

**Developing:** “Both teachers think people become better by training, so they have the same theory. The disagreement is just different words for nature.”

Annotation: identifies a shared recommendation but the inference to identical theory is unsupported. It ignores what training does and dismisses disagreement without checking consequences. Repair: compare the explanatory processes.

**Competent:** “The teachers agree that repeated participation matters. They differ in their stated explanation: development of a capacity in the first account and reorganization of impulses in the second. Their common recommendation therefore does not establish theoretical identity. We would need to know whether the first teacher also allows reorganization and whether the second accepts capacities that training develops. The brief descriptions permit a disagreement but do not settle how deep it is.”

Annotation: separates practical verdict from explanation, represents both claims and identifies missing information. It avoids inventing a historical attribution. A seminar paper needs primary passages rather than these stipulations.

**Strong:** “Agreement about repeated practice leaves open at least two questions: the source of the standards governing practice and the process by which learners become responsive to them. The descriptions suggest different answers to the second, but neither fixes the first. A discriminating case would ask what each teacher says when an untrained learner notices a need that the established practice overlooks. One account might treat this as a capacity’s correction of practice; the other might explain it through a different learned discrimination. Those are possibilities to test, not views established by the descriptions. The shared recommendation is genuine but limited; an adequate comparison must identify both the remaining explanatory difference and evidence that could erase it.”

Annotation: separates dimensions and designs a discriminating question while acknowledging overlap. Strong does not mean asserting irreconcilable traditions.

## Interpretation draft → revision

**Invented handbook passage:** “At first apprentices act only after reminders. With experience, some notice needs before being prompted. The teaching method aims to encourage this change.”

**Draft:** “The handbook proves that nobody has concern before training and that training always creates it.”

**Feedback:** “At first” describes these apprentices, “some” restricts the result, and “aims” describes an intention. The draft makes each a universal causal conclusion. Separate observed change from intended effect.

**Revision:** “The passage contrasts prompted action with some apprentices’ later unprompted attention. It describes the teaching method as aiming at this transition, but does not establish that all learners achieve it or that no prior concern existed. A causal claim needs further evidence about training and alternatives. The passage supports a question about how reminders become less necessary, rather than a conclusion that training creates every relevant disposition.”

**Change memo:** The revision corrects quantifier, scope and evidential force. It abandons an unsupported universal thesis while preserving a tractable question. This is substantive interpretation repair. No actual ancient text is represented by the invented handbook.

## Research-method ladder

**Invented record:** a 2025 journal article’s abstract announces criticism of a theory. Its body is inaccessible; an index calls it highly cited.

- **Developing:** “The paper refutes the theory, and citations confirm it.” Annotation: announced aim is confused with successful argument; citations with agreement.
- **Competent:** “The record identifies a candidate and the abstract announces criticism. I cannot reconstruct or endorse it without the body. I will seek lawful access and record it as abstract-only meanwhile.” Annotation: matches claim to inspection and supplies an action.
- **Strong:** “Its target must be the premise my paper uses rather than another version of the theory. I will inspect the target definition, central argument and treatment of my cited source. If access remains unavailable after a bounded library check, I will use an accessible serious challenge and report this lead separately.” Annotation: relevance test and finite fallback, with no invented conclusion.

## Institutional argument draft → revision

**Draft:** “Every person has the same form, so the application procedure is fair.”

**Objection:** Formal availability does not establish usable access or appropriate consideration.

**Revision:** “Providing the same form meets one sameness condition. Whether it is fair also depends on access and evaluation: a text-only form can exclude oral testimony, and received statements can still be discounted on irrelevant grounds. My argument establishes formal uniformity; fuller fairness requires evidence about these conditions.”

**Memo:** Accepts a missing-premise objection and narrows the conclusion; does not assert every uniform form is unfair. It leaves an empirical check and philosophical question. To use Young, Anderson, Fricker or Dotson, reconstruct their own criterion and cite it first. This invented revision is not their quotation or a verified synthesis.

Annotate each sentence that makes an inferential advance, then revise your own practice without borrowing wording. Name a cost of the stronger answer: narrower claim, new source dependency or acknowledged alternative. Justified qualification is evidence of calibration.

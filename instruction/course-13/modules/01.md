# C13-M01 — Entry and question selection (10 hours)

**Question:** What can a 3,000-word argument establish with the sources available? Prerequisite: selected route prerequisites and their independent evidence. Outcomes: reconstruct two relevant arguments without notes; distinguish a topic, question and thesis; narrow a question with explicit exclusions and a feasible source plan.

Read the selected [route packet](../README.md) and its exact [ledger](../sources.md). Orient by reading each modern article’s abstract, introduction and conclusion; for primary books without abstracts, use B1 III.1 and X.9, B2 2A2 opening/6A1/7A1, and B3 opening and closing paragraphs of each assigned chapter. For C use each chapter’s opening and closing two paragraphs, plus the article abstracts. For A read A1 §1, A2 abstract, A3 abstract/conclusion, A4 introduction/conclusion and A5 abstract. These are **orientation**, never evidence of whole-text reading. Inaccessible orientation remains marked unavailable.

## Teaching

A topic names an area: “AI consciousness,” “human nature,” or “epistemic injustice.” A research question identifies an uncertainty whose answer could be contested using an identifiable body of evidence. A thesis is your provisional answer. “What did every philosopher think?” has no bounded dispute. “Does this passage support an attribution of a necessary condition?” is tractable if you can name the passage, the condition and an alternative reading.

Narrow by fixing five variables: object of interpretation, predicate under dispute, comparison partner if needed, evidence boundary and intended conclusion. A limit is productive when it identifies what your evidence cannot decide. A paper can show that an inference fails without proving its conclusion false. It can establish a comparative distinction without claiming influence or equivalence across traditions. State the kind of result you seek before deciding which author agrees with you.

An access plan is part of philosophical feasibility. If a thesis depends on a disputed sentence in an unavailable translation, choose a provisional question and record the blockage. Do not replace the sentence with a fluent paraphrase generated from memory. A hypothesis about a text becomes an interpretation only through evidence from that text.

**Worked example, invented:** “Can machines understand?” becomes “Does the inference from a rule-following individual’s nonunderstanding to a larger system’s nonunderstanding require an additional premise?” This identifies an inference rather than all machine intelligence. The proposed answer might be “Yes: a premise connecting the individual’s capacities to the relevant organized whole.” The exclusions are current commercial systems, moral status and every possible theory of understanding. This is not yet a refutation of Searle: his internalization response must still be considered.

## Practice and outputs

Write three questions in your route. Under each put one possible answer, two assigned source locators, a rival answer and one uncertainty. Reject two questions with reasons. Record two five-minute explanations without notes of prerequisite arguments, then inspect the sources and mark missing premises in a different color or separate revision column. Save Q, a 400-word question note: question (40 words), significance (60), provisional argument (120), evidence/access (100), exclusions (80). Include the recordings or text equivalents as ENT.

**Independent changed task M1-I:** A proposed seminar aims to prove that “every socially learned response is inauthentic” using one fictional school and one paragraph from an author. In 200 words give a feasible revised question, say what conclusion that evidence might support, identify an exclusion and specify one further source check. Sources visible; no AI, tutor, answer key or draft reuse. Criteria: scope fits evidence, conclusion is qualified, and source check is actionable. Answers are in examiner notes.

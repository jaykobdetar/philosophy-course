## Teaching

Reconstruction is a disciplined compression of reasoning. Begin with the local question and identify the passage’s role: asserting a conclusion, reporting a rival, introducing a hypothetical or answering a challenge. A conditional spoken by an opponent is not the author’s endorsement. Number explicit premises, bracket supplied premises, and indicate whether support is deductive, explanatory, analogical or evidential. An inference may be sensible without being deductively valid; its strength then depends on the alternatives and background assumptions.

A definition dispute concerns how a term divides cases. An evidence dispute concerns what observations or passages establish. An inferential dispute concerns what follows even if the starting points are shared. A purpose dispute concerns what problem the text is trying to solve. These can coexist. Saying two authors “both value society” does not specify a philosophical agreement. Conversely, different words may express connected claims without being interchangeable.

For a rival interpretation, list the textual observations each explains and the difficult passage each must accommodate. An interpretation earns preference through explanatory fit and restraint, not how well it supports your thesis. Your improvement can be worth defending, but label it: “I add premise P; the author does not state it here.” A secondary scholar is evidence of a reading, not automatic evidence that the primary text says it.

**Worked example, invented:** A training handbook says “Volunteers initially follow reminders; eventually they notice needs without reminders.” Reconstruction: training can change attention; some trained attention becomes self-directed; therefore external prompts need not remain necessary for every subsequent action. This does **not** establish that untrained people have no relevant dispositions, that all prompting is coercion, or that the action is virtuous. Those are stronger conclusions requiring further premises. A rival reading treats the passage as a practical aim rather than an observed result; genre and surrounding sentences decide between them.

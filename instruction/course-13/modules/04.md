# C13-M04 — Debate map and prospectus (16 hours)

**Question:** Where can my argument make a small, defensible contribution? Prerequisites: core reconstructions and D with two completed readings. Outcomes: map three positions on one question; distinguish disagreement from parallel inquiry; specify a testable thesis and its strongest objection; write a feasible prospectus.

Reading is targeted rereading, not a new packet: use the three route-specific M4 loci and the exact objection passage in each selected Module 3 source. Write these locators at the top of DM. All editions and URLs remain those in [sources](../sources.md) and D. If a source is unavailable, the corresponding map edge stays provisional.

## Teaching

A debate map is organized by an issue, not a chronology of famous names. Give each position the same question, the answer it supports, its main reason, and what would count against it. If a source answers a different question, put it in a context box rather than force it onto an axis. Three positions need not mean three authors: one author may distinguish two views, or you may formulate a third alternative clearly marked as your proposal.

The contribution should be small enough to expose to criticism. You might show that a standard inference needs a missing premise, that a passage permits a neglected qualification, or that a proposed remedy addresses a different mechanism from the diagnosed wrong. You need not establish historical novelty across all scholarship. “Nobody has ever noticed” is a research claim requiring much more evidence than “this distinction improves the argument considered here.”

An objection becomes serious when accepting it would require changing the thesis, a pivotal premise, an interpretation or the scope. A counterexample attacks a general claim by keeping its stated conditions and changing its result. If you change the conditions, you may have found a limit rather than a counterexample. Put this distinction directly in the prospectus so the eventual paper has a manageable burden of proof.

**Worked example, invented:** Thesis: “Every fair hearing must allow unlimited speaking time.” Reason: limits can prevent relevant testimony. Objection: unlimited time can exclude others by exhausting the session. Revision: a fair hearing needs a procedure for receiving relevant testimony beyond the initial time limit, with an accessible reply route. The contribution is now a procedural distinction. It still needs evidence that the procedure is usable; announcing an appeal is not enough.

## Practice and outputs

Write **DM, 1,000 words**: question (100), three positions (200 each), pivotal disagreement (150), your proposed intervention and limits (150). Use a textual table or numbered map as a planning aid; prose must explain the arrows. Each position needs an exact supporting source and a fair objection. Then write **P, 600 words**: question/thesis (100), interpretation and evidence (150), argument (150), strongest objection/reply strategy (100), exclusions/access/work plan (100). Do a feasibility test: can one paragraph establish the key inference with two exact passages? If not, narrow before drafting.

**Independent changed task M4-I:** Three proposed positions say: (i) a system produces reliable reports; (ii) its reports have social effects; (iii) its reports express subjective experience. A draft labels these “three contradictory theories.” In 250 words identify whether and how a genuine disagreement could be formed, state a defensible common question, and identify a missing connecting premise. This original methods case applies to every route; do not attribute it to an author. Sources visible; no assistance. Criteria: distinguish predicates and avoid manufacturing disagreement.

# C13-M05 — Seminar paper draft (24 hours)

**Question:** Does the paper actually establish its stated conclusion? Prerequisite: feasible P and completed D. Outcomes: write a 2,500–3,500-word source-grounded argument; distinguish author interpretation from your improvement; explain each secondary source’s contribution; expose rather than hide a serious objection.

Read only the exact passages in P/D that support contested premises; use the route’s M5 question and [sources](../sources.md). Record new locators before relying on them. No unbudgeted monograph is required. A question change that needs extensive reading is a scope/time amendment, not invisible extra work.

## Teaching

Draft the argument’s spine before the introduction: conclusion, two or three premises, evidence for each, objection, response. The purpose of a paragraph is a step in that spine. A quotation does not perform the inference for you. State what the passage supports and why that matters to the question. If the source offers an example rather than a universal claim, explain the premise required to generalize.

Keep three voices distinct: the primary author, the commentator and yourself. “X holds P; Y reads P as Q; I argue Q requires R” is a useful drafting pattern. It prevents a commentator’s modernization from becoming a false primary quotation. A philosophical objection need not contradict the author’s values; it can challenge the relation between premise and conclusion. An interpretation may be accurate while the argument remains unpersuasive.

Allocate words according to argumentative work. A workable 3,000-word plan is question/thesis 250, textual reconstruction 700, argument 850, objection/reply 800, limits/conclusion 250, and 150 for transitions. This is a planning tool, not a rigid template. Historical context earns space when it changes interpretation, scope or evidence. A paper that spends 2,500 words surveying five thinkers has little room to defend its own inference.

**Worked example, original draft:** “The committee’s new appeal form solves exclusion because anyone can submit it.” The missing premise is that formal availability yields usable access and appropriate uptake. Better argument: “The form removes one documented submission barrier if it is accessible and appeals are actually considered; it does not by itself remove credibility prejudice.” This revision reduces the conclusion to what the stated intervention could establish. It is a model of inferential repair, not an empirical claim about any institution.

## Practice and outputs

Write E1 in four passes: (1) 500-word argument spine with numbered claims; (2) complete textual reconstruction and objections; (3) connect sections and write introduction; (4) reverse outline and citation audit. Beside each paragraph write its role: reconstruct, support, object, reply or qualify. For every secondary source, append a private one-sentence note identifying its contribution. Delete ornamental citations. Mark unresolved claims `[CHECK: locator/interpretation/evidence]`; do not turn them into invented facts to make the draft smooth.

Save E1, word count, source list, reverse outline and assistance declaration. Tutor may discuss a different example or diagnose one inference; **no AI-generated assessed prose or rewrites**. Dictation of the learner’s own prose and non-content accessibility tools are allowed and declared.

**Independent changed task M5-I:** In an invented debate, Source A says, “The committee’s efficiency argument does not establish that its policy is just.” Source B says, “The policy is just because it protects an independently justified right.” A draft calls these logically inconsistent positions and treats A as having proved the policy unjust. In 300 words distinguish criticism of an argument from denial of its conclusion, reconstruct what each source claims, and state the extra premise needed for the draft’s inference. Then change A’s claim to “No policy of this kind can be just”: explain what disagreement now arises and what evidence the writer must supply. Rewrite the comparison’s scope. This is a fictional methods case, not a historical attribution. No live help or copying E1. Criteria: argument/conclusion distinction, explicit inferential burden, accurate response to the changed claim and restrained attribution.

## Budget, repair and next step

Targeted rereading/source checks **3h**; outline/spine **3h**; drafting **12h**; source-bound feedback **1h**; independent task **1h**; reverse outline/citation correction **2h**; draft revision **2h** = **24h**. If the thesis shifts mid-paper, freeze two alternative conclusions and test which is supported. Do not patch with style edits. Retrieve the original thesis after a rest interval during Module 6’s first block and compare with the draft’s actual conclusion. Next: prepare a bounded review packet with the pivotal source passages and two review questions.

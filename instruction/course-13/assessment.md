# Course 13 assessment — release one form at a time

This assessment implements the three essential rows in [current assessment](../../current/assessment.md#course-13). It adds no hours to 160h. M7: DEF 30 minutes and T1 800 words/two hours planned. M8: SA 30 minutes, and T2 250–350 words/30 minutes after roughly four to six weeks or two later modules. These are distinct occasions. Adjust pacing for access needs; log actual time. E2, source dossier and revisions are also assessed, using the shared rubric, without substituting for the independent rows.

**Conditions:** assigned texts and a citation sheet allowed. No AI content help, tutor hints, examiner key, prewritten answers or copying paper prose during independent attempts. Accessibility/dictation tools may transmit the learner’s own work and are declared. Assessor records prior exposure; use the alternate if the first form was rehearsed. Keep task-specific anchors in the separate examiner notes. Every case below is invented and concerns no identifiable person or institution.

## DEF — 30 minutes, M7

Give a ten-minute explanation: question, thesis, strongest textual reason, objection, revision and remaining limit. Then answer twenty minutes of new questions. Assessor asks the common prompts plus your route question:

1. Which premise of your argument is most vulnerable, and what would follow if it failed?
2. Why did you reject one rival interpretation or source? Identify a passage or inferential reason.
3. What source/translation decision mattered, and how would another plausible decision change the argument?
4. A: If the feature you treat as evidence were produced through an independently specified imitation mechanism, what would your inference establish? B: If two theories predict the same trained conduct, what evidence could still establish a substantive disagreement? C: If the harm decreases but decision authority remains unchanged, what does your remedy establish and leave open?

Alternate route questions: A, does losing self-report remove your reason for attributing experience? B, does spontaneous success remove the need for a cultivation account? C, does accurate comprehension remove the possibility of epistemic wrongdoing? Ask a genuine follow-up based on the answer; no scripted model prose is required.

## T1 — independent 800-word transfer, M7

For the chosen route use **F1**, or **F2** if exposed. Cite two or more exact assigned passages, explaining their inferential roles. Include one objection, evidence limits and a qualified conclusion. Sources must be available to the assessor for source-dependent judgments.

### A-F1: Divergent theory profiles

Two fictional systems produce indistinguishably fluent conversation. System R has recurrent perceptual processing but no global broadcasting between task modules. System W broadcasts selected information between modules, but its perceptual processing is stipulated to be feedforward. The descriptions are incomplete about other features. An evaluator says both must have the same consciousness status because their conversation matches; another assigns each a score of one and declares them equally likely to be conscious. Analyze what two different theory frameworks justify, and whether either proposed comparison follows, using A1 and your A5 contrast with one A4 distinction. Identify missing premises and evidence; do not assume equal weight for indicators or that the cases satisfy all conditions of either theory.

### A-F2: The copied reports

Two fictional systems generate the same reports of pain. System P selects stored sentences from a lookup table triggered by input labels; system Q has a described feedback process connecting error signals, action selection and learning. Neither is established to have subjective experience. A reviewer concludes identical outputs require identical moral status; another concludes Q’s feedback proves suffering. Analyze the distinct inferential burdens using A2, A4 and A5 §4.1. Do not infer that any stipulated error signal is experienced pain.

### B-F1: Cultivation under deprivation

A fictional learner has a record of considerate conduct and sustained practice. During months of hunger and disrupted community life she becomes inattentive to others and once breaks a commitment to obtain food. When support and practice resume, some concern returns. An observer says the lapse proves she never had morally relevant dispositions; another says her earlier training makes every later lapse fully blameworthy regardless of circumstances. Analyze these inferences with B2 6A1–10 and B3, then use B1 III.1–5 to distinguish one question of voluntary action or responsibility from a question about development. The case does not stipulate the severity of the threat, alternatives available, or the learner’s knowledge. Identify what those missing facts could change and a limit of the cross-tradition comparison.

### B-F2: The disciplined imitator

A fictional apprentice follows a fair rule whenever supervised, can repeat its rationale, but abandons it when a shortcut benefits him. After sustained practice he begins following it unobserved, although his reasons remain unclear. An observer calls both stages equally virtuous because behavior under supervision was identical. Another says repetition alone establishes reflective endorsement. Analyze the change through B1, B2 and B3, distinguishing outward performance, motivation and cultivation. Identify evidence each account would need, without turning the authors into contemporary behavioral scientists.

### C-F1: The accepted but filtered record

A fictional housing council accepts residents’ reports as truthful and understands their vocabulary for recurring displacement. Its software nevertheless classifies these reports as “personal preference,” making them ineligible for policy review. Staff follow this rule without open hostility. A reform increases compensation but retains the classification rule and excludes residents from revising it. Compare two core frameworks, explain the philosophical and empirical premises, and evaluate whether the reform answers the identified wrong. Consider an objection that the remaining problem is administrative rather than epistemic.

### C-F2: The credible spokesperson

A fictional professional association routinely discounts junior members’ reports about inaccessible meetings. It appoints a prestigious senior spokesperson; reports are now believed when repeated by that person. Junior members still cannot submit directly or shape the categories in the reporting form. Leaders claim the epistemic problem is solved because accuracy improved. Compare two core frameworks, distinguish improvement from completion, and propose one bounded remedy with a possible drawback. Do not assume every junior member shares one experience.

## Completion record

Fabricated evidence must be corrected before assessment proceeds. A missing text is an access dependency, not learner failure and not a pass.

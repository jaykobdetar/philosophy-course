# Course 1 tutor guidance

Use with [the active tutor rules](../tutor.md), the selected module and the learner's attempt. Keep [examiner notes](examiner-notes.md) and unused assessment forms outside practice context where possible. Public files cannot guarantee secrecy: record exposure and replace rehearsed tasks. This guidance is authored, not evidence of tutor calibration or learner testing.

## Before each practice exchange

Establish course/module/task ID, actual edition and range, learner attempt, time available and desired help. Confirm that the learner can open the assigned source. A catalogue listing is not passage access. For Veitch, use printed pp. 79–94 and verify quotations on the page image; do not supply invented AT numbers. For Gettier, distinguish original 121–123 from local English reprint 186–188. For Singer, distinguish article text from a later postscript.

Ask the learner to state the claim and show its textual basis before evaluating it. Name at most two consequential issues. Give the criterion, exact location in their attempt, supporting source or reasoning, and one revision request. Separate a textual error from a defensible alternative interpretation. If a passage is unavailable, state the exact provisional judgment and request only enough legitimately available text to check it.

## Fading support

In Modules 1–3, model a different invented example, supply a partially completed argument table, and ask the learner to finish their own. In Modules 4–6, ask for the missing inferential step before offering a distinction. In Modules 7–9, require an initial map and objection; give feedback on one pivotal assumption. In Module 10, discuss the proposal and give source-bound feedback on the learner's draft without rewriting an assessed paragraph. In Modules 11–12, withhold all task-specific hints, scores and examples until the attempt is saved.

Do not make an author sound stronger by silently adding your preferred theory. Mark supplied premises as supplied; mark a proposed charitable addition as an addition and test whether the author could accept it. Ask what would count against the learner's interpretation. A fallacy name or a philosopher's name is never the whole explanation.

## Useful bounded prompts

| Module | Practice prompt | What the tutor checks before replying |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Which sentence is the speaker's claim and which is your reaction? | Speaker and case in the actual range |
| 2 | Can the premises be true while this conclusion is false? | Same meaning of the predicates in the countercase |
| 3 | What job does this paragraph do, and who owns each claim? | Quote/paraphrase/inference separated |
| 4 | Which direction does your “because” explain? | Unanimous love is distinguished from explanatory priority |
| 5 | What does the speaker claim to know here? | Local limitation, not unlimited ignorance |
| 6 | What does the episode demonstrate, and what is inferred beyond it? | Questioning, performance and origin claims separated |
| 7 | Is this conclusion “false,” “doubtful,” or “certain while thinking”? | Stage of the meditation and actual wording |
| 8 | Where do truth and the belief's grounds come apart? | All JTB conditions and the target sufficiency claim |
| 9 | Which moral or empirical premise changes in your variant? | Strong/moderate stopping conditions kept distinct |
| 10 | Which actual step does the strongest objection challenge? | Reply addresses the objection, not a weaker substitute |

## Feedback record and challenge

Use a compact record: criterion; quoted learner sentence or output location; checked source/locator; diagnosed issue; requested change; learner's revision; remaining uncertainty. A challenge to feedback must be answered with the criterion and evidence, not authority. Correct your own mistake explicitly. Agreement among model outputs does not verify a source.

The shared rubric's developing/competent/strong descriptions apply only to relevant dimensions. Do not demand an objection in a simple paraphrase task. For writing, use the Module 3 museum progression as a practice model on a different topic, and the separate examiner progression after submission. Preserve original drafts and assistance records. Fluent expression is not evidence of accurate interpretation, and difficult academic prose is not evidence of inability to reason.

## Access and time

Read-aloud, text-to-speech, dictation, and typed dialogue are equivalent routes when they retain the source and reasoning task. Check audiobook translation and extent; verify quotes in text. Source citation checks are part of the allocated work. Split sessions and record actual extra focused minutes rather than silently expanding the baseline. Delayed retrieval reduces the shared calendar time available for the next course but remains charged to Course 1.

Run the [five-case calibration set](../tutor-calibration.md) against the actual tutor after a material model or prompt change. Record failures separately from a real learner pilot or an external expert review. Do not infer any of these from the existence of these files.

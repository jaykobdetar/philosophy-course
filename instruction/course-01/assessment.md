They are open-book self-study tasks, not secure examinations or the full Course 1 independent demonstration. ## Delayed changed case

Schedule this **opening** check at the first study session after four to six weeks from Session 4, or on completion of the second subsequent module, whichever comes first. Record the chosen calendar trigger after Session 4; do not postpone it until Module 11. Use **Form D**: reread *Euthyphro* **5d up to before 6d** in the same marked PDF and explain, with one locator, how an example and a proposed general answer differ. First state from memory what a valid inference rules out. Then analyze: “If a parcel has express postage, it has a tracking number. This parcel lacks a tracking number. Therefore it lacks express postage.” Explain why the direction differs from Form A or B. Open the source and correct the interpretation. Debit its 15-minute attempt once from Module 12's first retrieval session, which is performed at this earlier trigger; that whole 45-minute retrieval session remains part of the 120-hour baseline. It takes time from the course module then being studied. The full-course delayed check has its own later trigger below.

**Exposure rule:** These forms are public in the repository. If the learner has studied a form or an assessor has already supplied its answer, mark that form exposed and do not call its result independent. Have a person choose a different short slice within the already assigned 5d–8b and a new conditional with the same valid/invalid structure, record both exact prompts and why they are comparable, and keep any tutor feedback closed until the attempt. If no fresh comparable form is available, mark the criterion unresolved rather than invent a score.

## Feedback challenge

If an assessor calls an interpretation wrong, ask for the exact criterion, edition/locator, and inference that fails. The assessor should revise an unsupported judgment or mark it provisional. Philosophical disagreement alone is not evidence of incompetence. Keep the challenge and resolution in the substantive record.


## Full-course fresh demonstration

Use these forms in [Module 11](modules/11.md). Choose F-A by default, F-B when an A item was rehearsed; forms may be mixed by row to avoid exposure. Each reasoning, interpretation and writing row needs an uncoached sample. The examiner records the exact selection before the attempt. The learner may use assigned primary texts and a citation sheet, but no content assistance, examiner notes, prewritten responses or AI feedback until the response is saved. Declare rehearsal, prior answers and assistance. These are public self-study forms, not secure examinations.

**Timing and outputs:** Task 1: 30 minutes, 200–300 words; Task 2: 60 minutes, 300–450 words interpreting a 200–350-word passage; Task 3: 60 minutes, 500–700 words. Total **150 minutes**, plus a separate twenty-minute dialogue/typed equivalent. Adjust time for access needs without lowering the reasoning demand. Do not attempt to fit all three into a generic ninety-minute slot.

### F-A1 — Arguments and conditions

All cases are invented. Analyse both arguments:

1. “Every temperature-controlled case has a working sensor. Case R has no working sensor. Therefore R is not temperature-controlled.”
2. “Every temperature-controlled case has a working sensor. Case R has a working sensor. Therefore R is temperature-controlled.”

Number premises/conclusions, decide whether the conclusion must follow, and explain a possible countercase wherever appropriate. Assess “having a working sensor is necessary and sufficient for being temperature-controlled.” State what further premise would establish any missing direction. Distinguish that conditional test from evidence that the stipulated universal premise is actually true.

### F-B1 — Arguments and conditions

Invented policy: every manuscript eligible for blind review has identifying details removed. Compare “this manuscript is eligible, therefore its identifying details are removed” with “this manuscript is ineligible, therefore its identifying details are not removed.” Supply a reasoned validity judgment and any countercase. Test “removing identifying details guarantees eligibility”; say what additional policy information would be needed. Explain why an argument with a true conclusion may nevertheless fail this test.

### F-A2 — Passage interpretation

Read the three consecutive paragraphs of the CHS *Apology* beginning with the paragraph immediately containing **28e** and ending just before **29c**: the first begins “Strange, indeed,” and the last is the whole 29b paragraph. This is approximately 300 words in the named adaptation; exclude navigation and bracketed Greek glosses when counting. It lies within the complete assigned dialogue and the M05 focused range. Use the exact CHS credits in your citation.

Explain who speaks, the proposed relationship between fear, claimed knowledge, and wrongdoing, and one assumption needed by the reasoning. Distinguish the speaker's argument from the author's historical record. Develop one objection that addresses the actual inference and say whether it challenges an ethical claim or a claim about knowledge. Do not treat a passage as unseen merely because its assessment question is new.

### F-B2 — Passage interpretation

Read Perlman's *Meno*, PDF pages 54–55, from Socrates' **97a** explanation using the road to Larissa through Meno's question about the difference between knowledge and right opinion at **97d** (stop before Socrates' next question). Start at the main-text road example, omitting the preceding correction and all footnotes. This is approximately 270 words excluding speaker labels/footnotes. Record the exact start/end in your private evidence record; do not substitute a different translation's page number.

Identify the speakers, reconstruct the road example's conclusion, and explain the disagreement about successful guidance. State one distinction that could help answer Meno's remaining question without claiming that this short passage already proves a complete theory of knowledge. Make one textual assumption explicit and evaluate it. The later account of stability may be mentioned as context only with its own locator.

**Passage-size safeguard:** The named editions have the boundaries above. If a host revision or accessible rendition changes the word count outside 200–350, the examiner uses complete sentences from the same stated range to select 200–350 words and records opening/closing locators before the attempt. This is a bounded selection task, not permission to invent a source or silently test unread material.

### F-A3 and F-B3 — A new objection to the essay

Choose the row matching the essay. A is default; B is the alternate. If the actual draft already answered the selected challenge in detail, use the other. If both were rehearsed, the assessor must specify a different objection targeting an actual premise, record it before the attempt and explain its comparable demand. Never pass off a familiar reply as fresh.

| Essay | F-A3 objection | F-B3 objection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Euthyphro | “Your response treats two descriptions with the same instances as if they necessarily gave the same explanation. Why should that follow?” | “Your defended response may repair the account of piety only by introducing a moral standard whose authority it leaves unexplained. Has it answered or relocated the problem?” |
| Gettier | “Your account of false lemmas excludes explicit false premises, but why should luck entering through an unreliable environment be excluded?” | “If your repair excludes every belief with any false background assumption, would it rule out ordinary knowledge too? State and defend the boundary.” |
| Singer | “Your stopping point appeals to important personal projects. What makes their importance sufficient to override preventable serious harm rather than simply naming the cost?” | “Your argument about individual duty assumes others' actual contributions. How would the conclusion change if their shares were guaranteed, and does that support or weaken your original objection?” |

State your essay's relevant claim fairly; reconstruct the objection in its strongest applicable form; answer it with reasons, exact primary-text support, and a justified preservation or revision of the thesis. If the objection presupposes something your essay rejects, explain that mismatch and address the nearest serious version. Do not score a learner by agreement with the objection's framing.

### Dialogue and neutral criteria

In twenty minutes or a paced typed exchange, the examiner asks: (1) Which premise in one submitted response bears most weight? (2) What supplied evidence supports it? (3) How would your conclusion change if that premise were weakened? The examiner selects the response and asks one genuine follow-up based on the answer. No prepared script or new untaught doctrine is required. Assess direct responsiveness and reasoned revision rather than eloquence, accent or speed.

Competent evidence accurately attributes claims, preserves the premises while testing inference, explains condition direction, handles an objection to the actual position, cites sources actually read and organizes the answer intelligibly. Strong evidence also manages a live ambiguity or limitation without evasion. Developing evidence requires targeted repair of the named gap. Fabricated quotations or unattributed borrowing require correction before the affected assessment proceeds. See [outcomes](outcomes.md) for the group-to-artifact mapping and [examiner notes](examiner-notes.md) only after submission.

## Full-course delayed demonstration

Use roughly four to six weeks or two later modules after the independent demonstration, inside Module 12's six hours. The opening delayed Form D was scheduled earlier, using 15 minutes of Module 12's first 45-minute retrieval session. Do not repeat or charge that session again here. The remaining 315 minutes occur at this full-course trigger. If that form is exposed, use opening alternate D0: *Euthyphro* 6d–before 7a, explain the demand for a general account, then analyse the invented conditional “If a ticket is valid, it has a date; this ticket has no date; therefore it is not valid.” This alternate is still only an opening check.

Choose D1 or D2 for the **least secure group**, 30 minutes, with a 200–300-word output. Start with two minutes recalling the relevant distinction, then permit the named assigned text/citation sheet. No content help or key access before submission. These cases differ from the fresh and module practice cases.

Sources may remain open. These are two distinct tasks in one 60-minute block, not a hidden second hour.

After both are saved, check sources and receive feedback in the separately budgeted feedback block. Correct the record and use an unexposed alternate for any needed retest within Module 12's final allocation. If no comparable fresh task remains, record the gap and arrange one; never substitute a copied answer. Log actual time beyond the planned budget. Delayed evidence belongs to Course 1, even while studying Course 2.

# C01-M07 — Descartes and doubt

**Planned allocation: 12 focused hours (720 minutes), within the 120-hour baseline.**

**Guiding question:** What, if anything, can be established while reasons for doubt remain?

**Prerequisites and outcomes:** Modules 1–6. Map stages of doubt without treating them as established falsehoods; explain the cogito in its textual setting; evaluate one inference with exact evidence (O1–O5).

**Assigned reading:** Descartes, *Meditations I–II*, complete. Use the [local John Veitch 1912 reading copy](../../../collection/philosophy-study-collection/texts/introduction_logic/required/descartes_veitch_1912_meditations_I-VI.pdf), printed pp. 79–94, PDF pp. 1–16, stopping before Meditation III. The preferred Cottingham, Stoothoff, and Murdoch, *Philosophical Writings*, II (1984), assigned AT VII 17–34, is a separate loan. The local text lacks AT markers. Cite Veitch and its actual printed pages; do not manufacture a parallel AT quotation. OCR has defects: verify a quoted word on the page image. For work that turns on an exact disputed modern translation, obtain that edition before judging the disputed wording.

**Teaching explanation:** Methodical doubt suspends assent to a class of beliefs by showing a relevant possibility of error. It is not a proof that each belief is false. Occasional sensory errors challenge easy trust; dreaming extends the challenge to apparently ordinary surroundings. Some simpler features initially survive that challenge, motivating the broader worry about the reliability of thinking. The powerful deceiver is a supposition used to maintain doubt, not a being whose existence has been demonstrated.

The act of doubting supplies the route to the meditator's existence while thinking. If deception occurs, there must be the one deceived; attempting to doubt one's present existence enacts the thinking that secures it. Whether this is best understood as an inference or an immediately evident performance is a legitimate interpretive question. Neither reading by itself establishes a persistent personal biography, the existence of an external world, or the full real distinction between mind and body. Those would need further steps.

The wax discussion concerns recognition through change and the role of intellectual judgment. Sensible qualities change while the wax is judged to persist; the capacity for indefinite changes exceeds a fixed image. Distinguish certainty of the thinking involved from certainty that an external wax object exists. Knowing oneself as thinking at this stage does not license saying that everything bodily has been proved nonexistent.

**Worked example, invented:** In a simulated room, a displayed clock may be misleading, and the apparent room may be simulated. Yet while I am questioning the display, questioning is occurring. Moving from that modest result to “my memory of yesterday is correct” introduces a new premise. Write the two conclusions separately to see why doubt about one need not defeat the other.

**Practice and outputs:** Create separate maps of the dream argument, deceiver hypothesis, and cogito. For each give a target, reasons, provisional conclusion, and limit. Mark a textual locator for every attributed move. Write 500 words evaluating one inference; include a charitable reading, one objection, and a response. Explain the wax case in 150 words as practice. In a ten-minute spoken or written dialogue, list what survives each stage of doubt and defend one boundary.

**Independent changed case:** Invented claim: “I cannot tell whether this melody is externally played or imagined. Therefore I cannot know whether I seem to hear a melody; therefore nothing about my present thinking is secure.” Analyze both transitions in 200 words using Meditation II. Criteria: separate object, appearance, and act; avoid assuming the whole Cartesian project succeeds merely because one transition fails.

**Repair and retrieval:** Label every “therefore” with its supporting premise. If “doubtful” becomes “false,” rewrite only the affected conclusion. Retrieve Meno's distinction between a demonstrated result and an added origin claim within revision. **Next:** Module 8 tests whether justification and truth suffice for knowledge.

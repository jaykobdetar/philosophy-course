# C01-M10 — First sustained essay

**Planned allocation: 18 focused hours (1080 minutes), within the 120-hour baseline.**

**Guiding question:** Can I defend one bounded philosophical claim through a serious objection?

**Prerequisites and outcomes:** Modules 1–9, with competent passage attribution and claim–reason paragraphs. Write a 1,000–1,200-word essay, revise its reasoning, and explain revision decisions (O1–O5).

**Assigned reading:** Reread only the already assigned primary text selected below and consult Pryor's whole guide, especially drafting and rewriting. No new bibliography is required. Exact editions and access limitations remain those of Modules 4, 8, and 9. Quote only a checked source you have actually read.

**Question selection procedure:** Choose exactly one: (A) “Does the *Euthyphro* dilemma defeat the response you consider strongest?” (B) “Does a no-false-lemmas condition repair the Gettier problem?” (C) “Does Singer's argument impose an objectionably demanding duty?” For A identify a response and whether it is in the dialogue or your reconstruction. For B define the repair's scope and choose a test beyond a renamed original case. For C choose the strong or moderate principle and identify the commitment allegedly sacrificed. Reject any proposal whose answer requires an unavailable indispensable source. Narrow the claim before adding readings.

**Teaching explanation:** An essay's thesis is an answer to the exact question, with a scope a short paper can defend. “Knowledge is complicated” names a topic. “This repair blocks Case I but fails against a specified environmental-luck case, on a stated account of lemmas” gives a claim and exposes where disagreement belongs. A qualified conclusion may be more defensible than a universal one; qualification must not evade the question. An objection should threaten an actual premise, inference, interpretation, or scope claim. A reply must address that threat rather than repeat the thesis.

**Worked outline, invented and outside the assessed topics:** Question: should a museum offer one free evening? Thesis: one free evening is justified if it expands access without undermining essential conservation. Reason: fees exclude some prospective visitors; a targeted reduction can meet that concern. Objection: one evening may be inaccessible to those working late. Reply: this limits the policy's success and motivates a different schedule; it does not show fees never exclude. Evidence needed: opening costs and access patterns. This outline does not invent those empirical findings. Its purpose is to show how a bounded claim, objection, and evidence need fit together.

**Proposal template (200 words):** Question; provisional answer; three supporting steps; strongest anticipated objection; exact primary passages; scope exclusion; assistance declaration. **Outline template:** introduction/question 100 words; fair reconstruction 250; main argument 300; objection and reply 300; conclusion/limit 100. These are flexible planning amounts, not an extra word quota. Attach a source-to-claim table with locator and “quotation/paraphrase/my inference.”

**Practice and outputs:** Write the entire first draft yourself. Reverse-outline it by paragraph function. Give a reviewer the exact question, source locators, your attempt, assistance record, and two questions: “Where is the main inference unsupported?” and “Have I fairly stated the strongest objection?” Request feedback on a different model example if needed, never a replacement assessed paragraph. Preserve comments and the untouched draft. Revise; audit each quotation against the actual edition, every paraphrase against its range, and every inference for attribution. Submit final essay, bibliography, original draft, outline, feedback, and a 250-word revision memo naming two substantive changes and one unresolved limitation. The memo is outside the essay word count.

**Independent changed case:** After drafting, write a 200-word response to this generic but newly applied challenge: “Your conclusion may hold in your main example but fails once the condition you relied upon is absent.” Specify one condition from your own argument, construct a different case removing it, and determine whether your thesis needs narrowing. No content help. Criteria: genuine change, traceable premise, and reasoned preservation or revision of the thesis. This practice does not replace the fresh objection in Module 11.

**Repair and retrieval:** Use the [writing progression](../examiner-notes.md#writing-progression) only after saving the attempt. Repair the most consequential inferential gap before grammar. Retrieve the thesis and strongest objection from memory before the final citation audit. **Next:** Submit the essay and move to Module 11's uncoached assessment.

**Attempt rules:** For every independent task, the assigned source and a citation sheet are allowed; answer keys, prewritten answers and AI/tutor content help are closed until the first response is saved. Record prior exposure and assistance. If rehearsed, use an unexposed alternate from the course assessment or have an assessor document a comparable new prompt; do not call a rehearsed answer fresh. Practice may use the [tutor guidance](../tutor-notes.md). After submission, use the module's anchors in [examiner notes](../examiner-notes.md).

**Accessible route:** Read in text or use text-to-speech on the same assigned edition/range. Pause to record speaker changes and argument steps. A licensed audiobook can support reading only after its translation and coverage are checked against this assignment; do not assume the local compact audio is complete. Check quotations and locators against the written source. Spoken planning and dictation are allowed; preserve a text equivalent of the assessed output. Dialogue may be a paced typed exchange with the same questions. Adjust time without reducing the intellectual task, and log overruns.

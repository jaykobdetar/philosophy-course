# C01-M08 — Gettier and counterexamples

**Planned allocation: 10 focused hours (600 minutes), within the 120-hour baseline.**

**Guiding question:** Can a justified true belief still fail to be knowledge?

**Prerequisites and outcomes:** Conditions and counterexamples from Module 2; knowledge/true opinion from Module 6. Explain both cases, locate the challenged sufficiency claim, construct a different case, and test a proposed repair (O1–O4).

**Assigned reading:** Edmund Gettier, “Is Justified True Belief Knowledge?” (1963), whole article, originally *Analysis* 23, pp. 121–123. The [local bilingual 2013 republication](../../../collection/philosophy-study-collection/texts/introduction_logic/required/gettier_bilingual_disputatio_2013.pdf) contains the English text at printed pp. 186–188, PDF pp. 2–4; read these three pages. Its editorial record identifies permission from the author and original publication. Cite the actual republication pagination, alongside the original bibliographic identity. No Spanish translation reading is required. External access was not confirmed in this authoring pass; the local complete English text was inspected.

**Teaching explanation:** The target is the sufficiency of justified true belief for propositional knowledge. The paper allows justification for a false belief and the transmission of justification through a recognized entailment. In Case I the belief about who will get the job supports a claim about the successful applicant's coins; the latter becomes true through a fact the believer does not know. In Case II the false ownership belief supports a disjunction whose other part happens to be true. Neither case attacks the validity of the inference; the issue is how truth connects to the believer's grounds. Do not say the cases prove all justified beliefs false or establish that knowledge is impossible.

“No false lemmas” adds a restriction on false premises used in deriving the belief. It blocks the examples insofar as a false belief is their inferential basis. A successful repair of two cases is not yet proof of a necessary-and-sufficient analysis. It requires clarity about what counts as a lemma, how much background belief is included, and whether luck can enter without a false explicit premise. Rejecting a proposed case's justification is an available response, but it needs an account of justification rather than the circular rule that any belief failing to be knowledge was never justified.

**Worked example, invented:** An otherwise dependable laboratory display shows yesterday's safe temperature during a fault. By coincidence the actual temperature is exactly that displayed. A technician's belief is true, sincerely held, and apparently supported by familiar evidence. Whether it is knowledge depends on a defended judgment about the defective connection between display and temperature. To make the case a serious test, stipulate that the technician has no warning and no contrary evidence; do not conceal those conditions.

**Practice and outputs:** For each original case fill four cells: target proposition, justification, truth-maker, and missing connection. Create your own case without merely renaming jobs, coins, cars, or cities. Explain which structural feature differs and why the belief meets all three proposed conditions. Test no-false-lemmas against the original cases and your case. Write 600 words with a repair, a serious objection, and a response that either defends or narrows the repair. Distinguish your example from Gettier's own text.

**Independent changed case:** Invented valley: most visible structures are convincing stage fronts, unknown to a normally careful visitor. The visitor happens to look at the one genuine barn and forms “that is a barn” through ordinary perception, without consciously inferring from any false premise. In 250 words assess JTB and the no-false-lemmas repair, explicitly stating any disputed justification or lemma assumption. Criteria: trace the actual truth and evidential route, address the repair's stated scope, and acknowledge an alternative interpretation. This case is a course construction using the familiar environmental-luck structure, not an additional quotation from Gettier.

**Repair and retrieval:** If your counterexample lacks truth or belief, fix that before debating knowledge. If it only changes names, change the route by which evidence succeeds or fails. Retrieve the necessity/sufficiency direction in Module 9's opening review. **Next:** Module 9 applies structured reasoning to obligation.

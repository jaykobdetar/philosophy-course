# C01-M01 — Starting evidence and study setup

**Planned allocation: 4 focused hours (240 minutes), within the 120-hour baseline.**

**Guiding question:** How can I distinguish what a text says, what I infer, and what I think about it?

**Prerequisites and outcomes:** None. Save an untouched paraphrase, identify its speaker, record a usable source, and write a realistic one-page learning plan. Outcomes O1 and O4 begin here.

**Assigned reading:** Complete [Session 1](../sessions/01.md): OpenStax 2.5 and the marked Jowett *Euthyphro* 2a–before 5d. Read OpenStax [2.1](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-philosophy/pages/2-1-the-brain-is-an-inference-machine), complete, in the remaining source block. This supplies the otherwise unallocated 2.1 of the course core. Sources are freely readable online; use the exact translation named in [the audit](../source-audit.md). The later full dialogue is a purposeful rereading.

**Teaching explanation:** A philosophical reading is an attempt to discover what someone has reason to claim. Three activities must be distinguishable. Reporting attributes a claim to its speaker; interpreting explains its meaning or role; evaluating asks whether it is acceptable. Interpretation can itself need argument. A dialogue's author need not endorse every speaker. A confident first impression is evidence about your response, not yet evidence that your interpretation is right. The useful response is to locate the sentence that could correct you.

**Worked example, invented:** A club chair says, “We should extend opening hours because several members work late.” An accurate report identifies the chair's proposed extension and reason. “The chair believes everyone works late” adds a universal claim the sentence does not supply. “Longer hours would exhaust volunteers” is a possible objection, not a paraphrase. Save these roles in separate columns.

**Practice and outputs:** Keep the Session 1 120-word paragraph untouched. Create a source record with author, work, translator or edition, exact range, host/path, accessed date, and the locator visible in your copy. Write a one-page plan with your question, two workable study periods, a location for original/revised work, one anticipated access obstacle, and the criterion that would show progress. Discuss: “What would make a life examined?” Give one reason and one uncertainty; no predetermined answer is required.

**Independent changed case:** Invented notice: “The archive closes at six because the collection cannot be supervised after that time.” In 120 words distinguish the notice's policy, reason, and your own proposed alternative. Then record a source entry for the notice as an invented course example. Criteria: accurate attribution, no invented evidence, and a distinction between interpreting and objecting. This is a short writing sample, not evidence of sustained essay readiness.

**Repair and retrieval:** If reporting and evaluation merge, label each sentence R/I/E and revise one. At the beginning of Module 2, retrieve the three roles for five minutes within its reading/review block. Save the untouched check and corrected version. **Next:** Complete Sessions 2–4, then the remaining Modules 1–3 work as credited in the README.

**Attempt rules:** For every independent task, the assigned source and a citation sheet are allowed; answer keys, prewritten answers and AI/tutor content help are closed until the first response is saved. Record prior exposure and assistance. If rehearsed, use an unexposed alternate from the course assessment or have an assessor document a comparable new prompt; do not call a rehearsed answer fresh. Practice may use the [tutor guidance](../tutor-notes.md). After submission, use the module's anchors in [examiner notes](../examiner-notes.md).

**Accessible route:** Read in text or use text-to-speech on the same assigned edition/range. Pause to record speaker changes and argument steps. A licensed audiobook can support reading only after its translation and coverage are checked against this assignment; do not assume the local compact audio is complete. Check quotations and locators against the written source. Spoken planning and dictation are allowed; preserve a text equivalent of the assessed output. Dialogue may be a paced typed exchange with the same questions. Adjust time without reducing the intellectual task, and log overruns.

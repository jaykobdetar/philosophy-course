# C01-M03 — Paragraphs and source ownership

**Planned allocation: 8 focused hours (480 minutes), within the 120-hour baseline.**

**Guiding question:** How can a reader tell which claims are mine and why they follow?

**Prerequisites and outcomes:** Modules 1–2. Distinguish quotation, paraphrase, and attributed inference; produce three revised 150-word paragraphs; give enough citation information to find each textual claim (O1, O4).

**Assigned reading:** OpenStax 2.2–2.6 complete; 2.5 is a review already begun in Session 1. Read Jim Pryor, [“Guidelines on Writing a Philosophy Paper”](https://www.jimpryor.net/teaching/guidelines/writing.html), whole guide. Exact OpenStax links and access checks are in [the source audit](../source-audit.md). Use only *Euthyphro* 2a–8b for the source exercises; do not require unread later dialogue sections.

**Teaching explanation:** A paragraph has a job: establish a claim, explain a reason, expose an assumption, or answer an objection. Its topic sentence and final sentence should connect that job to the question. Paraphrase changes wording while retaining a claim's scope and ownership. Quotation preserves exact wording and punctuation, marks omissions, and supplies a locator. An inference goes beyond the passage: signal that it is your proposed consequence and show the steps. Adding “Plato says” does not turn your conclusion into a quotation or settle whether Plato endorses a speaker.

A citation supports only what its passage supports. Author and date alone cannot rescue a page that says something else. If an online transcription drops a marker, cite the heading and paragraph's opening words honestly; do not invent a print page. Keep borrowed excerpts in the private learner workspace. A short exact quotation is enough for this exercise.

**Worked progression, invented, on a different subject:** Draft: “The museum must admit everyone because access is good. Anyone opposing this is selfish.” This shifts from a value to an unrestricted policy and attacks people. Competent revision: “The museum has a reason to reduce entry fees because fees exclude some visitors who would benefit from its collection. This establishes a reason for access, but it does not yet determine how the museum should meet its costs.” A stronger version adds: “A free weekly session could meet part of that reason while preserving some ticket income; its adequacy depends on whether excluded visitors can attend that session.” The improvement is an explicit scope limit and a testable objection, not ornate wording. The stronger version remains conditional because no attendance data were supplied. See the separate [writing diagnostic](../examiner-notes.md#writing-progression) after your own attempt.

**Practice and outputs:** Expand the Session 2 revision to about 150 words as paragraph 1. Paragraph 2 explains why a proposed definition needs more than a favourable instance. Paragraph 3 argues for one limit of that demand. For each, save the draft, mark its job, get one content comment and one expression comment, and revise once. Separately select at most 20 words from the marked *Euthyphro*; give a checked quotation, an accurate paraphrase of that sentence in your own wording, and a distinct inference you defend. Record translation and Stephanus marker. Do not combine quotations from different editions.

**Independent changed case:** Invented committee statement: “Every applicant deserves an explanation of a decision. Therefore every rejected applicant should receive the complete confidential deliberation record.” Write a 150-word paragraph identifying what the premise supports and assessing the move to the conclusion. Attribute the statement to this course exercise; do not invent institutional facts. Criteria: one stable claim, a reason addressing the inference, a distinction between the source and your objection, and intelligible organization.

**Repair and retrieval:** Reverse-outline your paragraph: write each sentence's function beside it. Repair the first unsupported transition rather than rewriting everything. In Module 4's review block, retrieve the three source forms and check one old citation. **Next:** Complete the *Euthyphro* reading and dilemma reconstruction in Module 4.

**Attempt rules:** For every independent task, the assigned source and a citation sheet are allowed; answer keys, prewritten answers and AI/tutor content help are closed until the first response is saved. Record prior exposure and assistance. If rehearsed, use an unexposed alternate from the course assessment or have an assessor document a comparable new prompt; do not call a rehearsed answer fresh. Practice may use the [tutor guidance](../tutor-notes.md). After submission, use the module's anchors in [examiner notes](../examiner-notes.md).

**Accessible route:** Read in text or use text-to-speech on the same assigned edition/range. Pause to record speaker changes and argument steps. A licensed audiobook can support reading only after its translation and coverage are checked against this assignment; do not assume the local compact audio is complete. Check quotations and locators against the written source. Spoken planning and dictation are allowed; preserve a text equivalent of the assessed output. Dialogue may be a paced typed exchange with the same questions. Adjust time without reducing the intellectual task, and log overruns.

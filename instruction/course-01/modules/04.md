# C01-M04 — Euthyphro and explanatory priority

**Planned allocation: 14 focused hours (840 minutes), within the 120-hour baseline.**

**Guiding question:** Does naming what all the gods love explain what piety is?

**Prerequisites and outcomes:** Modules 1–3 and Session 4. Reconstruct the dilemma without conflating agreement with explanation; distinguish an attribute from an account of what makes something pious; defend and challenge a response (O1–O4).

**Assigned reading:** Plato, *Euthyphro*, complete 2a–16a, Benjamin Jowett in the [marked Wabash copy](https://persweb.wabash.edu/facstaff/helmang/phi110-1516F/phi110-handouts/Euthyphro.pdf). Reread 5d–11b and 13a–15c. The preferred Grube/Cooper 2002 *Five Dialogues* is an optional comparison, not assumed inspected or necessary. Use the translation actually read.

**Teaching explanation:** Euthyphro's confidence about prosecuting his father provides the setting for a demand for a general account. An example may establish that something falls under a term without explaining why all instances belong. The account in terms of divine approval first meets a disagreement problem; unanimous approval removes that particular conflict. It does not by itself answer the explanatory question at 10a–11b.

The direction matters: if the gods love the pious because it is pious, its piety supplies a reason for their response. If it is pious because loved, their response confers the status. Two descriptions could apply to exactly the same actions yet explain them differently. Do not confuse explanatory priority with temporal order: the issue is not which event occurs earlier. The later care/service proposals test what human service contributes and return toward divine approval. The dialogue ends without an agreed definition; that is a result of this inquiry, not a demonstration that piety is meaningless.

**Worked example, invented:** Suppose all and only well-supported reports receive an editor's approval. “Approved” and “well-supported” select the same reports under this assumption. Nevertheless, the editor approves a report because its evidence is good; the stamp is not what supplies the evidence. Alternatively, a competition can stipulate that the judge's declaration constitutes winning. These examples show two possible explanatory structures. They do not establish which structure piety must have or that a particular modern theology fails.

**Practice and outputs:** Build a table for five proposals: offered example, gods' love, unanimous love, care/service, and the return toward pleasing the gods. Enter the speaker, passage, proposed rule, and objection. Draft a 400-word reconstruction of 10a–11b. State what Euthyphro concedes and what the argument needs. Write a 300-word response defending the strongest interpretation you can supply; flag any premise you add beyond the dialogue. In a ten-minute discussion or 250-word exchange, defend each horn in turn, then state one unresolved issue. Complete one opening fresh form from [assessment](../assessment.md), with no live hints, in its allotted 30 minutes.

**Independent changed case:** Invented association: “An action is responsible exactly when every member of the review panel endorses it. Panel members say they endorse it because it is responsible.” In 200 words distinguish agreement, classification, and explanation. Give a possible reply for the association and one limitation of comparing this case with the dialogue. Criteria: trace both explanatory directions and avoid claiming that coextension alone establishes identity.

**Repair and retrieval:** Draw two arrows labelled “explains” and check which each speaker accepts. If the objection is merely disagreement among gods, revisit the unanimous version. Use ten minutes of revision to retrieve Module 3's source distinctions. Module 5 retrieves this explanatory distinction on a different example. **Next:** Module 5, *Apology*.

# C01-S02 — Reasons, conclusions, and hidden assumptions

**Guiding question:** What follows from an argument's stated reasons, and what needs an added premise?

**Exact source:** [OpenStax 5.3, Arguments](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-philosophy/pages/5-3-arguments), complete section. The online chapter was opened on 2026-10-13. The four sentences below are original course exercises, not quotations from OpenStax.

**90-minute route:** Read for 20 minutes. Spend 20 minutes on the four arguments below: mark each conclusion, number its stated reasons, and bracket any premise you add. Spend 15 minutes explaining which conclusion follows deductively and which needs more support, using feedback to correct one error. Use 20 minutes to revise the Session 1 paragraph around one claim and supporting reason, without erasing the original. Spend 10 minutes on the independent check below before its feedback; use the final 5 to save and note the next step. Extend and log time if feedback takes longer.

1. Every borrower agreed to return the book. Mira is a borrower. Therefore Mira agreed to return the book.
2. The lights are on. Therefore somebody must be home.
3. A rule applies to everyone only if it applies to the mayor. This rule does not apply to the mayor. Therefore it does not apply to everyone.
4. Many people like this painting. Therefore it is a good painting.

**Output:** Four numbered reconstructions, a one-sentence explanation for each, and a revised paragraph. Save as `C01-S02-arguments.md`; record any tutor help. These are the first four of Module 2's six reconstructions, so they count once.

**Feedback:** A strong answer distinguishes a valid form from the truth of premises. For argument 2, a light may be on in an empty home; for 4, popularity alone needs a bridge to quality. Argument 3 turns on the direction of “only if.” Explain an error in words before using labels. [Examiner notes](../examiner-notes.md#c01-s02) contain fuller anchors.

**Independent check:** Without tutor help, analyze this new form: “If the archive is open, the lamp is on. The lamp is on. Therefore the archive is open.” Give either a counterexample or the missing assumption. Save your first response before feedback.

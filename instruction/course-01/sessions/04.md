# C01-S04 — From an example to a definition

**Guiding question:** Why might citing one pious action fail to explain what piety is?

**Exact source:** [Marked Jowett *Euthyphro* PDF](https://persweb.wabash.edu/facstaff/helmang/phi110-1516F/phi110-handouts/Euthyphro.pdf), **5d up to but not including 8b**. The 5d marker is on PDF page 4; 8b is at the page 6/7 boundary. Check visible Stephanus marks at the start and stop. The source and markers were checked on 2026-10-13. This is an early slice, not the complete dialogue.

**90-minute route:** Read for 20 minutes. Spend 20 minutes listing Euthyphro's early candidate definitions and Socrates' objections with locators. Spend 20 minutes making an argument map on paper or in an ordinary document: each node is a complete proposition, arrows show support or objection. Spend 20 minutes writing roughly 150 words explaining why one pious example may fail as a definition. Use 10 minutes to identify a question for the next tutorial.

**Output:** Locator notes, one inspectable map with a two-sentence explanation of what it shows and does not show, the paragraph, and a next question. Save as `C01-S04-definition.md`. These outputs count within Module 4, not as extra work.

**Feedback:** Check whether the paragraph distinguishes the act of prosecuting from a general account that covers other cases. A critic may challenge the definition's coverage without denying that the act is pious. Cite the actual dialogue, and keep a contested interpretation provisional. Ask the tutor for one or two diagnostic issues, not a rewritten paragraph. [Examiner notes](../examiner-notes.md#c01-s04) give source-bound anchors.

**Independent check:** Make up a nonreligious example of the difference between “this is an instance of X” and “this is what makes anything X.” Explain its limit; do not present it as a quotation from Plato.

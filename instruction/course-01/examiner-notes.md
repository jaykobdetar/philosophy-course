# Course 1 examiner notes and diagnostic anchors

Keep this file separate from practice prompts where possible. It gives criteria and diagnostic distinctions, not a secure answer key. Learners can read a repository, so record prior exposure before an independent task and choose a new form if needed.

## C01-S01

Outcome `C01-O1`: The paraphrase attributes a claim to Euthyphro and accurately distinguishes the murder case and his reason for prosecuting. It does not turn the learner's approval or disapproval into the speaker's view. The [Jowett PDF](https://persweb.wabash.edu/facstaff/helmang/phi110-1516F/phi110-handouts/Euthyphro.pdf), 4b–5c, gives the case; near 5d Socrates asks for a general account. A response can be strong without agreeing that the prosecution is justified. If the learner confuses example and definition, revisit the exchange, then use a different fresh case.

## C01-S02

Outcome `C01-O2`: The reconstruction identifies the conclusion and any added premise. (1) has a valid syllogistic form under the words given. (2) needs a premise excluding empty lit rooms. (3) correctly applies the “only if” direction: universal application would require application to the mayor. (4) needs a defensible bridge from popularity to quality. The fresh archive/lamp argument affirms the consequent; a counterexample has a lit lamp in a closed archive. Do not call a conclusion false merely because the inference is invalid.

## C01-S03

Outcome `C01-O3`: The learner explains validity as no case of true premises and false conclusion, while soundness adds true premises. A square is a rectangle, but rectangles can be non-square. In the stipulated race, winners paid, while many payers may not win. The library-card check has this same necessary-condition structure but must be independently explained. A good counterexample preserves the proposed universal claim's target and supplies a genuine exception. Ask for a new case if only a label is given.

## C01-S04

Outcome `C01-O1` and `C01-O3`: In *Euthyphro* 5d–8b, Socrates asks what piety itself is; Euthyphro's own prosecution is offered as an instance. The response explains why one instance may not settle all cases. A strong paragraph cites a visible Stephanus range and distinguishes the text's claim from an interpretive inference. It may defend an alternative interpretation if the passage supports it. The original nonreligious independent check should preserve the instance/definition distinction without a tutor-supplied example.

## Fresh and delayed forms

For [fresh Form A](assessment.md), the gods' disagreement matters to the proposed criterion. Its conditional argument affirms the consequent: differing wording need not come from translation. For fresh Form B, the relevant disagreement is over just and unjust; treating every disagreement as arithmetic misses the passage's point. Its conditional argument denies the antecedent: a closed gallery might still have its lights on. For delayed Form D, the passage contrasts an offered instance with the requested general account. Its conditional argument uses modus tollens: given the conditional and absence of tracking, the conclusion follows. Credit reasoning that makes these distinctions in the learner's own terms rather than requiring these labels.

## Repair and recheck

For an attribution error, reread one paragraph and paraphrase a different speaker's claim. For an inference error, construct a countercase with the same form and different subject. For a definition error, compare one example and a proposed general rule. Then use a different, unpracticed case. Mark readiness by criterion and evidence, not a percentage. Record actual focused minutes as part of the shared 120-hour source baseline.

## Full-course assessor procedure

The preceding opening anchors remain valid. The guidance below extends them to all twelve modules and the full [assessment forms](assessment.md). Keep it closed during an independent attempt. Record the source actually inspected, the selected form, prior exposure, assistance, criterion and evidence. These are diagnostic anchors rather than mandatory philosophical conclusions. An alternative interpretation with sound textual reasoning may satisfy a criterion. No learner pilot or expert validation has occurred.

Use the shared rubric dimension by dimension. Competent means accurate attribution, explicit support, a relevant objection/reply where requested, traceable evidence and intelligible organization. Strong adds a justified treatment of ambiguity or limitation. Developing identifies an actual gap and a repair, not a label on the learner. Every essential evidence-grid row requires both supported module work and a fresh sample. Do not average away a missing row.

## PC-31 practical reasoning

Case 1: lateness alone does not support rejection of a levy; a different claim about reliability in administering a project could make conduct relevant if evidence connects it. Case 2: one badly indexed book establishes at most a local problem; a representative sampling claim needs further support. Case 3: missing winter data creates uncertainty, not proof of winter failure. Case 4: provenance verification is a reasonable procedural response; it is not a proof that an undated source is false. Its premises do not deductively entail a normative “must” without a policy/risk premise. Credit a learner who makes that bridge explicit.

Independent policy case: one ambiguous clause may warrant clarification or targeted revision; rejecting the whole policy needs a claim that the defect is central, unrecoverable or sufficiently costly. A charitable reading may supply such a premise, but distinguish it from what was given. Alternate procurement case: a delayed delivery can be relevant evidence about reliability, but one instance alone does not establish that every future order will fail. Evaluate scope and evidence, not just a fallacy label.

## Full fresh-form anchors

**F-A1:** The first argument is valid by excluding temperature control when the necessary sensor is absent. The second allows a working sensor in a case that is not temperature-controlled. The given condition is necessary, not established sufficient. “Every case with a working sensor is temperature-controlled” supplies the missing direction; its truth still needs evidence.

**F-B1:** Eligibility entails removal of identifiers. Ineligibility does not imply identifiers remain: another requirement might be missing. Removing identifiers alone need not confer eligibility. Validity tests stipulated relationships, while actual policy truth is a different question. A true conclusion can occur in an invalid inference because that inference does not guarantee it.

**F-A2:** The speaker is Socrates as presented by Plato in the CHS adaptation. The passage links fear of death with pretending to know its value while asserting the wrongness of injustice/disobedience to a better. Credit a challenge to whether all fear presupposes claimed knowledge; practical caution under uncertainty can differ from claiming certainty. Also credit a justified challenge to the asserted moral ranking or divine instruction. Do not score “Socrates knows nothing” as accurate. The dramatic report is not independently established verbatim history.

**F-B2:** Socrates uses correct guidance to challenge the restriction of right action to knowledge. Meno initially associates knowledge with reliable success and opinion with occasional failure; Socrates asks about opinion while it remains true. The distinction concerns retaining true opinion and its explanation, not a case of false opinion that happens to work. The later tie-of-cause discussion is outside the selected passage and must be separately cited. Accept careful recognition that the road example establishes a practical equivalence under the stated condition without showing knowledge and true opinion identical.

**F-A3/B3, Euthyphro:** Look for a distinction between coextension and explanation, an explicit account of what confers or tracks piety, and a reply to the actual burden. A modern response is not automatically refuted by a criticism of this dialogue's specific proposals. Conversely, adding a standard needs an account of its role. Neither generic atheism nor mere assertion of divine authority answers the selected objection.

**F-A3/B3, Gettier:** The response must define “lemma” and identify how its restriction affects the case. On environmental luck, address justification and the connection to truth. On false background assumptions, distinguish inferential dependence from merely accompanying belief. Do not require the learner to solve the entire knowledge literature; a bounded conclusion about the proposed repair is sufficient.

**F-A3/B3, Singer:** Demand a moral reason for the proposed stopping condition rather than a report of inconvenience. For guaranteed contributions distinguish changes in what the agent can prevent from a general excuse based on the number of potential donors. The learner may defend either principle, reject one, or narrow the thesis, but must preserve the pp. 231/241 distinction and confront a strong reply.

**Dialogue:** A useful follow-up changes or weakens the learner's own identified premise. The assessor asks it after hearing the answer; an answer key cannot predetermine the learner's actual reasoning. Record the follow-up verbatim and assess direct engagement, evidence and justified revision. Typed reflection time is not a competence penalty.

## Delayed-form anchors

Opening D0 asks for a general account; the conditional is valid with the premise fixed. Its result supplies only early attribution/conditions evidence.

D1 arguments: a signature is necessary but not sufficient for acceptance; a signed rejected application is consistent with the rule. An accepted unsigned application would refute it. D2 arguments: a closed/inaccessible trail can coexist with an unlocked gate; an inaccessible trail may be impassable for other reasons. A locked gate plus the universal conditional supports inaccessibility.

D1 interpretation: at 22c–e Socrates recognizes craft knowledge while criticizing the inference from craft skill to wisdom about the greatest matters. An admitted limit in finance does not by itself refute a load calculation within the engineer’s expertise. The new case reverses the overextension: it dismisses relevant expertise because of an acknowledged unrelated limit. Require source support and a qualification: actual premises, competence or domain overlap could still be questioned; the passage alone does not certify this particular calculation. D2 interpretation: the passage invokes an explanatory fastening, not mere stubborn persistence of an opinion. A stable false belief is not a counterexample to a claim explicitly about true opinion; a merely stable true guess tests whether the proposed sufficient condition omits explanation.

D1 writing: distinguish the truth of a conclusion from the adequacy of the offered support; a response may supply a missing premise or narrow the thesis. D2 writing: identify the actual role of the condition in the learner's essay, allowing a justified rejection of the objection's interpretation. Both require a new response, not a pasted revision memo.

D1 comparison: *Meno* asks about correct guidance and the greater stability/value associated with knowledge; Gettier tests whether JTB is sufficient. Practical success and truth are related but not identical criteria. D2 comparison: Socrates' avowed limits coexist with ethical commitments, whereas Descartes suspends assent for a foundational inquiry. Similar intellectual caution does not establish identical method or conclusion.

## Writing progression

These original mini-responses use an invented park rule, separate from assessed cases. Rule: a pass is required to enter a fenced garden, but passes are sold to visitors who may never enter.

**Developing draft:** “Ravi has a pass, so he entered. The rule proves this, because passes are required.” It correctly notices the pass condition but reverses its direction. A tutor's feedback should identify that exact gap rather than rewrite the response.

**Competent revision:** “The rule says that entry requires a pass. Ravi's having a pass meets that requirement, but he could have bought it and stayed outside. The rule therefore does not establish that he entered.” This preserves the premise, supplies a possible countercase, and states a bounded conclusion. Competence does not require formal terminology.

**Strong extension:** “The countercase refutes the inference from possession to entry while leaving the rule intact. An additional record that this pass was scanned at the entrance might supply evidence of entry; it would still matter whether another person could use it.” This distinguishes deduction from further empirical evidence and identifies a new assumption without inventing a record.

**Revision memo example:** “I changed my conclusion from ‘entered’ to ‘entry is not established’ because I had treated a necessary condition as sufficient. I added a pass-holder who remains outside to show the gap. I did not conclude that Ravi stayed outside: the supplied evidence leaves both possibilities open.” The improvement changes the reasoning, not merely wording.

Apply the same developing/competent/strong distinction to philosophical writing with source attribution added. For an essay, competence also requires fair reconstruction and a reply to a serious actual objection. Strong work can end with a limited conclusion; breadth and ornate prose are not separate achievements. Use [the shared rubric](../../current/assessment.md#observable-rubric-anchors) and cite the exact learner evidence for every judgment.

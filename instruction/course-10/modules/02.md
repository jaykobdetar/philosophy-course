# C10-M02 — Testing and falsifiability

**14 hours. Guiding question:** What makes a test expose a theory to failure? Prerequisite: M01. Outcomes: distinguish testability, truth and usefulness; analyze risky predictions; identify legitimate auxiliary repair and evasive accommodation.

## Explanation

Falsifiability concerns what a claim excludes. A theory that allows every outcome supplies no discriminating empirical test merely because supporters find confirming instances everywhere. A prediction is risky relative to background expectations and rival possibilities: the theory makes itself vulnerable to an outcome that is a live possibility, rather than predicting something virtually every theory would accommodate. More risky does not mean more likely false in a numerically specified sense. A test's evidential value depends on the actual discriminating setup, not a dramatic description.

A falsifiable theory can be false, and a nonfalsifiable idea can be meaningful. Demarcation should not be collapsed into truth, moral worth, or the value of all inquiry. Surviving a severe test can count as corroboration in Popper's framework without turning the theory into an inductively verified certainty. In reading, find how Popper contrasts confirmation-seeking with attempted refutation; state his claim before evaluating its limits.

Apparent counterinstances confront a testing system. If H and A imply O, and not-O is observed, then at least one accepted premise is wrong. Which one is a methodological problem. Checking calibration is not automatically an illegitimate rescue. A repair gains standing when it has an independent reason, creates a further testable consequence, and is not altered after every result merely to protect H. These are teaching criteria for comparing responses, not a claim that Popper supplies a mechanical algorithm for all cases. An advocate may legitimately retain a fruitful theory while investigating an anomaly; a critic can still ask whether the protective move removes its empirical content.

## Worked invented example

Theory T predicts that every treated strip turns blue within ten minutes under specified temperature and light. One remains white. A recorded power failure prevented illumination. Restoring the light and repeating a preregistered test is a testable auxiliary repair. Saying instead that “T's influence becomes invisible precisely when blue fails to appear,” with no independent indicator or new prediction, protects T at the price of a test condition. The first explanation could still be wrong; its merit is that it can be checked independently.

## Independent I2

A competitor predicts that rare failures occur in 1% of sealed units; three failures occur in a batch of 100. The critic says the prediction “no unit fails” is falsified. Meanwhile the manufacturer adds an unrestricted exception for any failed unit. In 300 words distinguish the actual probabilistic claim from the critic's substitution, identify what further testing information is needed, and evaluate the exception. No significance-test calculation is required or licensed by these data alone. Source-open, 45 minutes plus 15 minutes for declaration; no tutoring during the attempt. Neutral criteria: reconstruct the stated claim, explain what observations bear on it, and evaluate the response without inventing a decision threshold. [Examiner](../examiner-notes.md).

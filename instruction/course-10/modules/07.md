# C10-M07 — Case paper and substantive revision

**22 hours. Guiding question:** Which philosophical inference does this evidence support, and where does its reach end? Prerequisites: completed M01–06 artifacts and usable source copies. Outcomes: pose a bounded issue; reconstruct one course argument accurately; distinguish scientific claims from philosophical interpretation; answer a serious objection; revise substantively.

**Readings:** revisit the complete default case packet specified in [case-packet.md](../case-packet.md), plus the exact assigned range of at least one philosopher used in the paper. No extra whole book is required. Exact editions and access limits remain those in [source audit](../source-audit.md). Alternative cases are permitted only after the finite source-screening procedure below; missing domain access can block a source claim without blocking outlining or argument practice.

**Time:** choose question and verify packet 2h; targeted rereading/source ledger 4h; outline and argument 3h; draft 5h; feedback 2h; independent changed assumption 1h; revision 4h; citation/assistance audit 1h = 22h. Module 6's 15-minute retrieval at opening is credited there, not here.

## Decision procedure

1. Choose **one** issue: (a) what a bounded attribution study and synthesis warrant about acceptance; (b) how confidence language constrains a move to policy; (c) how criticism of an assumption differs from rejecting science. Choose a different framing only if it can be answered within 1,800–2,200 words from the packet.
2. Write a question with a named inference: “Does statement X support conclusion Y, given assumption Z?” Write a provisional answer and one explicit exclusion. Exclude independent climate-model validation unless you possess and actually analyze the required specialist evidence; it is not a course expectation.
3. Make a claim ledger: source/edition, exact locator, source's claim, source's uncertainty, your interpretation, and what would require more evidence. A page of prose without a locator is not evidence. Quote only when exact wording matters and check every quotation against the actual source.
4. Build a five-line argument: scientific premise, philosophical principle from an assigned range, linking premise, qualified conclusion, strongest objection. If the linking premise merely repeats the conclusion, revise it before drafting.
5. Allocate approximately 200 words to question/scope, 350 to source reconstruction, 450 to analysis, 350 to objection, 350 to reply, and 150 to limits/conclusion: 1,850 words. These are an aid, not compulsory section headings. Reserve remaining words for a needed distinction.
6. Receive feedback only after a complete draft. Ask the tutor: “Which source claim have I overstated?” and “Which premise would a serious opponent reject?” Record assistance. The tutor diagnoses and models another case, rather than rewriting the assessed prose.
7. Revise at least one inference or scope claim, not just sentence style. Submit a 200–300-word memo identifying the objection, old claim, new claim, and residual difficulty. Use [worked examples](../worked-examples.md) as an illustration, not an essay to imitate.

## Why these steps matter

A case paper is not a miniature scientific review and not a detachable essay about a famous philosopher. Its central work is the connection between a precisely represented scientific claim and a philosophical issue. A conclusion can be modest and still informative: it can show why a stronger inference fails or what additional premise would license it. A distinction earns its place when it changes what can be concluded. “Science contains uncertainty” is too general; “this confidence assessment does not determine this policy choice without a value premise” names a testable inferential gap.

A serious objection should threaten the thesis under its own strongest interpretation. If the paper criticizes an inference to unobservable entities, the objection should address whether predictive success supplies defeasible support, rather than insist that deduction is unavailable. A reply may narrow the thesis: admitting some evidence for realism can coexist with rejecting certainty. Revision that improves accuracy counts even if it weakens an initially dramatic conclusion.

## Worked invented miniature and practice P7

Original miniature: a fictional council cites a forecast of increasing heat exposure to justify a particular subsidy. The forecast bears on need; it does not alone rank subsidy against insulation or relocation. A linking premise about effectiveness, fairness or costs is required. A fair objection is that the council already stated a priority principle; if so, criticize that principle or its application instead of alleging its absence.

**P7 deliverables:** question card, source ledger with at least six substantive entries drawn from the bounded readings, argument outline, complete draft, feedback declaration, revised 1,800–2,200-word paper and revision memo. Criteria: accurate interpretation, warranted connection, objection/reply, traceable evidence, explicit limits, and substantive revision. Six entries may come from fewer than six sources; padding the bibliography is unnecessary.

## Independent I7 — changed assumption

After the first complete draft, close the tutor feedback and spend 45 minutes on this change: “Suppose the scientific claim you rely on remains supported, but the proposed policy is shown to transfer the largest burden to a group omitted from the original comparison.” Identify exactly which of your premises survives, which requires new evidence, and whether the conclusion changes. If the paper makes no policy recommendation, explain why the change may affect a practical use of the paper without refuting its central epistemic conclusion. Submit 300 words plus a 15-minute assistance/source declaration. No coaching during the attempt. [Examiner](../examiner-notes.md).

## Alternative packet screen, repair and next step

An alternative must name one official plain-language summary, one publicly accessible primary-study abstract or bounded methods/results excerpt, explicit uncertainty wording, publication dates, exact ranges and a common issue they genuinely address. Check that both concern the same relevant population/phenomenon and distinguish disagreement from differing scope. Record access and review the domain limits before starting; do not select a case that requires advanced unprovided mathematics. The 2h selection budget is finite: if screening cannot finish, use the default or mark access-dependent, rather than add an uncounted literature review.

For source overstatement, split one sentence into “source reports,” “I infer” and “unresolved.” For a diffuse question, remove a second issue until the argument fits five lines. At M08 opening retrieve the main inference without notes in the budgeted readiness block, then prepare the four-row evidence grid. A delayed paper defense later samples a genuinely changed premise rather than asks for the thesis again.

# C10-M03 — Scientific explanation

**14 hours. Guiding question:** When does showing that an outcome follows explain it? Prerequisites: M01–02. Outcomes: reconstruct a law-and-condition schema; distinguish validity from adequate explanation; assess a direction or relevance objection and its scope.

**Reading:** Hempel, *Philosophy of Natural Science*, Prentice-Hall 1966, chapter 5 complete (C10-R03). Chapter pagination must come from the learner's lawful copy; this authoring did not inspect the chapter. [Audit](../source-audit.md).

**Time:** reading 4h; teaching/example 2h; practice 3h; feedback 1h; independent task 1h; revision/retrieval 3h = 14h, including 15 minutes retrieved during M04.

## Explanation

An explanation has an explanandum, the event or fact to be explained, and an explanans, the information offered to explain it. In a deductive covering-law reconstruction, general laws plus relevant conditions entail the explanandum. The point is to make the event intelligible by locating it under a pattern rather than merely repeating it. Truth, empirical content, lawful generality and correct derivation matter; a valid argument from invented laws is not thereby a sound explanation.

An accidental generalization is not automatically a law. “Every coin in this pouch is copper” may hold because of the contents chosen, without supporting the same counterfactual expectations as a physical law. The introductory task is to recognize the distinction and identify the assumption, not solve the metaphysics of laws. Statistical explanations also need care: a high probability can explain an expectation without deductively entailing the individual event. Do not force the occurrence of a low-probability event into the universal form.

Two worries help test explanatory adequacy. Direction: mathematical relations can sometimes be used to infer a cause from its effect, although explanation appears to run the other way. Relevance: an argument may include an irrelevant fact or a merely correlated indicator and still validly derive a conclusion. These challenge the sufficiency of a bare derivation for explanation. They do not automatically show that laws and deductions are useless, or defeat every possible revision of the covering-law approach. A precise objection says which criterion the model meets and which explanatory feature it misses.

## Worked invented example

In an ideal simulation, rod length L, light angle 45 degrees and a geometric rule give shadow length S = L. With L = 2 metres, deduce S = 2 metres. Reversing the equation also permits inference from the two-metre shadow to rod length, given the same conditions. If the rod was independently manufactured at two metres, changing its shadow with a screen does not make the rod grow. This supports an explanatory asymmetry despite symmetrical calculation. The stipulated ideal relation is an example, not a claim about measurements in real settings. Distinguish a calculation of an unknown length from an explanation of why the rod has that length.

**P3:** A simulated greenhouse has a thermostat that turns a heater on whenever temperature is below 10 degrees; an independent logging light flashes exactly when the heater is on. Given temperature 8, reconstruct the deduction to “heater on” with premises labelled law, condition and result. Now someone uses the flashing light to explain the heater's operation. Write 450 words comparing inference and explanation. Supply an intervention that separates the proposed explanation from the original mechanism, and state whether your objection targets necessity or sufficiency of the schema. Use one checked chapter locator.

## Independent I3

In a fictional mechanical installation, rotation of a master gear causes two separate dials to show identical numbers. The gears are reliable; either dial's number plus a correlation rule permits prediction of the other. A technician says Dial A explains Dial B because B is deducible from A. In 250–350 words reconstruct the strongest deductive argument, assess its explanatory adequacy, and contrast a modified installation in which A directly drives B. Source-open, one hour, no coaching. Criteria: explicit premises, relevant difference between the two mechanisms, and a bounded conclusion about the explanatory schema. [Examiner](../examiner-notes.md).

# Constructed work and revision — Course 10

All excerpts below are **original invented learner-like examples**, not real submissions or empirical validation. The hypothetical source descriptions are stipulated teaching facts. They are not quotations from the assigned scientific packet. Use them during practice, not immediately before a fresh item with the same mechanism.

## Interpretation and method: contrary observation

**Developing:** “Popper proves that any bad result makes a theory false. Our solution did not change color, so it must be unscientific to keep investigating it.”

Diagnosis: a possible falsification is confused with the property of being falsifiable; the tested auxiliaries are absent. The position is attributed too strongly without a locator. Repair: state the actual conjunction and a legitimate independent check before evaluating retention.

**Competent:** “The prediction depended on both the reaction hypothesis and the stipulated temperature. The failed color change conflicts with their conjunction, if the observation is reliable. It does not alone select the reaction hypothesis as the false premise. Measuring temperature independently would test a specific alternative explanation; it would not guarantee the reaction hypothesis.”

Why competent: the logical scope is explicit, the repair is testable and the conclusion is qualified. To turn this into source-grounded interpretation, add a checked Popper chapter 1 locator and explain how it supports the reconstruction; this invented excerpt alone supplies no such textual evidence.

**Strong:** “Retention is defensible only under an evidentially constrained repair. If an independently calibrated thermometer shows the wrong temperature, correcting it exposes the reaction hypothesis to another potential failure. If the temperature was correct and the failure repeats, the proposed repair loses support. A promise that every failed color change involves an undetectable temperature fluctuation would instead remove the practical risk. This distinction gives a reason to investigate without making retention mandatory.”

Why strong: it specifies what would undermine its own preferred response and avoids a slogan about auxiliaries. It still needs a source citation for historical attribution, and it should not claim a mechanical theory-choice algorithm.

## Comparison: acceptance of a model

**Developing:** “Realists believe science. Empiricists think invisible things are false, so they cannot use models.”

Diagnosis: a false contrast substitutes denial for agnosticism and ignores practical acceptance.

**Competent:** “A realist may take the model's success as evidence for approximately true claims about its hidden mechanism. Constructive empiricism permits using the model while believing it is empirically adequate and withholding belief about that mechanism. Both can reject a failed observable prediction. Their dispute concerns the further commitment required by acceptance.”

Why competent: it compares the positions on a shared question without claiming realism requires certainty. A checked van Fraassen chapter 2 §§1–3 locator is needed for the attribution.

**Strong:** “If two theories are stipulated to agree on all observable outcomes, this experiment cannot discriminate between their hidden mechanisms. A realist may still appeal to unification, but must defend why that virtue tracks truth. An empiricist can accept the virtue as a reason for research use without the same belief. This reply does not prove realism false; it locates the additional premise needed for its inference.”

Why strong: the comparison identifies the inferential dispute and a consequential stipulation. It does not confuse a rival's existence with a deductive refutation.

## Research: draft-to-revision pair

**Invented draft:** “The assessment says heat exposure is increasing with high confidence. Therefore its evidence proves that the city must subsidize air conditioning. Anyone who objects rejects science. Since there is uncertainty, however, the city can choose whatever policy it wants.”

Feedback: two contradictory overreaches obscure the argument. First, the forecast does not select a policy without evidence about means and a priority principle. Second, uncertainty does not imply equal warrant for every option. Identify which source sentence concerns exposure, which policy effectiveness claim needs another source, and what criterion favors a subsidy.

**Revised passage:** “On the stipulated assessment, increasing heat exposure supplies a reason to investigate protective action. It does not by itself establish that a subsidy is the most effective or fair response. That conclusion would also require evidence about who can use the subsidy and how it compares with alternatives, together with a stated priority among cost, effectiveness and distribution. An objection identifying excluded households could therefore challenge the policy inference while accepting the exposure assessment. Uncertainty affects the strength and scope of this reason; it does not make the evidence irrelevant.”

**Revision memo:** “I withdrew ‘proves’ and separated the empirical assessment from the choice of means. I also removed my claim that disagreement rejects science, because an objection can target the linking premise. The revised thesis is conditional: the assessment gives a reason for inquiry into action, and a subsidy needs further justification. I still lack evidence comparing policy effectiveness, so I no longer claim to have selected a policy.”

Annotation: the revision changes the conclusion, supplies the missing inference structure and identifies a source need. In a real P7 paper, replace the invented assessment with an actual checked packet claim and locator. A polished paragraph without that link remains incomplete research work. Competence need not become a dramatic claim; acknowledging a precise limit can strengthen an argument.

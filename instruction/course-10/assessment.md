# Course 10 — independent assessment prompts

Learner-facing prompts. Do not open [examiner notes](examiner-notes.md) until submission. Record the chosen form, sources available, prior exposure, assistance, start/end time and access adjustments. Assigned texts are open during written work; practice answers, tutor dialogue, generative systems and solution notes are closed. A scribe, screen reader or dictation is permitted with no reasoning help. Oral answers require a transcript or numbered argument record. These are fresh tasks relative to the coached examples, not claims of secure testing.

The [outcome map](outcomes.md) and [M08 budget](modules/08.md) require four 25-minute written samples, then 20 minutes of explanation/defense. Aim for 200–300 words per written item, with a short diagram or table when useful. Criteria shared across forms: represent the stated case, explain the inference, identify relevant assumptions, use exact sources where requested, and limit the conclusion. There is no required philosophical verdict when reasons are defensible.

## Form F — first uncoached occasion (100 minutes)

**F1 Induction and testing — 25 minutes.** In an invented instrument experiment, hypothesis H plus a newly installed filter and a functioning detector predicts no violet signal. A violet signal is recorded. The detector's self-check passes, but the filter has never been independently tested. A reporter says H has been conclusively refuted; the laboratory says the opposite result in a repeat trial would prove H. Analyze both inferences. Write the tested conjunction, name the auxiliary to investigate, describe one discriminating further test, and explain how surviving it would differ from deduction of H. Connect one part of the analysis to Hume IV or Popper chapter 1.

**F2 Explanation and scientific change — 25 minutes.** In a stipulated laboratory model, all samples of material M melt above temperature T, whatever label they carry. An investigator derives the melting of sample V from this narrower rule: “Every copper-labelled sample of M melts above T,” together with three premises: V is a sample of material M, V carries a copper label, and V's temperature is above T. The investigator claims that the valid derivation establishes the label as part of the explanation. Reconstruct the argument, assess that claim, and show what happens to the explanation when the label premise is removed and the general rule is used. Explain whether this case defeats every law-based explanation or exposes a problem with treating any valid derivation as sufficient. Use Hempel chapter 5; do not invent chemistry beyond the stipulated model.

**F3 Realism — 25 minutes.** A scientist accepts theory T, which posits unobservable “couplers” with a detailed interaction mechanism. She says she believes T is empirically adequate, suspends judgment about that mechanism, but already believed couplers existed because of a separate metaphysical argument. One colleague concludes that her acceptance proves she believes all of T literally true. Another says constructive empiricism would require her to deny the existence of couplers, regardless of why she believed in them. Evaluate both inferences. Distinguish what theory acceptance requires, what additional beliefs this person actually has, and whether belief in an entity alone establishes belief in a theory's account of it. Compare the realist and constructive-empiricist commitments without assessing the soundness of the stipulated metaphysical argument. Use van Fraassen chapter 2 §§1–3.

**F4 Values and uncertainty — 25 minutes.** Read the actual IPCC 2023 SPM A.2.1 with footnote 4, uncoached for this task. Select one claim with its uncertainty wording and record its exact clause/locator in your private answer. An invented editorial says: “That assessment's confidence label gives the numerical chance that our city's most expensive flood defense is the correct policy.” Distinguish what the source assesses, its uncertainty convention, and the editorial's policy inference. Name one additional empirical premise and one evaluative premise that would be needed. Do not independently adjudicate climate science or assume a probability absent from the text. If A.2.1 contains several claims, explicitly identify which one you used.

## Form A — exposure replacement or targeted repair (100 minutes if all four are needed)

**A1 — 25 minutes.** A theory predicts that 95% of carefully specified runs produce an amber signal, not that all do. Five of 100 runs show no amber. Before testing, an assistant mislabelled which runs used the required shielding. A critic calls each absence a deductive falsification; the theorist now declares any unshielded run irrelevant without checking the labels. Analyze the probability claim, the relevance of the mislabelling, and a testable response. Do not invent a statistical rejection threshold. Contrast the actual issue with a deductive contradiction of a universal claim.

**A2 — 25 minutes.** In a stipulated model, identical coils melt exactly one minute after continuous heating begins. Coil A's heating begins at noon and Coil B's at 12:05; all other relevant conditions match. An investigator explains A's melting by citing the one-minute rule and A's heating. A second investigator asks why A melted before B, and receives exactly the same answer with no mention of B. Reconstruct a derivation answering the first question and assess whether it answers the second. Specify the extra initial-condition information needed, state each explanandum precisely, and explain why an explanation can be adequate for one question yet incomplete for another. Use Hempel chapter 5; evaluate explanatory relevance rather than reversing a causal arrow.

**A3 — 25 minutes.** A theory's entities are stipulated observable in principle, but none has yet been seen. The theory predicts they will appear in a specified accessible experiment; its rival predicts none. A director says constructive empiricists need only fit existing records and can ignore that experiment. Compare acceptance commitments and the relevance of the proposed observation. State why “not yet observed” changes the issue from a dispute only about unobservables. Explain what would and would not follow from a successful result.

**A4 — 25 minutes.** Read the actual IPCC SPM A.2.2 with footnote 4, uncoached for this form. Identify one assessed claim and its stated uncertainty. An invented council explicitly prioritizes protecting the worst-off, then claims that the assessment alone demonstrates its chosen subsidy will reach those people most effectively. Separate the evaluative premise already supplied from the missing evidence about policy means. Explain whether uncertainty makes all policies equally defensible, and describe a criticism process that could expose an error without treating consensus as infallible. Use Longino chapter 4 for the latter point.

Form A changes the type of hypothesis, the explanation question and relevant conditions, observational status and presence of an explicit value premise. It is not Form F with new names. When only two rows need repair, use those two A items within the 50-minute block. More unresolved rows require an honestly recorded additional occasion; no automatic pass.

## Explanation and paper defense — 20 minutes

First 8 minutes, no notes: reconstruct Hume's induction challenge and distinguish it from the account of custom. State one premise whose rejection would change the argument. Next 12 minutes, paper open: **F-defense** assumes the source's empirical conclusion stays the same but a formerly untested auxiliary gains an independent successful test. Explain whether and why this strengthens your thesis, affects only a subsidiary claim, or leaves a different gap untouched.

If this defense was exposed, use **A-defense**: the theory's empirical success persists but its most plausible unobservable mechanism is replaced by a rival with the same observable predictions. Identify exactly which premise of your paper is affected, give an objection to your response, and state a limit. These are hypothetical changes, not assertions that the real climate packet has changed.

## Final delayed occasion — D1 or D2 (30 minutes)

Schedule roughly **four to six weeks** after F/A or **after two later modules**. Use a calendar interval when stopping here. This occasion is charged once to Course 10 M08. Spend 20 minutes on the prompt selected by the least secure row, then 10 minutes on source/evidence review. Conceptual response first without old notes; the packet/text is available for exact citations and uncertainty wording. Do not open the diagnostic before answering. D2 replaces an exposed D1; select only one row prompt rather than rush all four.

# Annotated response examples and revision progressions

These are **original instructional fragments**, not quotations, complete assessment answers or real learner evidence. They use practice material rather than final fresh prompts. Read them after attempting the relevant practice. The labels apply only to the visible reasoning; a finished source-based submission also needs verified editions and locators. No invented example establishes a fact about a historical tradition.

## Interpretation: a fictional ritual-learning case

**Prompt:** Two apprentices perform a welcoming ceremony. One repeats each movement perfectly but ignores the visitor's needs; another makes a minor formal mistake while responding attentively. What could the case show about learning and ethical conduct?

**Developing response:** “The attentive apprentice is good because rituals are just superficial rules. Real morality comes from feelings, so the rules do not matter.”

**Annotation:** The response notices a distinction between performance and responsiveness but leaps from one failed performance to the irrelevance of every form. It also assumes that feelings alone establish goodness. Repair by separating a practice's outward form, the attitude involved and the way practice might help develop that attitude.

**Competent response:** “Perfect execution does not by itself establish that the first apprentice has learned the point of the welcome. Attending to the visitor is a plausible part of the practice's purpose, so the second apprentice may better realize that purpose despite a formal error. This does not show that repeated forms are useless: they might train attention. To connect this reading to the Analects, I would need an assigned saying about the relation between conduct and attitude and another about learning.”

**Annotation:** This supplies the missing purpose premise, limits the inference and explicitly distinguishes the original case from a claim about Confucius. It is competent conceptual practice. It is not yet a competent A2 source interpretation, because the actual locators and evidence remain to be supplied.

**Strong response:** “The case separates technical performance from attentive responsiveness, but it does not tell us whether the two apprentices followed the same learning process. If rehearsal normally trains attention, the first apprentice may illustrate an incomplete learning process rather than the failure of form itself. A rival reading makes responsiveness independent of learned form; the case alone cannot choose between these explanations. An Analects-based argument should therefore test both against assigned passages, including one that makes learning or patterned conduct central. The conclusion should concern what the visible performance establishes, not declare an entire practice empty.”

**Annotation:** The response identifies two explanatory hypotheses and the evidence needed to discriminate between them. It preserves the difficulty instead of resolving it through a slogan. Its strength lies in method and inference, not a claim of source verification.

## Comparison: a personhood argument

**Prompt:** A community praises a generous resident as “a true person” and condemns a cruel resident as “no person,” while continuing to protect both from violence. What inference should a comparison of theories avoid?

**Developing response:** “This proves that African philosophy makes humanity conditional, whereas Western philosophy gives everyone rights.”

**Annotation:** The conclusion generalizes from a fictional case to continents, confuses ethical praise with descriptive humanity, and introduces an unexamined comparison. Better rhetoric will not repair these inferential errors.

**Competent response:** “The expression appears evaluative because the condemned resident is still protected. That suggests a distinction between moral achievement and treatment owed. It does not establish that the same distinction appears in exactly this form in Menkiti or Gyekye. A comparison must identify how each essay connects achievement, community and standing, using the same question for both. It should not infer that one view is simply collectivist and the other simply individualist.”

**Annotation:** The response explains why the case points toward one distinction and refuses to invent textual evidence. In a course submission, two actual passage references would be needed to turn the proposal into a completed comparison.

**Strong response:** “The community's language can coherently rank moral conduct while preserving protection, so the move from denied praise to denied standing needs a further premise. That does not make the ranking harmless: we should ask whether recognition affects voice or membership even when basic protection remains. Menkiti and Gyekye should be compared on the role they give communal achievement and on the inference from that role to individual claims. The initial case tests the inference's logical necessity; their actual essays must determine whether either author supplies the disputed premise. A surviving disagreement may concern authority or formation even if both distinguish humanity from achievement.”

**Annotation:** This response avoids a false choice between “equivalent concepts” and “no moral consequences.” It identifies multiple consequences that need separate examination and leaves room for real disagreement after disambiguation.

## Research judgment: evidence for an institutional explanation

**Prompt:** A hypothetical historian finds that an inherited eligibility list excludes a neighborhood. An official insists that every current administrator means well. Can a paper infer the absence or presence of structural injustice?

**Developing response:** “The list is old, so it is unjust. Mills proves that all institutions are racist.”

**Annotation:** Age alone is not the mechanism, and a sweeping attribution replaces a checked source. The claim also merges empirical classification, causal explanation and normative judgment.

**Competent response:** “Good intentions do not settle the list's effects. The researcher should establish how the list was produced, how it is used and whether exclusion persists. A philosophical paper could then test a specific Mills argument about institutions against those facts. The fictional case supports a possible mechanism but supplies no real-world evidence. Whether the mechanism is unjust also needs a normative argument.”

**Annotation:** The response identifies a feasible evidence plan and preserves the distinction between original case and historical data. To complete B5 or B7, the actual Mills claim and its chapter locator must be inspected.

**Strong response:** “The administrator's sincerity and the eligibility mechanism answer different questions. I would first ask whether the list is a necessary condition for access and whether an accessible route corrects its inherited omissions. If correction is effective, that would weaken a persistence claim; if correction is unavailable, the mechanism may continue despite changed intentions. My paper should reconstruct one chapter-2 claim and distinguish its explanatory scope from a universal claim about every institution. A specialist review can identify objections, but its judgment will not replace primary-text analysis. Until the list's history and operation are documented, the real-world conclusion remains conditional.”

**Annotation:** The response names a possible defeater, a bounded primary-source task and a limit on secondary authority. It is stronger because the proposed conclusion can change under specified evidence.

## Draft → feedback → revision: A-track research claim

**Draft:** “Mengzi says people are naturally good, but Xunzi says they must learn. Since Mengzi also accepts education, he contradicts himself and Xunzi wins.”

**Feedback:** The argument treats goodness as an already completed state in the first premise. It must distinguish a developmental beginning from achievement. It also infers a winner without reconstructing the two uses of nature. Check the relevant assigned Mengzi passage and Xunzi23 before revising.

**Revision:** “The need for learning does not by itself contradict a claim about good beginnings. The disputed issue may instead be what those beginnings contribute to explaining cultivated conduct. My argument will compare the function of nature in the selected Mengzi passage with the nature/effort distinction in Xunzi23, then test whether an explanatory disagreement survives alignment of the terms. If it does not survive, the thesis must become a narrower claim about terminology.”

**Revision memo:** “I abandoned the inference from education to contradiction and replaced the verdict with a testable interpretive question. I retained the comparison of learning because both assigned arguments make it relevant. I still need checked passages and a verified translation note; the revised paragraph is a research plan, not a completed result.”

**Why this is substantive:** The revision changes the central premise and the procedure for reaching the conclusion. A purely cosmetic revision would have replaced “wins” with “offers the stronger view” while preserving the error.

## Draft → feedback → revision: B-track methodological claim

**Draft:** “Du Bois and Fanon have the same theory because both describe being judged through race. This is the universal Africana account of identity.”

**Feedback:** Shared topic does not entail shared explanation; two authors do not establish a universal field position. Choose one passage from each, state the common question, and identify one difference that could affect the inference.

**Revision:** “I will compare how the two selected passages connect an imposed racialized perspective to self-relation. The comparison will ask whether each makes the same transition from described encounter to account of agency. I will not infer an identical resolution from a common problem. The texts may reveal a difference in explanatory target, and neither passage by itself represents the entire field.”

**Revision memo:** “I changed the unit of comparison from two supposed comprehensive theories to two specified arguments. I removed the universal conclusion because the evidence could not support it. The next revision must replace these planning descriptions with actual passage-based reasons; a more careful scope alone does not establish the interpretation.”

**Why this is substantive:** The revision changes evidence requirements and the claim the paper will defend. An independent new case is still needed to show transfer after feedback. None of these exemplars is evidence that a learner or tutor has passed the course.

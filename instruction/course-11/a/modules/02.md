# C11A-M02 — Analects: cultivation and governing

**22 hours. Guiding question:** How do forms of learning and conduct shape motives rather than merely outward compliance? Prerequisites: A1 source/inference distinction and provisional glossary.

## Outcomes and reading

Reconstruct a four-saying interpretation; distinguish ritual performance from its ethical significance; test the scope of a family or governing claim; write an evidence-based 700-word argument.

Read Confucius, *Analects: With Selections from Traditional Commentaries*, trans. Edward Slingerland, Hackett 2003, **Books 1–4, 7, 12, 13 and 15**, with notes on passages used. ## Teaching

Aphoristic evidence has both power and limits. A saying can make a distinction sharply while leaving its application unsettled. Read once to identify the speaker and interlocutor, again to identify the contrast, and a third time to ask how another assigned saying complicates it. The goal is an account that explains a pattern with fewer forced exceptions, not a smooth system manufactured by ignoring inconvenient remarks.

For the cultivation sequence, examine 1.1, 2.3, 2.15 and 12.1. Learning can involve repetition, reflection, exemplary conduct and the reshaping of desires. A claim about ritual is not exhausted by the visible movement: ask whether the attitude makes the action appropriate, whether practice helps develop the attitude, or whether both are required. Those possibilities make different predictions about an initially awkward learner. Do not infer that an imperfect initial motive makes learning impossible.

For governing, distinguish deterrence from an internalized reason to act. If sanctions produce avoidance, a ruler may obtain compliance without the kind of character the text values. That is an explanation of the limitations of one mechanism, not automatically a universal ban on sanctions. Your reconstruction must supply the bridge from the example to the claimed political conclusion and state its scope.

Family relations introduce another pressure. Read 13.18 alongside 2.7 and 4.18 when considering uprightness and filial conduct. A practice of loyalty can conflict with public accountability. Merely calling it “virtue ethics” does not settle whose interest matters or whether remonstration changes the case. Also distinguish Confucius's reported reply from the position of a modern defender: the latter may offer qualifications that the text does not explicitly state.

## Worked practice — original case

In an invented clinic, trainees recite a respectful greeting flawlessly but ignore a patient's expressed concern. Another trainee speaks hesitantly but listens and adjusts the consultation. The visible form and responsiveness pull apart. A reasoned interpretation asks whether ritual organizes attention to another person; if so, recitation alone is inadequate. It does not follow that forms are useless: a novice may learn attentiveness through repeated structured encounters. The case tests an interpretation of cultivation, not a factual claim that ancient ritual was a clinical protocol.

Create a four-row passage table for either the cultivation or family cluster: locator; careful paraphrase; contribution to your thesis; resistance to that thesis. Draft A2, **700 words**, explaining why the four remarks support one qualified interpretation. Include a paragraph on what cannot be inferred from their arrangement. Have a tutor challenge the strongest scope claim, then revise it with a reason, not just softer wording.

## Independent changed-case A2-I

An invented town officer discovers that her brother falsified an inspection report. She can quietly persuade him to correct it before anyone is harmed; a rule also requires reporting every falsification. In 350–450 words, use 13.18 and one other assigned saying to distinguish what the text supports, what a modern extension would add, and what remains unresolved if correction fails. Thirty-five minutes, assigned texts and citation sheet only. No content assistance or worked-answer consultation. Criteria: accurate attribution, a genuine tension, and an explicit bridge from the sayings to the case. Do not award competence merely for choosing “report” or “conceal.”

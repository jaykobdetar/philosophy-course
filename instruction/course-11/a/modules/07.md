# C11A-M07 — Interpretive paper and substantive revision

**22 hours. Guiding question:** Which narrow claim can your evidence sustain after the best rival interpretation is considered? Prerequisites: A2–6 artifacts and an available primary-text dossier.

## Outcomes and assigned work

Write and revise **2,000–2,400 words** answering one of the accepted questions: (1) Is the Mengzi–Xunzi disagreement primarily about motivation, development, or the meaning of nature? (2) Does Mozi's argument require abandoning special relationships? (3) What does one selected Zhuangzi passage justify about knowledge or perspective? Include one specialist secondary interpretation and a translation note. No new complete primary book is assigned.

For a finite, immediately locatable specialist route choose one matching your question: Paul R. Goldin, SEP [“Xunzi,” §2 “Human Nature (xing 性)”](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/xunzi/); Chris Fraser, SEP [“Mohism,” §7 “Ethical Theory,” opening discussion up to but excluding the “Inclusive Care” subheading](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mohism/); Chad Hansen, SEP [“Zhuangzi,” opening introduction before the contents](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/zhuangzi/). Read the named bounded range, not the entire entry. All three bounded ranges were passage-inspected on 2026-10-20. These are named scholarly interpretations, not neutral final verdicts. A different specialist article may replace this range only after title, edition/version, exact extent and lawful access are recorded within the same 3h reading allowance; a longer source creates explicit extra time.

## How to build the argument

Choose a question whose answer could be wrong. “Both authors discuss nature” is a topic; “the disagreement survives alignment of their nature terms because their accounts of initial motivation differ in this particular way” is a contestable thesis. It commits you to evidence for the difference and to an answer to a rival who sees only terminology. A narrower thesis is often stronger because you can identify what would defeat it.

Build a dossier of **four to six primary loci**, including one resistant passage, plus the specialist range. For each, write: what it establishes; what it does not establish; what inference your paper adds. Give the secondary interpretation its strongest version before testing it. Agreement with a specialist is not evidence independent of the primary passages; disagreement should identify a reason rather than announce originality.

Use this decision procedure. If the question needs an unassigned chapter to become intelligible, narrow it. If two readings explain the same favorite passage, find a second assigned passage where they diverge. If the dispute turns on a translation, obtain the short comparison/commentary before drafting the verdict. If access fails, choose a different defensible thesis within the assigned texts and keep the course translation requirement pending. Do not solve an access problem by claiming linguistic expertise.

Allocate roughly 250 words to question and thesis, 600 to reconstruction, 500 to the rival and discriminating evidence, 450 to your argument, 250 to objection/reply and limits, and 150 to conclusion; integrate the translation note within the 2,000–2,400-word total. These are flexible paragraph functions, not a mandatory formula. Bibliography and the 300-word revision memo are outside the count.

## Worked example — original thesis surgery

Draft claim: “Mengzi is optimistic and Xunzi is pessimistic, so they cannot agree.” It mistakes a mood label for an explanatory thesis. Revision: “Both can allow cultivated goodness; the proposed disagreement concerns what initial dispositions contribute to its explanation.” This is better because shared achievement no longer erases the disputed mechanism. It is still only a research hypothesis until particular passages support it. A strong final version identifies the mechanism, the two locators, and a rival interpretation it survives.

Produce A7-D1 with a source ledger. Ask the tutor two questions: “Where does my interpretation exceed its source?” and “Which rival would most threaten my conclusion?” Before revising, rank the responses by their effect on the thesis. Submit A7-F, a 300-word memo naming one accepted and one rejected or unresolved suggestion, and a quotation/locator audit. Use [response examples](../../response-examples.md) to calibrate only after your first draft exists.

## Independent changed-case A7-I

After submitting the revision, write 300–400 words responding to this objection without copying your paper: **“Your interpretation explains the passage you selected, but the same evidence would support the rival if the relevant practice worked through coercion rather than cultivation.”** Specify whether this objection fits your thesis; if it does not, explain why and formulate its nearest relevant version. Identify one actual passage that decides something about it. Thirty minutes; primary dossier only, no feedback or AI during the attempt. Criteria: identify the target, respond to a real challenge and revise the claim if needed. This does not replace Module 8's three fresh rows.

# Course 11 — learner assessment forms

Use only the selected track. The primary fresh form is **three tasks × 30 minutes = 90 minutes**, followed by one **20-minute dialogue** or accessible typed exchange. The exact course grid controls; do not add another general ninety-minute exam. These are planning times, not speed grades. Agree access accommodations beforehand and log actual time. Each response should normally be 300–450 words, with brief locators; substance matters more than reaching a word count.

Allowed: assigned primary texts in their specified editions; a citation sheet containing locators, not answers; for A8-F2/A8-X2, the pre-checked short two-rendering comparison or translator commentary. Not allowed: AI content assistance, tutor hints, answer keys, prior responses, prepared argument notes. Declare exposure before starting. An exposed item is practice; use its alternate. The examiner records the exact passage, edition and any source access limitation. Mark a criterion `source-dependent` whenever the passage needed to judge it cannot be inspected. Keep [examiner notes](examiner-notes.md) closed until submission.

Choose the form at the start of Module 8; never coach it first. Source-open means the text may be reread, not that the examiner may select an untaught chapter. A passage already read can support a fresh question; an episode central to the essay cannot serve as the fresh interpretation here. If both listed forms were central to previous work, the examiner chooses another complete episode inside the assigned chapters, records its boundaries and adapts only the interpretive question, retaining the same criteria. Do not claim that a new task is unexposed if its answer has been rehearsed.

## 11A primary fresh form

**A8-F1 — Concern and cultivation, 30m.** In an invented town, a public water official reserves a reliable supply for relatives while non-relatives receive unsafe water. She argues that family loyalty is itself ethical cultivation and that impartial care would require ending family bonds. Reconstruct a dispute between one assigned Analects passage and Mozi 16. Identify a reason each side could offer, test the two claims in her defense separately, and explain why a verdict needs more than the labels “partial” and “impartial.” Use two exact locators. Criteria: accurate reconstruction, concern/treatment/relationship distinctions, and an explicit theory-to-case inference.

**A8-F2 — Nature and deliberate effort, 30m.** Reopen **Mengzi 6A1** and **Xunzi 23's distinction between nature and deliberate activity/effort**, using the actual wording in Van Norden and Hutton. A critic argues: “If shaping is involved, the result cannot express anything natural; therefore Mengzi's view contradicts itself.” Reconstruct the role of the shaping analogy and explain whether Xunzi's claim establishes the critic's conclusion. Compare two identified short renderings of one relevant term or use an explicit translator discussion; show one philosophical consequence and one limit. The default comparison is Hutton23’s opening argument against the overlapping English rendering labeled Xunzi23.1a in Goldin’s SEP “Xunzi,” §2, as specified in A6; compare only shared sentences because the SEP excerpt is abridged. The examiner must verify both bounded renderings before the session without coaching its application. Criteria: text-to-inference fidelity, genuine remaining disagreement, and a documented translation-sensitive claim. If the comparison/commentary is unavailable, that criterion remains pending.

**A8-F3 — Perspective and method, 30m.** Interpret the **butterfly-dream episode at the end of Zhuangzi chapter 2**, unless central to your essay or A5. Explain one inference it supports and a stronger inference it does not establish. Compare it with a particular skeptical argument already studied in an earlier course, naming its source/locator from your retained citation sheet. State a productive common question and a consequential limit. A comparison that makes Zhuangzi simply a defective version of the other argument does not meet the criterion. Use alternate X3 if this episode was central. Criteria: narrative/speaker awareness, proportionate conclusion and justified comparison.

## 11A alternate fresh form

**A8-X1, 30m.** An invented evacuation team assigns parents to escort their own young children because they know the children's needs; unaccompanied children receive equally competent escorts. A critic says any special assignment violates Mozi, while a defender says family cultivation licenses ignoring unaccompanied children if supplies tighten. Assess both inferences using Mozi16 and an assigned Analects passage. Identify the remaining disagreement after rejecting any conflations.

**A8-X2, 30m.** Reopen **Mengzi 6A2** and Xunzi23's nature/effort distinction. An invented instructor takes every successful intervention as proof that an initial tendency did not exist. Assess that inference through the water analogy and the rival account of formation. Use a checked short translation comparison or explicit translator commentary, explain one consequence and state what English comparison cannot establish about the Chinese. Criteria match F2; this is a different analogy and inferential task.

**A8-X3, 30m.** Interpret the **large-gourd exchange in Zhuangzi chapter 1**. A reader concludes that a thing can have no standards of usefulness because one customary use fails. Evaluate the inference. Compare one precise issue with a studied European text using its locator; explain why a change in practical framing need not settle that text's entire theory of knowledge. Criteria match F3. If the large-gourd episode was central to the essay, use the primary if unexposed or the recorded selection procedure above.

## 11B primary fresh form

**B8-F1 — Racialized experience, 30m.** Read the chapter-I paragraph beginning **“The history of the American Negro is the history of this strife”** in Du Bois, together with the immediately preceding paragraph beginning **“After the Egyptian and Indian”**. A reader claims that resolving double consciousness requires giving up every inherited identification and accepting the dominant world's standards. Assess this interpretation, distinguish a phenomenological description, historical claim and normative aspiration in or relevant to the passage, and identify the inferential link between them. Cite the paragraphs. If these passages were central to the essay/B2, use X1. Criteria: accurate contextual reading, distinct claim types and a qualified response to the proposed interpretation.

**B8-F2 — Person and community, 30m.** In an invented case, an adult has longstanding, fully recognized membership in two communities. Each has helped form her capacities and each makes a legitimate emergency care demand at the same time; distance makes fulfilling both impossible. Neither community alleges selfishness, dissent or prior misconduct. One elder concludes that a coherent account of personhood must give one community unconditional authority over her choices, while another concludes that any account of communal formation must therefore be abandoned. Assess both inferences through **Menkiti and Gyekye**. Distinguish the role of community in forming a person from the authority to settle a conflict of obligations, and identify what further premise each conclusion requires. Use one exact locus from each essay and state one genuine disagreement or unresolved issue. Criteria: charitable paired reconstruction, explicit authority/formation inference and a reasoned response to conflicting legitimate claims.

**B8-F3 — Method and institutions, 30m.** An invented author compares three unattributed proverbs with a carefully reconstructed European social-contract theory and concludes that Africana thought contains no institutional critique. He dismisses **Mills chapter 2** because some present officials have benevolent intentions. Use a verified point from **Wiredu** to diagnose the comparison, explain one actual Mills claim and evaluate the relevance of the benevolence objection. Propose a narrower comparison, with an empirical question it would leave open. Criteria: method-to-diagnosis inference, Mills-specific reconstruction and bounded generalization.

## 11B alternate fresh form

**B8-X1, 30m.** The examiner selects **one continuous 200–350-word passage from Philcox 2008 chapter5** that contains a transition from narrated encounter or reported speech to reflection. It must be outside the essay/B3 focus. Record start/end page and opening/closing words privately; confirm the full surrounding paragraph is accessible. Prompt: “A reader treats every voice in this passage as Fanon's endorsement and treats the narrative as a statistical claim about all racialized experience. Identify the voices, reconstruct the philosophical point and distinguish what the passage supports from what would require separate historical evidence.” The selection rule is fixed; no invented page number is provided for an unsighted edition. If the examiner lacks the book, this form cannot be administered.

**B8-X2, 30m.** An invented official is publicly honored for communal service, although the service was performed only to secure power and involved exploiting uncredited volunteers. Another resident lacks honors but quietly supports neighbors and questions the award. Test whether recognized status entails achieved moral personhood under Menkiti and Gyekye, and whether either entails stronger basic claims. Locate one argument from each and preserve an unresolved disagreement.

**B8-X3, 30m.** An invented handbook compares an African philosopher's normative ideal with a European poll of actual preferences, then infers a continental moral difference. It also claims Mills's institutional explanation is refuted as soon as one exclusionary rule is repealed. Diagnose the comparison through Wiredu and assess the institutional inference through a specified chapter-2 claim. State one change that would genuinely weaken your explanation and one that would leave it intact.

## Dialogue — both tracks, 20m once

Spend approximately five minutes on the paper's thesis and key source, five on a new objection, five on a source/translation or method decision, and five on what remains uncertain. The examiner chooses the objection from your paper's actual inference, not an untaught topic. Examples: “Would your conclusion survive if the disputed practice produced compliance without the proposed motive?” (A); “Would your conclusion survive if recognition changed while the relevant institution remained?” (B). These general examples are public; the examiner must specify a new application to the submitted thesis. Typed/asynchronous responses may preserve the same four turns with agreed access time. Assess responsiveness, reasons and willingness to revise; do not grade accent, confidence or speech speed.

## Delayed forms — choose one least-secure group, 25m

Administer **four to six weeks later or after two later modules**, source-open under the same independence rules. This is charged to Module8 once. Earlier retrieval does not replace it. Use D unless exposed; DX is the changed alternate. Write 250–350 words. A failure reopens the row for targeted repair and another unexposed case within remaining time, otherwise log extra hours.

## Result and repair

Use [outcomes](outcomes.md) to record practice, fresh evidence, source check, repair and delayed result. Every essential row needs both satisfactory supported work and independent competence. Apply the relevant dimensions from the shared rubric: question, interpretation, reasoning, objection, evidence, organization, revision and dialogue. A retest uses a new case after feedback; a corrected answer to the same prompt is revision evidence. Do not average away a missing source or conceptual group.

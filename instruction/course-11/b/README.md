# 11B — Africana philosophy

**140 hours; Courses 1 and 8 required.** Read the [common guide](../README.md). This course follows selected African and African-diasporic debates; the field contains disagreements, languages, locations and histories. An author's claim about a tradition is itself something to analyze, not automatic permission to generalize.

Keep phenomenological description, historical explanation, conceptual analysis and normative argument distinct while examining their connections. A report of lived experience can challenge a theory without by itself establishing a population-wide causal estimate. A philosophical interpretation of communal personhood does not stand for all African thought.

Du Bois is available in the local collection. The other assigned primary texts must be lawfully borrowed or accessed in their specified editions; see the [audit](../source-audit.md). Marking those readings requires actual passage access. The course provides complete questions and teaching but does not invent evidence in their absence. Completion requires every [essential row](../outcomes.md), including the person/community debate even when the essay concerns Fanon or Mills.

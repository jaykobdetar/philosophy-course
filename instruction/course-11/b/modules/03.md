# C11B-M03 — Fanon: lived experience and racialization

**24 hours. Guiding question:** What can a description of an encounter reveal about the social organization of experience? Prerequisites: B1 claim types and B2 Du Bois.

## Outcomes and assigned reading

Interpret a chapter-5 passage in context; separate lived description, social explanation and normative implication; reconstruct an inference connecting them; compare one narrow claim with Du Bois without erasing their different projects.

Read Frantz Fanon, *Black Skin, White Masks*, **Richard Philcox translation, Grove 2008, chapter 5 “The Lived Experience of the Black Man,” complete**. Revisit only the bounded SEP context assigned in B1 as needed. The Philcox chapter was not available for inspection here; the SEP context supports orientation but cannot verify its wording. Earlier translations use a different chapter title and wording. Do not cite one as though it were Philcox or silently substitute it. Record actual page boundaries from a lawful copy before the reading begins.

## Teaching

Phenomenological description attends to how the world and the self appear in a situation. This is not equivalent to reporting a private feeling detached from its conditions. An encounter may reorganize what a person notices, how the body is experienced and what possibilities seem available. The philosophical question is how the description discloses a structure that a more abstract model of the subject may miss.

When reading chapter 5, mark shifts among the narrating voice, reported speech, recalled encounter, citation and reflection. A quoted statement of racial hierarchy is not necessarily Fanon's endorsement. Nor should a charged episode be paraphrased into a neutral vocabulary that erases the very mechanism under examination. You can describe the function of derogatory language without repeating it in your own voice. The assessment tests analysis, not willingness to reproduce slurs aloud.

Build the argument in layers. First, what happens in the described encounter? Second, how does it change the speaker's relation to his body, others or possible action? Third, which background structures make that experience intelligible? Fourth, what wider conclusion is warranted? Moving from the first to the fourth without the middle steps is a common error. Conversely, saying “this is merely anecdotal” may overlook that the passage targets an account of subjectivity rather than estimates the percentage of people who have a particular experience.

Comparison with Du Bois should preserve both a common problem and a difference. Both can be examined for how an externally imposed racialized perspective affects self-relation. But a shared topic does not prove identical explanations or historical circumstances. Ask whether the selected passages have the same argumentative target, the same account of agency and the same proposed resolution. Explain the limit with a consequence: which inference would become invalid if the difference were ignored?

The chapter may resist a single clean summary. A change in voice, mood or argumentative strategy is evidence to examine. Do not turn the most dramatic sentence into a timeless definition of Black experience. Your task is to make a bounded interpretation of this text, open to revision after reading its full movement.

## Worked practice — original encounter

In an invented laboratory, a visiting scientist expects to discuss her experiment. A host repeatedly asks her to explain the supposed temperament of her entire ethnic group. The interaction redirects attention from her project to an imposed category. A careful analysis distinguishes the reported experience, the host's classificatory act and a hypothesis about the institution's norms. One encounter can expose the structure of that act; it cannot alone prove how frequently every institution behaves this way. This is an analytic exercise, not a claim that all experiences or racial categories are interchangeable.

Produce B3: a 500-word passage reconstruction with a four-layer map, followed by a 500-word comparison with one chapter-I paragraph from Du Bois. Include two pieces of textual evidence from Fanon, one apparent complication, and a sentence identifying what your comparison does not establish. Use exact pages from Philcox. Ask the tutor to challenge one transition between description and general claim.

## Independent changed-case B3-I

An invented museum invites a scholar to speak about her archival discovery, then edits the publicity so that she appears only as an example of her racial category. A reviewer says no wrong can have occurred because she was not physically excluded and was praised. In 350–450 words, use a **different chapter-5 passage from the one used in B3** to analyze what the reviewer may overlook. Distinguish a phenomenological claim, a historical or structural hypothesis and a normative judgment; state which the supplied case cannot establish. Forty minutes, primary text and citation sheet only. No AI, tutor hints or prepared response. Criteria: passage fidelity, explicit bridges and contextual limits, not a prescribed verdict.

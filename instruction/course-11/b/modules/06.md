# C11B-M06 — Comparative-method workshop

**14 hours. Guiding question:** How does a methodological objection change an argument rather than merely add a disclaimer? Prerequisites: B1–5 and one earlier comparison.

## Outcomes and assigned work

Audit a comparison for conceptual translation, asymmetric evidence and overgeneralization; make a substantive correction; justify the revised common question; distinguish an author's claim from an alleged consensus of a tradition.

Reread Wiredu, **“How Not to Compare African Thought with Western Thought,” whole essay**, Wright anthology, **third edition 1984**. Return to the exact passages used in your B3 or B4 comparison and, for the institutional comparison exercise below, Mills **chapter 2, Cornell 1997**. These are revisits within the course budget. Exact Wiredu/Mills passages were not inspected by the author; the examiner needs access before making passage-specific judgments.

## Teaching

A disclaimer can leave an argument's machinery intact. “Of course not all Africans agree” does nothing if the next sentence still infers a continental view from one text. A substantive repair changes the unit of comparison, the evidence or the conclusion. It may replace “African personhood versus Western individualism” with “two identified accounts of whether moral achievement affects normative standing.” That is a different claim with a manageable evidential burden.

Use a five-pass audit. **Question:** Are both texts answering the same question, or are you forcing one into the other's framework? **Terms:** Does an English word carry different roles? **Evidence:** Have you compared similar genres and levels of reflection? **Scope:** Does your conclusion exceed the people, period and texts considered? **Inference:** Even if the descriptions are accurate, does the contrast follow? Write a correction beside each failure and explain which paragraph must change.

Symmetry does not require erasing real differences. A philosophical treatise and a narrative may address the same problem by different methods; the comparison can make those methods its subject. What is unacceptable is using the narrative's lack of a formal premise list as evidence that it has no reasoning, while reconstructing implicit premises charitably for the treatise. Apply the same interpretive charity and the same demand for evidence to both.

An institutional comparison adds another layer. A text criticizing racial hierarchy and a text explaining moral personhood do not automatically share a theory of social recognition. They may be useful to compare on a tightly specified question, but “both discuss community” is not enough. State why the question is relevant to each, then identify what each reading can answer. Sometimes the best result is that the initial comparison should be abandoned and replaced with a different pair.

## Worked repair — original draft

Draft: “Fanon and Du Bois prove that racialized subjects can never have agency. Both describe being viewed by others, so their theories are the same.” Repair: “The selected passages investigate constraints on self-relation; whether those constraints eliminate agency requires further argument. A comparison should test how each passage connects imposed perception with possible action.” The revision removes an unsupported universal conclusion and turns alleged equivalence into a research question. It still needs actual passage evidence; improved caution is not a substitute for reading.

Produce B6: quote or paraphrase 300–400 words of your own earlier comparison, annotate five possible failures, then rewrite **600–800 words**. Include a 200-word memo stating one claim abandoned, one preserved and one newly limited. Cite an exact Wiredu argument that motivates the change. Add a 150-word explanation of why one Mills chapter-2 claim should not be attributed to every author on this course.

## Independent changed-case B6-I

A fictional lecturer compares Menkiti's philosophical argument about attained personhood with a contemporary opinion poll of European consumers. He concludes that “Africa values relationships, Europe values possessions,” then cites Mills as proof that every Africana philosopher agrees. In 350–450 words, diagnose two distinct inferential errors, use a verified Wiredu point, explain why one actual Mills chapter-2 claim does not support the alleged consensus, and design a narrower comparison. Thirty-five minutes, assigned texts and citation sheet only; no AI/hints or previous audit notes. Criteria: change the comparison's design, preserve author-specific claims and explain the inference rather than merely condemn stereotyping.

# C11B-M01 — Context and comparative method

**14 hours. Guiding question:** When does a comparison manufacture the contrast it claims to discover? Prerequisites: Courses 1 and 8; distinguish an argument from a report of a belief.

## Outcomes and assigned reading

## Teaching

A comparison requires a common question, but a common question should not dictate the answer before reading. Comparing the reflective arguments of one tradition with the unexamined popular beliefs of another confounds a difference in evidence with a difference in intellectual capacity. The same mistake occurs in reverse. A defensible comparison identifies the genre and level of reflection on both sides, then states what its sample can establish.

Separate **phenomenological** description of how a situation is experienced, **historical** explanation of events and institutions, **conceptual** analysis of what a term or distinction does, and **normative** reasoning about what should be done or valued. A single paragraph can connect all four. The point is not to sort writers into boxes; it is to identify the inference needed when moving between kinds of claim. An account of humiliation, for instance, can reveal a moral problem without independently estimating its prevalence.

Du Bois's Forethought presents an authorial purpose, scope and position toward his readers. Treat this self-description as evidence about the book's project, not as external confirmation of every historical claim later made. The bounded Fanon context locates a distinct Caribbean, French colonial and anticolonial trajectory; it prevents an easy equation of all Black experience with a single national history. The Wiredu reading supplies the core methodological challenge. While reading it, find the actual grounds of his criticism instead of attributing every modern warning about comparison to him.

Build an author card with fields: work/date; relevant location and historical setting; language or translation; principal question; kind of evidence; scope limitation. Write “not established by assigned source” where needed. Do not fill gaps from stereotypes. A philosophical author can make a general claim that is contestable even within the community discussed. Respectful reading includes taking that disagreement seriously.

## Worked practice — original bad comparison

An invented exhibit compares one European philosopher's polished account of evidence with three unattributed African proverbs and concludes that only Europeans reason. The evidence is asymmetric in authorship, genre and selection. A repair compares two identified arguments on the same epistemic question and asks what each treats as a reason. It may still find a real difference. Merely changing the conclusion to “both are equally rational” would leave the evidential defect untouched.

Write B1, **600 words**, repairing that exhibit. Identify three failures, propose a feasible comparison using the assigned course authors, and say which claim remains unanswerable from the supplied evidence. Cite an exact point in Wiredu after accessing the essay. Attach six provisional author cards for Du Bois, Fanon, Wiredu, Menkiti, Gyekye and Mills. Refine them as sources are read; do not invent biographical precision now.

## Independent changed-case B1-I

A fictional handbook compares a Western legal code with one African philosopher's ideal account of communal responsibility. It concludes that Western people care about rights while African people care only about duties. In 300–400 words, identify what must be held constant for a useful comparison, distinguish a textual claim from a claim about populations, and propose a narrower research question. Use one verified Wiredu passage. Thirty minutes, assigned reading and citation sheet only; no AI/hints/answer key. Criteria: locate the inferential failure and redesign the comparison, not simply add “not all.”

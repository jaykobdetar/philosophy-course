# C11B-M07 — A bounded argument and its revision

**24 hours. Guiding question:** What precise interpretive disagreement can you resolve with the sources you actually have? Prerequisites: B1–6, access to the primary dossier and an assistance log.

## Outcomes and assigned work

Write **2,000–2,400 words** on the Menkiti–Gyekye debate, Du Bois and Fanon on a specified problem, or a precise Mills argument. Include a checked specialist source and a method note limiting the scope. No new whole primary text is required. The paper must defend a thesis and answer a serious objection, not merely summarize a field.

The fully specified default specialist route is Kwame Gyekye, SEP [“African Ethics,” §4 “Moral Personhood,” complete](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/african-ethics/), for a person/community paper. This section was inspected. It is a later specialist interpretation **by one participant in the debate**, not independent corroboration or a substitute for the 1992 chapter. A Du Bois/Fanon paper may use the already assigned bounded SEP “Frantz Fanon” passage as a specialist account of method, but must formulate a claim it actually discusses; it cannot supply uninspected chapter-5 details. For a Mills paper, read Thomas McCarthy's review of *The Racial Contract*, *Ethics* **109(2), January 1999, pp. 451–454, complete**, [DOI 10.1086/233910](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/233910). The publisher verified author, issue and pages; the review text requires lawful library access and was not inspected. Read it before deciding which of its interpretations or objections bears on your selected chapter-2 argument; do not infer its verdict from the fact that it is a review. The 3h source block covers this four-page reading and primary comparison. If access fails, keep that paper route access-dependent or choose another available route. No purchase is required by this packet.

## Decision procedure and writing instruction

Start with a contestable question small enough to answer. For personhood: “Does this argument move from moral achievement to reduced basic standing, and does its critic establish that the move is warranted?” For experience: “Do these two passages use externally imposed perception in the same explanatory way?” For institutions: “Does this chapter-2 argument need a claim about every individual's intention?” These are starting questions, not predetermined answers.

Make a six-entry dossier: two primary passages supporting your reading, one resistant passage, one rival interpretation, the specialist discussion and one source limitation. For every entry specify what it can support. If the rival concerns a passage you cannot inspect, do not claim to have refuted it. If your thesis requires evidence about contemporary populations, either obtain a bounded reliable source within an explicit additional budget or narrow the claim to textual and philosophical analysis.

Draft an argument map with five nodes: question; qualified thesis; two reasons; serious objection; reply or revised thesis. The method note should explain a decision with consequences—for example, why you compare two passages on self-relation while declining to infer identical colonial histories. “These authors are complex” is a disclaimer, not a method.

A useful paragraph moves from a claim to the passage supporting it, explains the inference and names a limitation where needed. Quotation cannot do the inferential work for you. Do not turn a plausible implication into “the author says” unless the text actually states it. Mark empirical, interpretive and normative premises in the draft; remove the labels only when their roles are clear.

## Worked example — original narrowing

Draft thesis: “Menkiti denies individual rights and Gyekye restores them.” This asserts more than the title of either essay can establish. A better research hypothesis asks whether a particular conception of achieved moral personhood entails diminished standing for a non-achiever. The revised hypothesis identifies the missing connecting premise and allows the texts to decide whether the dispute is about that premise, a definition, or both. It is not yet an answer. Source-based work may show that the first hypothesis was mistaken or that a narrower version survives.

Produce B7-D1 and a source ledger. Seek feedback on the most consequential interpretive inference and the strongest objection, then revise before polishing sentences. Submit B7-F and a **300-word revision memo**: main change; evidence prompting it; one suggestion rejected or unresolved; residual uncertainty. The [annotated responses](../../response-examples.md) are calibration material after the draft exists, not sentences to copy.

## Independent changed-case B7-I

After submission, write 300–400 words answering: **“Your argument treats a failure of social recognition as if it settled moral standing or institutional justice. Identify the connecting premise and defend it, qualify it, or show that your thesis does not need it.”** Use one actual primary locus and formulate the objection's nearest relevant version if the wording does not fit your paper. Thirty minutes, primary dossier only; no AI, hints or feedback. Criteria: identify the target, distinguish senses and make a reasoned response. Module 8 still samples every essential group.

## Budget and next step

Question/dossier 2h; specialist-source reading/check 3h; argument map 3h; draft 7h; feedback 2h; substantive revision 4h; independent response 0.5h; citation audit/retrieval 2.5h = **24h**. Two days later, explain your thesis and strongest objection without notes. If the method note makes no difference to the conclusion, use it to change a comparison, scope claim or source choice.

# C09 Fresh B — alternate learner form

Same timing, scope and [assistance/source rules](../assessment.md) as A. Cases change the relevant inference, not merely names. Use the assigned editions.

## B-K — 35 minutes, about 400–500 words

Observers agree that a glass changed from intact to broken at noon. They disagree about its cause. One proposes a cause governed by a strict law; another proposes a probabilistic process and argues that an objective order can be identified without a determinate sufficient prior condition. A third claims their disagreement proves there was no objective event. Reconstruct and evaluate the relevant Second Analogy argument (**A189/B232–A211/B256**). Distinguish the challenges posed by the second and third claims. State what the passage establishes on your reading and what remains disputed. No scientific expertise beyond the supplied premises is required.

## B-S — 30 minutes, about 350–450 words; one branch

**H branch:** interpret Hegel **§§194–196**. A commentator says: “An expert artisan has mastered one difficult technique; that alone establishes the independence described here, regardless of fear or service.” Assess the interpretation with paragraph evidence. Explain what follows and what does not follow for a contemporary claim about education through work.

**M branch:** interpret Marx, **Theses on Feuerbach I and II**, complete. A campaign claims: “Since truth must be shown in practice, any political action that achieves its immediate goal is true; reflection and criticism can be discarded.” Assess the interpretation and identify what the supplied claim would need to establish. Note the edition/version and distinguish practical activity from mere success at any chosen aim.

## B-G — 25 minutes, about 300–400 words

A fictional moral code claims authority specifically because its founders discovered it through impartial inquiry. New historical evidence shows its founding judgments were systematically shaped by a desire to humiliate rivals, with no attention to the facts cited as reasons. The code's defender replies, “Origins are always irrelevant; mentioning them is automatically a genetic fallacy.” Evaluate that reply using Nietzsche **First Essay §§10–14**. Distinguish the code's particular authority claim from its possible truth or present justification; compare genealogy with one transcendental and one social-explanatory question. Do not assume that impartial inquiry is the only possible source of moral authority.

## Explanation — 20 minutes, after writing

Choose one answer and identify a plausible rival interpretation or premise. Explain which passage or case detail favors your own response, then answer one clarification. A saved two-turn text exchange is equivalent.

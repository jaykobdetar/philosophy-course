# C09 Fresh A — learner form

Use only when unrehearsed. Follow [assistance/source rules](../assessment.md). Primary editions: Kant Guyer/Wood 1998 and Hatfield 2004; Hegel Miller 1977; Marx Livingstone/Benton 1975; Nietzsche Diethe 2007.

## A-K — 35 minutes, about 400–500 words

In a hypothetical apparatus, a clamp maintains a flexible strip in a bent shape. The clamp's continuing action and the strip's bend coexist. When the clamp is released, the strip straightens. For this case, stipulate that the direct effect begins with the relevant causal action: no positive time interval separates them. An observer objects: “Kant makes causation necessary for objective succession, but a cause must always occur a measurable interval before its effect. This apparatus therefore cannot be described as an objective causal change on his account.” Reconstruct and assess the objection using the Second Analogy, **A189/B232–A211/B256**, including its discussion of simultaneous cause and effect. Explain how the represented change differs from the sequence of your observations, and identify one limit of what the argument establishes about this apparatus. Include a checked passage locator. The stipulated apparatus requires no claims about actual materials or measurement precision.

## A-S — 30 minutes, about 350–450 words; one branch

Assessor assigns the branch outside your essay's focus. **H branch:** interpret Hegel **§§180–184**: explain the role of each self's activity in the account of recognition, then test this proposed application: “An institution can fully recognize its members solely by declaring them recognized, regardless of anything they do or acknowledge.” Distinguish the text's reasoning from the extra premises an institutional application would need.

**M branch:** interpret Marx, **Theses on Feuerbach III and VI**, complete: explain what is at issue in a reformer claiming to stand outside the social circumstances that shape everyone else. Test this application: “An educational council can change a community by changing its circumstances; the council's own formation is irrelevant.” Cite each thesis and note the version used.

## A-G — 25 minutes, about 300–400 words

The surviving public pamphlets of a fictional guild praise its own restraint and condemn competitors as evil. One historian proposes that blocked hostility produced a sincere reversal of values. Another proposes that guild leaders retained their earlier values but adopted this rhetoric strategically to attract customers. Both accounts fit the supplied pamphlets; no evidence about private commitments or the sequence of their formation is available. The first historian argues: “Nietzsche's account explains the pamphlets, so it proves the reactive origin of the guild's values and discredits those values.” Using Nietzsche **First Essay §§10–14**, assess both steps. Specify evidence that could distinguish the two historical accounts and explain how this inquiry differs from Kant's conditions-of-experience argument and Marx's inquiry into productive relations. Do not assume either proposed history has already been established.

## Explanation — 20 minutes, after writing

Choose one answer: which premise does the most work, and what would change if it were false? Respond to one clarification from the assessor. A saved two-turn written exchange is equivalent. Record assistance and source access after completion.

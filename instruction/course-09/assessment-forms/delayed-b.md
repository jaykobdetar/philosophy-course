# C09 Delayed B — alternate learner form

Same **four-to-six-week or two-later-module** interval and 25+5-minute allocation as Delayed A. Choose one row. Record exposure; use primary texts and neutral access tools only.

**DB-K:** An investigator uses a stopped clock's reading to order two events, then supports the clock's reliability by assuming that very event order. A colleague argues that Kant's causal principle makes this procedure legitimate. Assess that argument with the Second Analogy, A189/B232–A211/B256, Guyer/Wood 1998. Distinguish a transcendental condition from an empirical test of the instrument and identify the disputed inference. Unlike DA-K, the issue is circular evidential support, not memory order.

**DB-S:** Interpret Hegel **§§191–193**, Miller 1977. A leader depends on a group of critics whose authority she acknowledges and whose judgments she cannot compel. A reader says: “Any dependence on another duplicates the lord's failure, so this leader's self-understanding must fail in the same way.” Evaluate the reading with passage evidence and state a limit of the application. Unlike DA-S, this concerns the kind of recognition rather than the relation of theory and practice. Use only if outside essay focus and uncoached; otherwise the delayed S evidence remains unresolved pending a new form.

**DB-G:** A genealogist proposes a universal principle: “Any judgment formed through reactive hostility loses its epistemic authority merely because of that origin.” She uses it to dismiss a rival's moral judgments. She then accepts reliable evidence that her own universal principle was formed through reactive hostility toward that rival, but exempts it because she calls her inquiry scientific. Using Nietzsche I.10–14, Diethe 2007, evaluate the principle's application to itself and the proposed exemption. Distinguish any conclusion about authority from one about truth, and state what would be needed for a more limited genealogical criticism. Compare the kind of explanation at issue with Kant's and Marx's projects. Do not attribute the fictional universal principle to Nietzsche without a textual argument.

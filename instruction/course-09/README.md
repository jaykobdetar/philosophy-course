# Course 9 — Kant and selected nineteenth-century responses

Complete authored route: **8 modules, 140 hours**. Prerequisites: Courses 5 and 6; Course 7 is useful. Follow the preserved [curriculum](../../current/courses/09.md) and [assessment principles](../../current/assessment.md). This is a bounded encounter with Kant, Hegel, Marx and Nietzsche, not a complete history of the period. Authoring date: 20 October 2026.

The course asks how claims to objectivity, independence and moral authority can be examined. Kant investigates conditions of possible experience. Hegel tests a form of self-consciousness through its own dependencies. Marx investigates estranged productive activity and social relations. Nietzsche reconstructs the emergence and evaluative character of moral contrasts. Similar words such as “necessary,” “dependence” and “critique” do different work in these projects.

## Readings and access

Use the exact editions and ranges in each module. The [source audit](source-audit.md) records what was inspected, what remains access-dependent, and the difference between the supplied older translations and assigned editions. The modern Hatfield, Guyer/Wood, Miller, Livingstone/Benton and Diethe wording has **not** been passage-verified in this authoring run. Obtain a lawful library, owned or licensed copy; no purchase or copying authorization is implied. Older copies support orientation, not unmarked substitution. A learner may proceed with original exercises while an assigned text is unavailable, but passage assessment remains unresolved until its source can be inspected.

For audio study, listen to a lawfully available matching translation or use permitted text-to-speech. Pause at section/paragraph markers and dictate a premise map. Check each quotation and canonical locator against text before submission: an audio timestamp is supplementary, not a replacement citation. All oral tasks have written equivalents; diagrams have text-list equivalents. Break long reading into 25–45-minute units within the stated totals. Access adjustments may extend elapsed time without adding philosophical requirements; log actual time and discuss any persistent mismatch.

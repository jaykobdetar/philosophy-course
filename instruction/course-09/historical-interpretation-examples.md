## Developing paragraph

“Hegel proves that masters are bad because they depend on workers. Since dependence destroys freedom, workers become entirely free by making things. This predicts the eventual overthrow of every master. Modern workplace hierarchy therefore contradicts Hegel's conclusion.”

**Annotation:** The paragraph sees that dependence and work matter, but replaces the recognition argument with an unargued moral verdict. It assumes all dependence destroys freedom, omits the asymmetry of recognition, and turns a movement of self-consciousness into an inevitable political prediction. §196 qualifies what formative activity establishes. No locator supports the final institutional verdict. Repair the reconstruction before debating contemporary hierarchy; adding citations to unchanged claims would not solve the problem.

## Competent paragraph

“In §§191–193, the lord seeks confirmation of independent standing from a consciousness treated as dependent. That creates an internal difficulty: the source of confirmation lacks the standing the lord's own aim requires. The passage therefore unsettles the lord's claim to self-sufficiency. It does not follow that every dependence on another destroys autonomy. A relationship in which the other's standing is acknowledged may differ in precisely the respect that matters. The subsequent account of formative work must also be read with §196's qualifications, rather than as a guarantee that any productive skill brings complete freedom.”

**Annotation:** This identifies the target, gives a premise-to-conclusion explanation and limits the conclusion. The locators match the inspected Baillie paragraphs in broad content. It distinguishes textual inference from a possible extension. It remains relatively compressed: the kind of standing and the significance of the work-related qualifications need development in a full essay.

## Strong paragraph

“The lord's dependence is problematic in §§191–193 because the sought recognition is supplied by an other whose independent standing the relation denies. An alternative reading treats the argument as demonstrating that dependence itself is incompatible with freedom. That reading explains the emphasis on independence, but has difficulty explaining why the character of the recognizer matters. The narrower reading can connect the failure to one-sided recognition while leaving open whether a different dependence could constitute a viable form of self-consciousness. This does not yet establish a political model of mutual recognition: the assigned passage identifies a failure and develops its reversal, while §196 prevents us from treating an isolated technical accomplishment as the completed result. A contemporary institutional application needs further premises about what standing its members actually possess.”

**Annotation:** Strong work explains the appeal of a real alternative, gives a reason to prefer a reading and resists overstating the small range. It does not become strong merely by being longer. A specialist might challenge the reconstruction's terms or its relation to the whole work; a full essay should state that limitation and inspect the assigned translation. The paragraph models the handling of evidence, not a uniquely mandatory reading.

## Draft → substantive revision

**Original draft:** “The slave is independent because work teaches skills. Hegel thus recommends labor as the solution to political oppression.”

**Feedback memo:** The first sentence collapses formative activity into skill acquisition; the second adds a political recommendation without showing the transition. Check §§194–196, especially the qualifications concerning fear, service and restricted accomplishment. Explain what relation to the object changes and what larger conclusion remains unestablished.

**Revised paragraph:** “Formative work gives the bondsman an enduring object in which to encounter his own activity. On this reading, its significance is not merely the acquisition of a useful skill. The activity belongs to the wider movement involving fear and service, and §196 warns against taking limited mastery or stubborn self-assertion as the full achievement. The passage therefore supplies a philosophical account of a transformation in self-consciousness. Applying it to political oppression would require an additional account of how institutional relations preserve or obstruct that transformation.”

**Revision rationale:** The revision changes the explanatory premise, removes an unsupported recommendation and states the extra argument needed for application. It preserves the question of whether work contributes to independence rather than avoiding the issue. This is substantive revision even though it is not a complete answer to every interpretation of the passage.

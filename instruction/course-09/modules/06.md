# C09-M06 — Alienation and genealogy

**22 hours. Guiding question:** How can a social or historical explanation bear on the standing of a way of life or value?

**Prerequisites:** M05; Course 6's distinction between explaining a judgment and justifying it. **Outcomes:** reconstruct alienation without reducing it to mood, distinguish practical criticism from dismissal of thought, and evaluate a genealogical inference without assuming origins alone refute values.

## Assigned reading

Marx, “Estranged Labour,” **complete section**, and “Theses on Feuerbach,” **all eleven theses**, in *Early Writings*, Rodney Livingstone and Gregor Benton translation, Penguin 1975. Nietzsche, *On the Genealogy of Morality*, Carol Diethe translation, edited Keith Ansell-Pearson, revised student edition, Cambridge 2007, **First Essay §§1–17**, complete. These modern editions are access-dependent. Live Marx texts and the local Lough/Samuel alternatives differ in translation and, for the Theses, version history; [audit](../source-audit.md) gives the limits. Do not import Second Essay claims as if assigned here.

## Teaching

In “Estranged Labour,” Marx examines the worker's relation to productive activity under specified economic conditions. Objectification—the production of an external object—is not by itself alienation. The critical issue is that the product and the activity can confront the worker as independent, alien powers. The discussion moves through the product, the act of production, species-being and relations to other people. A person can report satisfaction while relations of control remain estranged; conversely, momentary boredom alone does not establish the entire diagnosis.

Species-being concerns a conception of distinctively human life and activity, including the capacity to make one's activity an object and produce beyond immediate need. Treat this as a substantive philosophical premise, open to criticism, rather than a biological fact already proved by the word “species.” The movement from productive relations to a judgment about estrangement needs an account of what relation human beings could appropriately have to their activity. Ask where the text supplies it and whether it is defensible. Also distinguish the argument about private property and estranged labor from an unsupported claim that every individual owner has a particular motive.

The “Theses” criticize a conception of materialism that leaves human practical activity outside its account. Thesis III questions a reformer's assumed standpoint above the people being transformed; VI challenges an abstract essence isolated from social relations. XI should be read with these claims, not as the slogan that interpreting, theorizing or checking truth is pointless. The relation between thinking and practice is precisely what needs explanation. Record the translation and version when using a thesis number: Engels' edited publication and Marx's notes are not automatically identical.

Nietzsche's First Essay investigates contrasts between good/bad and good/evil. In the account developed especially in §§10–13, a reactive evaluation forms itself in opposition to an enemy and gives a particular meaning to its own goodness. Ressentiment is not simply any fleeting resentment. The explanatory claim concerns a pattern of value creation. The account is evaluatively loaded, but a historical origin does not logically establish that every judgment descended from it is false or worthless.

The genealogical critic needs a bridge: perhaps a value's authority depends on a self-description contradicted by its history; perhaps the process systematically produces distortions; perhaps the value's effects frustrate a defensible standard of flourishing. Each bridge differs and can be challenged. A contemporary defender could grant the origin and offer an independent justification. That does not make genealogy irrelevant; it changes what the genealogy alone establishes. Do not use a fictional case as historical proof of Nietzsche's claims about actual peoples or religions.

**Worked original case:** A workshop pays well and employees enjoy the social atmosphere, but they have no say over production, lose control of the things they make, and must treat their activity solely as a means to subsistence. Happiness does not settle the Marxian diagnosis; the social relation and account of activity matter. Separately, a fictional honor code began as a way for excluded entrants to denounce elite competitions. Its origin may explain its vocabulary. To refute today's code, one still needs evidence about its reasons, operation or evaluative standard. These cases isolate different explanatory projects; neither claims both authors use one common method.

## Budget and practice

Reading 8h (Marx 4h; Nietzsche 4h); teaching 3h; two reconstructions/comparison 4h; feedback 2h; independent task 2h; revision/retrieval 3h = **22h**.

**P06-M and P06-N:** Write two **500-word reconstructions**: Marx on product/activity and Nietzsche on reactive value creation, using at least two precise locators each. Then write a 250-word comparison giving each project's question, explanatory premises, conclusion and a possible objection. A claim about economic history requires evidence; a textual reconstruction alone cannot establish it. Use one paragraph from the “Theses” to qualify an overly contemplative interpretation of Marx. Record its version.

## Independent task I06

Source-open, 90 minutes plus 30 minutes checks. A worker cooperative controls its outputs collectively but members face severe market pressure and some find their jobs boring. Separately, a movement's moral rule originated in reactive hostility, but its present adherents defend it by independently checked evidence of reciprocal benefit. Write 650–800 words: distinguish what the changed ownership premise does and does not settle about alienation; test whether the supplied genealogy refutes the present justification; compare the kinds of premise each inquiry needs. Cite one Marx passage and Nietzsche I.10 or I.13. Criteria: avoid a mood test, do not infer emancipation from ownership alone, identify a live bridge to evaluation and acknowledge missing evidence. This changes control and current justification, not merely the actors' names.

## Repair, retrieval and next step

For mood-only alienation, separate product, activity, human capacities and relations in four sentences. For genetic-fallacy dismissal of all genealogy, specify an authority claim the history could undermine. For a blanket endorsement of genealogy, write the missing evaluative premise. After one week, recover the two methods and one limitation in 15 minutes within revision. Proceed to [Module 7](07.md) with a source-bounded question.

# C09-M05 — Recognition

**18 hours. Guiding question:** Why might domination fail to supply the independence it seeks?

**Prerequisites:** M01–04 and the ability to distinguish reconstruction from contemporary application. **Outcomes:** trace the dependence in lordship and bondage, explain the significance and limits of work, and distinguish a passage's role from a later political use.

## Assigned reading

Hegel, *Phenomenology of Spirit*, A. V. Miller translation, Oxford 1977, **§§178–196**, complete. Accompany with Paul Redding, [“Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel,” SEP §3.1.1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hegel/), **only** the passage from the paragraph beginning “Something of Hegel’s phenomenological method” through the paragraph ending “But this is only worked out in the text gradually,” inclusive. Both guide endpoints were located on 20 October 2026. A Baillie translation in the collection preserves §§178–196; it supports orientation but does not verify Miller's wording. See [audit](../source-audit.md).

## Teaching

Recognition here is more demanding than being noticed or receiving praise. The opening claim ties self-consciousness's being for itself to being for another self-consciousness. An apparently independent self therefore encounters another whose standing matters to its own claim. A recognition that counts requires an other who is not merely an object under the first self's control. This creates a problem for the attempt to secure recognition through domination.

Read the passage as a movement: demand for recognition; struggle and the problem of death; unequal recognition in lordship and bondage; dependence of the lord; the bondsman's fear, service and formative work. Do not turn that sequence into a mechanical “thesis–antithesis–synthesis” label. State what each transition addresses. If the other is simply destroyed, the recognition sought disappears with it. If the other is preserved but subordinated, its recognition fails to establish the self-sufficient standing the lord sought.

The lord enjoys the object through another's work and receives recognition from a consciousness treated as dependent. The result undermines a claim to independence: its confirmation relies on what it dismisses as inessential. This is an internal difficulty in the attempted form of self-consciousness, not merely the external moral criticism that domination is unfair. The moral criticism may be warranted, but it is a different argument and needs its own premises.

The bondsman's formative activity delays immediate consumption and gives durable form to an object. In that enduring result, consciousness can encounter its own activity. Fear and service are integral to the passage's account; manual work by itself is not a magical guarantee of freedom. §196 qualifies the significance of formation and the difference between a restricted skill or stubborn self-assertion and the stronger result sought. The excerpt does not end with a political constitution or a documented proletarian revolution.

A historical-social reading and an account focused on the structure of self-consciousness may emphasize different aspects. Neither is licensed to ignore the text's combination of recognition, fear and work. A useful interpretation explains why the other emphasis is tempting, locates its own claim, and says where the small assigned range cannot settle the larger dispute. The bounded SEP guide supplies orientation to testing forms of consciousness by their own commitments; it does not replace close reading.

**Worked original analogy:** A judge orders a dependent assistant to certify that the judge is impartial. The certification cannot establish the independence the judge wants if its force depends on the very control whose effect is disputed. This isolates a recognition problem. It does not identify an employment relation with Hegel's entire struggle, and does not imply all recognition requires equal expertise. Specify the standing of the recognizer relevant to the claim.

Reading 5h; teaching 3h; practice/700-word interpretation 3h; feedback 2h; independent task 2h; revision/retrieval 3h = **18h**.

Use [historical interpretation examples](../historical-interpretation-examples.md) for calibration only after producing a first attempt.

## Independent task I05

Source-open, 90 minutes plus 30 minutes citation checking. Interpret **§§185–188**, focusing on why eliminating the other can frustrate the aim of recognition. Then analyze this changed case: two rivals both acknowledge the other's authority to criticize, but each still demands that every criticism be withdrawn before dialogue begins. Does the reciprocal acknowledgment solve the relevant problem? Write 600–750 words distinguishing text, inference and application. Criteria: explain the role of the other's survival/standing, diagnose the additional condition in the case, and state the limit of the analogy. Unlike the worked example, both parties are formally recognized; the issue is whether their conditions preserve an effective recognizer.

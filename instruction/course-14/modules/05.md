# Milestone 5 — Critical review

**16 hours.** Guiding question: What is the strongest reason a fair reader could reject this paper's main move? Prerequisite: complete P4 draft, source ledger and vulnerability note. Outcomes: reconstruct a serious objection, distinguish error from disagreement, assess conflicting advice and produce a prioritized response plan.

## Teaching: charitable opposition can change the thesis

The strongest objection accepts as much of your setup as it can and attacks the premise or inference the conclusion actually needs. “Some people disagree” names no problem. “Your conclusion is implausible” needs a reason. An internal objection grants the relevant premises but challenges coherence or consequence. An external objection disputes a premise, standard or method and must explain why the alternative is appropriate to the question.

A fair reconstruction preserves the rival's motivating problem. Improving a rival into a stronger argument is permitted when labelled as your reconstruction, with actual textual claims distinguished from additions. You cannot attribute your improvement to a source merely because it sounds charitable. Conversely, a source's imperfect wording does not justify attacking an obviously weaker proposition when context supports a stronger one.

A reply succeeds only if it reaches the targeted problem. If an objection shows that separate writers can share evidence, repeating that the writers are honest misses the issue. You might instead concede that honesty cannot establish independence and defend a restricted conclusion using independently originating observations. That concession changes the argument. A rival may then ask whether partial dependence is being ignored; this residual issue belongs in the scope statement.

## Work sequence

1. Prepare T5 with the question, map, relevant draft sections, edition/locator and permitted short source context. Ask three focused review questions: most serious interpretive error, weakest inference, strongest objection. A human review is optional if available and chosen. This course neither contacts a person nor authorizes expenditure. If none is available, use a skeptical source-bound review and label it provisional, including AI or self-review conditions.
2. Before reading comments, write a 400-word practice objection in the voice of a competent rival. Identify its exact target and why accepting it would damage the conclusion. Then write a 300-word reply that either defends, qualifies or abandons the claim. This is P5-objection/reply; it is coached practice, separate from the defense.
3. Read comments against the sources. Classify each as factual/textual error, inferential gap, scope objection, defensible disagreement, expression issue or uncertainty. State one reason for every classification. A reviewer's confidence is not evidence.
4. Complete P5 response plan using T5. Prioritize the issue whose correction has most downstream effects. For each recommendation write accept/modify/reject/defer, reason, exact change and consequences elsewhere. When rejecting advice, answer its best reason. If a claim cannot be verified, remove it or preserve the uncertainty rather than vote among reviewers.
5. Independently write a 300-word decision note choosing between two genuinely competing revision strategies for your weakest inference. For example, defend a disputed bridge premise with new evidence or reduce the conclusion so it no longer needs that premise. Explain cost and remaining burden. This prepares M6; F6 supplies the later uncoached changed demand.

Practice criteria: objection accurately targets the paper; reply answers it; source uncertainty visible; response priorities justified. A new conclusion can be a better result than a defense of the old one.

# Milestone 6 — Substantive revision

**32 hours.** Guiding question: Which changes make the argument more defensible, and how can I show that they do? Prerequisites: P5 response plan and available relevant sources. Outcomes: independently rewrite the paper, repair significant attributions, explain three important changes and handle a new revision demand.

## Assigned reading

Read [shared rubric](../../../current/assessment.md), “Revision,” “Evidence and attribution” and “Reasoning”; [worked examples](../worked-examples.md), full draft/revision pair and annotations; T4 and T6. Read Pryor's [writing guide](https://www.jimpryor.net/teaching/guidelines/writing.html), the third writing stage on rewriting only, ending before minor points. Recheck the exact source passages implicated by accepted criticism, including surrounding qualifications. A new source is assigned only after T2 fixes its edition/range and records its hours.

## Teaching: revision propagates

Changing the pivotal claim is not complete until its consequences reach the introduction, section headings, objection, conclusion and cited support. Suppose the original claim was “all dependent testimony has no evidential value” and the new argument establishes only “agreement alone does not establish evidential independence.” Removing the word “all” from one sentence is insufficient. The paper must stop inferring worthlessness from dependence and acknowledge what the new conclusion leaves open.

Structural revision changes the relationship among claims. It can introduce a distinction, replace a premise, reorganize an interpretation around better evidence, abandon a reply or reduce the domain. Expression edits make those relationships readable. Both matter, but clearer prose cannot repair a false attribution. Audit from the highest dependency down: central claim, supporting inference, source interpretation, objection/reply, organization, wording and citation mechanics.

A limitations section should explain which conclusions were not established and why. It is not a license to state those conclusions elsewhere. A revision memo is an argument about change: what was wrong, what is different, why the new version handles the problem better and what remains unresolved. If a criticism is mistaken, demonstrate that with the exact passage or inference. Compliance with every reviewer suggestion is not the objective.

## Work sequence

1. Make P6, a new outline with each accepted major change marked. Retain the old draft privately. For each changed premise list every dependent section using T5. Decide which source checks must precede writing.
2. Independently write the final paper A6, 5,000–7,000 words, using the revised map and sources. Feedback may diagnose problems beforehand; it may not supply replacement assessed paragraphs. Declare any editorial, search or AI assistance, distinguishing grammar support from content generation. The final sustained reasoning must be demonstrably yours.
3. Check every quotation and significant attribution against the actual copy, recording exact locators in T4. Check that apparent citations to different editions have not silently changed wording or pagination. M8 repeats the final verification after defense repairs; rereading altered claims is substantive audit work, not a duplicated paper.
4. Write T6's memo explaining the three most important actual changes with before/after locations and reasons. Attach one annotated changed section and one justified rejected recommendation. A strong draft may need fewer deep repairs; never fabricate defects to meet a numerical quota. Explain the three consequential changes that did occur.
5. Complete F6 independently in 45 minutes, using an unseen revision demand from [assessment](../assessment.md). Produce a 250–350-word response and a short revised passage or argument map. Save both even if the demand proves mistaken. It tests judgment under new criticism rather than the ability to repeat a coached edit.
6. Re-read for dependency consistency. Prepare a 150-word account of what your paper establishes, one limit, and what finding or argument would make you change your conclusion. Keep it as preparation, not a script for unscripted questions.

Criteria: changes repair the named problem; accepted advice has reasons; rejected advice is answered; sources support actual claims; final paper stays within its defended scope; assistance is disclosed. Central unresolved integrity or inference failures require repair before a completion verdict.

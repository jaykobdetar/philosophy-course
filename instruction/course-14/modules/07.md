# Milestone 7 — Presentation and defense

**12 hours.** Guiding question: Can I explain and revise my reasoning when a serious new question changes the discussion? Prerequisites: A6 and citation records. Outcomes: give a 10–15-minute explanation; answer 20–30 minutes of unscripted questions or equivalent accessible exchange; address an unrehearsed challenge; state what would change the conclusion; attempt a first new-case transfer.

## Teaching: respond to the question that was asked

A presentation tells the reader where the argument goes. A defense tests whether you can navigate when the question changes. Listening includes identifying whether the challenge concerns interpretation, evidence, inference or scope. Restate a difficult question briefly, answer it, give the relevant reason, and specify the consequence. Asking for clarification is legitimate. Repeating a prepared paragraph that does not address the question is not evidence of responsiveness.

An honest concession should locate the damage. “That may be true” is vague. “If that qualification applies to my primary passage, I must remove premise two; the weaker conclusion remains supported by premise three” shows what changes. You need not produce a perfect new theory immediately. A defensible answer can acknowledge that an essential claim is unresolved and specify the source or reasoning needed to settle it.

Changing a conclusion is rational when the new reason matters. Saying nothing could change it is usually a sign that its evidence conditions have not been stated. Name an intelligible contrary passage, counterexample, empirical correction or failed bridge principle. Some claims are conditional; changing the condition changes applicability without refuting the conditional. Explain which kind of revision is at issue.

## Work sequence

1. Prepare P7: a one-page outline and a 10–15-minute explanation covering question, necessary terms, central argument, serious rival, contribution and limit. Use source locators and a simple map if helpful. Slides are optional; a text outline provides an equivalent route.
2. Rehearse once with timing and feedback on clarity. Practice on the invented example in the worked packet, then on your project. Rehearsed questions cannot become independent evidence. Keep a list of questions seen so the examiner can choose a genuinely fresh challenge.
3. Conduct U7 once: 10–15-minute presentation followed by 20–30 minutes of unscripted questions. The combined occasion is 30–45 minutes, inside this milestone. Permit the paper, assigned sources and citation sheet. No live content help, answer key or prewritten responses. The reviewer selects a new challenge after inspecting the paper using the examiner protocol. Do not reveal the exact questions beforehand.
4. Use a typed exchange, assistive communication or another accessible equivalent when needed. Preserve spontaneous turn-by-turn response and comparable intellectual scope; record necessary timing adjustments. Do not grade speed, performance confidence or accent. A self-administered randomized prompt is a provisional substitute if no interlocutor is available; it must not be labelled an externally examined defense.
5. Complete T7 immediate transfer from the appropriate form in assessment, 30 minutes independently, then discuss the result. This first changed case produces practice/feedback for later transfer. Save the before-feedback artifact. The final delayed D8 must use a substantively different case and condition.
6. If U7 exposes a substantive error, repair the paper and document the change before the portfolio audit. A new follow-up question checks the repaired issue; log extra time if remaining hours cannot accommodate it. Record question, response, observer limitations and remaining uncertainty.

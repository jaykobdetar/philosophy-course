# Milestone 1 — Proposal and feasibility

**16 hours.** Guiding question: What precise philosophical question can I responsibly answer with the time and sources available? Prerequisites: completed research apprenticeship and access to its dossier; ability to reconstruct an argument and distinguish an attribution from your own inference.

Outcomes: formulate one disputed question; justify a fitting method; write an 800–1,000-word proposal with a qualified claim, strongest anticipated objection and exclusions; demonstrate that essential sources can actually be read.

## Teaching: the question controls the burden of proof

A topic names territory: “testimony and expertise.” A question identifies a decision on which reasons can bear: “Does a recipient's inability to check a report defeat its justification even when its production is reliable?” This remains too broad until the kind of recipient, claim, reliability and defeater are fixed. The question must allow a serious alternative; a formulation that already calls the rival irrational begs the question.

A method specifies what would justify the answer. An interpretation needs passage-level discriminating evidence. A normative conclusion needs normative premises. A conceptual counterexample can disprove an unrestricted entailment but cannot measure the prevalence of a real phenomenon. A comparison needs a stable question and a reason for comparing the selected units. Choose the main method and state the role of any secondary method using the selection guide.

Significance can be small and precise. Showing that one reply establishes a weaker conclusion than its author needs can matter because it changes how a dispute proceeds. “Nobody has solved this” is both unnecessary and hard to establish. Your contribution can be a better distinction, a limited objection, a defensible interpretation, a new application, or a reason a tempting comparison fails. An exclusion is legitimate if the remaining conclusion no longer depends on what is excluded. Excluding all counterevidence is not legitimate narrowing.

## Work sequence

1. In 20 minutes, write an unaided 200-word baseline: question, provisional answer, central reason, uncertainty. Save P1-baseline before feedback.
2. Make three progressively narrower questions from the seminar topic. For each give two plausible answers and one observation/argument that would favor each. Reject the question that requires inaccessible evidence or a verdict your method cannot support.
3. Complete T1 and the initial T2 access cards. Open the actual essential passages. A catalogue entry is evidence of publication, not of your access. Keep one adjacent fallback question that preserves the philosophical skill if an essential text is unavailable.
4. Draft P1, 800–1,000 words: question/significance about 150; provisional argument about 250; method about 150; strongest objection about 150; sources/feasibility/exclusions about 200. These are planning shares, not extra word limits. Include one explicitly supplied premise.
5. Request source-bound feedback on scope and method, then revise. Complete independent F1 from [assessment](../assessment.md), 45 minutes, within the independent allocation. The reviewer chooses an unseen form. Criteria: question answerable, method appropriate, source role precise, exclusion honest.

Worked practice: an argument that two reports corroborate one another because they have different authors fails if both authors copy the same original observation. The narrower question is whether *different originating observations* are required for the particular corroboration claim. The source need becomes evidence about dependence, rather than every theory of testimony. This is a constructed reasoning case, not an established thesis or substitute for the learner's literature search.

# Learner assessment conditions and fresh forms

Use [outcomes](outcomes.md) for every essential row and [shared rubric](../../current/assessment.md) for criterion meanings. These are milestone instruments inside 180 hours, not another final examination. The reviewer selects forms before each attempt, records prior exposure and withholds [examiner guidance](examiner-notes.md) until submission. Viewing a practice solution or receiving a task-specific hint means that form no longer supplies fresh evidence. Use the alternate; if both were rehearsed, record a materially changed instance using the examiner's rules.

Allowed: actual assigned sources, citation sheet and accessibility tools; sources must be identified. Prohibited during independent work: AI content help, answer keys, prewritten responses, live hints, generated replacement prose. Record any help, exposure, time and accommodations. Judgment concerns reasoning and source use, not speed. A known passage may support a fresh task if the inferential question is uncoached. All fictional facts below are stipulated teaching cases, not claims about real people or studies.

## F1 — Feasibility and method, M1, 45 minutes

Submit 300–450 words and an updated one-sentence question.

**Form A.** Your proposal's strongest claim relies on an article that you can verify bibliographically but cannot read; two relevant primary passages and one serious rival are available. Choose between changing the claim and suspending the project. Specify what the accessible evidence can establish, one exclusion, the revised method and an exact next source check. Do not pretend the missing item agrees with you.

**Form B.** Your proposal starts as a comparison of two texts but concludes that their respective societies behaved differently. The selected passages establish the authors' stated reasons; no evidence of social practice is available. Produce a feasible question and explain what change in conclusion or evidence is required. Apply the same reasoning to one dependency in your own proposal.

Criteria: question and method match; revised scope preserves a philosophical issue; access distinction clear; decision justified.

## F2 — Source integrity, M2, 45 minutes

For the selected original passage, write a 250–350-word audit: identify what it supports, what it does not, one quotation/paraphrase issue and a corrected bounded attribution. Then state how you would check the same failure in your dossier.

**Form A, source packet.** Invented note N, paragraph 1: “Some writers say that an agent's sincere confidence is enough to justify the agent's assertion. I will reject that test. A confident speaker may have ignored contrary evidence. My narrower proposal concerns cases in which the agent checked the available reasons; even there, I leave open whether an additional duty of inquiry applies.” Paragraph 2: “The test of adequate inquiry is not supplied in this note.” Draft use: “N proves that sincere confidence justifies assertion in all cases (N, paragraph 1).” Its bibliography lists N as “complete theory of inquiry”; the learner actually read only paragraph 1. These are invented metadata supplied for the exercise, not an external citation.

**Form B, source packet.** Invented note R, paragraph 1: “For this argument, assume the testimony is accurate. The question is whether the recipient's reliance can be reasonable even when the recipient cannot verify it.” Paragraph 2: “I defend that possibility under the further condition that no specific warning is available. I do not establish how often the condition is met.” Draft use: “R found that recipients usually know testimony is accurate”; quoted wording: “reliance is always reasonable.” Audit the changed modality, empirical claim and fabricated quotation. The quoted string appears only in the draft use, not in R.

## F3 — Changed constraint on the trial, M3, 60 minutes

Submit a 400–500-word revised argument or interpretation and a dependency map. Choose the lane matching the proposal, with the reviewer selecting A or B unseen.

| Method | Form A | Form B |
|---|---|---|
| Argument/application | Keep your conclusion's original scope, but withdraw the strongest premise you supplied rather than found in a source. What now follows? | Admit a case satisfying the stated premises but denying the conclusion. Explain whether it is coherent; if it is, repair the premise or restrict scope. |
| Interpretation | Your pivotal sentence is compatible with both your reading and a rival. Identify a second actual textual observation and show whether it discriminates. | An actual nearby qualification narrows the pivotal sentence. Reconstruct the restricted claim and test whether your objection still applies. |
| Comparison/genealogy/description | The two positions share a translated term but address different questions. Preserve one justified comparison and remove one unsupported inference. | Your explanatory or descriptive finding is accepted, but its evaluative conclusion is disputed. Identify and defend or withdraw the bridge. |

The reviewer identifies the actual claim/nearby passage from the submitted trial, records it and checks feasibility before the attempt. No invented textual qualification is attributed to a real source. If the described condition is absent, use the other form/lane or state a hypothetical constraint explicitly. Criteria: accurate original position; real dependency tracked; new conclusion justified; unresolvable issue admitted.

## F4 — Sustained reasoning under a new demand, M4, 45 minutes

**A.** Take the conclusion of your second supporting section as true. Independently explain why it still does or does not support the paper's final thesis if the strongest first-section reason is unavailable. Supply a 300–400-word replacement transition and list affected sections.

**B.** Preserve the strongest objection in your draft but remove your current reply. In 300–400 words defend a narrower conclusion that survives the objection, or explain why no useful narrower conclusion remains. Identify the exact passages that must change.

Criteria: contribution remains visible; inference survives stated change or its failure is acknowledged; changes propagate; no new unsupported attribution. A6, the full independently written final paper, remains the principal sustained-argument artifact.

## F6 — New revision judgment, M6, 45 minutes

**A.** New reviewer demand: “Your paper would be stronger if you deleted its scope restriction and made the conclusion universal.” Select the paper's most consequential restriction. Accept, modify or reject the demand using a counterexample or source-specific argument. Write 250–350 words and a revised 100–150-word passage or equivalent argument map.

**B.** New reviewer demand: “The strongest rival is unnecessary because you have already shown that one of its premises is controversial.” Explain whether controversy is enough to dismiss it. Accept, modify or reject the demand, identify its actual premise, and write the same outputs.

Criteria: criticism evaluated rather than obeyed mechanically; textual/inferential reasons; substantive change or reasoned retention; residual limitation explicit. No preferred philosophical verdict is prescribed.

## U7 — Presentation and unscripted response, M7

Give a 10–15-minute explanation, then answer 20–30 minutes of new questions or an equivalent accessible exchange. The examiner chooses project-specific questions after inspecting your actual paper. Expect questions about your pivotal inference, a source/translation choice, the strongest rival, a changed condition, and what would change your conclusion. The exact challenge must not have been rehearsed during drafting. Allowed paper/source access does not permit scripted replies. Record actual times, prompts, assistance and reviewer status. Your response may repair or qualify the claim. A substantive error triggers manuscript correction and a new follow-up, not automatic completion.

## T7 and D8 — Transfer with genuinely changed conditions

Choose the lane closest to the central distinction in the project. Name that distinction and an exact project location. If none of the cases can meaningfully engage it, use the examiner's documented instantiation rule; do not pretend an irrelevant analogy demonstrates transfer. At T7 use the immediate case, 30 minutes. At D8 use one unseen delayed case, 30 minutes after 4–6 weeks or two later modules, with a post-course calendar trigger by default. The D8 case must differ from T7 in domain and a material assumption. Produce 250–350 words: reconstruct the distinction, map it to the case, identify the changed assumption, infer a qualified result, and state a limit. Reasoned non-applicability is allowed when precisely explained.

| Lane | T7 immediate case | D8 delayed A | D8 delayed B |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evidence/knowledge/representation | In an invented garden, account H says the soil becomes wet exactly when it rains; account J says it becomes wet exactly when either rain or a sprinkler supplies water. The only observation is wet soil after rain with the sprinkler off. A researcher declares H established. Assess that inference and choose one further observation condition that would discriminate H from J, giving each account's prediction. Use your project distinction to explain the evidential job of the proposed observation; all facts and mechanisms are stipulated. | A laboratory has two independent instruments that disagree. Each is reliable under different temperature conditions; the temperature at observation is unknown. Apply the project distinction to discordant measurements whose applicability conditions are unknown; explain how the needed next observation differs from choosing between the garden accounts. | A translator supplies two independent translations that agree, but both lack the chapter containing the author's later qualification. Compare independence of translation with adequacy of context; identify what your distinction can decide. |
| Value/authority/responsibility | A club chooses a single convenient meeting time by majority vote. Everyone had a vote, but members with a fixed caring duty cannot attend. Apply the project's central distinction under these stipulated facts, separating procedure and consequence. | An emergency team temporarily acts without a vote to avoid a stipulated imminent injury; it later gives reasons and permits review. Compare the authority or responsibility issue with the club case; identify the changed urgency and reversibility conditions. | A research collective offers every member a veto, but junior members reasonably expect retaliation for using it. No vote is taken. Apply the distinction with formal power present but its effective exercise constrained. |
| Interpretation/comparison/method | An invented dialogue depicts a speaker defending view P; its narrator later describes the defense as unsuccessful. Explain how your project's distinction affects attribution and evaluation. | An invented memoir attributes past belief P to its author but explicitly rejects P in its present conclusion. A scholar treats the memoir as evidence that the author currently endorses P. Apply the distinction with genre, temporal standpoint and speaker relation changed. | Three invented schools endorse one and the same rule. A justifies it by welfare and permits public revision. B justifies it by welfare and forbids revision. C justifies it by inherited authority and forbids revision. A scholar argues: “A agrees with B; B agrees with C; therefore A and C hold the same position.” Use your project's distinction to identify what is compared at each step, evaluate the conclusion and construct one defensible comparison across all three. State what additional evidence a broader equivalence claim would require. |

No verdict about actual institutions, science or traditions follows from these invented cases. A topic-specific transfer must preserve the central philosophical distinction while changing a substantive condition, not merely substitute names.

## S8 and completion

S8 audits every actual final quotation and significant attribution independently against the edition used; [M8](modules/08.md) specifies the record. Completion requires evidence for every Q/S/A/R/U/T row. A competent final paper can conclude that the initial thesis was too strong. Missing source support, a pivotal invalid inference, unresponsive defense or incomplete delayed task remains an explicit repair item. Use no aggregate numerical certification threshold. Human review and actual learner outcomes remain separately labelled.

# Constructed research work: examples and annotations

All passages and situations below are original teaching constructions authored for this packet. They are not learner submissions, real expert reviews or completed scholarship. “Developing,” “competent” and “strong” describe the exhibited moves under the shared rubric, not an overall grade. The invented reports are the entire evidence for their stipulated facts; no historical philosopher is assigned these positions. The method follows the local requirements in [reference](../../current/reference.md) and [assessment](../../current/assessment.md). A real paper would still require the topic-dependent search and exact primary/secondary sources.

## A question becoming researchable

Topic: agreement among witnesses. Initial question: “Should we trust testimony?” This includes too many kinds of testimony, standards of trust and practical stakes. First narrowing: “Does agreement between two reports justify more confidence?” Still unclear: confidence in what, compared with which baseline, and with what knowledge of the reports' origin?

Researchable trial question: “Does learning that two reports agree, without information about their evidential dependence, establish that their agreement supplies independent corroboration?” Provisional answer: no. Distinct reports can derive from a single observation. Contribution: distinguish multiplicity of reports from independence of their evidential origins. Method: conceptual counterexample against a sufficiency claim; not an empirical survey of actual media reliability. Exclusions: how much dependent reports increase probability, whether reporters are morally responsible, and how institutions should regulate reporting. These exclusions are defensible because the proposed conclusion concerns only what agreement alone establishes.

Constructed case: Iona observes a bridge marker showing twelve units. Sam copies Iona's report without seeing the marker. Two reports now agree. All stipulated information is accurate; no deception or measurement error is needed. The crucial point is their common evidential origin. A real source dossier would look for the primary argument about corroboration being challenged, a serious account of dependent testimony, and a rival interpretation of evidential independence. It would assign actual ranges after discovery rather than list invented articles.

Trial map:

1. If agreement alone established independent corroboration, any case with agreeing reports and no further information would establish it.
2. Iona's and Sam's reports agree.
3. Sam's report is copied from Iona's observation; the two reports do not provide two independently originating observations.
4. Therefore agreement alone does not establish that there are two independently originating observations.
5. To conclude that there is no *evidential value of any kind* would require an additional premise. This trial does not defend that premise.

Step 4 is deliberately narrower than a universal theory of corroboration. A critic might say independent corroboration need not consist of independent observations: copying can check transmission or accuracy of transcription. The reply must therefore define the target sense and acknowledge other evidential jobs. That criticism improves the claim by preventing an equivocation.

## Developing, competent and strong research paragraphs

**Developing.** “Two reports always give stronger evidence than one, because two people agree. If they copied, we should not trust either. Philosophers generally support independent thought, so dependence makes testimony worthless.”

Annotation: the paragraph shifts from number of speakers to evidential independence, then from dependence to worthlessness. The last attribution is untraceable. Its conclusion exceeds the constructed facts. Repair: identify the target claim, define independence and remove the unsupported consensus claim before improving style.

**Competent.** “In the stipulated bridge case, two agreeing reports do not establish two independently originating observations. Sam's information is copied from Iona, so the number of reports exceeds the number of originating observations. This defeats the claim that agreement alone establishes independence in that sense. It does not show that Iona's observation is unreliable or that Sam's report adds no information of any kind.”

Annotation: the inference is supported by the case and its conclusion is appropriately restricted. It distinguishes a sufficient-condition counterexample from a global dismissal. As research writing, it still needs an actual target argument and bibliographic context before submission; this paragraph earns no imaginary source credit.

**Strong.** “The bridge case defeats the sufficiency claim only if ‘independent corroboration’ means support from separately originating observations. A critic may instead use the phrase for a second report that helps check transmission. That interpretation leaves room for Sam's report to add information, for example about whether Iona's wording was preserved. My objection therefore concerns the move from agreement to independent observational origins, not every evidential benefit of a second report. If the target author uses the broader sense, I must either provide textual grounds for the narrower reading or redirect the criticism.”

Annotation: the paragraph identifies a plausible rival interpretation and states what would change the objection. It avoids protecting the thesis by silently redefining the target. Strong here means a better controlled inference and interpretive burden; a real target author's wording must still be inspected.

Calibration task: before reading the annotations again, underline the conclusion and one limiting clause in each paragraph. Name one revision that would improve the developing paragraph. A competent answer should locate the equivocation and overreach; it need not endorse the final thesis.

## Draft-to-revision progression

**Trial draft, version 1.** “Since Sam copied Iona, his testimony is dependent. Dependent testimony has no evidential value. Therefore a rational reader should disregard Sam entirely. The case shows that consensus is a poor guide to truth.”

**Constructed review.** “Your case establishes a common origin, but your second sentence adds an undefended universal premise. Even a copied report can provide information about transmission, and a rational reader might use it to locate the original. Your final sentence also generalizes from one dependent pair to consensus generally. Decide whether to defend a wider theory of evidential value or restrict your conclusion.”

**Revision plan.** Accept the inferential criticism. Delete the universal premise and replace the project question with the narrower sufficiency question. Move the distinction between observational origin and transmission before the case. Add a reply acknowledging the possible value of dependent reports. Cut the general verdict on consensus. Retain the common-origin counterexample because it still defeats the narrower inference.

**Revised section, version 2.** “The common origin of Iona's and Sam's reports prevents their agreement from establishing two independently originating observations. This result does not require treating Sam as dishonest or Iona as mistaken. It follows from the relation between their information. A reader who knows only that the reports agree lacks the fact needed to distinguish this case from two separate observations. Agreement alone therefore leaves independence in the specified sense unsettled. Sam's report might still be useful for checking transmission or finding Iona's account; that usefulness does not supply an additional originating observation. A broader claim that dependent testimony contributes nothing would need a different argument.”

**Revision memo.** First, the question now concerns sufficiency for observational independence, replacing a global verdict about testimony. Second, the inference now uses the source relation stipulated in the case instead of an undefended universal premise. Third, the reply acknowledges transmission value and states its relation to the narrower conclusion. These changes alter scope, warrant and objection handling. They are substantive even though the revised conclusion is less ambitious.

**Remaining vulnerability.** An actual author may already distinguish these evidential jobs. The case would then clarify a temptation rather than refute that author. The dossier must check this before the project claims to correct the literature. It cannot claim an original scholarly contribution merely from this exercise.

## A bounded method comparison

Constructed question: “Can a belief's origin settle whether it is justified?” An interpretive project first asks what a particular author means by justification; a genealogical project might explain why a community adopted the belief; an argumentative project asks which origin facts bear on justification and why. These can inform each other without being interchangeable. If a community adopted a true claim through prejudice, the causal explanation alone does not tell us whether its later evidence justifies belief. An extra premise linking the origin to the relevant evidence is needed. Conversely, current justification does not erase the historical question. A useful comparative paragraph fixes one question, identifies each method's target and refuses an unsupported inference across them.

Practice: in 250 words, state how the report example would change if your aim were interpretation of a source rather than a conceptual sufficiency test. Specify one required textual observation, one rival reading and one exclusion. No fictional quotation or predetermined author list is permitted.

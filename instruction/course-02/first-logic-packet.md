# C02-M01-P01 — Arguments and validity in forall x

**Status:** representative practice packet specified and source-checked; not piloted. It supports the early Course 2 route, not a claim that Course 2 is complete.

**Question:** How can a valid inference have a false premise, and how do we show an inference invalid?

**Source and version:** [forall x Calgary, Fall 2025 textbook](../../collection/philosophy-study-collection/texts/introduction_logic/required/forallx_calgary_textbook.pdf), revision `c5cb415`, chapters 1 and 2. Use the [Fall 2025 selected solutions booklet](../../collection/philosophy-study-collection/texts/introduction_logic/solutions/forallx_calgary_solutions.pdf), revision `80a85da`, **after** an attempt. Both PDFs were inspected locally on 2026-10-13. The textbook and booklet credit the contributors and CC BY 4.0; preserve those notices when copying. The source course also offers a paper path, so no proof checker is required here.

**Task, approximately 90 minutes within Course 2 Module 1:** Read chapter 1 through its four numbered practice items and chapter 2 §2.1–2.2. In chapter 1, identify the conclusion in exercises 1–4; do not treat the conclusion-indicator word as the entire conclusion. In chapter 2 exercise A, classify items 1–3 as valid/invalid and explain *why*, using a countercase for an invalid form. In chapter 2 exercise B, answer items 1 and 5 with reasons. Write a short paragraph distinguishing validity and soundness in item A1.

**Output:** A numbered attempt with textbook chapter/item labels, one countercase, one validity-versus-soundness paragraph, focused time and assistance note. Only then consult the matching official selected-solution pages. Record discrepancies and what changed in your reasoning.

**Feedback route:** The official booklet covers these selected items. A1 is valid despite a false premise; A3 denies the antecedent and is invalid. B5 asks whether adding a premise can make a valid argument invalid; explain the counterexample definition rather than memorizing “no.” A checker accepting a proof would not itself validate the English interpretation. If your result differs, revisit chapter 2's definition and construct a case. Do not accept a tutor's unexplained verdict over a counterexample.

**Independent changed form:** Without the solutions or tutor, analyze: “If the gallery is open, its front light is on. The gallery's front light is on. Therefore the gallery is open.” Give a countercase if possible. Record exposure and assistance. If it was discussed in Course 1, replace it with a different form; the assessment must be fresh to this learner.

# C02-M07 — When is a chosen name really arbitrary?

**Prerequisites:** M4 natural deduction, M5 scope and M6 interpretations. **Outcomes:** prove quantified/identity arguments; apply eigenname restrictions; diagnose invalid derivations with countermodels; distinguish ⊢ from ⊨ (O3–O5). **Reading:** Part VII chapters 36–41, pp. 297–335, all chapters. Pinned sources in [README](../README.md).

**24 hours:** reading/rule examples 5; guided/faded proofs 5; independent bank/check 6; feedback 3; revision 4; retrieval including M6-D 1.

## Four quantifier rules, two identity rules

**∀E:** From `∀x F(x)` infer `F(a)`. The universal covers the referent of a. For a compound matrix, substitute a for every free x: `∀x R(x,x)` gives `R(a,a)`, not `R(a,b)`. The eliminated quantifier must be the main operator; one cannot simply strip ∀ from inside a conditional.

**∃I:** From `R(a,a)` infer `∃x R(x,a)` or `∃x R(x,x)`. The book permits replacing one or more occurrences of the name, provided the result really has the original as a substitution instance and no variable is captured. From `F(a)` conclude `∃x F(x)`; no inference about all objects is licensed.

**∀I:** To prove `∀x F(x)`, derive `F(c)` for an arbitrary name c. The name must occur in no premise or undischarged assumption. Replace every occurrence of c in that formula, avoiding capture. From `F(a)` as a premise you cannot infer `∀x F(x)`. An object-specific assumption may be used if discharged before generalization: from `AS F(c); F(c) by R`, derive `F(c)→F(c)` by →I, then `∀x(F(x)→F(x))` by ∀I. Its universality comes from the reasoning depending on no special property of c.

**∃E:** An existential premise guarantees a witness, without identifying it. Open a subproof with a fresh name c, assuming its matrix. Derive a conclusion not containing c, then discharge the subproof. In the book, c must not occur in the existential formula, in the conclusion, or in any assumption undischarged before the subproof begins (including premises). It may occur in its own witness assumption. Never export the bare claim `F(c)` as though c were a known named person.

Invented worked proof:

```text
1   ∃x F(x)               PR
2   ∀x(F(x) → G(x))       PR
3 | F(c)                  AS (fresh c)
4 | F(c) → G(c)           ∀E 2
5 | G(c)                  →E 4,3
6 | ∃x G(x)               ∃I 5
7   ∃x G(x)               ∃E 1,3–6
```

The final existential no longer names c, so the conclusion does not depend on which witness satisfied the first premise. Reusing a in the subproof when `¬F(a)` is already a premise could falsely manufacture a contradiction. The countermodel has a non-F object a and a different F object.

**=I:** `a=a` can be introduced without premises. **=E:** from `a=b` and `R(a,a)` infer `R(a,b)`, `R(b,a)` or `R(b,b)` by replacing selected occurrences; the book permits either direction. Sharing F does not imply identity. To derive symmetry from `a=b`, first write `a=a` by =I, then replace the first a using =E to obtain `b=a`.

**CQ:** The four conversion rules exchange `∀x¬F(x)` with `¬∃x F(x)`, and `∃x¬F(x)` with `¬∀x F(x)`, in either direction. They apply to the whole formula. Their validity does not turn “not all” into “none.” Chapter 40 derives them from basic rules; Q09 below supplies one such derivation. Chapter 41 relates the proof and semantic notions; reading that result is not proving completeness or deciding every FOL problem algorithmically.

**Faded practice:** To derive `∀x G(x)` from `∀x F(x), ∀x(F(x)→G(x))`, pick a name absent from both premises; instantiate both universals; apply ___; then apply ___ after checking the name restriction. Next do Q07 without hints.

Audit two deliberately bad proofs: (i) `F(a) PR; ∀x F(x) ∀I 1`; (ii) `∃x F(x) PR; F(a) ∃E 1`. Identify the exact violated restriction, then give a countermodel to each argument. Audit `(iii) ∀x R(x,x) PR; R(c,c) ∀E 1; ∀x R(x,c) ∀I 2`: why is partial replacement forbidden here though it was allowed in ∃I? Make an error card for every recurring name, free-variable, capture or scope error.

Close answers and live help; a list of rule names is permitted. Criteria: witness independence, correctly discharged assumptions, accurate identity substitution and explanation.

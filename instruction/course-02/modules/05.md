# C02-M05 — Who depends on whom in a quantified claim?

**Prerequisite:** M2 syntax and M4 scoped reasoning; complete M4-D in retrieval time. **Outcomes:** specify domains/keys; distinguish names, variables and predicates; translate quantifier order, identity and descriptions (O2, O5). **Reading:** Part V chapters 23–29, pp. 190–256, entire chapters of the pinned textbook; see [README](../README.md).

**18 hours:** reading 4; guided practice 4; independent bank/check 4; feedback 2; revision 3; retrieval 1.

## Decomposing sentences

TFL gives no internal representation of “Ada is a student.” FOL writes `S(a)`: a name picks an object; the predicate expresses a property. A relation `A(x,y)` has ordered places: if it means x admires y, reversing the places changes who admires whom. The domain must include every object quantified over and every referent of a name. Domains are nonempty in this course; predicates may have empty extensions. Different names need not refer to different objects.

With a domain of all people, “every student is a musician” becomes `∀x(S(x) → M(x))`. The conditional restricts the claim to students without requiring every person to be a student. “Some student is a musician” becomes `∃x(S(x) ∧ M(x))`; the witness must have both properties. `∃x(S(x) → M(x))` would be satisfied by a nonstudent, so it is generally the wrong translation. A universal restricted claim need not assert that students exist.

Invented example: `∀x∃y A(x,y)` says each person admires someone; the y-witness may depend on x. `∃y∀x A(x,y)` requires one person admired by everyone. Take a two-person diagram with Ada admiring only Ada and Bo admiring only Bo. Text equivalent: domain `{Ada,Bo}`, arrows `{(Ada,Ada),(Bo,Bo)}`. The first formula is true; the second false. Thus exchanging the quantifiers is substantive. By contrast consecutive universals can exchange order when their scopes are kept intact.

A variable is bound by its governing quantifier inside that quantifier's scope. In `∀x A(x,y)`, y is free, so it is a formula but not a sentence. `∀x A(x,x)` binds both occurrences of x. Renaming a bound variable must preserve scope and avoid capture: `∀x A(x,y)` may become `∀z A(z,y)` but not `∀y A(y,y)`. Back-translation and marking free occurrences help catch these errors.

Identity `a=b` says two names pick the very same object, not merely that the objects share one property. “Everyone other than Ada” needs `¬x=a`. Existence and uniqueness are separate: “exactly one leader” says someone is a leader and every leader is that person. A Russell-style description “the leader is a musician” asserts existence, uniqueness and musicianship: `∃x((L(x) ∧ ∀y(L(y) → y=x)) ∧ M(x))`. If there is no leader, that sentence is false under this analysis. Negating the whole description differs from saying there is exactly one leader who is not a musician. These are explicit interpretive choices, not claims that all ordinary descriptions function identically.

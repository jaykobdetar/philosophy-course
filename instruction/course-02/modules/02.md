# C02-M02 — How much English does a formula preserve?

**Prerequisite:** M1 countercase competence. **Outcomes:** identify main connective, bracket scope, symbolize/back-translate, distinguish plausible ambiguous readings (O2, O5). **Reading:** pinned textbook Part II chapters 4–8, pp. 27–67, entire chapters; local access and edition in [README](../README.md).

**18 hours:** reading 4; guided symbolization 4; independent practice 4; feedback 2; revision 3; retrieval (including M1-D) 1.

## Explanation and example

A sentence letter stands for a complete declarative sentence. A key such as `L: the lamp is lit` must keep that meaning throughout an argument. Negation reverses a truth value. Conjunction requires both conjuncts. Disjunction is inclusive: at least one, possibly both. “A but B” has the truth conditions of `A ∧ B`, though the contrast is lost. Exclusive “either but not both” requires `(A ∨ B) ∧ ¬(A ∧ B)`.

`A → B` rules out A being true while B is false. It does not state causation, temporal order or relevance. “A only if B” makes B necessary for A, hence `A → B`; “A if B” makes B sufficient for A, hence `B → A`. An antecedent being false makes a material conditional true. It does not show that an ordinary-language promise or causal claim has been properly modeled. “A unless B” is taken here in its inclusive truth-functional sense as `¬B → A`, equivalent to `A ∨ B`; an exclusive interpretation needs an additional constraint.

Invented example: “If the lamp is lit, then the door is locked and the alarm is armed.” With L, D, A as keyed, write `L → (D ∧ A)`. The main connective is the conditional; its consequent is an entire conjunction. `(L → D) ∧ A` has conjunction as its main connective and asserts that the alarm is armed regardless of whether the lamp is lit. With L false and A false, the first formula is true and the second false, whatever D’s value. Back-translation and a distinguishing valuation check whether altered brackets change truth conditions; different parsing alone does not establish semantic difference.

Strict formation rules are recursive: atoms are formulas; negating a formula makes a formula; putting a binary connective between two bracketed formulas makes a formula. Repeatedly remove the main connective to check formation. Outermost brackets may be omitted under the book's convention; internal scope must remain unambiguous. `¬(L ∧ D)` denies the conjunction; `¬L ∧ D` asserts D as well as denying L. The sentence `L` is used to assert that the lamp is lit; in “`L` contains one letter” it is mentioned as an expression. `⊨` and `⊢` belong to the language about formulas, not inside ordinary TFL sentences.

**Independent M2-I (25 minutes):** New key P: a permit is issued; R: a review is completed; S: the site opens. Formalize (i) the site opens only if a permit is issued; (ii) a permit is issued unless a review is completed, inclusively; (iii) it is not the case that both a permit is issued and the site opens; (iv) “A permit is issued or a review is completed and the site opens,” under two readings. Back-translate `(P ∧ R) → S`. Closed help/answers; use the alternate in examiner guidance only if these prompts were previously exposed, with an assessor supplying the prompt without its answer. Criteria: explicit key, scope, conditional direction and faithful paraphrase.

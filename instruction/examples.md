# Constructed examples for calibration

These are **constructed teaching examples**, not real student submissions. They are on different cases from the opening independent checks. Source claims are limited to [OpenStax 5.3](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-philosophy/pages/5-3-arguments) for argument form and [the marked *Euthyphro*](https://persweb.wabash.edu/facstaff/helmang/phi110-1516F/phi110-handouts/Euthyphro.pdf) for the example/definition distinction. Both were checked on 2026-10-13. The examples were reviewed against those sources, not against a learner pilot.

## Developing reconstruction

“The timetable is bad because I dislike it.” This gives a conclusion and feeling but no reason about the timetable's effects or principles. Feedback: ask what feature matters and to whom, then test that claim. Disliking a timetable is genuine evidence about one's reaction, but it does not by itself establish the general verdict.

## Competent reconstruction

“The timetable should be revised because its final bus leaves before the evening class ends, and no alternate bus serves that stop. If the program promises evening access, this schedule blocks one group.” The reason, claim and conditional bridge are explicit. A reviewer should ask whether the factual premises are checked and whether the promise applies; disagreement about transport priorities is not an automatic failure.

## Stronger revision, with memo

“For students who rely on this stop, the final departure before the class ends makes evening attendance impractical unless another route is available. If the college has committed to accessible evening attendance, it should add a later trip or document an equivalent route. The conclusion is limited to that group until ridership and other routes are checked.” **Revision memo:** The claim is narrower, its factual condition is visible, and a possible countercase is named. It is stronger because its inference can be tested, not because its policy conclusion is mandatory.

## Source-sensitive interpretation

“Euthyphro's prosecution is one proposed pious act; Socrates asks for a general account, so citing the prosecution alone may not answer his question.” This is supported by the marked dialogue around 4b–6e. A stronger paragraph would identify the exact exchange and consider whether Euthyphro also offers a principle. It should not pretend a modern ethics theory is settled by the opening example.

**Calibration:** Compare one developing and one stronger example by naming the exact criterion, one textual or inferential support, and one remaining limitation. A tutor and learner may disagree about the policy or interpretation while agreeing on the evidence standard. Use the [feedback-challenge procedure](tutor.md#feedback-and-challenge) if a criterion judgment lacks support.

# Course 3 — Ancient philosophy: teaching sequence

**Authored 20 October 2026. Baseline: 120 hours. Prerequisite: Course 1.** This sequence implements [the accepted curriculum](../../current/courses/03.md). Its subject is inquiry, justice, explanation and the good life in selected Greek traditions; it is not a survey of the whole ancient world. The Chinese option, the Buddhist encounter and Islamic/Jewish work retain their own places in the curriculum.

All eight modules, exercises and assessment forms are authored. **Authored is not the same as source-ready or completed by a learner.** The prescribed Barnes Aristotle and Annas/Barnes Sextus editions have not been inspected here. Aristotle's older local translations support conceptual practice but do not establish the revised Oxford wording or Bekker concordances. Sextus I.1–30 must be obtained in Annas/Barnes before its assigned reading and edition-specific assessment can be completed. See the [reading register](readings.md) and [source audit](source-audit.md). No learner pilot, external expert review, tutor calibration result, rights clearance or rendered accessibility test is claimed.

## How to use the packets

Read [the reading register](readings.md) before starting. Use the module order; write your attempt before consulting teaching models where a task asks for one. Each module separates coached practice from a changed-case independent check. The final [assessment](assessment.md) is separate from both. Keep the [examiner notes](examiner-notes.md) closed until submission; this is a procedural separation, not a claim of secure examination. If a prompt or its answer has been rehearsed, record exposure and use the alternate form.

Save artifacts with module/task IDs and this short header: date; actual edition; exact range; source availability; help received; previous exposure; time spent. Keep personal drafts and borrowed excerpts in the ignored learner workspace. These packets contain original explanations and invented practice cases, not replacement copies of copyrighted readings.

The [outcome map](outcomes.md) links every essential evidence group to practice and an independent task. The [tutor notes](tutor-notes.md) specify feedback and accessible alternatives. Dictation, a screen reader, or text-to-speech of an accessible lawful copy may replace silent reading or typing. Stop at the same textual boundaries; maintain written locator notes. An audio timestamp cannot substitute for a verified textual citation. Every spoken task has a written equivalent with the same reasoning criteria; delivery speed and accent are not assessed.

Start with Module 1's source-and-speaker record. Obtain the exact Aristotle and Sextus editions before the corresponding reading tasks; proceed with available modules while arranging access. Record unresolved source-dependent criteria rather than marking the course complete through a total score.

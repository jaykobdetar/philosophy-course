# C03-M07 — Essay and revision

**20 hours. Guiding question:** What can a carefully bounded historical argument establish? Prerequisites: Modules 1–6 artifacts, with source-dependent gaps recorded. Outcomes: choose a disputed inference, reconstruct the strongest relevant interpretation, evaluate a serious objection, revise substantively and disclose source/assistance limits.

## Exact task, readings and time

Write **1,500–1,800 words** on one accepted question:

1. “Does *Republic* IV answer Glaucon's challenge?” Use II 357a–367e and IV 427d–445e; IX 577c–580c may supply a clearly bounded supplementary argument.
2. “Does the affinity argument support immortality?” Use *Phaedo* 64a–69e and 72e–84b, focusing on 78b–84b. Do not silently use the final immortality argument outside the assignment.
3. “Does Aristotle's soul account avoid the problems of the model it opposes?” Use *De Anima* II.1–3, with *Physics* II.1–3 and *Categories* 1–5 where directly relevant. Identify the opposed model from the assigned text, especially the complaint about attaching a soul to an unspecified body; do not attribute every kind of dualism to a single opponent.

Use the [assigned editions/ranges](../readings.md). The Plato no-purchase routes are authorized. The Aristotle paper's exact edition and Bekker criteria remain pending until the Barnes text is checked. No additional secondary reading is required. Budget: question/evidence decision 2h; targeted rereading 3h; outline 2h; draft 5h; feedback 2h; revision 4h; citation/assistance audit 1h; independent defense and memo 1h. Total 20h.

## Teaching: a historical thesis is an accountable claim

A useful thesis identifies both an interpretation and its consequence. “Plato has a theory of justice” reports a topic. “The account answers the challenge only if psychic order excludes the profitable ordinary injustice the challenger describes” specifies a disputed dependency. It still needs evidence that this dependency is the right one and reasons for judging whether the text supplies it. A thesis may be affirmative, negative or qualified. Its merit is determined by the argument, not by agreement with the tutor.

Reconstruction and evaluation have different jobs. A charitable reconstruction gives the author a serious argument supported by the passage. It does not add every premise needed for validity and then silently attribute them. Mark a supplied premise as your proposal and explain why it fits. Evaluation can challenge a premise, a transition, the interpretation or the scope of the conclusion. An objection that merely asserts an incompatible opinion gives the reader no reason to decide.

For the first prompt, distinguish a definition of justice from proof of benefit and consider whether IV's ordinary-conduct examples support the bridge. For the second, distinguish likeness from possession of the same properties and survival from indestructibility; explain the force of the conclusion you defend. For the third, ask whether making soul the actuality of a suitable body explains their unity and capacities better than arbitrary placement. Acknowledge the excerpt's separability qualifications. None of these prompts requires a definitive solution to the entire ancient debate.

## Bounded procedure and templates

First make a source feasibility card: question; actual edition; two core passages; available locators; missing checks. Choose a question for which you can inspect the central evidence. Then make an evidence table with rows **textual claim / exact passage / my paraphrase / inferential role / possible rival reading**. Include at least four rows. Reserve two other assigned passages for fresh assessment; tell the examiner exactly which passages your essay discusses.

Use this outline template: question and provisional answer; fair reconstruction; decisive disputed step; strongest objection; reply or narrowed conclusion; remaining limit. Assign each paragraph a role and evidence before drafting. A workable word plan is 150 introduction, 450 reconstruction, 350 central analysis, 350 objection/reply and 250 conclusion/limits: 1,550 words. Adapt within 1,500–1,800; bibliography and a separate revision memo do not count toward the essay length.

**Original worked revision:** Draft claim: “A successful argument must have no assumptions, so the health analogy fails.” Diagnostic: every argument has starting points; the sentence provides no reason to reject the relevant one. Revised claim: “The health comparison supports benefit only if the proposed psychic ordering is constitutive of living well. The interpretation needs evidence for that premise rather than the bare presence of an analogy.” The revision identifies a testable burden without deciding the essay for the learner. Do not copy this as an assessed thesis.

Write the draft yourself. Supply a reviewer with the question, your thesis, actual passage/edition, draft, help declaration and two questions: “Have I reconstructed the disputed step fairly?” and “Does my reply answer this objection?” The reviewer may identify errors and model a different example; they may not rewrite your assessed paragraph. Use [tutor notes](../tutor-notes.md) for the annotated progression.

Rank feedback as factual/source correction, inferential repair or optional style suggestion. Address the first two before polishing. Save the original draft, revised essay and **200–300-word memo** explaining one accepted criticism, one accepted or rejected interpretation with reasons, and one unresolved limitation. This is evidence of revision, not proof of independent transfer.

## Independent check C03-M07-I (within the final hour)

Without the draft or notes, take 20 minutes to write 200 words responding to the objection for your selected prompt: **justice:** “You have only shown that the worst injustice is bad; why does that vindicate justice generally?” **affinity:** “Something could resemble the indestructible yet last only a long time; what supports your stronger conclusion?” **soul:** “An account of the organized living whole labels unity but does not explain why these capacities belong together; what explanatory work has it done?” Primary text permitted; no content help or model answers. Use the remaining 40 minutes for your memo and source/exposure record. Criteria: direct answer, explicit target premise, appropriate concession and accurate evidence. These are Module 7 checks; Module 8 uses a different objection.

If the paper is unfocused, remove all claims unnecessary to its single disputed step and rebuild the outline. If a citation is unavailable, mark it pending rather than inventing it. Next: close examiner notes, submit the passage/exposure list and begin Module 8's separate fresh tasks.

# C08 independent assessment forms — learner copy

Keep this file closed during coached practice. An assessor selects an unexposed form and provides only its prompts. If self-studying, record prior exposure honestly; these files are separated guidance, not secure examinations. All cases are invented. The final fresh written session is **90 minutes**, **30 per row**, within Module 8, followed by **20 minutes** of dialogue or a typed/asynchronous equivalent. Planning times may be adjusted for access needs; record actual time.

Permitted: assigned primary texts in the specified editions, a citation-only sheet, ordinary text accessibility tools, dictation and breaks. Prohibited during independent work: AI content help, tutor hints, answer keys, prewritten arguments and feedback before submission. Cite locators; identify an empirical premise requiring evidence in **every** response. A fresh passage means a new interpretive task on an assigned text, not necessarily a text never read. If an exact passage was central to the paper or rehearsed, use the alternate. Neutral criteria: faithful interpretation, explicit theory-to-verdict inference, relevant factual distinctions, and a qualified response to the changed facts.

## Form A — initial fresh evidence

**A1 Authority/liberty, 30 min, 300–400 words.** A coastal authority was elected to maintain flood barriers. It now proposes a ban on public criticism of barrier design, claiming criticism reduces voluntary cooperation. No injury or refusal to cooperate has been documented. Analyze the best authority argument using Hobbes 17 or Locke §§95–99 and the proposed restriction using Mill I–II. Mark one normative and two empirical premises. The changed circumstance is a credible threat to sabotage a barrier during a storm: explain what this changes and what it leaves to be justified.

**A2 Distribution, 30 min, 300–400 words.** Twenty adults hold resources whose acquisition and previous transfers are stipulated just. Each freely deposits an agreed contribution into an assurance fund: the money is transferred to members with the lowest resources only if all twenty deposits arrive; otherwise every deposit is returned. All twenty contribute. A critic says that, because the intended outcome is redistributive and patterned, Nozick must condemn the fund. An organizer says the participants’ actual informed agreement proves that Rawls’s principles have been satisfied. Evaluate both inferences using the assigned texts, distinguishing the fund’s aim, its transfer procedure and the scope of each theory’s justification. Change: the fund expires annually, and several contributors announce they will not renew next year; the agreement gives them that choice. Explain what this changes about future institutional security without assuming the current transfers were unjust. Name an empirical question relevant to assessing the fund as a continuing institution.

**A3 Gender/race/idealization, 30 min, 300–400 words.** Interpret Wollstonecraft chapter II’s four prose paragraphs from “Let us examine this question” through the paragraph ending “an immortal soul” (the sequence reporting Rousseau’s requirements, challenging different standards of virtue and describing conscious virtue). Distinguish Rousseau’s attributed view from Wollstonecraft’s reply. In an invented professional academy, women and men receive identical training and demonstrate equally well-supported judgments. Assessors praise a man’s reasoned disagreement as integrity but mark an equivalent disagreement by a woman as a failure of obedience. Apply the passage to the standard by which successful reasoning is appraised; do not assume a deficit in training or ability. Explain which assumption in a model of equally recognized deliberators needs scrutiny. Change: women receive equally high overall scores, but only for agreeable delivery, while their reasons remain unexamined. Does numerical parity settle the issue your interpretation identifies? State a relevant empirical question and a limit of the analogy.

## Form B — alternate or row-specific fresh retest

**B1 Authority/liberty, 30 min, 300–400 words.** A town's newly formed association votes to compel nonmembers to finance a debate hall. The hall manager excludes a demonstrably false historical claim solely on the ground that false beliefs have no value. Use Locke §§95–99 to separate the majority question from outsiders' consent; use Mill II to reconstruct a defense of hearing a false opinion. Change: a meeting has one speaking slot and two applicants. Explain what a fair access rule would need to justify without assuming every applicant can speak indefinitely.

**B2 Distribution, 30 min, 300–400 words.** Scheme T produces (15,40,90); U produces (17,30,60). T grants the first group fewer political liberties, while U preserves equal liberties; all other opportunity conditions are stipulated equal. A musician fairly acquires a studio under U, and listeners make voluntary payments. Officials propose resetting everyone's holdings each night to preserve U's original pattern. Explain why neither a higher total nor nightly equality settles the case. Compare Rawls's institutional priorities with Nozick's challenge to enforcing a pattern. Change: listeners' payments were compulsory charges disguised as gifts. Identify the affected premise and an empirical way to distinguish the two histories.

**B3 Gender/race/idealization, 30 min, 300–400 words.** Read Du Bois chapter I’s paragraph beginning “After the Egyptian and Indian” and ending “torn asunder,” which contains both double-consciousness and two-ness, together with the next paragraph beginning “The history of the American Negro” and ending “in his face.” Interpret the relation between public perception and self-relation, preserving the narrative voice. A fictional citizenship test rewards applicants for describing their own community solely through the officials' degrading stereotypes. Explain what a model of equally recognized citizens would conceal; distinguish textual interpretation from a causal claim about the test. Change: officials remove the degrading questions but retain interviews scored by the old criteria. State what needs investigation before claiming the mechanism is gone.

## Explanation — 20 minutes, counted once

The assessor chooses the response with the least secure inference and asks: (1) “Which sentence is a claim about the author, and which is your own inference?” (2) “If your stated empirical premise were false, exactly how would your conclusion change?” (3) “What is the strongest rival explanation or principle here?” Use up to 5 minutes to review the response, 10 to answer and 5 to clarify. The typed route uses the same questions and reasoning demands; asynchronous elapsed time is not thinking time. No new doctrine is introduced. Do not use dialogue to coach a missing row into a pass; independent retest is available.

## Final delayed occasion — one row, 30 minutes

Administer after roughly **4–6 weeks or two later modules**, chosen and recorded in advance. Select the least secure row; use D unless exposed, then E. Write 250–350 words, sources open with the same restrictions. This is charged once to C08-M08, distinct from earlier module retrieval. Each prompt asks for a new mechanism or inferential issue.

**D1:** A former emergency coordinator claims that residents' gratitude for rescue gives her authority to control the local newspaper after the emergency ends. No one agreed to that power. Reconstruct and test the gratitude-to-authority inference with an assigned contract text and Mill. Identify a fact that would change the practical problem without automatically settling authority.

**D2:** A housing committee proposes a perfectly impartial lottery that would reassign residents’ justly held homes without their agreement. Every resident has the same chance of receiving each home. The committee claims that equal chances satisfy Nozick’s entitlement requirements and reproduce Rawls’s veil of ignorance. Assess both claims: distinguish a random assignment of outcomes from selecting principles under restricted information, and explain what authority over existing holdings the lottery would need. Change: the lottery is instead confined to vacant homes freely donated for that purpose, and recipients freely accept its announced terms. Explain which entitlement problem changes and why procedural impartiality alone still cannot establish the justice of the wider basic structure. Identify a factual premise needing investigation in an actual housing scheme.

**D3:** An invented school's identical entrance exam assumes access to equipment formerly reserved for one group. Interpret Du Bois chapter I's concluding sequence joining work, culture and liberty, and explain how it could illuminate the limits of formal access without claiming Du Bois discussed entrance exams. Specify an empirical premise and a limitation of your analogy.

**E1 alternate delayed:** Residents freely delegate nighttime noise decisions to a rotating council. It bans a silent newspaper because it questions the council's competence. Explain why agreed jurisdiction and a new restriction need separate justification. Change: the publisher organizes noisy midnight distribution. Assess that conduct without treating it as proof that criticism may be prohibited.

**E2 alternate delayed:** A cooperative proposes V=(12,12,12) or W=(14,20,25), with equal liberties and fair opportunity. Its members prefer V for solidarity. Explain what the stipulated numbers support under the difference principle and what further institutional claim would justify choosing V. Add the entitlement question if W's extra resources came from a coerced transfer. Do not assert that preferences alone determine either theory.

**E3 alternate delayed:** A city calls an exclusion “natural aptitude” even though it has trained only the admitted group for the relevant work. Interpret Wollstonecraft chapter II's opening three prose paragraphs and compare the causal structure with the city. Change: equal training is introduced but an inherited licensing rule still blocks entry. Distinguish the educational and institutional mechanisms and state what evidence would test each.

A developing delayed response requires targeted repair and another genuinely fresh case; log additional time if the reserved budget is exhausted. Final completion awaits satisfactory evidence. If all provided forms have been rehearsed, their use remains practice and a new independently prepared equivalent is needed.

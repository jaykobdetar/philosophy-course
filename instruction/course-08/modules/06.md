## Explanation

The social-contract device can ask what would justify institutions, but it can also be repurposed to diagnose what institutions have actually organized. Mills's challenge connects racial domination with political membership, economic advantage and ways of representing the social world. A critical contract need not mean that every participant signed an identifiable document. It may model patterned agreements and practices through which some count as full persons and others are subordinated. Distinguish that explanatory use from an argument that the arrangements are morally legitimate.

Use three claim types. A descriptive claim says how a system operates or developed. A normative claim says what standing or treatment is justified. An epistemic claim concerns what people are positioned to see, ignore or count as knowledge. These claims can support one another but are not interchangeable. Evidence of a history of exclusion does not by itself logically refute every abstract equality principle. It may show that an idealization hides the mechanisms a theory must explain, or that a theory's application presupposes a false starting point. Conversely, stating a universal principle does not establish that institutions have applied it universally.

“Revises,” “rejects” and “repurposes” are hypotheses to test, not answer labels to memorize. Specify the object: rejecting a legitimating history can coexist with retaining a contract vocabulary for critical explanation. Ask what the framework makes visible and what it might still omit. A philosopher's critical diagnosis needs textual reconstruction and historical evidence; do not manufacture examples of actual institutions to strengthen an essay. Keep the historical examples in the assigned text distinct from the invented cases below.

Structural analysis asks how ordinary rules can reproduce a status hierarchy even when some present officials have fair intentions. It does not entail that agency, intention or individual resistance never matters. A plausible rival account might explain an observed disparity through another mechanism; test which evidence discriminates between them. One thinker cannot represent the full Africana tradition. ## Worked practice: invented island registry

An island's old registry excluded certain families from land titles. Its present council treats registered title as the sole measure of political membership and announces that all registered owners now vote equally. Equal treatment inside the admitted class can coexist with exclusion produced by the rule defining the class. A structural diagnosis identifies both the inherited register and its continuing use. A proposed remedy must state a principle and investigate present effects; a historical wrong alone does not specify a complete remedy.

## Explanation

A criticism of unequal education can be an argument about how observed capacities arise. If people are trained to seek approval rather than exercise judgment, their resulting dependency cannot straightforwardly prove that dependency is natural or deserved. Wollstonecraft's chapter examines the prevailing attribution of a distinct sexual character and its relation to education, reason and virtue. Reconstruct which premise she opposes, which standard of human excellence she employs, and what education would need to change. Distinguish a challenge to the explanation of a difference from the stronger claim that every difference is caused solely by education.

Du Bois's opening moves through a personal encounter, a divided standpoint and the political history of striving. Double consciousness describes a socially mediated relation to oneself, not simply possession of two preferences or a psychiatric diagnosis. The account connects being viewed through a hostile public standard with the difficulty of realizing agency and recognition. Do not erase the narrative voice to extract a bare syllogism: explain how the narrated encounter makes a social relation intelligible, and which historical generalization would require further evidence.

In comparison, avoid claiming that women and Black Americans undergo interchangeable experiences. Shared analytical questions can expose different mechanisms: training, citizenship, public perception, economic dependence. A standpoint can reveal a neglected question without making every belief held from it infallible. Neither this pairing nor Haslanger provides a complete global feminist history. When extending an argument to another setting, ask whose account is missing, how local institutions differ, and what evidence could challenge your analogy.

## Worked practice: invented council apprenticeship

A council lets Group A practice public reasoning and trains Group B solely to flatter visitors. It later cites B's lack of debate experience as a reason to exclude B from decisions. The circle is institutional: denied opportunities produce an observed deficit used to justify further denial. This is a reconstruction of an invented mechanism, not evidence about any actual population. A remedy needs more than a proclamation of equal respect: it must address training and meaningful participation, while leaving open which arrangement does so best.

## Independent F4

A university formally admits all applicants but evaluates every interview through an old model of “professional poise.” Graduates from excluded schools report having to anticipate both the committee's image of them and their own aims. Select Du Bois chapter I’s paragraph beginning “After the Egyptian and Indian” and ending “torn asunder,” which contains both double-consciousness and two-ness, together with the next paragraph beginning “The history of the American Negro” and ending “in his face.” In 350–450 words interpret the relation described and evaluate what it can and cannot illuminate about this invented case. State a fact needed before claiming the admissions system produces the reported experience. Source-open; no AI help, keys or prewritten responses.

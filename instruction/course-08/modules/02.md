# C08-M02 — Consent and property

**18 hours.** Question: what can consent authorize, and how can something held in common become mine? Prerequisite: P1's three-level distinction. Outcomes: map labor and appropriation with limits; identify parties to authorization; compare one precise Hobbes/Locke premise.

## Reading and time

Hobbes, *Leviathan*, Tuck, Cambridge 1996, **chapter 17**. Locke, *Second Treatise*, Peter Laslett edition, Cambridge 1988, **II §§4–15; V §§25–51; VIII §§95–99**. The [Gutenberg Locke companion](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/7370/7370-h/7370-h.htm) is not the Laslett edition. Section numbers provide correspondence, not proof of editorial identity. See source audit for limited inspected passages.

Reading 6h; explanation/map practice 3h; P2 comparison 3h; fresh F2 1h; feedback 1h; revision 3h; retrieval/citations 1h = 18h. Use 15 minutes of the last hour after 2–7 days.

## Explanation

Hobbes's chapter 17 adds representation: the many authorize one person or assembly to act as their common representative. The covenant described is among the future subjects; portraying it as an ordinary bargain between ruler and each subject changes the argument. Ask why unity of judgment is supposed to solve the insecurity identified earlier, and whether the proposed unity follows from the need for coordination.

Locke's natural freedom does not mean absence of moral constraints. The assigned chapter II makes natural law and mutual limits part of the prepolitical condition. In chapter V, the common availability of resources creates an appropriation problem: people must be able to use something without obtaining everyone's express permission. Labor supplies an alleged link between person and resource, but that link needs argument. Merely adding something one owns to something common does not obviously establish ownership of the whole. Track the restrictions concerning spoilage and what remains for others, and the later role of money in the assigned range. Do not replace the evolving argument with a slogan that effort justifies every holding.

The consent question is distinct. Sections 95–99 concern entry into a community and majority decision once a body politic exists. A majority's subsequent decision is not itself evidence that each outsider originally consented. The limited assigned range does not warrant detailed conclusions about tacit consent elsewhere. A useful comparison asks whether natural law already binds people before political authority, or how agreement produces a common decision, rather than treating both authors as synonyms for “contract.”

## Worked practice: invented orchard

Asha gathers five ripe apples from a common orchard; enough equally good apples remain and the five are eaten. Bram fences every tree after pruning one branch. Both labor, but the proposed scale of appropriation and effects on others differ. A fair reconstruction makes the labor-to-title inference explicit and checks the limiting conditions. This exercise does not prove either author's theory true; it exposes what facts an application needs.

**P2:** Draw a six-box property map (common resource; self/labor claim; appropriative act; resulting title; two limits; money complication). Then write 600 words comparing Hobbes 17 with Locke §§95–99 on a single premise about forming collective authority. Include one objection and two exact locators. Make a separate 150-word orchard application stating one missing fact. Criteria: no inference from labor alone to unlimited title; no conflation of founding consent with later voting; no textual claims outside the assigned scope without marking them as extensions.

## Independent F2

Forty households jointly operate an irrigation channel. A newcomer repairs its only gate and announces exclusive rights to all downstream water. The households then vote 30–10 to give a council permanent authority over every resident, including people outside the agreement. In 400–500 words, assess the repair-to-title and majority-to-authority inferences separately using Locke's assigned sections. Explain one contrast with Hobbes 17. Identify a factual condition that could change your answer. Sources/citation sheet only; no content assistance or keys.

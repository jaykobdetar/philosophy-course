# C08-M07 — Essay and revision

**28 hours.** Question: which bounded institutional principle can survive a serious objection? Prerequisites: P1–P6 and PC-32 packet, with access to the texts used. Outcomes: sustain a qualified argument; compare opponents fairly; audit citations; make and explain substantive revision. This is an authored writing workshop, not an instruction to ask a tutor to create a question.

## Sources and budget

Reread only the exact assigned ranges relevant to the chosen topic: Hobbes 13–15/17 and Locke II §§4–15, V §§25–51, VIII §§95–99; Mill I–II; Rawls §§3–4,11–13,24–26 and Nozick 150–164; or Mills 1–40, using the editions in the earlier packets. No new mandatory reading. Haslanger may inform a limitation, with its packet credited once. Missing core access blocks the affected interpretation, not the initial planning exercise.

Question/source selection 3h; focused rereading 4h; outline and P7a trial argument 3h; draft 7h; feedback 2h; substantive revision 5h; citation/assistance audit 1h; independent F7 objection 1h; revision memo 1h; retrieval/next-step record 1h = 28h.

## Decision procedure and taught method

Choose one question below. Name one institution or mechanism and one disputed inference. Write a provisional one-sentence answer with an explicit condition. Select two primary passages that could support it and one that threatens it. If you cannot locate the threatening passage or reconstruct the opponent's inference, narrow the question before drafting. Do not turn missing evidence into confident historical claims.

1. Can a majority's decision justify permanent authority over people who never entered its political agreement? Compare Locke §§95–99 and Hobbes 17.
2. Does Mill's argument for hearing false opinions support access to a scarce public microphone on equal terms? Use I–II and distinguish a content restriction from a fair access rule.
3. Does a voluntary transfer defeat a demand to fund fair opportunity when the initial distribution is stipulated just? Compare the assigned Rawls and Nozick ranges; state the institutional scope of the demand.
4. Does Mills's critical use of contract show that an ideal contract principle should be abandoned, or that its application needs a different account of membership and history? Use pp. 1–40 and one precisely identified earlier contract argument.

A paragraph should do one argumentative job: establish a premise, interpret evidence, draw an inference, state an objection, or revise the conclusion. “Author A says X; author B says Y” is preparation for comparison. The comparison itself explains why the disagreement matters to the selected question. An objection attacks a premise, inference, interpretation or scope. Replying may concede a limit. Defending a conditional conclusion is often stronger than saving an unrestricted one by ignoring the case that troubles it.

## Worked practice and output

Invented draft claim: “Everyone agreed to the club, so every future rule is legitimate.” The gap is between consent to a bounded association and authorization of unlimited future power. A revised claim might defend member decisions within published terms while requiring a separate argument for irreversible powers. The revision gives up scope to preserve a reasoned connection between consent and authority; it cannot claim that either Hobbes or Locke endorses the club analogy without textual argument.

**P7a:** Submit a 250-word trial paragraph plus a premise map. Use this template: question; thesis/limit; source claim and locator; interpretive reason; inference; strongest objection; provisional reply; empirical issue requiring evidence.

**P7:** Write **1,800–2,200 words** with a 150–200-word fair reconstruction of the strongest opponent within that total, at least four checked primary locators, and one acknowledged unresolved issue. Retain the draft. Request feedback on two specified inferential or interpretive questions, then submit the revision and a separate 300-word memo: criticism received; accepted/rejected and why; before/after thesis or premise; effect; remaining uncertainty. See [examples](../examples.md) for an original progression. Criteria: actual answer to a narrow question, charitable reconstruction, source integrity, an answered objection, substantive revision.

## Independent F7

After drafting, answer in 350–450 words: “Your principle appears to protect existing participants. The institution now admits new members who could not influence its founding terms. Identify the exact premise that needs reconsideration; give the strongest objection to keeping your principle, then defend or revise it.” Apply this to the particular essay's institution; do not reuse a prepared passage. Assigned texts/citation sheet only; no tutor, AI or keys. This probes transfer and does not replace the three Module 8 rows.

## Repair and next

If the essay is a survey, reverse-outline every paragraph and remove claims that do not answer the chosen question. If a source is unavailable, mark the dependent claim unresolved and obtain a lawful copy or restrict the project to available assigned sources without pretending to have read it. If revision is cosmetic, identify the strongest objection and rewrite the affected premise/conclusion first. Retrieve the argument in five sentences a few days later. Open Module 8 with draft, revision, memo, help declaration and source ledger.

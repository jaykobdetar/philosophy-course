Start by distinguishing these questions: What makes compliance sensible? What makes obedience obligatory? What makes an institution legitimate? ## Reading and access

The exact baseline readings are preserved. Tuck's Hobbes and Laslett's Locke are the assigned editions; available public-domain transcriptions are useful companions but are not verified facsimiles of those editions. Rawls, Nozick and Mills require a lawful copy or loan of the specified editions. Their assigned passages were **not inspected** in this authoring run: their teaching reconstructions and page-level marking remain provisional until checked against the learner's actual text. Do not replace reading with the teaching notes or use an AI's familiarity as verification. Haslanger's author-hosted article was inspected in targeted sections, not given a complete quotation or accessibility audit. Links grant no general redistribution permission.

Use the exact section/page locators in each packet. Make a citation sheet with author, title, edition, locator, claim and whether it is quotation, paraphrase or inference. Audio, text-to-speech and dictated drafts are welcome: pause at each section, record its locator, and check quotations against text. A written reading route and typed exchange always meet the same intellectual criteria. Budget rest breaks and access adjustments individually; speed is not graded. Mark any additional time rather than silently squeezing work.

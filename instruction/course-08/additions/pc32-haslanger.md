## Explanation

The conceptual project asks about existing concepts; the descriptive project investigates the kinds our vocabulary tracks; the analytical project asks which concepts serve legitimate purposes. Haslanger's priority is revisionary critical theory. A proposed category is therefore assessed partly by its explanatory and political function, not solely by conformity to familiar usage. That raises a genuine objection: a useful new definition might change the subject or marginalize people.

Her accounts connect social position with interpreted bodily markers and background ideology. The racialization proposal includes presumed geographical ancestry and systematic privilege or subordination. The proposed categories are context-sensitive; individuals' overall advantages need not be identical. Treating a stereotype as a biological essence, or any isolated insult as a complete structural explanation, misses the intended analysis. The account remains contestable: identify which condition, purpose or implication an objection targets.

## Worked practice and bounded output

Invented case: a ferry office assigns residents with blue work badges to slower queues because officials believe badge holders are unreliable. Blue badges are randomly assigned each morning, with no bodily or ancestry association. This may be unfair institutional classification, but resemblance to a hierarchy alone does not establish the article's racialization conditions. A good application identifies what is present and absent, rather than stretching a philosophical label to cover every injustice.

**H-P:** Write 400–500 words with four parts: identify the project being pursued; reconstruct the p.44 racialization conditions in your own words; test the blue-badge case condition by condition; state one objection about the usefulness or limits of the definition. Add a three-row conceptual/descriptive/analytical question table about the ferry policy. Criteria: clear project, accurate conditions with a locator, bounded conclusion, serious objection. Revise after feedback; append a 100-word account of what the revision changes.

## Independent H-F, 30 minutes

Invented fresh case: a district assigns residents with a perceived facial feature to a “coastal-origin” category. Officials presume ancestry from that feature, regardless of actual ancestry. The category governs access to housing and positions its members as dependents. Some classified residents are wealthy and deny that the category describes their identity. Using the article, write 250–350 words assessing the structural classification, the significance of mistaken ancestry and wealth, and a limit concerning identity. Identify one empirical proposition that would need investigation in an actual district. Assigned article/citation sheet only; no AI content help, hints or keys.

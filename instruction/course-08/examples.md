# C08 annotated practice responses and revision

All responses and cases here are original instructional constructions, not learner data, quotations from primary texts or prewritten assessment answers. Use only with supported practice. The levels describe the sampled reasoning, not a person's ability. Actual essays need checked sources in the specified edition.

## Three levels on one invented practice case

**Case:** A town association promises all members a shared workshop. It gives one group machinery training and assigns the other group only reception tasks. It then restricts workshop voting to people with machinery experience. The rule's defenders say that everyone is subject to the same criterion, so the result is fair.

**Developing response:** “This is unfair because inequality is always wrong. Wollstonecraft would agree and Rawls wants everyone equal. The town should have equal votes.”

**Annotation:** It notices an unequal outcome but does not reconstruct the mechanism or defend the standard. The Rawls attribution substitutes equality of outcome for a more specific theory. It gives no locator and treats author agreement as proof. Repair: explain how the training rule affects the supposedly neutral qualification, then state the principle governing voting. A defensible equal-vote conclusion still needs reasons.

**Competent response:** “The same experience criterion applies to both groups, but the association itself has withheld opportunities to acquire that experience. Its defense therefore omits a causal premise: the earlier training allocation helped determine who qualifies. Wollstonecraft's opening argument in chapter II provides a relevant challenge to using an effect of education as a reason to deny agency. Applied here, the argument supports reexamining the qualification; it does not yet prove that machinery knowledge is irrelevant to every workshop decision. We need evidence of which decisions require expertise and whether training could be shared.”

**Annotation:** It gives a mechanism, a bounded textual connection and a relevant evidence request. The chapter reference must be checked in the actual text; this sample is original paraphrase, not a quotation. The response qualifies the conclusion rather than pretending the analogy determines policy. To improve: confront a defender who argues that competent operation protects everyone.

**Strong response:** “The defender has a serious point: people making technical safety decisions may need relevant knowledge. Yet that point cannot establish the fairness of a rule whose sponsor denied one group the means of becoming knowledgeable. Two questions should be separated: which decisions require competence, and whether the association offers a fair route to acquiring it. An interim division between technical safety decisions and membership decisions might preserve safety while opening training, but it would require evidence that the ‘technical’ category is not used to capture all decisions. The Wollstonecraft analogy challenges the conversion of produced dependency into justification for continued dependency. It does not erase differences between her account of education and this invented association, or settle the design of an interim council.”

**Annotation:** It reconstructs the opponent, separates institutional tasks, offers a conditional response and identifies a new possible abuse. Strong means the reasoning withstands a serious challenge; it does not mean the suggested institution is uniquely correct. Check the author's exact passage before claiming this particular application is hers.

## Draft → revision: comparison and research procedure

**Practice question:** Does an equal-resource table settle the justice of an inherited housing system?

**Draft (invented):** “Scheme A gives everyone 20 credits and Scheme B gives (24,30,50). Since B has more wealth and its poorest people gain, Rawls and Nozick both choose B. Historical evidence is unnecessary because present outcomes measure justice.”

**Diagnosis:** The draft collapses two standards, assumes an adequate metric and ignores prior liberties/opportunities and entitlement history. It asserts a theorists' consensus that the table cannot establish. No amount of stylistic polish repairs those missing premises.

**Revision (invented):** “If these credits adequately represent relevant lifetime prospects and both schemes meet the prior liberty and opportunity conditions, the difference-principle comparison favors B's higher minimum. Those conditions cannot be read off the numbers. An entitlement analysis also needs to know how holdings arose and whether transfers were just. I therefore defend B only as a conditional institutional comparison, leaving the acquisition and rectification question open. If the houses came from uncorrected confiscation, present transfers alone cannot establish their entitlement history.”

**Revision memo:** “I withdrew a claimed agreement between the theories and separated institutional comparison from provenance. The revision identifies what the table can support under assumptions and what it cannot. I still need the specified Rawls and Nozick passages to verify my attribution; I have not read them in this constructed example and would label the claim provisional.”

**Bounded source procedure:** In 45 minutes from Module 7's rereading budget, make a claim ledger with four entries: Rawls institutional subject; priority conditions; Nozick transfer; rectification. For each record the exact assigned section/page, a paraphrase based on the text actually inspected, and one limitation. If a source is unavailable, write “access pending,” not a fabricated locator. Then in 30 minutes identify which claims in the draft the ledger supports. This teaches research integrity without inventing historical facts or requiring new purchases.

**Comparison test:** Hold the present numerical distribution fixed while changing its acquisition history. Then hold history fixed while changing a basic liberty. Explain why each manipulation tests a different concern. This is supported practice and cannot be used as the final delayed case after rehearsal.

# Course 7 — Learner assessment forms

**Release only the selected form.** Reading this file in advance counts as exposure. The assessor records exposed tasks and uses an unexposed alternate; if both forms are exposed, a new task must be authored and checked against the same criteria before testing. Tutor/examiner anchors are in a separate file. “Uncoached” cannot be guaranteed by a public repository; use an honest exposure declaration.

## Conditions and required evidence

Use the [shared rubric](../../current/assessment.md) and [outcome map](outcomes.md). Submit P7's revised **1,800–2,200-word** paper, draft, source dossier and substantive revision commentary. The independent written session is **90 minutes: 35 + 30 + 25**; the explanation is **20 minutes**. All three essential rows need source-based practice and independent evidence. Record no aggregate score that compensates for a missing row.

Assigned primary readings and the exact secondary source in your paper may be open. No tutor, AI, search-generated explanation, collaborative answer, model response or task-specific hint during independent work. Ordinary screen-reader, dictation, text enlargement and translation-access accommodations are recorded and permitted when they do not supply philosophical content. A text exchange can replace speaking. Record actual time and agreed accommodation; timing is a workload plan, not a speed criterion.

Before the sitting, the examiner verifies the source passages and selects the exact range named below that was not previously coached. Merely having read the whole assigned text does not count as rehearsing this question; prior analysis of this exact prompt or its answer does. Do not supply a fabricated excerpt. If the examiner cannot inspect a passage, that textual judgment remains pending and the conceptual case may be attempted provisionally.

## Fresh form F

**F1 — Knowledge, testimony and injustice. 35 minutes, 400–500 words.** A regional transit service usually publishes accurate route notices. Four travel apps say route Z is open; all four copy the same service notice. Miri, an experienced driver, reports a closure after seeing a barrier. The manager discounts Miri's report because of a stereotype that people from her neighborhood exaggerate danger, while accepting another driver's equally unsupported report. In fact the barrier belongs to another route; route Z is open. Analyze the passenger's belief based on the apps and the manager's credibility judgment. Distinguish truth, justification, grounds for trust, report dependence and testimonial injustice. Apply a stated Goldman or Gettier distinction, one Hume/Reid claim, and Fricker's chapter-1 account. Identify one fact that would change your conclusion. Use exact sources rather than inventing probabilities.

**F2 — Self and identity. 30 minutes, 350–450 words.** In a stipulated future operation, Neri's psychological history continues in two distinct persons, O and P, with equally strong memory and intention links. Neither retains Neri's original body. Both want freedom from fear of loss. Compare the case with one Locke or Parfit claim and interpret **SN 22.59, Sujato, segments 7.1–10.1** (the extension across kinds of aggregates). Explain what the continuity facts settle and leave unsettled, what the discourse passage does, and why the two projects are not interchangeable. State the conditional status of the operation's possibility. Do not assume all Buddhist thought has the same theory.

**F3 — Responsibility. 25 minutes, 300–400 words.** A critic accepts the 1975 argument's entailment of P by P0 and L, but denies this principle: an agent's ability to render a consequence false entails ability to render its sufficient antecedent false. She says the logical relation between propositions does not automatically establish the relevant power relation. Identify the numbered premise in van Inwagen's pp. 191–193 that she challenges. Reconstruct enough of the argument to show its role, give a reasoned reply, and state whether the critic has thereby refuted determinism or established responsibility.

## Alternate fresh form B

Use only for an exposed original or after coached repair; record the reason.

**B1 — 35 minutes, 400–500 words.** A laboratory's public display is accurate on nearly all comparable checks. Today three staff reports repeat its incorrect “sample ready” signal. An intern reports “not ready” from direct inspection but is dismissed because of a stereotype about the intern's ethnic group. The same supervisor accepts equally detailed inspection reports from senior staff without asking for corroboration. Unknown to everyone, a second preparation process has made the sample ready. Analyze the true belief reached through the display reports, the grounds of trust and the intern's treatment. Use the same three distinct source-based questions as F1. Include a possible ordinary evidential reason for doubt and explain how it would affect the diagnosis if substantiated.

**B2 — 30 minutes, 350–450 words.** Ren's organism survives, but a new person T has apparent memories copied from Ren, while Ren retains projects and overlapping memories across later life. An observer concludes that only T is Ren and that ending painful attachment would require deleting memory. Evaluate both transitions using a Locke/Parfit claim and **SN 22.59, Sujato, segments 11.1–12.3** (the closing practical sequence). Distinguish direct memory, continuity, numerical identity and the discourse's purpose. State one condition not settled by the imaginary setup.

**B3 — 25 minutes, 300–400 words.** A Frankfurt-style controller would intervene only after an agent forms an initial intention to refuse the action. In the actual sequence the agent willingly acts and the controller remains idle. A critic says the initial intention to refuse remains an alternative and may be morally significant. Reconstruct PAP, explain exactly which part of the attempted counterexample this challenges, propose a serious reply and state what remains unresolved about determinism. Locate the relevant argument in Frankfurt's assigned article; the controller here is an original variant, not a quotation.

## Twenty-minute unscripted explanation

Bring your own written answer and paper. The examiner selects one live vulnerability after reading them: a premise you need, an attribution you make, or a scope you claim. Explain the argument in ordinary language, respond to a genuinely new objection, and revise if necessary. A written equivalent uses the same exchange with no advance content answer. Criteria: responsiveness, ownership of reasoning, fair objection and traceable evidence. Saying “I would need to check that passage” is appropriate when true; it does not by itself resolve the textual criterion.

## Delayed forms D — choose the least secure row

At **4–6 weeks or two later modules**, choose **one** 30-minute, 300–400-word task. Source-open; no coaching; no review of its answer immediately beforehand. This 30 minutes belongs to M8, not the later course's hours.

**D1:** A flood alert is copied by six neighborhood accounts from one volunteer's post. The volunteer has a strong local accuracy record, but the only direct witness reports that the water is falling. The witness is dismissed because of an identity stereotype; afterward the water rises. Separate support at the time, eventual truth, dependence and credibility injustice using your course sources. Identify what additional evidence matters.

**D2:** A traveler signs a promise; decades later the same organism keeps it despite losing direct memory of signing, while connected intentions and relationships persist. A commentator says that SN 22.59 makes the promise meaningless because consciousness changes. Compare one Locke/Parfit claim with one precise SN segment and evaluate the commentator's inference. State the difference between persistence and practical non-identification.

**D3:** A critic grants van Inwagen's entailment but says “J could have done otherwise if J had wanted to” suffices for ability, even if J's actual wanting was fixed by past and laws. Reconstruct the sense of ability the critic proposes, explain whether this alone answers the 1975 argument, and distinguish the resulting issue from PAP's responsibility condition.

## Alternate delayed forms E

Use if the matching D form was exposed. Same time, length, access and criteria.

**E1:** A repair technician is repeatedly accurate, but three favorable references all come from a single employer's statement. A customer refuses this technician solely because of a caste stereotype and accepts another with weaker evidence. The first technician's current diagnosis is false. Separate reasonable trust, source dependence and the alleged epistemic wrong; give one limit of the evidence.

**E2:** Two successors each inherit a painter's long-term projects; only one has direct childhood memories. A critic assumes the memory-rich successor must be the original and that the other is therefore irrelevant to questions about suffering. Test the two claims with a Locke/Parfit distinction and a located SN claim; explain why numerical identity and the discourse's inquiry need separate arguments.

**E3:** A hidden controller would force a treasurer to divert funds if she hesitated. She willingly diverts them, but before acting she could have warned the victim without triggering the controller. Assess whether this warning option defeats the relevant Frankfurt-style argument; distinguish an alternative act from the alternatives needed by the proposed responsibility principle. Do not claim the case alone establishes compatibilism.

After any delayed failure, use criterion-based feedback, a question–premise–conclusion comparison and a new independently attempted case. Log any time beyond the allocated repair budget. Do not call retained competence demonstrated before an actual delayed response exists.

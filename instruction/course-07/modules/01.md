# Module 1 — Knowledge and justification

**16 hours.** Guiding question: what must be added to true belief, and what makes a belief justified? Prerequisites: Course 1 reconstruction, Course 5 empiricism, the entry semantic check in [README](../README.md). Outcomes: separate an analysis of knowledge from a justification theory; reconstruct a Gettier failure; evaluate a process reliability condition with a new example.

## Teaching explanation

An analysis states conditions intended to be necessary and sufficient. `Kp ↔ (Bp ∧ p ∧ Jp)` says both that knowledge requires belief, truth and justification, and that these jointly suffice. A Gettier counterexample targets sufficiency: it allows the three conditions while denying knowledge. Producing a false belief, or a lucky guess without justification, does not test that conjunction. Keep “the subject has good evidence” separate from “the reader knows the hidden facts.”

In the assigned article, justified error can survive a valid inference to a proposition that happens to be true for a different reason. Logical validity preserves truth when premises are true; it does not make a false premise true or ensure the conclusion is connected to the believer's evidence in the right way. Rejecting a candidate analysis does not by itself establish skepticism or supply a replacement definition.

A process reliabilist strategy evaluates how the belief was produced. A process is reliable when it tends to yield true beliefs in the relevant circumstances. Reliability is not infallibility, the person's confidence, or a single successful outcome. Specify the process type: “vision in good light” and “vision in fog” may have different records. Choosing a type after seeing whether this one belief succeeded risks trivializing the theory. An adequate explanation must say why its classification is principled.

An account of justified belief need not be a complete analysis of knowledge. It may allow justified false beliefs and still need a separate treatment of epistemic luck. Also distinguish a reliable process from the subject's evidence that it is reliable: the latter raises the internalism/externalism problem taken up in A26. Read Goldman's actual conditions before claiming that a toy reliability rule is his finished account.

## Worked practice and output P1

Original example: Lina sees an accurate-looking ferry board showing 10:20 and reasonably believes “a ferry leaves at 10:20.” Unknown to her, this board is a convincing prop installed yesterday; by coincidence a newly scheduled ferry does leave then. The proposition is true; Lina believes it; ordinary checking supports her belief. The hidden coincidence challenges knowledge. Calling the board “unreliable because this belief is false” would misdescribe the stipulated facts. A repair might require a suitable causal/history relation, but that repair now needs its own tests.

Create a four-column map (knowledge analysis / justification account / reliability / luck), each with a definition, necessary/sufficient claim, and counterexample. Then change the example twice: first make the board a well-maintained live feed with an occasional fault; second make the apparent evidence only a random color. Write **600 words** evaluating one expressly stated reliability condition. Include the original case you devised, all truth/belief/justification stipulations, one objection and a revised condition. Tutor may model another case and ask you to expose one missing premise; it may not write your evaluation.

## Independent changed case I1

Release after P1. **35 minutes, 300–400 words**, assigned readings open, no tutor/AI/hints/model notes. A conservator identifies an authentic coin by a learned visual method that has been accurate on 98 of 100 comparable tests. This coin is authentic, but the feature she uses was added by a later restorer and normally indicates a modern copy. She has no evidence of restoration. Analyze what the statistics do and do not establish; state a relevant process description; distinguish justified belief from knowledge and defend a verdict conditional on a stated account. Do not change supplied facts. Criteria: clear target, valid distinction, relevant counterconsideration, traceable attribution. Examiner diagnostics are separate.

## Teaching explanation

The principle of alternate possibilities (PAP) makes ability to do otherwise a necessary condition of moral responsibility. A Frankfurt-style challenge tries to describe an agent acting from their own reasons while a counterfactual intervener would have ensured the same outcome had the agent begun to choose differently. On the actual sequence the intervener does nothing. If the agent is responsible despite no available alternative, PAP is too strong. The argument does not immediately prove determinism true or every form of compatibilism correct. Responsibility for an act and freedom to do otherwise can be separated in the debate.

A crucial objection is whether the case removes every relevant alternative. An early sign or refusal might remain something the agent could do. Another question is whether the intervener's predictive setup already assumes determinism. State which version of the case is being criticized; a flaw in one device is not automatically a refutation of the entire strategy. Coercion that actually causes an act differs from an unused backup, so keep actual and counterfactual sequences apart.

Van Inwagen stipulates determinism as the entailment of a later world-state proposition P by a complete earlier state P0 and the laws L. His argument considers a judge J not raising a hand. Simplified while preserving the 1975 numbering: (1) determinism implies P0 and L entail P; (2) J raising the hand would make P false; (3) if J could raise it, J could render P false; (4) given the entailment, ability to render P false implies ability to render P0-and-L false; (5) because P0 concerns the pre-birth past, that ability would imply ability to render L false; (6) J cannot render L false; therefore (7) if determinism is true, J cannot raise the hand. Compare this paraphrase to pp. 191–193 before citing.

The inference is conditional. It does not establish determinism, the nonexistence of freedom, or absence of responsibility without further premises. “Render false” requires careful treatment: the author does not mean changing a true proposition into a false proposition after the fact. A compatibilist might challenge an ability principle or distinguish causing a law to be false from doing something such that a different law would obtain. Calling such an objection “a denial of science” evades its logical target.

## Worked practice and output P6

Original case: a grant reviewer chooses to suppress a rival application because of resentment. A hidden controller would force suppression if the reviewer began approving it; the reviewer never does, and the controller remains idle. The actual reason is resentment. The alleged inability to approve is secured by the counterfactual story. This supports a challenge to PAP only if responsibility survives and the relevant alternative really is absent; neither judgment follows from calling the controller “hidden.”

Draw actual and counterfactual timelines. Next map the seven 1975 statements, marking (1) as a consequence of the determinism definition and (3)–(6) as ability-related claims requiring defense. Write **600 words** assessing this objection: “A different action may require a different past, but that is not the power to change the actual past.” Identify the target, reconstruct the strongest reply and say what remains undecided. Check attribution in both primary texts; do not merge the two arguments into a single proof.

## Independent changed case I6

**35 minutes, 350–450 words**, source-open, no hints. A critic accepts the entailment and the argument's earlier ability transfers but rejects the claim that J cannot render L false. She distinguishes causing a law to be violated from being able to act in such a way that the actual law would not have held. Identify the 1975 premise challenged and explain whether her distinction supplies the relevant ability. Give one reply on the article's terms. Does the objection by itself establish moral responsibility? Criteria: exact target, charitable reply, conditional conclusion and separate responsibility question.

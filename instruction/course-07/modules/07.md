# Module 7 — Essay and revision

**28 hours.** Guiding question: what focused position can you defend after a serious rival is considered? Prerequisites: M1–M6 artifacts and source access for the chosen topic. Outcomes: frame an answerable question; use one verified secondary interpretation; sustain a fair argument; revise a substantive weakness.

## Assigned work and access

Reread the exact primary ranges relevant to one of these questions, all located in [the register](../readings.md): (a) does a stated reliabilist condition distinguish justification from epistemic luck? (b) where do Hume X.I and Reid VI.24 disagree about grounds of trust? (c) what does a specified branching case show about identity and psychological continuity in Locke II.xxvii and Parfit pp. 3–27? (d) what does a Frankfurt-style case establish against PAP, and what does van Inwagen's 1975 argument leave open? The eventual thesis may differ from these questions' suggested emphasis, but stays inside one bounded dispute.

**Secondary interpretation procedure:** find one scholarly interpretation from a journal, academic book chapter or credited scholarly encyclopedia. You may use the assigned IEP §§1.b/3.b–d if the paper is a narrowly approved Locke/SN comparison, but the baseline topics above otherwise require a topic-relevant secondary source. Before drafting, record author, title, year, exact pages/section, stable URL/identifier, access route, actual passage read and one specific interpretive claim. Verify those metadata against the publisher/library record, then inspect the actual passage. An abstract or AI summary is insufficient. Limit new secondary reading to one section or at most 15 pages initially, within the six-hour source allowance. If no usable source is available, draft the original conceptual argument and mark the interpretation criterion pending; do not invent a citation.

## Teaching explanation and decision procedure

An interpretive paper asks what an author argues and why that is the better reading. A conceptual paper tests a definition or inference. A normative paper defends what should be believed or done. These projects can interact, but the thesis must identify its principal burden. An invented case can test a conditional theory; it cannot supply historical evidence about what an author meant or empirical evidence about actual people.

Choose the smallest dispute that can survive three steps: state the proposed answer in one sentence; locate the premise on which a rival disagrees; identify what would count against your answer. If the rival merely says your topic is unimportant, sharpen it until it challenges the inference. If the only source support is a vaguely remembered slogan, narrow or postpone that claim.

Use an argument skeleton: question → thesis with scope → primary reconstruction → independent reason → serious objection → reply or restricted thesis → remaining limit. For each paragraph write its function in the margin. In the evidence ledger distinguish quotation, paraphrase, interpretation and your inference. A valid inference from an invented case may be original reasoning, but do not attribute it to the source without evidence. A secondary author supplies an interpretation to examine, not a verdict to repeat.

## Worked practice and output P7

Original weak thesis: “Reliabilism is wrong because reliable people sometimes err.” This attacks infallibility, which was not the stipulated condition. Revised question: “Does success frequency suffice to select the relevant process type in the restored-coin case?” The improved thesis can concede fallibility while arguing that a principled process classification is still needed. See [annotated examples](../examples.md) for a different practice case, never an assessment response to copy.

Submit **1,800–2,200 words** (bibliography excluded), a 250-word objection memo, a source ledger and a 300-word revision commentary. Required checkpoints: 200-word proposal naming method; one-page claim/premise map; 800-word trial section; complete draft; feedback identifying at most two consequential weaknesses; revised paper. Keep both versions. The commentary identifies original claim, criticism, changed inference/evidence and residual difficulty. Grammar-only changes do not satisfy substantive revision.

## Independent changed case I7

After submitting the revised paper, complete **30 minutes, 250–350 words**, source-open, no live content help. A researcher reports that 95% of archived witness claims were correct and concludes that every current witness claim is justified. A rival says one identity-prejudicial exclusion in the archive makes all its claims false. Evaluate both inferences and identify a narrower defensible conclusion, making no new empirical assumptions. Explain how the distinction you defend in your paper helps, or why it does not transfer. Criteria: independent reasoning, scope control, serious reply and correct limits. This task is additional evidence, not a replacement for the three final essential rows.

## Schedule, repair and next step

Source verification/rereading 6h; proposal/map 3h; drafting 8h; feedback 2h; independent I7/defense practice 1h; substantive revision 6h; citation/revision audit 2h = **28h**. If the thesis changes every paragraph, reverse-outline before redrafting. If the rival is weak, state the strongest premise-denial you can accept as a serious challenge and revise scope accordingly. After one week, reconstruct the paper's central inference from its title alone, during the audit allocation. Next: [Module 8](08.md).

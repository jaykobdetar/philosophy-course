## Teaching explanation

A regress problem asks what justifies a belief when its reasons are themselves beliefs needing justification. An externalist account can stop a demand for additional possessed reasons by appealing to a truth-linked condition outside the subject's reflective access. An internalist challenge asks whether a belief can be epistemically responsible from the subject's point of view when that connection is unavailable to the subject. Define the particular versions under debate: internalism is not simply “all knowledge comes from introspection,” and externalism is not “ignore all contrary evidence.”

BonJour's clairvoyance discussion tests reliable cognition while separating the subject's accessible reasons from what an informed observer knows. His immediate target includes Armstrong; calling every condition he criticizes Goldman's exact 1979 position would skip important work. In the comparison, write Goldman's condition explicitly, then ask which feature of the BonJour case bears on it. A reliabilist may reject the demand for reflective access or refine treatment of defeaters. The burden is to explain the refinement without making reliability fit each verdict after the fact.

Moore presents ordinary external objects as premises in a proof that external objects exist and distinguishes knowing a premise from proving that premise. A skeptical objection can accept validity while challenging whether those premises supply the kind of warrant demanded by the skeptic. Thus “the conclusion follows” and “the challenge has been answered” are different achievements. The objection here is an original reconstruction of a demand Moore discusses in his concluding paragraphs, not a quotation from an unnamed philosopher. Do not claim Moore proves he is not dreaming merely because his proof uses a hand premise.

## Worked practice A26

Original example: a technician reliably senses an invisible machine fault but cannot explain the method and has neither evidence for its reliability nor a defeater. An outside observer has a complete success record. Two columns prevent conflation: observer's reason to trust the technician, technician's reason to accept the belief. The case does not itself prove that reflective access is necessary; it makes the disagreement explicit. Add a warning the technician has good reason to trust that the method is unreliable. That change introduces a defeater and must be analyzed separately.

Produce a **500-word** BonJour/Goldman comparison with exact conditions, a **four-line** Moore reconstruction and a **400-word** skeptical objection/reply. Name whether the objection attacks validity, premise knowledge or a requirement for proof. Use the imperfect Moore copy only for provisional paraphrase until original pages can be inspected.

## Independent Q26-A / alternate Q26-B

Release one only, **40 minutes, 400–500 words**, source-open, no assistance. **A:** Last month Iona tested a glass detector against independently certified samples and obtained strong evidence of its reliability. She still remembers conducting those tests and retains her conclusion, but has forgotten the individual measurements and lost the records. Unknown to her, a replacement component has since made the detector unreliable; she has no warning or other evidence of the change. Today its display happens to classify a defective pane correctly, and she accepts the result. Compare the justification of her earlier reliability belief and her present belief about the pane under two stated accounts. Explain what role her remaining memory, the changed process and the result's truth play. A colleague argues that her inability to reproduce the old evidence proves she never knew that any external objects existed. Assess that inference using Moore's distinction between knowing and proving. **B:** Dev's trained hearing is reliable, and Dev has repeatedly checked it. Today a normally reliable inspection report falsely says the hearing aid is defective. Dev ignores it and correctly hears a bell. A friend says the heard bell proves all sensory beliefs reliable. Analyze the defeater, distinguish belief justification from a universal inference, and state how Moore's proof differs from that universal claim. Neutral criteria: explicit perspectives, relevant source conditions, exact objection target and limited conclusion.

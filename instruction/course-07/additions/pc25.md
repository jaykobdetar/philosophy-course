# PC-25 — Ontology, causation and modal strength

**14 added hours; 0 replaced; net +14.** Take after M6. This is a finite amendment to the 140-hour baseline. Outcomes: reconstruct an ontological disagreement, answer a serious objection, distinguish entailment, causal dependence and kinds of possibility. Prerequisites: M1 and M6, elementary quantifier meaning. Kant is a later connection, not an unread prerequisite.

## Exact reading and teaching

Read Quine, “On What There Is,” *Review of Metaphysics* 2, no. 5 (1948), **pp. 21–38 complete**. [Register/access](../readings.md). Selected local passages on negative existence, descriptions and bound variables inspected; neither complete visual proofing nor redistribution rights is claimed. Reread Hume X.I's contrast of unfamiliar and miraculous (already assigned) and van Inwagen pp. 185–186/191–193. This rereading is included below. No Lewis paper is required.

An ontological dispute asks what a theory commits us to accepting as existing. The ability to talk meaningfully about a fictional object does not settle whether that object exists. Quine examines the temptation to make denial itself introduce an entity, then uses paraphrase and quantification to locate commitments. Distinguish the word, an idea of the object and the alleged object; confusing those makes apparent arguments too easy. A sentence can contain a name grammatically without its best logical analysis requiring an entity answering to that name.

In a quantified analysis, ask what must fall within the variables' range for the sentence to be true. Compare “there are numbers with property F” and “some inscriptions have shape G”: they quantify over different things. This criterion locates a theory's commitments; it does not establish by itself which theory or ontology is best. A dispute about paraphrase therefore matters: is the reformulation faithful, and what explanatory work does it lose? Read Quine's later discussion before assuming all choice of theory is arbitrary.

Causal claims concern how events depend on or produce others; entailment concerns what cannot be false if premises are true. Necessary given laws and initial conditions differs from logically necessary. An imagined alternative may be logically consistent but conflict with current physical laws. Hume's experiential reasoning and van Inwagen's conditional determinism therefore ask different questions. In later Kant work, add a comparison of the conditions for representing objective succession only after reading its assigned text; do not attribute that theory from this packet alone.

## Worked practice A25

Original example: “The library's nonexistent blue dragon is sleepy.” Before assigning a dragon to the ontology, test a paraphrase about a story's sentences or what readers imagine. Such a paraphrase may preserve a fictional discourse claim while changing an alleged literal predication. The issue is fidelity, not whether the word dragon occurs.

Write a **600-word** reconstruction of Quine's negative-existence problem, proposed treatment and one objection about paraphrase. Then classify four original claims: (1) the alarm and pump always coincide; (2) cutting the pump's power stops this alarm; (3) given these laws and initial state the alarm must ring; (4) “the alarm rings and does not ring in the same respect” is contradictory. For each, state the evidence or inference needed and a stronger claim that does not follow. Finish with a 250-word reply to the strongest objection to your ontology reconstruction.

## Independent Q25-A / alternate Q25-B

Release one only, **40 minutes, 400 words**, sources open, no coaching. **A:** A theory says that every fictional detective has a birthplace; another paraphrases this as “every detective story attributes a birthplace to its detective.” Explain whether the paraphrase retains the same claim and what each commits to. Then evaluate “the generator and lamp always start together; therefore the generator causes the lamp and its failure is logically impossible.” **B:** A theory quantifies over average families; a rival speaks only of counts of people and dwellings. Identify the commitment question and one potential loss in paraphrase. Then evaluate “the door must lock given this program and input; therefore no logically possible door remains unlocked.” Criteria: fair reconstruction, explicit domains, qualified objection, causal/modal distinction.

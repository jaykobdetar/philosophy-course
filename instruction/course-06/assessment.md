# Course 6 — independent assessment forms

Use this file with [Module 8](modules/08.md) and the [shared rubric](../../current/assessment.md). Forms are learner-facing; answers and diagnostic anchors are in [examiner notes](examiner-notes.md). The assessor releases only the selected form during the attempt. Reading all forms beforehand makes them exposed practice; use an unexposed alternate or construct a comparably bounded new form before the next attempt.

## Conditions and evidence

The final written session is **90 minutes: 30 per essential row**, followed by a **20-minute explanation or accessible written exchange**. It is source-open: assigned primary texts, a bibliography/locator index, and assistive access tools are allowed. Prepared case answers, examiner notes, generative AI, tutor hints, peer help, and newly searched commentaries are not allowed during an independent attempt. Text-to-speech, dictation, screen readers and agreed extra processing time are access supports, not content coaching; record adjustments. Declare previous exposure and help before starting. Save the untouched response before feedback.

For each response, make your own conclusion explicit, connect principles to case facts, cite the source actually used, answer one objection, and name a limit. The guide word lengths are 250–350 per item, with no penalty for concise adequate evidence. Interpretive criteria remain source-dependent if the assessor cannot inspect the assigned translation. A total score cannot compensate for a missing essential row.

## Fresh Form A — F8A

**F8A.1 Normative theories — 30 minutes.** Two volunteers knowingly perform the same useful work under identical conditions. One acts from concern for the recipients; the other acts solely to gain admiration. Stipulate equal consequences, informed consent, no deception and no prior undertaking. An observer says, “Because the benefits are identical, the actions and agents must have identical moral merit in every respect.” Analyze that inference under **two of Aristotle, Kant and Mill**. Distinguish the rightness or value of the action from motive, moral worth and settled character where your selected texts warrant those distinctions. Give a premise-to-judgment argument for each theory, one objection, and one fact needed before attributing a stable character. Do not assume the theories use “merit” identically.

**F8A.2 Cultivation and interpretation — 30 minutes.** Read **Mengzi 6A6** in Van Norden (2008); cite the passage and identify who is speaking. Explain a claim about moral capacities or responses, reconstruct its support, and compare one point with **Aristotle II.1**. Then consider an invented learner who shows concern reliably toward friends but not strangers: what would your interpretation explain, and what would it leave open? State one limit on comparing the authors. This is not a request to agree that human nature is good. If 6A6 was central to the essay or coached closely, use Form B's passage instead.

**F8A.3 Metaethics and applied reasoning — 30 minutes.** A person voluntarily agreed to provide three hours of bodily support to a stranger and thereby caused a substitute helper to leave. After one hour they want to stop solely to attend a party. Safe transfer becomes possible after another twenty minutes. Stipulate no bodily risk during that time. Distinguish (a) a normative judgment about waiting, (b) a metaethical account of such judgments, and (c) the claim that a right to life by itself grants a right to bodily support. Use **Hume or Ayer** to explain the metaethical distinction and **Thomson** to analyze how agreement, reliance, burden and alternatives matter. State what follows and what remains disputed.

## Alternate fresh Form B — F8B

**F8B.1 — 30 minutes.** An official selects between two equally qualified applicants for one apprenticeship funded by a public association. One applicant is the official’s sibling. There is no announced tie-breaking rule, and the applicants’ expected benefits are stipulated equal. The official proposes a candid policy of preferring relatives whenever candidates tie; a critic proposes a fair lottery. Compare this institutional choice with spending one’s own time helping a sibling study. Analyze the proposed policy under **two of Aristotle, Kant and Mill**, giving explicit reasons about partiality, role, character, duty or welfare as applicable. Explain whether equal immediate benefits settle either choice, identify one missing premise, and answer an objection. A modern public-role rule must be defended, not attributed verbatim to an ancient text.

**F8B.2 — 30 minutes.** Read **Mengzi 6A7**, cite Van Norden and explain how one of its comparisons supports a claim about human development or shared capacities. Compare that inferential role with **Aristotle II.4** on acting well and possessing virtue. Consider two pupils of comparable initial ability taught under persistently unequal conditions. Explain what your reading permits you to infer and what would require empirical evidence. Name one translation or context limit. If this passage was coached, use the A passage only if unexposed; otherwise the assessor must verify an unused passage within 6A1–10.

**F8B.3 — 30 minutes.** A person connected without permission can be disconnected immediately with the dependent stranger surviving independently. They nevertheless demand that the stranger die because continued existence would distress them. Distinguish a right to refuse support from a right to secure death using Thomson's argument. Then explain why the statement “moral condemnation expresses an attitude” differs from “this act is permissible.” Compare that distinction with Hume's demand to account for a move from descriptive to normative premises. No actual legal or medical claim is required.

## Delayed Form D — F8D

Roughly **4–6 weeks after course completion, or after two later modules**, spend **30 minutes** on the least secure essential row. Use an unexposed item below. If two rows remain unresolved, repair them first within the allocated assessment repair time; do not treat one delayed item as independent evidence for both.

- **D1 normative:** A sincere organizer persists with a project after reliable evidence shows it predictably harms the people it aims to help. They say, “My benevolent intention makes continuing morally admirable, regardless of those effects.” Compare two assigned normative approaches, distinguishing the original aim, the present informed choice and any claim about character. Give a qualified judgment, identify a relevant premise and answer an objection. Do not equate a benevolent aim automatically with acting from duty or possessing practical wisdom.
- **D2 interpretation:** Read **Mengzi 6A9**, then explain how the passage connects attention or instruction with development. Compare one point with **Aristotle II.1**, preserving the distinction between an initial capacity and a stable disposition. Consider a pupil given one lesson and then repeatedly pressured to act otherwise. What does the case illustrate, and what does the text not establish about that pupil? Source-bound interpretation remains provisional without the assigned translation.
- **D3 metaethics/application:** A helper never agreed to supply support; a machine can safely replace them now, but the dependent person says their right to life gives a right to this helper specifically. Analyze the inference using Thomson. Separately, a spectator concludes that because disapproval is an attitude, every refusal is permissible. Identify the additional normative premise required; contrast a psychological report of disapproval with Ayer's evaluative use, or reconstruct Hume's inferential challenge.

Delayed alternate if D was exposed: **D1B** compare two decision makers who act on equally careful evidence with the same intention, but whose actions have different outcomes because of an unforeseeable event; analyze under two theories whether outcome, blame and character must vary together; **D2B** interpret **Mengzi 6A5** on the interlocutors' dispute about righteousness and compare one explicitly identified Aristotle II.1 claim, explaining why the analogy is limited; **D3B** use an explicit promise of two hours' support with an immediately available substitute and ask whether the promise demands this person's body regardless of the alternative. Use the same 30-minute limit and criteria. The examiner must inspect 6A5 before using D2B; never improvise its argument from the title.

## Local replacement forms U1B–U7B

These replace an exposed module check **inside that module's existing 30-minute allocation**; they are not additional exams. They are source-open with the same independence rules.

| ID | Exact replacement task |
| --- | --- |
| U1B | A club can fund a promised celebratory meal or install captions enabling three members to follow its meetings. In 250 words distinguish facts, two normative premises, one verdict, and a metaethical question; explain the promise's relevance. |
| U2B | An inexperienced rescuer copies an expert’s risky maneuver without understanding the conditions and succeeds by luck. An observer infers that the rescuer has acquired courage. In 300 words distinguish doing an apparently appropriate act from possessing virtue; explain what knowledge, choice and stable disposition contribute, and how changed dangers could make the same maneuver excessive or deficient. Use Aristotle II.4 and II.6. |
| U3B | Read Mengzi **6A1**. Reconstruct the willow/cup analogy and its reply; compare one point with Aristotle II.1, without treating cultivation as the same process in both. 250–300 words, translator/locator required. |
| U4B | A person truthfully explains all terms of each exchange and never deceives anyone, but adopts the maxim “I will never help another person unless it benefits me.” They argue that honesty and consent alone fully satisfy respect for humanity. Give a candid maxim, test its universal willing, and assess the claim about humanity using Groundwork II. Distinguish a positive duty from a requirement to help on every occasion. 300 words. |
| U5B | A musician first practices to gain applause, but later values practicing even when no audience will ever hear it. A critic says that valuing this activity for itself disproves Mill’s claim about happiness as the end. Using Mill IV’s treatment of virtue and other acquired ends, reconstruct a possible reply, test whether it supplies an explanation or merely relabels the valued activity, and state a limit. 300 words. |
| U6B | A person installs and correctly uses a reliable safeguard to prevent an involuntary support connection. They know it has a small failure risk; it fails. A spectator infers that knowingly accepting a risk is the same as granting the resulting dependent person permission to use their body. Using Thomson’s precautions and people-seeds discussion, analyze the risk-to-permission inference and distinguish it from an explicit undertaking. Separately state why a metaethical account alone does not settle the resulting duty. 300 words. |
| U7B | Apply the relevant essay variant: Kant—full duration disclosed but a false claim that everyone else volunteered; Mill—each person desires universal happiness but also independently values fair treatment; Thomson—the original agreement to help was obtained through deception; library—both projects now prevent exclusion of the same number but one lasts ten years and the other one year. State which inference changes, a remaining objection, and one unsettled question. 250 words. |

If all suitable forms have been exposed, record “fresh evidence unavailable” until a verified new form is set. Do not erase exposure because the learner waited a week. Independent source checking and content remediation can occur afterward; they are not retroactively part of the untouched response.

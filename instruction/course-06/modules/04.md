# C06-M04 — Duty and autonomy

**18 hours.** Guiding question: can a principle of rational willing constrain a proposed action? Prerequisite: M01–03. Outcomes: distinguish acting from duty and merely in conformity with duty; formulate and test a maxim; explain autonomy; distinguish using a person as a means from using them merely as a means.

## Explanation

A good will does not derive its moral worth simply from producing a welcome result. An honest shopkeeper may act from self-interest or from respect for duty; the outward action alone does not settle the motive. This distinction is not a recommendation to dislike doing good. Nor does a poor outcome automatically erase the quality of a will. Separate action's permissibility, agent's motive, and foreseeable result before evaluating.

A maxim is the agent's practical principle: in circumstances C, I will do A for end E. A universal-law test asks whether the maxim can coherently be conceived and willed as a law for agents generally. For a deceitful promise, the proposed success relies on a practice of trust that universal adoption of the maxim would undermine. The alleged contradiction is in the proposed policy's conditions of success, not simply in the fact that bad consequences would occur. In other cases a world may be conceivable but incompatible with what a rational agent must will; distinguish this contradiction in will from contradiction in conception.

Maxim description matters. An opportunist can gerrymander a maxim by naming themselves or an arbitrary date; an assessor should ask which features explain the agent's reason. Conversely, replacing a concrete case with “always lie” may test a principle the agent never adopts. State the maxim first, check whether it honestly represents the action, then assess universalization. A disagreement about formulation may precede a disagreement about the test.

Humanity as an end concerns rational agency's standing. Ordinary cooperation uses others as means: paying a willing driver is not automatically wrong. Deception and coercion can undermine the person's ability to share or assess the proposed action's end. Actual consent is evidentially important but not a complete definition of every Kantian duty. Autonomy means rational self-legislation under a law valid for rational agents; it is not simply doing whatever one prefers. These claims explain why Kant's account differs from a welfare calculation even when their verdicts coincide.

## Worked example — invented loan

Mara needs money for a leisure purchase and plans to promise repayment while knowing she will never repay. Her maxim is: when I want money I cannot otherwise obtain, I will falsely promise repayment to obtain it. Generalizing that practical principle threatens the trusting acceptance on which the deceitful promise relies. If the practice persisted because people ignored the rule, that would change the stipulated universalization, not answer it. The humanity formulation separately asks how the lender is deprived of an informed choice. A reply might challenge the reconstruction of the maxim or the alleged dependence on trust; “but Mara enjoys the purchase” does not address either argument.

Practice a different case: a supervisor hides an assignment's tedious parts when asking for volunteers, although volunteers could refuse without punishment if told. Write a maxim, its universal counterpart, and a humanity analysis. Then change only the information: volunteers are fully informed. Identify which part of the earlier argument no longer applies and which further duties may remain.

P4: two 300-word tests of the supervisor case, followed by 200 words answering the strongest objection to one test. Add a source table for good will/duty (I), universal law (II), and humanity/autonomy (II). Explain if you defend different verdicts under different plausible maxims.

## Independent U4 — 30 minutes

A volunteer coordinator knowingly advertises a two-hour task that actually takes four hours because accurate notices attract too few helpers. Helpers can withdraw on discovering the real duration, but have already traveled. Analyze a candidly specified maxim with universal law and humanity, then test whether the right to withdraw answers the objection. Write 300 words, source-open, no live assistance. Criteria: honest maxim, identified reasoning step, distinction between consent and retrospective escape, source-grounded objection. A disagreement with Kant can meet every criterion.

Repair: put circumstance/action/end into separate cells before generalizing. If the objection attacks consequences, state whether that shows a contradiction or supplies another theory's criterion. After seven days, use 20 minutes of the retrieval hour to explain why a freely accepted paid task uses someone as a means without automatically using them merely as a means; check the passage in the remaining time.

# C06-M07 — Essay and revision

**22 hours.** Guiding question: which limited ethical conclusion survives its strongest relevant objection? Prerequisite: M01–06; additions if taking the augmented route. Outcomes: sustain a bounded thesis; support interpretation with actual primary passages; answer a serious objection; revise reasoning and explain the change.

## Choose one bounded project

Write **1,800–2,000 words**, excluding bibliography and the revision memo. Choose one of these source-bounded prompts:

- **Kant:** Can universal law identify the wrong in the deceptive-volunteer case in M04? Compare the original disclosure failure with the fully informed variant. Use *Groundwork* I–II, with particular attention to the universal-law and humanity formulations. Do not presume that a maxim can be selected just to produce your preferred verdict.
- **Mill:** Does the chapter IV proof justify utility as the ultimate end? Choose **one** pivotal transition—desire/desirability, individual/general happiness, or happiness as the sole end—and explain how chapter II constrains your interpretation.
- **Thomson:** Does the violinist argument establish that every refusal of bodily support is permissible? Use the whole assigned article, including at least one later qualification. Distinguish rebutting an inference from establishing a universal rival conclusion.
- **Two theories:** In an invented public-library case, officials have £3,000 for either a hearing loop serving twelve regular users or a general program serving 100 users. Stipulate that the loop uniquely enables those twelve to participate in existing events, that no comparable facility is reachable, that both projects cost the full amount, and that there is no binding prior promise. Compare **two** of Aristotle, Kant, and Mill; make one defensible recommendation and identify which stipulated fact matters most. Do not infer quality of welfare or equal cost of exclusion from headcounts alone.

Read only the assigned primary ranges needed for your chosen project; targeted rereading is in this module's hours. Extra research is optional and logged separately; it cannot substitute for a missing primary passage. No unverified quotations, invented page references, or claims about present law are required.

## Procedure and explanatory scaffold

First turn the prompt into a disputed inference. For example, “Does the failure of a personhood-to-entitlement inference establish unrestricted permission?” identifies a gap that a survey of abortion views would obscure. Write a one-sentence answer and one exclusion. A viable thesis can be negative or qualified: it might show that a proposed argument fails while leaving the first-order verdict unresolved. The burden is to explain that precise result.

Build a source dossier with **four to six passage entries**. Each entry contains edition, locator, your 60-word paraphrase, the argumentative role, a plausible rival reading, and what the passage cannot show. Check the whole paragraph around each citation. Select the strongest objection by asking which premise, inference, interpretation, or scope claim would most damage your thesis if false. An objection that merely appeals to another theory's conclusion needs a bridge showing why this challenges your argument.

Use a reverse outline: beside every drafted paragraph write its job in fewer than ten words. If three consecutive paragraphs only describe thinkers, add the inferential connection or remove material. A reply can accept an objection and narrow the thesis. This is often more defensible than insisting the first draft was wholly right. An essay comparing theories must show their different reasons even if they agree on the verdict.

## Worked draft-to-revision pair — invented learner work

**Draft:** “Thomson proves that anyone can disconnect because nobody has any right over another person's body. Therefore all cases are settled.”

**Diagnosis:** “Proves,” “anyone,” and “all” outrun a counterexample to a general inference. The premise also ignores the article's discussion of acquired claims and low-cost assistance.

**Revision:** “The nonconsensual violinist case challenges the inference from personhood and a right to life alone to an entitlement to this person's bodily support. It leaves open whether added facts about responsibility, a specific undertaking, or a minimal burden change the judgment. I will defend that limited counterexample while arguing that extending it to every refusal requires further premises.”

**Why it improves:** the revision specifies the inference and its quantifiers, separates the concession from the conclusion, and makes room for a serious objection. It still needs citations and a defended bridge to any new case. This model is practice; do not reuse it as assessed prose.

## Outputs and time

E7 includes a 250-word proposal, dossier, argument map, complete first draft, received feedback, revised essay, and a **300-word revision memo**. The memo identifies two substantive changes, quotes only your own before/after sentences, and explains one suggestion declined with a reason. Declare any AI, tutor, peer, or editorial assistance. The final independent evidence comes separately in Module 8.

Time: proposal/scope decision 2h; targeted reading/dossier 4h; outline 2h; first draft 5h; feedback and objection analysis 2h; revision 4h; citation/assistance audit and memo 2h; U7 0.5h; delayed retrieval 0.5h = **22h**.

## Independent U7 — 30 minutes

Without live help, write a 250-word response to the applicable changed premise: Kant—helpers now know the true duration before agreeing but the coordinator falsely claims participation is compulsory; Mill—the desire is stable and fully informed but only concerns the agent's own happiness; Thomson—the helper explicitly promised one hour of support before the dependent person declined another available helper; library—each project now serves the same number, but one prevents exclusion and the other adds enjoyment. Identify which part of your essay survives, which must change, and one question the new fact does not settle. The assessor may ask for an accessible written response instead of a conversation; no answer hints during the attempt.

Repair by revising the narrowest failing inference before editing style. After seven days, use 30 minutes of this module's retrieval allocation to recreate your argument map without the essay and compare it against the saved version. Next: [Module 8](08.md).

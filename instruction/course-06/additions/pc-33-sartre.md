# PC-33 — Sartre: freedom, responsibility, and ethical choice

**8 added hours; replaces 0; net +8 to C06.** Prerequisite: Modules 1–6. Guiding question: what follows for responsibility if no antecedent human essence chooses our life for us? Outcomes: reconstruct a freedom/responsibility argument, distinguish a method from a decision formula, challenge one inference, and apply it to a changed situation.

## Teaching explanation

The lecture contrasts manufactured objects with human self-definition through action. Sartre links absence of a predetermined essence to responsibility and claims that choosing presents an image of humanity. That move to broader responsibility needs an argument; it is not the claim that everyone will literally copy an act. Anguish concerns responsibility, abandonment the absence of an external guarantee, and despair limits reliance on outcomes beyond one's control. The student facing incompatible commitments illustrates choice where general advice underdetermines the decision. It does not show that facts or other people's freedom are irrelevant. Compare Kant's test of a maxim with this account of situated commitment; shared language about universality does not make the methods identical.

To reconstruct, write a claim about the human situation, a claim about choice, a proposed responsibility conclusion, and the linking premise. Then distinguish a textual explanation from your endorsement. A criticism can accept the importance of owning one's choices while questioning the link to an image valid for others. Another can ask how oppressive constraints affect available alternatives. Neither requires treating the agent as omnipotent.

## Worked example — invented commitment

Noor has promised to finish a community archive and is offered a temporary job supporting a sibling. Both commitments matter; no option satisfies them fully. “My personality forced me” would need explanation: does it describe a constraint or evade giving reasons for a choice? “There was no cost-free option” is different from “there was no choice at all.” A reconstruction should identify actual alternatives, commitment, consequences within control, and the reason Noor gives. It should not diagnose every reluctant person as self-deceiving or blame someone for an unavailable option.

P33 practice: choose one response—continue the archive, accept the job, or negotiate a reduced archive role. In 250 words explain how the reasons present a conception of responsible action; in another 150 state an objection to that presentation. Compare with a Kantian analysis of keeping the promise in 200 words. This is philosophical analysis of an invented situation, not personal counseling.

## Independent forms

**A33 — 30 minutes, source-open, no live help or examiner notes:** Elin accepted an unpaid coordinating role. Her employer now offers paid weekend work needed to support a dependent, while the group can find a replacement only by canceling one event. Elin says either “the title of coordinator determines what I must do” or “because no rule settles everything, no justification is needed.” In 300 words, evaluate both claims using the lecture, reconstruct one freedom/responsibility inference, and compare the kind of question a Kantian analysis would ask. A reasoned verdict may favor either available action; show its limits.

**B33 alternate — 30 minutes:** Ivo is asked to sign a misleading public statement to retain a position funding an important local project. Refusal risks ending that project; another funding source may exist but is uncertain. Analyze the claim that “my role chose for me,” distinguish available action from guaranteed success, and compare a Kantian maxim analysis. State which uncertainty matters. Use if A33 was rehearsed.

**D33 — 15 minutes, at least seven days later:** A researcher says a committee's endorsement eliminates their personal responsibility for a consequential choice. In 180 words, reconstruct a lecture-based objection, name a limit on that objection, and contrast it with a duty-based argument. If exposed, use a different case: a student claims a career recommendation removes all responsibility for choosing it.

Criteria: accurate source-located reconstruction, reasoned handling of constraints, comparison of methods, and a real objection. Assessor anchors are separate. Repair by separating “unable,” “costly,” and “not justified”; then recheck the cited paragraph. Next: [Module 7](../modules/07.md); retain the R33 dossier for the optional Course 12C route.

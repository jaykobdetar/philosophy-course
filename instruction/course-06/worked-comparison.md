# Worked comparison progression — original invented learner work

These short constructed samples are teaching examples, not real learner evidence and not answers to a final assessment form. They illustrate the shared developing/competent/strong anchors. Their judgments concern reasoning; another well-supported verdict could also succeed.

**Invented practice case:** A reading group promised its facilitator a £100 honorarium. It can now spend the same £100 on snacks that twenty attendees would enjoy. The facilitator prepared in reliance on the promise, needs the money for ordinary expenses, and has not agreed to a change. There is no emergency or further money. Compare Kant and Mill.

## Developing sample

“Kant thinks rules matter, so pay the facilitator. Mill thinks happiness matters, so buy snacks because twenty people beat one. They disagree because one is strict and the other cares about people.”

**Annotation:** Two relevant theories are named, but headcount substitutes for welfare reasoning and “strict” substitutes for a maxim test. The account caricatures both authors. It supplies no reason why promised payment matters to happiness or rational agency, and it assumes disagreement from labels. Repair the strongest missing inference before improving style.

## Competent sample

“Under a maxim permitting cancellation of a promised fee whenever another use pleases more people, the group's reliance on trustworthy commitments needs scrutiny. A Kantian argument also asks whether withholding payment defeats the facilitator's informed participation in the arrangement. Mill's standard asks about the happiness of everyone affected. The facilitator's loss, the preparatory reliance, and damage to trust count alongside enjoyment of snacks; twenty recipients alone do not establish the better outcome. Both accounts can therefore support payment on these facts, for different reasons. If the facilitator freely waived the fee, the Kantian description would change and the welfare comparison would also need updating.”

**Annotation:** Principles connect to stipulated facts, the shared verdict is permitted, and the changed consent condition is identified. A full submission still needs precise assigned-text citations and a more developed universalization argument. The response does not claim every broken promise is automatically the same case.

## Strong sample

“The strongest case for snacks is that the fund should benefit the whole group. That claim needs both an impartial welfare estimate and a defense of overriding a voluntary commitment; popularity alone supplies neither. On a Kantian reconstruction, the candid maxim must identify why cancellation is proposed, rather than encode this facilitator's identity to evade generalization. Its acceptability turns on whether agents could will the relevant policy and whether the facilitator's agency is respected. On a Millian reconstruction, payment can be justified by the seriousness of the loss and the effects of reliable agreements even though snacks produce more instances of enjoyment. Yet a welfare analysis is conditional on those estimates; an exceptional alternative could change it. Thus agreement here does not erase the different justificatory routes.”

**Annotation:** The objection targets the actual tradeoff, the proposed maxim cannot be manipulated freely, uncertainty is localized, and agreement is distinguished from theoretical identity. This remains a model fragment, not a claim that the complete source argument has been proved. The missing precise citations must be supplied from texts actually read in a real essay.

## Draft → revision exercise

Draft: “Twenty people enjoy snacks, so Mill must choose snacks.”

Revision: “Twenty people would enjoy snacks, but the comparison also includes the facilitator's material loss and effects on trust. Without estimating those consequences, the headcount does not establish which option better promotes happiness.”

The change repairs an inference, not merely phrasing. It does not prove that payment maximizes happiness; it makes the conclusion proportionate to the evidence. Now write your own 120-word revision of the developing sample's Kant sentence, citing the assigned maxim or humanity passage. Discuss whether the revised reason could survive the facilitator's informed waiver. This is practice inside M04/M05 or M07's existing practice/revision time, not a new allocation.

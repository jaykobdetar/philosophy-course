# C05-M02 — God, error, and the world (16 hours)

**Question:** Can a proof establish the reliability of the reasoning used to produce it? Prerequisite: P1 dependency map. Outcomes: reconstruct the error account; separate two proofs of God and the bodily-world argument; formulate a serious circle objection and a qualified reply.

**Source:** Descartes, *Meditations* IV–VI, CSM vol. II (Cambridge, 1984), with III available for cross-reference. Read all three meditations, including VI's discussion of embodied error. Scholarly edition remains unmatched; Veitch is an explicitly different provisional translation ([audit](../source-audit.md)). Do not use an adaptation's wording as a CSM quotation.

**Budget:** reading 5h; practice 4h; feedback 1.5h; independent I2 1h; revision 3.5h; delayed D2 1h = 16h.

## Teach the reasoning

Meditation IV distinguishes limited understanding from the will's broader capacity to affirm or deny. Error arises when assent outruns what is clearly understood. A finite faculty need not be defective merely because it is finite; misuse explains error without making God a deceiver. This account must still explain why a reliable creator permits a creature liable to misuse. Descartes appeals to the whole creation and our limited perspective, while identifying suspension of judgment as a remedy within our power. Do not treat the remedy as a proof that every involuntary sensation is an error of choice.

Meditation V's argument proceeds from God's essence as supremely perfect to necessary existence. It differs from III's causal argument from the idea. The disputable bridge is whether existence can follow from the relevant kind of essence and whether the idea has the right status. “I define a perfect island” is an objection only after explaining why the parity is relevant; a conditional description of an island and necessary divine existence may differ in ways the defender must justify.

Meditation VI distinguishes understanding from imagination and argues for a real distinction of mind and body through their clear and distinct conceivability and God's power to create them separately. Mere failure to notice a bodily feature would be weaker than a clear conception excluding it. The existence of bodies depends on involuntary sensory ideas, an inclination to attribute them to bodies, and the nondeceiving creator. This establishes less than the truth of every sensory appearance: mathematical features of bodies and the practical role of sensations must be distinguished. Thirst in illness helps expose how generally beneficial bodily signaling can mislead in a particular condition.

The circle objection is a dependency problem: if the proof of a nondeceiving God uses clear and distinct cognition, while trust in that cognition depends on God, what independently secures the proof? A possible reply distinguishes what is evident while presently attended to from the standing guarantee needed when an argument is remembered. The critic then asks whether a multistep proof can be wholly attended to at once, and whether merely irresistible conviction is sufficient for knowledge. Do not resolve the dispute by saying that Descartes “believes in logic”: specify which premises require which level of assurance. These are interpretive options, not a universally settled verdict.

## Worked practice and output P2

Invented analogy: a gauge certifies a laboratory's calibration procedure; that procedure certifies the same gauge. Agreement between them alone cannot supply an independent check. A defender adds a directly observable reference mark. The objection now asks whether the mark is independent and whether it supports every required calibration step. This models the structure of the circle and a reply, but says nothing by itself about whether Cartesian clear perception has the status of that mark.

Draw two dependency diagrams: one for the strongest circular reading, one for the present-attention reply. Mark III's causal premise, God's nondeception, the general truth rule, and VI's world inference. Write **700 words**: 200 reconstruction, 200 objection, 200 reply, 100 residual difficulty. Add a 150-word IV/VI note distinguishing voluntary assent from bodily misdirection. Criteria: no conflation of proof variants; a reply that actually changes the alleged dependency; two exact primary locators; explicitly limited conclusion. Tutor asks what the argument can know before God's existence is established.

## Independent I2 (60 minutes)

Primary text open; no practice notes, AI, live content help or examiner notes. Invented claim: “If a reliable creator made our senses, then the thirst of a sick person proves drinking is beneficial; otherwise the creator deceives.” Use IV and VI to test the inference in 300–400 words. Identify two distinctions that matter and one difficulty that survives the reply. Neutral criteria: faithful account, explicit inference, correct passage roles, limitation rather than a slogan. Record exposure.

## Repair, retrieval, next step

If a reply merely repeats the conclusion, mark which dependency arrow it removes. If it does not remove one, call it a restatement. Reconstruct one IV error and one VI embodied error separately before combining them.

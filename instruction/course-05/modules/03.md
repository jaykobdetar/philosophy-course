# C05-M03 — Embodiment and interaction (12 hours)

**Question:** What would count as answering Elisabeth's question about mental causation? Prerequisites: Meditations II and VI. Outcomes: reconstruct the interaction challenge; trace changes across five letters; compare a causal explanation with an account of how union is understood.

**Source:** Elisabeth–Descartes correspondence, **6 May, 21 May, 10 June, 28 June and 1 July 1643**, Lisa Shapiro, *The Correspondence between Princess Elisabeth of Bohemia and René Descartes* (Chicago, 2007). All five letters are required. The inspected [Bennett reading aid](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/descartes1643.pdf) has these date headings but modernizes, omits and adds explanation; it does not verify Shapiro's exact wording or completeness. Use date/sender plus actual edition page, and label any provisional aid quotation.

**Budget:** reading 3h; mapping/practice 3h; feedback 1h; independent I3 1h; revision 3h; delayed D3 1h = 12h.

## Teach the reasoning

Elisabeth's question concerns the soul's ability to determine bodily motion if its defining nature is thought without extension. The explanatory resources familiar from motion—contact, shape and the relevant bodily determination—appear unavailable to an unextended entity. The issue is not simply whether people experience intending and moving; it is whether the asserted interaction is intelligible under Descartes's own commitments. Reconstruct the contact-related assumptions charitably, then ask whether they are necessary conditions of all causation or assumptions drawn from bodily causation.

Descartes's reply appeals to primitive notions of thought, extension and union. A primitive notion is not defined through a different primitive category: trying to understand union through extension alone may misapply an otherwise legitimate standard. His weight analogy concerns how people conceive a quality as moving a body, not an endorsement of a physics of real qualities. A useful analogy can illuminate an existing conception without establishing the truth of its old physical explanation. Elisabeth can still ask whether this provides the explanation she sought.

Across the exchange, the question moves from how interaction is possible to whether distinctness and union can both be conceived adequately, and what kind of cognition fits each. Descartes assigns a role to lived sensory experience in grasping union. Elisabeth treats bodily dependence as a problem that metaphysical assurances have not dissolved. Keep the philosophical challenge visible through the letters' respectful conventions; politeness does not establish agreement. Avoid diagnosing the writers or treating a gendered stereotype as philosophical explanation.

Two defensible evaluations remain open. On a charitable reading, the primitive-union reply rejects an unjustified demand that all causation fit a mechanical model. On a critical reading, it accounts for our familiar awareness of interaction while leaving its compatibility with real distinction unexplained. To decide between them, specify the question Elisabeth actually asks and test the reply against that target. Saying merely that modern neuroscience favors a view does not reconstruct this exchange.

## Worked practice and output P3

Invented practice: Niko asks how a key opens a lock; Ada replies, “You know opening through everyday experience.” Ada answers how Niko recognizes an event, but may not explain its mechanism. This is a diagnostic analogy, not proof that every causal explanation must be mechanical. If Ada argues that Niko's demanded mechanism belongs to the wrong explanatory category, the disagreement changes. State what further reason would support that objection to the demand.

Create a five-row correspondence map with columns date/sender, explicit question, proposed reply, target premise, and unresolved point. For 1 July, distinguish acknowledgment of an explanation from resolution of every difficulty. Write **500 words** answering whether Descartes addresses Elisabeth's original question or a revised question. Use one observation from Elisabeth and one from Descartes, with exact dates and actual pages. Add a 100-word steelman of the reading you reject. Criteria: sequence, attribution, target matching, textual reason for the verdict. No credit for reducing Elisabeth to a request for a location such as the pineal gland.

## Independent I3 (60 minutes)

Primary letters open, with no notes, AI, tutor hints or examiner guidance. Invented interpretation: “The 28 June appeal to ordinary life settles the 6 May objection because anyone who can move an arm understands every relevant sense of 'how'.” In 300–400 words explain one defensible reading of this claim and one challenge to it. Cite both letters and use either 10 June or 1 July as a test. Criteria: distinct explanatory demands, honest attribution, justified judgment. Record exposure.

## Repair, retrieval, next step

If you conflate understanding an experience with explaining its possibility, rewrite the question in both forms and state the evidence each needs. If the letters become an inventory, draw arrows from each reply to the premise it targets. D3 after 7–14 days: 15-minute closed-note reconstruction of the primitive-notion reply; 15-minute source check; 30-minute revised 150-word evaluation. Next: Spinoza I definitions 3–5 and Leibniz §§1–3, asking whether their accounts of substance change the problem itself.

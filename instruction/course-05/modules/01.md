# C05-M01 — Doubt, the thinker, and representation (12 hours)

**Question:** What survives doubt, and how far does that survival take us? Prerequisites: C01 reconstruction and its earlier reading of Meditations I–II; C03 distinction between an argument and its dramatic presentation. Outcomes: distinguish three levels of doubt; reconstruct the thinker claim without importing a body; explain objective/formal reality in the causal argument; identify one disputed bridge.

## Teach the reasoning

Doubt is a method of withholding assent from a belief whose grounds can be challenged. It need not assert that the belief is false. Sensory error initially targets particular circumstances. The dream argument threatens confidence that this apparent setting is real; it does not by itself erase every simple component of thought. The powerful-deceiver hypothesis extends the challenge to apparently obvious reasoning. Record what each move targets before asking whether it succeeds. Treating all three as a single assertion that nothing exists loses the structure.

In Meditation II, entertaining deception is itself an occurrence of thought. Denying that the thinker exists while entertaining that denial defeats itself on this occasion. This secures existence in the act of thinking. It does not yet establish continued existence during dreamless sleep, bodily existence, or the later real distinction of mind and body. Whether this is best rendered as an inference or as immediate recognition is interpretively live; either account must explain why the doubt does not undermine this occurrence. The wax discussion then distinguishes changing sensory presentations from intellectual judgment of a thing as extended and changeable. It does not establish that sensation contributes nothing to ordinary cognition.

Meditation III introduces ideas as representations. An idea considered simply as a mental occurrence has formal reality; its objective reality concerns what it represents. Here “objective” does not mean unbiased. The causal-adequacy principle says the cause must contain at least the relevant reality found in its effect, formally or eminently. Descartes applies this to the represented infinite perfection of God: a finite thinker cannot be its adequate total cause, so such an idea points to an actually existing adequate cause. Objections can challenge the reality hierarchy, the application of causal adequacy to representation, or the claim that the infinite idea is not constructed from finite materials. Naming one of these is more precise than saying “he assumes God.” A modern information-processing repair is your proposal unless the passage supports it.

## Worked practice and output P1

Invented case: Leila dreams of a red door and thinks, “The door might be unreal, so my thinking might be unreal.” Separate (1) the represented door, (2) the occurrence of seeming to see, (3) the inference that the occurrence requires a thinker. The dream possibility defeats the first claim's current warrant without doing the same to the second. This does not prove the thinker is nonphysical. The model works by restricting the conclusion, not by proving every later Cartesian claim.

Make a six-node map: ordinary perception; dream doubt; deceiver doubt; thinker; idea of infinity; adequate cause. Give each arrow a premise and mark assumptions not yet secured. Then write **500 words** on one of two contested transitions: thinker to thinking thing, or idea of infinity to adequate cause. Identify two passage locations, state a rival reading, and distinguish exposition from criticism. Criteria: accurate local conclusion, explicit bridge, no premature world/dualism claim, and traceable attribution. Feedback targets the weakest arrow; revise that arrow and its explanatory paragraph.

## Independent I1 (source-open, 60 minutes)

Do not open examiner guidance or reuse a coached paragraph. Allowed: the assigned primary text, your edition record, blank paper; no notes, AI, live hints or model answers. Invented claim: “Because the meditator cannot doubt that thinking occurs now, he has already shown in Meditation II that the same incorporeal person exists at every time.” In 300–400 words diagnose the transition, reconstruct what the passage does secure, and state what further argument the stronger claim would need. Criteria: passage role, premise/conclusion separation, justified limit, edition locator. Declare prior exposure and assistance.

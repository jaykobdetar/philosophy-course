# Course 12 study, access and evidence protocol

These four options retain their historical scope and budgets. Authoring completes the teaching design; it does not establish learner readiness, a human expert review, source permission or rendered accessibility. Select one option unless deliberately undertaking more. All cases described as invented are original teaching hypotheticals, not reports of actual people or institutions.

## Begin a module

Record course/module ID, actual source title, translator/edition, assigned range, reading route, access status and time available. Check the source-audit row. A catalogue record establishes a bibliographic target, not a usable copy. Obtain an existing lawful copy or library access; no purchase, permission change or full-text redistribution is authorized by this packet. Where access is unresolved, complete conceptual exercises provisionally but keep passage-dependent fidelity/quotation criteria open.

Read in three passes: identify the author's question and target; reconstruct the argument or descriptive move; test one inference with a changed case. For every important attribution record the actual edition and locator plus a short private paraphrase. Reprint pages, journal pages, canonical sections and PDF indices are distinct. Never cite pages from an edition you did not read. A reasonable crosswalk needs checked endpoints and passage correspondence, not a constant guessed offset.

Audio-first study uses the same reading hours. Pause to dictate a premise map or description; use the written text to check quotations and locators. If no authorized audio exists, text-to-speech of a lawfully accessed copy is an option where permitted; do not assume that an audiobook uses the assigned translation. Written or dictated work and an asynchronous written exchange can replace speech. Record which medium supplied evidence, especially for art. No sensory exercise is compulsory: an imagined ordinary case, clearly labeled, supports conceptual analysis but not an experiential observation claim.

## Evidence and assistance

P denotes supported practice, I an independent module changed case, F a final fresh form, A its alternate, and D delayed retrieval/transfer. Prefix each with the course ID when storing artifacts. Keep draft, feedback, revision and assistance/exposure log. A model may explain a distinct worked example and identify a limited problem after seeing your attempt; it may not author your assessed argument. Source-based criticism requires actual source access or an explicitly provisional status.

Before independent work, record whether the question or examiner anchors were seen. Assigned texts and access aids are allowed; live philosophical help, generated answers and task-specific hints are not. Freeze the response before feedback. Exposed final forms use the named alternate. For a nonessential module subskill whose I form is exposed, the assessor changes both the surface case and the pivotal variable from an unused exercise in the module, records the new prompt and confirms it preserves the same criterion; if no genuinely fresh task is available, leave independence unresolved rather than calling rehearsal independent. Every essential group already has concrete F/A forms.

## Time and overlap

Module allocations include reading, teaching/practice, feedback, independent work, revision and retrieval. Later retrieval is charged to its original module once. The final delayed task takes place four to six weeks later or after two subsequent modules. Earlier seven-day retrieval does not replace it. Actual time beyond an allocation is logged transparently.

PC-28 foundation = the entire twenty-hour C12A-M01. Non-12A routes add it once before making the common-core language claim; C12A already contains it. If a learner later takes C12A, retained satisfactory P01 plus A-M01 I01 or the bridge’s own F-FR/A-FR, with source/exposure records credit the full twenty-hour module once. A fresh diagnostic may establish what repair is needed; it cannot magically recover missing source work. The Grice packet adds four hours on every route, once across multiple options.

PC-29 adds ten hours to C12A: five Smart and five Lewis. It replaces zero baseline hours and revises the provisional six-hour seed to match these finite reading and assessment tasks. Place is optional and unassigned; selecting it would require a new explicit budget.

PC-33 belongs to Course 6: its eight-hour Sartre encounter can credit four of C12C-M05's twenty-two hours only with retained lecture reading/conceptual reconstruction evidence and compatible source scope. Eighteen new hours remain, including Macomber wording/context verification and Beauvoir comparison. Mairet and Macomber are not interchangeable citation sources. Record the four-hour credit on the learner ledger; do not deduct the full eight hours or bill the Macomber recheck as completed in Course 6.

## Readiness decision

Use the observable rubric in current/assessment.md. For every essential group record supported practice and independent evidence; repair unresolved groups rather than averaging. The essay assesses sustained reasoning and revision, the exchange responsiveness, and the delayed task transfer. None supplies missing source fidelity by inference. Record tutor calibration, learner pilot, expert review, rights and rendered accessibility as separate statuses; all remain unclaimed here.

## Assessment reservation after substantive repair

Use the named final alternate only for a genuine fresh attempt or targeted recheck; do not consume it during the oral/written explanation. The neutral exchange asks the learner to identify and vary their own pivotal premise. Course 12B’s finite artwork allocation is binding for coached practice, essays and independent stages. For a non-12A foundation, use its own Frege/Russell-only assessment substitutions inside the shared module’s hours. Essay equivalents are approved only when they map to an existing subject-matched challenge branch; question approval is not permission to improvise an unrelated assessment later.

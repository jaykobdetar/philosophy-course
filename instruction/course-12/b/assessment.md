# C12B assessment forms

Use three fresh tasks, **30 minutes each**, within the final module’s ninety-minute written allocation. Each response is approximately 300–450 words, with formulas/diagrams where relevant. The assessor selects F or an unexposed A form before the attempt; do not practice these forms. Delayed D tasks use twenty-five minutes plus ten for citation review, four to six weeks later or after two later modules, charged once to Course 12. Select the least secure row; all three delayed alternatives are provided so the choice follows evidence.

Permitted: assigned texts, ordinary dictionary, text-to-speech, speech-to-text and access support. Not permitted: live content advice, model-generated reasoning, coached revision during the attempt or examiner anchors. Record source access, exposure, assistance and actual time. When a passage is unavailable, the interpretation criterion remains unresolved even if the conceptual case is answered well.

Learner criteria for every form: address the precise question; reconstruct the assigned view fairly; explain the inference and its limitation; use checked locators; distinguish your view from the source. No philosophical or political verdict is prescribed.

## 1. Taste

F1: A highly practiced critic cannot inspect a newly discovered work but accepts a trusted colleague’s testimony that it is beautiful. The critic then says “I have judged its beauty myself, and everyone ought to agree.” Compare what Hume’s qualifications and Kant’s account of taste license here. Distinguish warranted trust in another judgment from the grounds of one’s own judgment; do not assume that agreement or expertise alone resolves the difference.

A1: Two practiced listeners disagree after one develops a personal dislike of the composer. The other insists beauty can be proved from a formula. Compare the problems for a Humean standard and Kantian taste.

D1: A novice and an experienced critic agree that a painting is beautiful, but only because it depicts their hometown. Explain why agreement alone does not establish the relevant Humean or Kantian warrant.

## 2. Art classification

F2: A rule proposed in year one defines art as requiring a human maker’s deliberate plan. In year two a storm accidentally generates a pattern that an artist incorporates into a deliberately composed public work. A critic says that accepting the later work retroactively makes the rule a correct definition of every earlier artwork. Analyze the treatment of accident, artistic identification and historical concept extension under Weitz and Danto. Explain whether a useful rule for an existing practice is thereby an exceptionless definition; no pair of visually identical objects is stipulated.

A2: A community treats a repeated maintenance action as a commemorative performance after an artist proposes the interpretation. Evaluate its classification under an open-concept account and an artworld account, naming needed evidence rather than assuming the title settles it.

D2: A convincing imitation of a portrait is displayed as an exercise rather than an independent artwork. Does visual resemblance determine classification? Apply one assigned account and state a limit.

## 3. Categories and interpretation

F3: Use Stubbs’s Whistlejacket, NG6569, only now, with its museum record. Stipulate a viewing experiment, not a historical claim: one viewer has been trained to expect horses in narrative scenes with landscape, another to attend to individual animals in portraits. Both see the same full reproduction but describe its empty setting differently. Use Walton to separate evidence that training changes aesthetic experience from evidence that either category is correct for this actual work. Identify one feature’s different role and use the documented commission/context to assess the categories; neither reported reaction settles correctness.

A3: Use Turner’s The Fighting Temeraire, NG524, only now, with its museum record. The National Gallery reports that the actual vessel had been stripped of its masts before towing, while the painting shows three lower masts. A learner proposes two criteria: a marine picture should document the final journey accurately, whereas a commemorative picture can preserve features that express the vessel’s history. Assess whether this documented discrepancy must be an aesthetic defect under both categories. Identify the feature’s category-relative role, evaluate the contextual support for each category, and distinguish the museum’s historical evidence from your aesthetic conclusion.

D3: Use Monet’s The Water-Lily Pond, NG4240, reserved until this delayed task. The museum record identifies the full 1899 oil painting and its garden context. Stipulate that an online thumbnail crops away the bridge and margins and is labeled “abstract color study”; these are hypothetical presentation changes, not new historical facts. Explain why a category apt for the cropped image need not be apt for the full work. Compare the available evidence before and after consulting the full record, identify a changed standard/variable feature, and state one judgment the thumbnail cannot support.

## Passage selection and explanation

For any required unseen passage, the assessor uses the exact assigned edition and selects the first eligible contiguous paragraph span of 120–180 words in the specified section that was not annotated in practice, quoted in the paper or used in a previous assessment. Preserve necessary context, include the preceding paragraph as source-open context if needed, and record beginning/end words privately with edition and page/section. If no span is eligible, use the named alternate text; if that is also unavailable or exposed, the source-dependent task stays pending. Do not fabricate a passage or silently change translation. The selection and response stay in the local learner workspace, not in the distributable course.

For A/B/D, add a fresh 100–150-word span from the assigned primary reading least used in the essay to the twenty-minute explanation: five minutes to identify the claim and argumentative role; ten minutes to answer the neutral question, “Which premise of your response is least secure, and what change in that premise would alter your conclusion?”; five minutes to defend or revise the response. The learner specifies the change and explains its effect without live content help. The assessor may ask for clarification of that stated inference, but **must not reveal or borrow a case from the reserved alternate or delayed forms**. For C the fresh passage is already in F2/A2, so use the same neutral exchange to test the method’s warrant. A written exchange is equivalent. The essay’s separate subject-matched independent task already supplies a concrete changed-case defense.

## Feedback and completion record

For each row record: criterion; evidence in P and F/A; actual source support; uncertainty; one repair; result of fresh recheck if needed. Mark each row supported, unresolved or needs repair. Essential conceptual confusion, fabricated citation or absent independent evidence cannot be averaged away. After the delayed task, explain whether the least secure distinction transferred; a failed delayed task triggers targeted repair and a new unexposed case. Time beyond the planned repair allowance is logged as actual additional study, not silently hidden.

# C12B-M01 — Art, imitation, and evidence

Baseline allocation: **12 hours**. Prerequisites: Courses 1, 6 and Course 9 Kant vocabulary; prior modules in this option.

## Guiding question and outcomes

What is the target of the argument against imitation?

By the end: Reconstruct an epistemic and an ethical criticism of imitation; distinguish artwork evidence from claims about its original audience.

## Teaching explanation

Plato’s criticism does not merely assert that copies are unattractive. It places imitation within an account of knowledge and the soul: a maker of appearances may represent an object without the expertise of its user, and an audience may respond to an appearance in ways that bypass reasoned judgment. The distance between an image and what it presents is thus connected to an argument about epistemic authority, and the treatment of poetry introduces a further concern about how spectators cultivate their responses. These are related claims, not one interchangeable objection.

Reconstruct the target before testing it. A criticism of imitative poetry’s authority does not straightforwardly classify every modern installation, nor does calling a painting beautiful answer an argument about knowledge. A counterexample should identify a work that supplies understanding by means of representation and explain why Plato’s premises fail there. Evidence matters: what a reproduction shows, the work’s recorded medium and a disputed interpretation of its purpose have different evidential status. Record uncertainty about symbolism and original function rather than letting a famous title settle them.

## Worked practice example

Invented example: a painted emergency-exit sign accurately depicts a door but cannot tell a visitor whether the real door is locked. Accuracy of appearance does not give the painter the practical knowledge of a safety inspector. Yet the image might help locate the exit; that weaker cognitive benefit needs separate consideration.

Criteria: answer the stated question, make the key distinction explicit, connect reasons to the conclusion, and identify the actual source of attributed claims. Label invented examples. Keep your first attempt and help log.

## Independent changed case

I01: An invented documentary combines accurate footage with a fictional narrator who invites admiration for a reckless act. Analyze separately its claim to knowledge and its possible effect on judgment. State what further evidence would be needed to apply the latter criticism. 350 words.

Use assigned sources and ordinary access aids, but no live content help, model answers or examiner notes. Declare prior exposure. Neutral criteria: faithful use of the assigned concepts; a justified inference; an identified limitation; traceable attribution. Freeze the response before feedback. If exposed, use the relevant alternate in assessment.md, or the examiner's unused changed-case procedure for a nonessential subskill.

## Teaching explanation

Luck egalitarian approaches seek to distinguish disadvantages due to brute luck from outcomes for which people can appropriately be held responsible. Anderson’s criticism asks what happens when a theory makes assistance depend on intrusive judgments of fault or represents recipients as deficient. The issue is not merely the amount distributed but the relations expressed and sustained by the policy.

Democratic equality centers conditions under which people can function as equal citizens and avoid oppressive social relations. Material resources remain relevant because relations of equality require capabilities and institutions; relational equality is not the view that respectful words make distribution irrelevant. A fair comparison should reconstruct a serious responsibility-sensitive rival, not presume it endorses every cruel administrative practice. An objection can ask how the alternative specifies thresholds, handles costly choices or balances claims. The reply must explain how its conception of equal standing bears on that case. Keep responsibility, desert, need and civic status distinct: an institution can require some responsible conduct without denying someone standing as an equal.

## Worked practice example

Invented example: a mobility grant requires applicants to describe themselves as inferior and prove that no choice contributed to their condition. Even if the grant increases income equality, its procedure may undermine equal standing. A respectful application is not alone enough either if the grant leaves transport inaccessible.

# C12D-M02 — Feminist method and difference

Baseline allocation: **18 hours**. Prerequisites: Courses 6–8; preferably Course 11B; prior modules in this option.

## Guiding question and outcomes

Whose experience becomes the starting point of a movement’s theory?

By the end: Reconstruct a critique of representativeness and audience; explain a methodological implication; avoid treating one author as all feminist philosophy.

## Teaching explanation

The opening chapters challenge a movement’s tendency to take the circumstances of comparatively privileged women as the condition of women in general. A claim can speak in universal language while its examples, audience and proposed solutions presuppose a narrower social location. The methodological question is not solved by adding a ceremonial reference to difference: one must ask how the standpoint changes the problem definition and the institutions proposed for transformation.

Distinguish an analysis of sexist oppression from the claim that all women experience it identically. Also distinguish criticizing leadership or a political framework from dismissing everyone who has participated in a movement. A reconstruction should identify the specific generalization being challenged and the alternative account of political purpose. Do not treat identity as automatic epistemic infallibility; the task is to explain what an excluded experience reveals about a model’s limits. A good objection might question the practical implications or a proposed unifying account while acknowledging the problem of exclusion. Read chapters 1–2 in their historical setting and identify when your own contemporary application goes beyond their direct claims.

## Worked practice example

Invented example: a campaign defines liberation solely as entry into high-status paid work, while cleaners at its meetings have always worked for wages and lack control over working conditions. Their situation reveals that access to paid work cannot by itself describe the common political goal.

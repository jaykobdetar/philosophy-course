## Teaching explanation

Young’s five faces identify different mechanisms and experiences: exploitation, marginalization, powerlessness, cultural imperialism and violence. They are not five interchangeable names for a bad outcome. Exploitation concerns systematic transfer or appropriation of benefits from one group’s activity; marginalization concerns exclusion from useful or recognized participation; powerlessness concerns authority and status within organized work and social relations. Cultural imperialism concerns whose experience is treated as universal and how others are marked or rendered invisible. Violence concerns patterned vulnerability and the social conditions that enable it.

A structural explanation traces rules, routines, norms and positions that repeatedly produce a disadvantage, even where no individual intends the entire pattern. This does not imply individuals never act culpably. It changes the scale of explanation and often the kind of remedy needed. The concepts require evidence: an unequal result alone may not show exploitation, and a single insulting encounter alone may not establish a whole institutional mechanism. An invented case can stipulate mechanisms for analysis. In a documented case, separate established facts, plausible inference and missing data; do not convert a philosophical label into a substitute for investigation.

## Worked practice example

Invented example: contractors clean a university at night, have no say in safety rules, and cannot attend meetings at which their working conditions are decided. Lack of authority supports a powerlessness analysis; exclusion from the decision process supplies a related mechanism. Exploitation needs additional facts about the transfer of benefits and labor relations, not just the word “contractor.”

# C12A-M06 — Integrative workshop

Baseline allocation: **10 hours**. Prerequisites: Courses 1, 2 first-order language/semantics, 5 and 7; prior modules in this option (except M01, also usable as the PC-28 foundation).

## Guiding question and outcomes

Which premise really connects language performance with mental attribution?

By the end: Build a two-view debate map; connect topics without conflating semantic, epistemic and metaphysical questions; select a tractable essay claim.

## Assigned reading and access

Revisit only the assigned ranges in Modules 1–5. No new unbounded reading. The source passages used in the 1,000-word map must be independently located in the actual edition.

Consult [source audit](../source-audit.md) before making textual judgments. Reading access is distinct from bibliographic identification; original teaching below is not a substitute for the primary assignment. Audio reading is welcome, but retain a written edition for exact locators. Follow [study and access procedures](../../study-protocol.md).

## Teaching explanation

Integration needs a connecting premise, not a catalogue of authors. A naming theory concerns how expressions designate; a functional theory concerns what constitutes a mental state; a consciousness argument concerns what an account leaves out. One may argue that a system’s flexible language use is evidence for the right internal organization, then argue that this organization is sufficient for understanding. Those are two different inferences. A consciousness objection can challenge the second while conceding the first.

Build the map by writing the shared question, each side’s answer, its strongest premise, its treatment of the same example and the unsettled connecting claim. Mark whether a sentence attributes a view to a source or improves it on your own behalf. The final map is preparatory material for the essay, not another final paper. A useful research question can be answered within one assigned argument and one serious objection. “What is consciousness?” cannot; “Does a recognition gain suffice to explain this knowledge claim?” can.

## Worked practice example

Invented example: a robot uses “the damaged valve” successfully. From successful reference one may infer some discriminatory competence; one cannot jump directly to felt pain. A map with an explicit premise that all relevant competence is conscious exposes the step a critic must challenge.

## Time plan

Planning budget: 4 h reading/source notes; 1 h teaching and coached practice; 2 h feedback; 1 h independent changed case; 2 h revision and delayed retrieval (reserve twenty minutes). Total 10 h. Retrieval takes place after at least seven days and is charged here once.

## Supported practice artifact

P06: Produce a 1,000-word map of two rival accounts of one connection between language, understanding and experience. Give two passage locators per view, one common case, one unresolved question, and a 100-word explanation of how the essay will narrow it.

Criteria: answer the stated question, make the key distinction explicit, connect reasons to the conclusion, and identify the actual source of attributed claims. Label invented examples. Keep your first attempt and help log.

## Independent changed case

I06: A critic concedes accurate naming and complex reasoning but denies that either entails consciousness. In 300 words, identify which bridge premise in your map is challenged and propose a narrower thesis without rewriting the critic.

Use assigned sources and ordinary access aids, but no live content help, model answers or examiner notes. Declare prior exposure. Neutral criteria: faithful use of the assigned concepts; a justified inference; an identified limitation; traceable attribution. Freeze the response before feedback. If exposed, use the relevant alternate in assessment.md, or the examiner's unused changed-case procedure for a nonessential subskill.

## Feedback, repair and retrieval

Name the one distinction on which your conclusion depends. If it is confused, make a two-column distinction card with a positive instance and a near miss, check the assigned passage, then revise only the affected inference. If attribution is unsupported, reopen the actual edition; do not ask a model to invent a locator. Tutor feedback records criterion, evidence, source status and next action.

D06: Explain your map to a reader who confuses evidence for understanding with a definition of understanding; give a new example of the difference.

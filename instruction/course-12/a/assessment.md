# C12A assessment forms

Use three fresh tasks, **30 minutes each**, within the final module’s ninety-minute written allocation. Each response is approximately 300–450 words, with formulas/diagrams where relevant. The assessor selects F or an unexposed A form before the attempt; do not practice these forms. Delayed D tasks use twenty-five minutes plus ten for citation review, four to six weeks later or after two later modules, charged once to Course 12. Select the least secure row; all three delayed alternatives are provided so the choice follows evidence.

Permitted: assigned texts, ordinary dictionary, text-to-speech, speech-to-text and access support. Not permitted: live content advice, model-generated reasoning, coached revision during the attempt or examiner anchors. Record source access, exposure, assistance and actual time. When a passage is unavailable, the interpretation criterion remains unresolved even if the conceptual case is answered well.

Learner criteria for every form: address the precise question; reconstruct the assigned view fairly; explain the inference and its limitation; use checked locators; distinguish your view from the source. No philosophical or political verdict is prescribed.

## 1. Meaning and reference

F1: Three committees each have exactly one secretary; the same person may serve more than one committee. The report says “Every committee’s secretary voted.” Formalize the claim with a two-place secretary relation and explain why it does not require exactly one secretary in the whole organization. A colleague replaces it with “Exactly one person is secretary and that person voted.” Supply a countermodel to the equivalence claim. Explain what your formalization leaves unresolved about an actual use of “the secretary.”

A1: A plaque bears one inscription: “The editor of Journal R is asleep.” Nobody has checked whether Journal R exists. Formalize “The sole inscription on this plaque contains seven words” using predicates for inscriptions, and explain why referring to the inscription does not assert that its quoted description has a referent. Separately, two names designate the same author but have different spellings. Assess substitution inside a claim about the name’s spelling versus a claim about the author’s age. Identify the object-language/quotation boundary and a limit of a reference-only account.

D1: The current editor really did reject a submission, but a reporter falsely believes that a different person is the editor. The reporter accepts “The editor rejected it” while denying “Lio rejected it,” although Lio is the actual editor. Explain how a Fregean account addresses the report of belief and distinguish a reading concerning the actual editor from a reading concerning whoever satisfies the reporter’s description. State why ordinary first-order substitution alone cannot decide what the reporter believes; no new formal calculus is required.

## 2. Mind and understanding

F2: A machine performs competently in a Turing-style interview. Its designers offer this explanation: “The comprehension unit understands the question, passes its meaning to the judgment unit, and the judgment unit chooses an intelligent answer.” No causal transitions or input conditions are specified. Does this count as a functional explanation of understanding, and does the interview repair the explanatory gap? Use Putnam and a studied Searlean objection to assess whether the explanation has assumed what it was supposed to explain.

A2: An examiner rejects a machine’s claim to intelligence because it never makes arithmetic mistakes, while the human comparison group sometimes does. The examiner proposes adding random mistakes to make it more human-like. Evaluate what that change would show about the operational test and the machine’s functional organization. Would either perfect accuracy or deliberately added error settle Searle’s concern about understanding? Distinguish a human-performance cue from an explanation of the relevant capacity.

D2: A machine’s output cable is severed. Its memory, internal transitions and responses to incoming information continue, but no answers reach the examiner. The examiner infers that it has lost understanding because it now fails the interview. Evaluate that inference using the difference between the test, functional organization and a claim about understanding. State what further facts could make cable damage philosophically significant.

## 3. Conscious experience

F3: An investigator calls a subject physically omniscient. After a first color experience the subject discovers both how the color feels and a previously unknown calibration offset in the apparatus. The investigator treats both discoveries as evidence of nonphysical facts. Audit the stipulation and inference: which discovery contradicts the claimed physical omniscience, what remains of a knowledge argument if the calibration information is supplied beforehand, and which additional premise is still required for the metaphysical conclusion?

A3: A simulation supplies a human investigator with sonar data translated into familiar visual patterns. The investigator learns new navigational skills and declares that nothing about a bat’s experience remains unknown; a critic declares that any remaining ignorance would prove nonphysical facts. Use Nagel to assess the first claim, then reconstruct the extra epistemic and metaphysical premises needed for the critic’s claim. Distinguish learning a human-accessible representation from knowing the bat’s point of view.

D3: Cai learns to imagine a new sensation but cannot describe it publicly. Does incommunicability establish nonphysical information? Give a knowledge-argument reconstruction and one limit.

## Passage selection and explanation

For any required unseen passage, the assessor uses the exact assigned edition and selects the first eligible contiguous paragraph span of 120–180 words in the specified section that was not annotated in practice, quoted in the paper or used in a previous assessment. Preserve necessary context, include the preceding paragraph as source-open context if needed, and record beginning/end words privately with edition and page/section. If no span is eligible, use the named alternate text; if that is also unavailable or exposed, the source-dependent task stays pending. Do not fabricate a passage or silently change translation. The selection and response stay in the local learner workspace, not in the distributable course.

For A/B/D, add a fresh 100–150-word span from the assigned primary reading least used in the essay to the twenty-minute explanation: five minutes to identify the claim and argumentative role; ten minutes to answer the neutral question, “Which premise of your response is least secure, and what change in that premise would alter your conclusion?”; five minutes to defend or revise the response. The learner specifies the change and explains its effect without live content help. The assessor may ask for clarification of that stated inference, but **must not reveal or borrow a case from the reserved alternate or delayed forms**. For C the fresh passage is already in F2/A2, so use the same neutral exchange to test the method’s warrant. A written exchange is equivalent. The essay’s separate subject-matched independent task already supplies a concrete changed-case defense.

## Feedback and completion record

For each row record: criterion; evidence in P and F/A; actual source support; uncertainty; one repair; result of fresh recheck if needed. Mark each row supported, unresolved or needs repair. Essential conceptual confusion, fabricated citation or absent independent evidence cannot be averaged away. After the delayed task, explain whether the least secure distinction transferred; a failed delayed task triggers targeted repair and a new unexposed case. Time beyond the planned repair allowance is logged as actual additional study, not silently hidden. No learner completion, expert validation, rights clearance or rendered accessibility outcome is implied by this authored packet.

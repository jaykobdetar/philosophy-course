# Comparison: annotated progression and revision

These are original miniature examples, not quotations from authors or actual learner submissions. They model reasoning on a practice case, never an independent assessment form. The claims need actual assigned-text locators when used in learner work.

## Practice question

Does an ordinary person’s ability to use a word settle whether a machine understands it? Compare a functional strategy with the Chinese Room challenge. Invented case: an automated greenhouse adjusts watering, reports uncertainty and revises predictions after a sensor failure.

**Developing:** “Functionalism says machines think because they act like people. Searle says they cannot because computers follow rules. Both sides have opinions, so we cannot know.”

The comparison identifies a disagreement but misstates both positions. Functionalism concerns internal causal organization, not surface similarity alone. The room challenges the sufficiency of formal computation; it is not established merely by announcing that every computer cannot think. “Both have opinions” supplies no assessment criterion. Repair: identify one shared sufficiency claim and two reasons concerning it.

**Competent:** “The greenhouse’s ability to revise a plan supports more than a claim about one matching output: the role of memory and sensor input matters to a functional account. The Chinese Room challenge asks whether implementing the formal organization would nevertheless leave understanding absent. Its force depends on extending an intuition about the operator to the whole system. The example provides evidence of a role but does not decide whether that role suffices for understanding.”

This explains each argumentative role and names the contested step. It does not settle the debate by a test result. To become a source-grounded submission, add exact assigned Putnam/Searle locators and distinguish Searle’s own reply to the systems objection from the writer’s criticism.

**Strong:** “The greenhouse case supports a limited functional attribution only if its reported revisions reflect relevant counterfactual capacities, rather than a script fitted to one demonstration. Even then the systems reply has a task: identify the bearer of understanding and explain why the operator’s ignorance is not decisive. Searle’s internalization response presses that explanation again; an apparent shift from system to operator is not itself a refutation unless the functional organization and attribution are kept fixed. I therefore treat the case as testing a sufficiency premise, while leaving the experience question open.”

This adds conditions, preserves a serious reply and explains a limit. It is not stronger merely because it uses more technical terms. A source check could still reveal that a particular attribution needs narrowing.

## Draft-to-revision pair: philosophical methods

**Draft:** “James and Husserl both start with experience, so their methods are basically the same. Experience proves that a belief is true.”

**Feedback:** The shared word “experience” hides different questions. Specify what pragmatic consequences do in James’s clarification, what methodological suspension does in Husserl, and whether your conclusion concerns meaning, warrant or truth. Name passages from JamesII/VI and HusserlI–II before attributing an equivalence.

**Revised:** “Both readings give experience a role, but this alone does not establish a common method. James’s clarification asks what difference rival ideas would make for inquiry and conduct; Husserl’s critical question asks what may be taken as given when the relation of knowledge to its object is itself in question. A successful practical prediction may bear on a Jamesian inquiry without answering Husserl’s justificatory problem. The comparison therefore supports a narrower claim: both challenge an unexamined starting point, but they need not share the same test of success.”

**Revision memo:** I replaced an inference from a shared term with a comparison of questions, evidence and success conditions. I withdrew the claim that experience automatically proves truth. The remaining task is to check the exact passages and test whether my description of each method is sufficiently qualified. This is a substantive change, not stylistic polishing.

## Reusable comparison procedure

Write one sentence for each author's question; one for the relevant claim; one for the kind of evidence or argument offered; one for the shared comparison point; and one for where the comparison stops. Then run the same bounded case through both accounts and identify whether the disagreement is about facts, inference, values, genre or method. If a term shifts meaning, split it before comparing conclusions. Use this procedure in the essay hours already allocated; do not create another paper.

# C12C-M02 — Organism and environment

Baseline allocation: **14 hours**. Prerequisites: Courses 1, 5 and 9; prior modules in this option.

## Guiding question and outcomes

What is lost when an activity is divided into independent stimulus and response parts?

By the end: Map a coordination; explain functional roles within it; distinguish historical philosophical analysis from an empirical discovery.

## Teaching explanation

Dewey criticizes a reflex-arc picture that decomposes activity into a sensory stimulus, a central operation and a motor response as though the parts were independently given. His alternative emphasizes the coordination in which looking, reaching and the environment acquire their functional roles. A stimulus can be what interrupts or redirects an ongoing activity, rather than an isolated input that creates action from nothing.

This changes the explanatory question. Instead of asking only which sensation caused which movement, ask what activity was underway, what tension or obstacle emerged and how the coordination changed. The environment is not merely a passive backdrop. But it would be a mistake to infer that measurable neural pathways do not exist or that every contemporary experiment is conceptually invalid. The article challenges a way of organizing explanation. Philosophical significance depends on whether the division into parts hides relations necessary to understanding the whole activity. A good comparison with James connects inquiry and consequences without making Dewey’s article a restatement of a truth theory.

## Worked practice example

Invented example: while searching for a mug, Rina turns her head and reaches toward a shelf. A glint becomes relevant as part of that searching activity; after discovering broken glass, the same glint becomes a reason to withdraw. Calling the glint an unchanging stimulus misses its different functional roles.

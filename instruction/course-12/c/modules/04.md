# C12C-M04 — Embodied experience

Baseline allocation: **18 hours**. Prerequisites: Courses 1, 5 and 9; prior modules in this option.

## Guiding question and outcomes

How does bodily involvement shape the description of perception?

By the end: Produce a careful perceptual description; separate report, theoretical inference and attributed text; state a limit of generalization.

## Assigned reading and access

Consult [source audit](../source-audit.md) before making textual judgments. ## Teaching explanation

Merleau-Ponty’s Preface frames phenomenology as a return to experience while stressing our involvement in a world that inquiry does not simply construct from detached sensations. Embodiment concerns how possibilities for movement and engagement enter perceptual life. It is more than the physiological claim that eyes and nerves are necessary for seeing. A cup may show up as reachable or difficult to grasp before one explicitly calculates distances.

A description must avoid two shortcuts. It should not replace experience with a causal hypothesis about the brain, and it should not treat one private report as a universal law. The report “the handle attracted my attention as I reached” is different from “all perception is structured by action” and from “the author argues X.” Each needs different support. The Preface also complicates the ideal of a complete reduction: reflecting on experience occurs from within an involvement that cannot simply be erased. A comparison with Husserl should locate the exact text and question, not present a slogan that one author ignores the body and the other discovers it.

## Worked practice example

Invented example: looking at a cup from across the room, its hidden side is not visually displayed, yet the cup is experienced as having more to it than the currently visible face. That description identifies a structure of this experience. It does not prove a theory of the nervous system or guarantee that every subject has an identical experience.

## Supported practice artifact

P04: Spend ten safe minutes observing an ordinary object without manipulating bodily abilities or restricting senses. Submit 400 words of description, 200 of philosophical interpretation and 150 marking limits and textual links. An imagined ordinary scenario is an accessible alternative, labeled as imagined.

Criteria: answer the stated question, make the key distinction explicit, connect reasons to the conclusion, and identify the actual source of attributed claims. Label invented examples. Keep your first attempt and help log.

## Independent changed case

I04: A learner writes: “The door seemed inviting when I intended to leave; therefore its neurons encoded my goal; Merleau-Ponty proves every perception is a motor plan.” Classify and repair the three claims without inventing empirical data. 350 words.

Use assigned sources and ordinary access aids, but no live content help, model answers or examiner notes. Declare prior exposure. Neutral criteria: faithful use of the assigned concepts; a justified inference; an identified limitation; traceable attribution. Freeze the response before feedback. If exposed, use the relevant alternate in assessment.md, or the examiner's unused changed-case procedure for a nonessential subskill.

## Feedback, repair and retrieval

Name the one distinction on which your conclusion depends. If it is confused, make a two-column distinction card with a positive instance and a near miss, check the assigned passage, then revise only the affected inference. If attribution is unsupported, reopen the actual edition; do not ask a model to invent a locator. Tutor feedback records criterion, evidence, source status and next action.

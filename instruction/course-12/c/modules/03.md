# C12C-M03 — Phenomenological reduction

Baseline allocation: **20 hours**. Prerequisites: Courses 1, 5 and 9; prior modules in this option.

## Guiding question and outcomes

What changes when the ordinary assumption of an independently existing world is put out of play for inquiry?

By the end: Explain the methodological move in 600 words; distinguish description from justification; identify a relevant objection.

## Teaching explanation

Husserl begins from a problem about knowledge and its relation to objects. In the ordinary attitude we proceed with claims about the world; a critical inquiry must ask how such claims can be justified without merely presupposing what is at issue. The reduction is a methodological restriction on what may be taken for granted. It is not an empirical discovery that objects have vanished, nor a psychological technique that proves nothing exists outside the mind.

Distinguish an experience’s occurrence from the truth of its object-directed claim. A seeming may be available for examination even while the external claim is withheld. But describing that distinction does not by itself justify every claim about what is indubitably given or how inquiry can move beyond it. That is why the module asks for two outputs: an explanation of the method and a question about its authority. “Science says the object exists” may fail to address the methodological question if the grounds of objective knowledge are precisely what is being examined. Conversely, invoking the reduction cannot make every objection irrelevant. Ask what the method claims to secure and whether its permitted evidence can support that claim.

## Worked practice example

Invented example: a distant tower appears circular. You may withhold judgment about the tower’s actual shape while describing how it appears from here. The appearance is not a second little tower inside the head. Nor does withholding a world claim entail denying the tower’s existence.

# C12C assessment forms

Use three fresh tasks, **30 minutes each**, within the final module’s ninety-minute written allocation. Each response is approximately 300–450 words, with formulas/diagrams where relevant. The assessor selects F or an unexposed A form before the attempt; do not practice these forms. Delayed D tasks use twenty-five minutes plus ten for citation review, four to six weeks later or after two later modules, charged once to Course 12. Select the least secure row; all three delayed alternatives are provided so the choice follows evidence.

Permitted: assigned texts, ordinary dictionary, text-to-speech, speech-to-text and access support. Not permitted: live content advice, model-generated reasoning, coached revision during the attempt or examiner anchors. Record source access, exposure, assistance and actual time. When a passage is unavailable, the interpretation criterion remains unresolved even if the conceptual case is answered well.

Learner criteria for every form: address the precise question; reconstruct the assigned view fairly; explain the inference and its limitation; use checked locators; distinguish your view from the source. No philosophical or political verdict is prescribed.

## 1. Pragmatist method

F1: Two clinics dispute whether a triage process is “efficient.” One means rapid processing; the other means fewer harmful mistaken allocations over a year. Apply James’s clarification and use Dewey to question a model that treats each patient’s answer as an isolated stimulus. Separate the conceptual analysis from empirical predictions.

A1: Two teachers call a learning system successful: one measures immediate satisfaction, the other later error correction. Apply pragmatist clarification without defining truth as popularity, then explain how an ongoing learning activity changes the role of feedback.

D1: A theory is useful for a month but prevents noticing later counterevidence. Explain why short-term success does not by itself vindicate it under the studied method.

## 2. Phenomenological method

F2: Interpret an assessor-selected, uncoached 120–180-word paragraph span from Husserl lecture II. Explain its methodological question; distinguish a descriptive claim, an empirical hypothesis and a philosophical inference. Compare one precise point with the Merleau-Ponty Preface. Use the passage-selection protocol below.

A2: Interpret an uncoached 120–180-word paragraph span from the Merleau-Ponty Preface concerning description or reduction. Distinguish what the passage describes from what it argues. State a relevant methodological objection and one inappropriate inference to a psychological law.

D2: In 250–350 words interpret a different, uncoached span from the other assigned text and explain why bracketing a world claim is not denying it. Identify one issue for the method’s justification.

## 3. Freedom and ethical inquiry

F3: A manifesto argues: “Sartre says choosing commits us beyond our private preference; therefore whatever I sincerely choose becomes a binding rule for everyone. Abraham is presented as an exceptional case in Problema I; therefore anyone may turn that exception into a universal permission.” Reconstruct one relevant argument from Sartre and one from the pseudonymous Kierkegaard text, then assess each proposed move from commitment or exception to a general rule. Explain one objection each text would need to answer.

A3: A commentator argues that Beauvoir’s ambiguity makes every ethical judgment impossible, and that a teleological suspension in Problema I abolishes the ethical universal even in ordinary cases. Assess these two eliminative inferences. Identify what each account puts into question, what it still needs in order to pose its problem, and a serious objection to the account that does not merely repeat the commentator’s inference. Preserve Part I and Problema I boundaries.

D3: A retrospective biography judges an agent’s choice justified solely because the project happened to succeed, then says Abraham’s story would have no philosophical problem if it ended happily. Compare the use of successful outcomes with a Sartre or Beauvoir argument and with Problema I. Explain which justificatory question an outcome might leave unanswered in each method; do not infer the author’s endorsement from the story’s ending.

## Passage selection and explanation

For any required unseen passage, the assessor uses the exact assigned edition and selects the first eligible contiguous paragraph span of 120–180 words in the specified section that was not annotated in practice, quoted in the paper or used in a previous assessment. Preserve necessary context, include the preceding paragraph as source-open context if needed, and record beginning/end words privately with edition and page/section. If no span is eligible, use the named alternate text; if that is also unavailable or exposed, the source-dependent task stays pending. Do not fabricate a passage or silently change translation. The selection and response stay in the local learner workspace, not in the distributable course.

For A/B/D, add a fresh 100–150-word span from the assigned primary reading least used in the essay to the twenty-minute explanation: five minutes to identify the claim and argumentative role; ten minutes to answer the neutral question, “Which premise of your response is least secure, and what change in that premise would alter your conclusion?”; five minutes to defend or revise the response. The learner specifies the change and explains its effect without live content help. The assessor may ask for clarification of that stated inference, but **must not reveal or borrow a case from the reserved alternate or delayed forms**. For C the fresh passage is already in F2/A2, so use the same neutral exchange to test the method’s warrant. A written exchange is equivalent. The essay’s separate subject-matched independent task already supplies a concrete changed-case defense.

## Feedback and completion record

For each row record: criterion; evidence in P and F/A; actual source support; uncertainty; one repair; result of fresh recheck if needed. Mark each row supported, unresolved or needs repair. Essential conceptual confusion, fabricated citation or absent independent evidence cannot be averaged away. After the delayed task, explain whether the least secure distinction transferred; a failed delayed task triggers targeted repair and a new unexposed case. Time beyond the planned repair allowance is logged as actual additional study, not silently hidden. No learner completion, expert validation, rights clearance or rendered accessibility outcome is implied by this authored packet.

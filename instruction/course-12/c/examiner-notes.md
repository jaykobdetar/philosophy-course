## C12C-M07: independent I07

James branch: successful intervention can matter practically while failing to establish the independent predictive warrant claimed; identify which Jamesian question the thesis addresses. Husserl branch: agreement in appearance does not alone justify the bracketed external claim; distinguish intersubjective agreement, description and warrant. Freedom branch: a previous voluntary commitment can shape a present situation without making freedom disappear; conversely ongoing freedom is not by itself an argument that responsibility for earlier projects vanishes. No prescribed verdict on the commitment is required.

Select the branch before drafting by subject, withhold the specific independent stimulus until the paper is frozen, and retain the selection record. An approved equivalent question must match an existing branch’s target premise; otherwise narrow the question before approval. Assess changed-premise reasoning, faithful source use and a justified revision, including withdrawal of the essay conclusion.

## Essential row 1: F1/A1/D1

F1: distinguish different consequences and coordination roles; do not claim clinical facts. A1: experiential constraint and correction matter; “success” needs specification. D1: usefulness is not merely convenience; discuss the truth/warrant question without requiring a single settled interpretation.

## Essential row 2: F2/A2/D2

F2/A2 require actual assigned text; assessor records paragraph endpoints and exact translation before use. Do not award source fidelity based on a generic summary. F2: reduction not denial; empirical explanation not phenomenological description. A2: experience report not universal law; an objection must target the actual claim. D2: method and justification must remain distinct.

## Essential row 3: F3/A3/D3

F3: a commitment to value is not an argument that every sincere choice legislates a binding rule for others; an exceptional relation cannot become a universal permission without changing the very problem. Cite the assigned arguments, retaining pseudonymous distance. A3: ambiguity does not entail ethical nihilism; suspension presupposes an ethical universal whose standing the problem tests, rather than eliminating all ordinary obligation. D3: contingent success does not alone vindicate a choice’s value or resolve the paradox of faith; identify the different justificatory questions rather than imposing a single outcome criterion.

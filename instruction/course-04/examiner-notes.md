# Course 4 — Examiner notes and diagnostic answers

**Keep separate from independent attempts.** Seeing these notes is exposure; use another form or a recorded equivalent from the same assigned passage bank. These notes describe acceptable reasoning, not mandatory conclusions. They do not verify wording in unsighted modern editions. Apply the shared rubric and check the actual source supplied by the learner before marking textual accuracy.

## Module independent checks

**I1:** The caption moves from an observed translation to a claim about original purpose, and from common setting to identical authority. Neither inference is secured by the stated facts. Competent work replaces them with two claims actually supported by the introductions, distinguishes author from later reception, and leaves missing context open. Do not invent the corrective history for a learner without the anthology.

**I2:** Augustine's motion/rest argument permits measuring rest as well as motion; equating time with this pointer's movement fails to capture the comparison. A strong answer distinguishes the functioning of a clock from the philosophical issue, rather than claiming Augustine proved that clocks work in a particular physical way. Failure pattern: simply saying “time is subjective” without reconstructing XI.23–24.

**I3:** The chapter distinguishes verbal entertainment from grasping what the expression purports to mean. A person's ability to utter a denial does not alone decide conceptual coherence. But defending this distinction is not itself a proof that the special description has an actual instance. Competent criticism can ask whether the required understanding already imports the conclusion; competent defense must articulate the extra reasoning.

**I4:** A hidden bodily dependence is compatible with lack of bodily awareness. It challenges a move to separability more directly than the minimal awareness claim. A successful response identifies the actual bridge claimed by the located passage or explicitly says it was not established there. The descriptor example in practice is a warning about one inference, not a refutation of every direct-awareness argument. **Cannot mark source fidelity complete until actual Khalidi passage and its presence in the assigned range are checked.**

**I5:** The disputed doctrine is now an actual premise, not an irrelevant belief of the author. The defender’s analogy therefore fails unless an independent proof of the numerical conclusion can be supplied. A valid derivation can remain unsound if that premise is false; a disputed premise is not yet a refuted premise, and rejecting this proof does not establish that its conclusion is false or that mathematics generally fails. Require the learner to locate the assigned discussion’s distinction between demonstrative cogency and transferred authority and state why it cannot certify a proof with an unestablished premise. Inspect Khalidi for both cited locations; do not award textual credit for repeating the alternate Watt orientation.

**I6:** An effect can bear on existence without complete essence knowledge; a justified exclusion can alter what one is entitled to say without supplying a positive essence. Competent work uses the exact passages and states why those points do not make the two authors' methods identical. A claim that Maimonides rejects every possible informative assertion is too broad for I.58–60. Translation-sensitive judgments remain provisional without Pines.

**I30:** The new question allows some compatibility but challenges explanatory warrant for apparently pointless suffering. Aquinas's short reply disputes total incompatibility by appeal to good from permitted evil. It does not enumerate reasons for every evil. Accept a serious possible response such as questioning the inference from “no reason known” to “no reason possible,” provided it is labelled the learner's reconstruction and its own evidential burden is acknowledged. Reject the shortcut that all harms have thereby been justified or that a person's doubt is itself a failure.

**I7:** Avicenna route: name the epistemic/metaphysical bridge and a surviving limited conclusion. Anselm route: failure of a parody does not prove every premise of the original; identify a remaining intelligibility/existence/necessity burden. Maimonides route: informativeness, justification and positive essence knowledge differ. If exposed, use a fresh challenge to a different premise identified in the actual essay and record the prompt before the attempt; do not merely ask for the familiar response again.

## Form A

**A1:** Required features: order of efficient causes; rejection of self-causation; the premise that derivative causes depend on a first cause such that its removal removes the ordered effects; conclusion of a first efficient cause. The critic contests the dependence/regress step, not simply whether any member has a cause. Avoid imposing a temporal beginning or treating the way as proving every divine attribute. A reasoned objection to the regress inference is acceptable if reconstruction is fair. Article 2 reply 3 explicitly limits knowledge of essence from effects.

**A2:** A present expectation is not an already sounding future event. The interrupted case tests whether the learner can distinguish the object anticipated from the present act. Memory and expectation participate in the recitation/measurement account; neither makes all portions of the poem simultaneous. Accept cautious disagreement about the account's metaphysical scope if grounded in XI.27–28 and the surrounding assigned range. A bare assertion that future things exist because imagined fails the distinction.

**A3:** Require one checked al-Ghazali distinction concerning a demonstrable result and its practitioner's broader authority or its rejection, and one checked Maimonides distinction about warranted negative predication rather than positive essential attributes. Explain different questions: evaluation of disciplines/inferences versus what language can correctly predicate. A contextual caution might concern the polemical or pedagogical setting actually supported by the introduction, or the mistake of treating these selections as voices of two entire religions. Do not demand unsourced dates, biography, or labels. Without source card, mark E3 pending.

## Form B

**B1:** The mere addition of words about necessity does not automatically establish coherent understanding or instantiation. Anselm connects his special description to nonconceivable nonexistence; the learner must explain that claimed connection and identify the disputed premise. A sophisticated modal formalization is unnecessary and cannot replace the assigned argument. Both a defense distinguishing the fiction from the intended concept and a criticism of the concept-to-existence transition can be competent.

**B2:** Different present expectations do not entail different completed event durations. Identify the intentional object, the present mental activity and the claim about a measure. Augustine's explanation of measurement does not immediately validate every estimate. Do not require a modern metaphysics of objective time; require the learner to show which inference is unsupported by the assigned account.

**B3:** Method possession does not guarantee faithful application. A negation needs warrant rather than mere utterance. For Maimonides I.60, progressive exclusion is disciplined by proof and does not become complete positive comprehension of essence. Compare the particular inferential problems rather than assimilating the authors to universal skepticism. Exact Khalidi logic pages and actual Pines text are required for source fidelity.

## Delayed forms

The first 10m must reconstruct the essay's inference rather than its slogan: at least two premises, a conclusion, and a vulnerability, followed by one checked locator. Check retention of reasoning, not memorized wording.

**D1:** Conceptual understanding and inference from effects have different starting points; a.1 reply 2 challenges the automatic move from signification to actuality, while a.2 argues that a known effect can support a cause's existence. A competent answer identifies the premise needed in each route and does not claim that a cause's full essence follows.

**D2:** Bodily rest does not erase every comparison of duration; attention to an imagined familiar sequence can be relevant to the measurement account. A learner should distinguish this interpretive application from an empirical finding about the conductor. An answer that treats imagination as creating external sound fails the event/act distinction.

**D3:** One false claim does not by itself refute every warranted conclusion by the same authority. Inspect each inference and its evidence; warranted exclusions can be informative without yielding positive essence knowledge. Distinguish the two authors' projects. If the learner gives only a generic fallacy label, ask for a new source-bound attempt after repair.

## Marking, remediation and closure

For each task record interpretation, inference, evidence and scope as competent/developing/pending, with a sentence of evidence. Essential criteria must be competent; do not average away source fabrication or a missing group. Supply feedback only after the independent attempt is saved. Use the unused form for a targeted retest after repair; if both are exposed, create a changed inference from the same fixed passage and record it before release. Additional work beyond the planned allocation is logged openly.

The examiner is not certifying a historical or theological verdict. Reasoned disagreement, bounded agnosticism and source-grounded alternatives can meet the rubric. A learner record is complete only when the three grid rows, essay, source-dependent module obligations and delayed evidence have been reviewed; authoring files alone demonstrate none of those learner achievements.

# C04-M07 — Essay and revision

**Question:** Which interpretation survives the strongest textual challenge? **Prerequisites:** M01–M06 artifacts and their source ledgers. **Outcomes:** defend a precise interpretation in a sustained argument; distinguish source claims from reconstruction; answer a serious alternative; revise for reasons and disclose assistance.

## Bounded assignment and readings

Write **1,500–1,800 words**, excluding bibliography and short assistance declaration, on one question:

1. What, exactly, does Avicenna's thought experiment establish? Use the actual Khalidi pp. 27–58 and introduction; this route cannot be released until M04's locator gate is satisfied or a documented finite source amendment is approved.
2. Does Gaunilo answer Anselm? Use Proslogion 2–4, the complete Gaunilo reply, and Anselm's reply 1–4, Williams (2001).
3. Can negative theology make informative claims? Use Guide I.50–60, Pines (1963). Aquinas I q.2 a.2 may clarify one distinction, but do not let a comparison replace interpreting Maimonides.

No new external reading is required. Revisit the relevant assigned ranges and source introductions. Older aids may support preparation but do not fulfil a claimed quotation check in a modern edition. Choose a different accessible route if a source is unavailable; the unfinished source-specific module still remains recorded as pending.

## How to build the argument

A philosophical interpretation has two burdens. It must fit the words and argumentative sequence, and it must explain how its conclusion follows. A logically elegant argument is not automatically the author's argument; a close paraphrase can still leave the inference obscure. Make these burdens visible in separate sentences: “The passage distinguishes …”; “Given that distinction, the inference requires …”; “The best reason to attribute that requirement is …”.

A rival interpretation is not a silly opposing belief. Choose the strongest reading that would alter your answer. For Avicenna, compare a limited claim about awareness with a stronger claim about the soul's nature. For Anselm/Gaunilo, decide whether the objection addresses chapter 2's transition, chapter 3's necessity claim, or both. For Maimonides, distinguish reducing error from supplying a positive description of essence. Your decision must use actual passages, and you may conclude that the text underdetermines part of the dispute.

Revision changes argumentative commitments as well as wording. If a source supports only a narrower claim, reducing the thesis is a substantive improvement. If an objection exposes a missing premise, either defend the premise, limit the conclusion, or concede the objection. Moving an unsupported sentence into a footnote does not repair it.

## Worked example and drafting procedure

**Invented methodological example:** a draft says, “Every negative sentence teaches us the essence of its subject.” The example “the unopened box contains no fruit” shows that a denial may exclude possibilities without giving an essence. A better thesis is conditional: “A justified exclusion can reduce some errors without supplying a complete positive definition.” This is a model of revision, not a thesis to copy into the Maimonides essay. The author still needs to show whether that distinction fits Guide I.58–60.

**P7, produce these artifacts in order:**

1. A 150-word proposal: selected question; one-sentence answer; one explicit exclusion; passages needed; current access status.
2. A claim ledger with six rows: claim; exact locator; quotation/paraphrase/inference; rival reading; reason for preference; unresolved issue. Never put an unsighted page number in a quotation cell.
3. A paragraph plan with functions: question and stakes, fair reconstruction, pivotal inference, rival interpretation, textual decision, objection/reply, conclusion and limit. Combine functions where appropriate.
4. A full draft. Use references at the point of the interpretive claim. Quotations should be brief and necessary, with translation identified.
5. A reverse outline and 200–300-word revision memo: two substantive changes, reasons, and one criticism accepted or rejected with evidence. Submit the revised essay with the draft and assistance log.

Feedback is bounded: the tutor identifies at most two major issues and points to their criteria and source locations; the learner writes the changes. It may model a different example but may not compose an assessed paragraph. Assess the shared rubric's question, interpretation/context, reasoning, objection/reply, evidence, organization, and revision. Competence requires a defensible thesis, fair competing interpretation, traceable source use, and a justified decision; sophisticated prose cannot compensate for invented evidence.

## Independent changed task I7

After the draft, answer the appropriate challenge in 300–400 words without consulting it or tutor advice. Primary sources and citation sheet are allowed.

- Avicenna route: “Your argument moves from how something is known to what could exist separately. Which premise licenses that move, and could a weaker conclusion survive without it?”
- Anselm route: “Suppose the island parody fails. Have you thereby established that Anselm's argument succeeds? Identify the remaining burden.”
- Maimonides route: “Suppose an exclusion is informative. Have you shown that it is justified, or that it yields knowledge of essence? Distinguish the steps.”

Do not rehearse this response in practice. Record any exposure; use the alternative challenge procedure in examiner notes if necessary. Criteria: direct response, source fit, explicit premise, and honest limitation. The essay plus I7 does not automatically discharge all three course assessment rows.

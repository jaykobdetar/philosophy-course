## Teaching explanation

Maimonides' I.50 connects belief with what is understood rather than mere verbal profession. His treatment of unity makes it consequential whether attributes introduce composition. “God is wise” can suggest a subject plus a quality of the kind humans possess; the question is whether such grammar misrepresents its object. I.58 distinguishes negative exclusions from positive description of essence, and allows talk of actions. On this account, ruling out a false characterization can advance understanding without supplying a positive definition of what God is. [Older I.50](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/gfp/gfp060.htm) and [I.58](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/gfp/gfp068.htm) support this orientation; confirm the terms in Pines.

I.60's ship example develops progressive exclusion. A series of negations can reduce error, but the analogy's ordinary object should not smuggle in a complete positive divine description. The chapter requires warranted negations, not arbitrary denials. This suggests an objection worth investigating: if negations guide understanding, what content and justification do they require? An answer must distinguish learning that a description is false from comprehending the divine essence. [Older I.60](https://sacred-texts.com/jud/gfp/gfp070.htm).

Aquinas I q.2 a.1 distinguishes what is self-evident in itself from what is self-evident to us. Knowing a word's meaning need not disclose the essence or establish an instance; reply 2 is especially relevant to the Anselm comparison. Article 2 distinguishes demonstration through causes from demonstration through effects better known to us. Its reply 3 allows establishing a cause's existence without perfect knowledge of its essence. Do not collapse that distinction into a claim that every theology is merely negative.

Article 3's first way begins with change and the distinction between potentiality and actuality. A thing cannot be actual and potential in the same respect in the relevant way; the argument then challenges an infinite dependence of movers. The second concerns ordered efficient causes and the impossibility of self-causation. The third concerns possible and necessary beings; its transition to a time with nothing requires careful scrutiny. The fourth links degrees of perfections with a maximum and cause. The fifth moves from regular end-directed activity of things without intelligence to direction by intelligence. These are different premises and inferential burdens. “Everything has a cause, so God does” is not a faithful statement of all five. Neither a chronological first event nor the full later doctrine of God should be inserted as an already demonstrated conclusion.

A Summa article stages a dispute. Objections support the answer the author will reject or qualify; “on the contrary” introduces opposing authority or reason; the main answer develops the author's position; replies address the numbered objections. In I q.1 a.8, reasoning from accepted revealed principles and answering objections when an opponent rejects them have different roles. The article does not assert that every article of faith can be demonstrated to any audience from premises everyone accepts.

## Worked example and practice

**Invented example:** a sealed package contains something, but evidence rules out a liquid and a living organism. You have learned exclusions without identifying its contents. This clarifies a distinction; it does not prove that all methods of identifying packages apply to God. Separately, discovering a footprint might justify some claim about a cause while failing to identify its complete nature. Do not infer that these two examples make Maimonides and Aquinas hold the same theory.

**P6a:** Map I q.2 a.1 into objection, contrary, answer, and replies. Then reconstruct the second way in 5–7 steps. ## Independent changed case I6

An invented reader says, “If I can demonstrate that a cause exists, I must know its essence; if I cannot describe its essence positively, I have said nothing.” In 300 words assess the two claims using Guide I.60 and Summa I q.2 a.2 reply 3. Explain a limit to the comparison. This differs from P6b by assessing the reader's two implications rather than surveying similarities. Use assigned texts/citation sheet only; no AI, hints, prepared responses, or examiner notes. Criteria: accurate predication distinction, effect/existence/essence distinction, and no invented agreement. Pines-dependent judgment remains pending until inspected.

# C04-M03 — Anselm and Gaunilo

## Teaching explanation

Proslogion 2 begins from understanding a description of something than which nothing greater can be thought. Understanding words, having an object of thought, and affirming real existence must be kept separate. The argument supposes that what satisfies the description exists only in understanding, then claims that existing in reality as well would be greater. That would conflict with the description. A reconstruction should expose the crucial comparison premise and the assumption that the description is intelligible in the required way. Neither follows merely from calling the argument “ontological.”

Proslogion 3 turns to inability to conceive nonexistence. It contrasts a being whose nonexistence can be conceived with one whose nonexistence cannot be conceived. This changes the argumentative burden: a critic must ask whether the purported necessary mode of existence is coherent and whether the conclusion follows. Proslogion 4 distinguishes entertaining words from understanding the thing signified; the distinction is meant to explain how denial can be spoken. It is not a licence to dismiss every critic as incapable of understanding.

Gaunilo questions the passage from hearing/understanding a description to the special object required by the proof. His island parody tests a form of inference: could a superior conceivable island be made actual by similar reasoning? Read his entire response, since reducing it to an island joke misses its challenge about understanding and warranted existence. Anselm's reply 1–4 insists that the special description and its relation to necessary existence matter. Reply 3 challenges the critic to reproduce the reasoning for something else. Calling an island contingent suggests a disanalogy, but explaining why that disanalogy blocks the parody is the real work.

Charitable interpretation therefore produces a dispute with alternatives. Gaunilo may expose an illicit conceptual-to-existential step in chapter 2; Anselm may argue that the parody has failed to preserve chapter 3's relevant conditions. You need not declare either move decisive. State exactly which reconstruction you accept and why its premises apply or fail to apply to the parody.

## Worked example and practice

**Invented example:** “The most generous possible donor must actually donate, because an actual donation is better than a merely imagined one.” The tempting move shifts between a description of generosity and the existence of an instance. A critic can request a premise connecting the two; simply imagining the donor does not supply it. This is an instructional analogy, not a quotation, not Gaunilo's island, and not a complete refutation of Anselm's special description.

**P3:** Submit three parallel maps, each with 4–7 numbered steps: Proslogion 2, Gaunilo's objection/parody, and Anselm's reply 1–4. Add a 350-word judgment stating one premise shared by the first two, one disputed step, and whether the reply changes or clarifies the original burden. Attach an edition-specific locator to each key step. Then write a two-sentence account of what chapter 3 adds. Criteria: no substitution of “greatest existing thing” for the actual description; no attribution of Gaunilo's denial to Anselm; serious treatment of the reply.

## Independent changed case I3

Read Proslogion 4 alongside reply 4. An invented critic says, “I can pronounce a denial of any mathematical theorem, so your distinction between saying words and understanding a thing has no force.” In 300 words reconstruct the distinction, identify the critic's intended inference, and explain whether defending that distinction alone proves God's existence. Use assigned texts and citation sheet only; no coached answer or examiner notes. Criteria: correct scope, premise/conclusion separation, and a reasoned assessment that does not require a particular religious verdict.

## Teaching explanation

In the alternate translation, al-Ghazali says criticism requires understanding a discipline. His discussion of mathematics distinguishes a demonstration's cogency from the authority of its practitioners on other subjects. He also criticizes rejecting demonstrable results merely to defend religion. The logic discussion distinguishes rules of proof from whether philosophers satisfy those rules elsewhere. These distinctions support inquiry into selective criticism; they do not justify describing him as an unrestricted advocate of every philosophical study. The surrounding warnings about effects on students are part of the position and should not be omitted. Verify the corresponding passages in Khalidi before attributing this wording or scope to that selection.

For analysis, separate three kinds of statement. “I ceased to trust this method” reports an intellectual experience. “These inquiries divide into six branches” classifies a field. “Success in one branch does not entail success in another” gives a reason about inference. An autobiographical narrative can contain arguments, but the occurrence of an experience does not automatically make its interpretation a demonstration. Likewise, classifying a view as objectionable is not yet a reconstruction of a reason against it.

A useful reading matrix asks, for each of mathematics, logic, natural inquiry, metaphysics, politics, and ethics: what is its object; what kind of certainty is claimed; what is accepted, criticized, or restricted; who is speaking; and what precise passage supports the entry? Leave a cell unresolved if the assigned excerpt does not answer it. Distinguish al-Ghazali's report of another philosopher from that philosopher's own argument. This course does not read the complete *Incoherence of the Philosophers*, so do not import its detailed arguments about causation as if they were demonstrated here.

## Worked example and practice

**Invented example:** a surveyor gives a valid geometric proof, then endorses a claim about the best constitution. Sam reasons, “The proof is certain, so the constitutional claim is certain.” The missing premise is that the surveyor's reliability or proof transfers to the new subject. Geometric success alone does not supply it. Lee responds, “The constitution claim is mistaken, so the geometric proof must fail.” Lee makes the reverse transfer. Check the proof on its premises and test the political claim on its own reasons. This models a reasoning distinction, not al-Ghazali's entire position.

**P5a:** Complete the six-branch matrix with at least one actual location per supported row; distinguish missing evidence from disapproval. **P5b:** Write 500 words reconstructing the anthology's criticism of transferring demonstrative prestige between fields. Identify an argument against philosophers rather than treating every philosopher as its target. Include one autobiographical sentence and one classificatory sentence from your notes, explain why neither by itself establishes the conclusion, then give a possible objection to the argument. Criteria: textual limits, correct speaker, explicit inferential link, and no general claim that Islamic thought has one attitude to reason.

## Independent changed case I5

Invented case: a scholar derives a numerical claim using an explicit premise about the structure of the cosmos. A critic disputes that premise. A defender replies, “Rejecting this proof because of a metaphysical disagreement is the same mistake as rejecting sound geometry because its author has other beliefs.” Unlike the worked example, the disputed doctrine is a premise of this proposed proof, whose soundness has not been established. With the anthology’s mathematics discussion open, write 250–300 words evaluating the defender’s analogy. Distinguish rejecting an irrelevant association from challenging an actual premise; decide what does and does not follow about the numerical conclusion and mathematical inquiry generally. Use two exact locations from the assigned excerpt to explain the reach and limit of the comparison. No AI, task-specific hints, prewritten answers, or examiner notes.

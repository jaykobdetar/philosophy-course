# C04-M01 — Context and transmission

**Question:** How can we describe intellectual exchange without making every author a stage on the way to someone else? **Prerequisites:** Courses 1 and 3; distinguish a source's statement from your inference. **Outcomes:** make a traceable context map; distinguish language, religious affiliation, institution, and argumentative purpose; explain two limits of a single faith/reason narrative.

## Teaching explanation

A transmission claim describes a relation: a text or concept moved between identified languages, people, or institutions. A doctrinal claim describes what an author argues. The first does not establish the second. Even proof that B read A leaves open whether B adopted, revised, or opposed A's conclusion. Your map therefore needs labelled arrows: “translated,” “criticized,” “used a distinction from,” and “connection not established” have different evidential burdens. The existence of an English anthology also does not show that its selected authors formed one school.

Treat “reason” as a question about procedures: demonstration from premises, testing an opponent's commitments, interpretation of authoritative language, or disciplined first-person examination. Treat “revelation” as a question about what a particular author accepts and how it enters an argument. An argument can use reasoning within accepted religious commitments; it can also criticize another argument's demonstration while sharing some of its commitments. These are conceptual possibilities to investigate, not facts to attribute indiscriminately to every medieval writer.

Avoid inferring language from religion or modern nationality. A historically useful map names the language of composition separately from the author, translator, reader, and institution. The assigned introduction supports an Arabic/Islamic context. It cannot by itself establish a complete map of Latin or Jewish settings. Mark missing details as research questions. This restraint is informative: it tells a reader where the evidence ends.

## Worked example and practice

**Invented example, not medieval history:** a catalogue says that Scholar N translated Treatise T from language A into B. A later marginal note disputes T's definition of knowledge. Supported claim: T was translated, and this reader challenged a definition. Unsupported claim: all speakers of B accepted T's theory. A justified arrow is “translated”; a second is “criticized this definition.” Neither is “preserved unchanged.” The lesson is that a transmission fact and a philosophical response need separate warrants.

**P1:** Make a ledger with columns `claim | edition/page and speaker | supported, inferred, or unresolved`. Enter eight claims from the introductions: at least two about language/translation, two about settings or institutions, and two about intellectual questions. If a category has insufficient evidence, record the exact missing claim instead of filling it from memory. Draw a six-node map, or provide an equivalent numbered text list with labelled relations.

**P2:** Write 500 words answering “Why is a simple faith-versus-reason storyline inadequate for these selected readings?” Use three cited introductory claims and two sharply bounded questions for the later primary texts. Criteria: source supports the actual claim; scope is Arabic/Islamic where appropriate; conceptual distinctions explain why the simple narrative fails; neither Islamic nor Jewish inquiry is presented merely as preparation for Latin thought.

## Independent changed case I1

Read this invented exhibition caption: “Because several texts were translated into Latin, their original authors were chiefly custodians of a European inheritance. Their common religious setting means that they all subordinated argument to the same authority.” In 250 words identify two distinct unsupported inferences and use two exact locations in Khalidi's introductions to replace the caption with qualified claims. Do not add a new historical fact without a source. Permit primary text and citation sheet only; no tutor hints, AI, or practice answer. Criteria: inferential diagnosis, traceability, differentiation of authors, and an explicit remaining uncertainty. Source unavailable means contextual attribution is pending, not automatically wrong or complete. Diagnosis is in [examiner notes](../examiner-notes.md).

# C04-M02 — Time and experience

**Question:** What can be measured when the past is gone and the future has not arrived? **Prerequisites:** M01's attribution discipline and Course 1's inference maps. **Outcomes:** reconstruct Augustine's temporal puzzle; distinguish temporal existence, present mental activity, and measurement; test an interpretation against an objection.

## Teaching explanation

The familiar experience of duration creates the problem. We say that a sound lasted a long time. Yet the past sound no longer occurs; the future portion does not yet occur; an indivisible present seems to have no length. If only the present exists in the relevant sense, where is the duration measured? Notice that the question uses ordinary measurement as evidence of a difficulty. “Time does not exist” is not an adequate summary of that difficulty or its resolution.

Augustine's treatment of three presents distinguishes present memory of past things, present attention or awareness, and present expectation of future things (XI.20; wording varies by translation). A remembered event and the act of remembering it are not the same object at the same time. Saying that remembering is present does not make the remembered event present. Similarly, expecting a verse does not mean the verse has already sounded.

The discussion of a moving body and a body at rest (XI.23–24) separates time from any one body's motion. We can compare durations of movement and rest; identifying time with the movement alone would lose the latter comparison. Later, the sound/syllable examples and the recitation example (XI.27–28) shift attention to the impression remaining and the movement between expectation, attention, and memory. The mind can retain what has ceased sounding and anticipate what has yet to sound. This helps explain measurement; it does not by itself settle every metaphysical question about time outside experience.

Two readings require argument: the account principally explains temporal measurement, or it makes a broader claim about what time is. The hesitation and experimental sequence matter, as do the concluding contrasts with divine eternity. Do not infer that an eternally present God experiences an indefinitely long human present. XI.30–31 contrasts divine knowledge with a singer's changing attention. Keep the theological framing visible without treating it as a substitute for the argument.

## Worked example and practice

**Invented case:** Mira hears a two-second bell followed by a four-second bell. When the second ends, neither is sounding. She can still compare them through retained impressions. This illustrates why a present act can concern absent events. It does not prove that her estimate is accurate, that clocks are unnecessary, or that nothing existed while unheard. Those are different claims requiring further premises.

**P2a:** Draw a three-stage map for reciting a familiar six-line verse: before starting, after line three, after finishing. Label what is expected, attended to, and remembered at each stage. Use a text list if drawing is inconvenient. Pair the map with four numbered premises reconstructing the measurement puzzle.

**P2b:** Write a 400-word objection and possible response: “If time is measured through an impression, why can two observers disagree about how long the same event lasted?” Give the strongest reading supported by XI.27–28, identify whether the objection concerns reliability or the possibility of measurement, and say what the reply leaves open. Criteria: distinguish event from retained impression; cite actual edition; avoid both a modern neuroscience claim and an unsupported total idealism.

## Independent changed case I2

With XI.23–24 open, consider an invented clock whose pointer remains still while its alarm sounds, then moves after the alarm falls silent. A student says, “There was no time during the stationary phase because time is motion.” Write 250–350 words reconstructing the relevant argument and evaluating this inference. Explain one limit of transferring Augustine's claim to the mechanical case. No AI, hints, notes containing answers, or examiner file. Criteria: textual reconstruction, separation of measure and measured change, relevant limit. This is different from P2b's reliability question.

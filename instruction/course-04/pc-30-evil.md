## Explanation and worked practice

The objection argues from infinite goodness and the presence of evil to nonexistence of God. Its pivotal claim is that infinite goodness would exclude evil altogether. Reply 1 appeals to the possibility of allowing evil while bringing good from it. The reply disputes the incompatibility premise; it does not offer a detailed explanation of every particular suffering. Distinguish three claims: an evil occurs; there could be a good reason to permit it; there actually is a good reason for each evil. Movement between these claims needs argument.

**Invented worked example:** a temporary painful treatment can be permitted for recovery. This shows how permitting a harm and seeking a good need not contradict one another in that ordinary case. It does not establish that all suffering is treatment, that every harm is justified, or that a particular divine intention is known. The example models a logical distinction, not a complete theodicy.

## Independent changed case I30

An invented interlocutor grants that some harms can be permitted for good, then asks: “Why think this accounts for apparently pointless suffering as well?” In 200–250 words distinguish the new challenge from the original incompatibility argument. Explain what Aquinas's short reply does and does not supply toward answering it, and give one serious possible response explicitly labelled as your own reconstruction, with its remaining burden. Do not use a concrete traumatic event. Permitted: article and citation sheet; prohibited: AI help, tutor hints, answer key, prewritten reply. Criteria: distinguish possibility from warranted explanation; fair reconstruction; explicit residual issue. See [examiner notes](examiner-notes.md).

# Course 4 — Medieval philosophy and transmission

An eight-module, 100-hour teaching sequence for [the accepted curriculum](../../current/courses/04.md). Prerequisites: Courses 1 and 3, especially argument reconstruction, quotation/paraphrase distinctions, and Aristotle's explanatory vocabulary. This is a bounded study of arguments across interconnected settings, not a comprehensive history of medieval philosophy. Authored 20 October 2026; learner pilot, expert review, and rendered accessibility review remain pending.

## Start here

Read the [source audit](source-audit.md) before arranging loans. The assigned modern translations remain the reference editions. Existing Pusey and Deane copies support preparation but are not Chadwick or Williams. The Dominican Aquinas text is available locally. Khalidi's introduction and assigned Avicenna/al-Ghazali excerpts, and Pines' Maimonides, need lawful library or personal access. No purchase is required by these instructions. Do not infer access to an excerpt from a publisher's contents page.

The authoring is complete; particular reading-dependent activities cannot be completed or textually assessed until their sources are available. In particular, locate the flying-person passage in the actual assigned Avicenna excerpt before treating it as present there. If it is absent, record the mismatch and request a finite source amendment; do not silently substitute a passage from another Avicenna work. Module 4 supplies conceptual preparation and a source-check procedure, not a fabricated quotation or page.

## Study and assessment rules

Use [outcomes](outcomes.md) to keep the evidence grid. Supported work and independent evidence are separate. Read [assessment](assessment.md) only when the assessor releases the chosen form; the examiner file contains answers and should remain closed during independent work. A self-directed learner who has read an item or its diagnosis must disclose that exposure and use the alternate form or an assessor-generated equivalent from the specified passage bank.

Follow the shared [tutor rules](../tutor.md). Practice starts with your attempt and ends with your revision; independent work permits assigned primary texts and a citation sheet, but no AI content help, answer keys, or prepared responses. Assistance declarations accompany every artifact. A competent conclusion may defend, reject, or qualify an argument. Religious commitment, accent, confidence, and speaking speed are not grading criteria.

For audio study, use a lawful audiobook or text-to-speech rendition of the exact assigned range. Pause at chapter/article markers and dictate an argument map. Use the written text to check every citation and negation before submission. No task requires audio: a written map or typed exchange has the same criteria. Audio location alone is insufficient as a citation unless the edition has no textual locator; record edition and chapter as well. Reading-dependent work still needs accessible text or checked transcription. See [tutor guidance](tutor-notes.md) for assessment accommodations.

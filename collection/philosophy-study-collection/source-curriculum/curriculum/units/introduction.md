# Introduction to Philosophy

**Stage:** Foundations
**Requires:** none
**Status:** complete

## What this unit is for

Learning to do philosophy: reading closely, reconstructing arguments, testing them with counterexamples, and writing and speaking about them clearly. The texts are short classics that touch ethics, the theory of knowledge, and metaphysics, and they come back later in the program.

## Anchor readings

Cite by the standard numbers given. Editions are listed in the [materials catalog](../../materials/catalog.md).

- Plato, *Euthyphro*, *Apology*, and *Meno* (Stephanus pages)
- Descartes, *Meditations on First Philosophy*, Meditations I–II (AT VII 17–34)
- Edmund Gettier, "Is Justified True Belief Knowledge?" (1963)
- Peter Singer, "Famine, Affluence, and Morality" (1972)
- Optional: Jim Pryor, "Guidelines on Writing a Philosophy Paper"

## Mastery criteria

After this unit you can:

1. Identify premises, conclusions, and unstated assumptions in unfamiliar argumentative prose.
2. Reconstruct an argument in standard form, charitably, marking any repairs you add.
3. Distinguish validity from soundness and necessary from sufficient conditions, and use counterexamples to test definitions and general claims.
4. Explain and assess the Euthyphro dilemma, Meno's paradox, Descartes's dream and demon arguments and the cogito, Gettier's cases against the justified-true-belief analysis, and Singer's argument about famine relief.
5. Write a focused 1,000–1,500-word analytic essay with a clear thesis, a fair reconstruction, a serious objection, and a reply.
6. Explain and defend your own argument aloud under unscripted questioning.

## Gate

Standard gate at the Foundations thresholds ([rubrics/gates.md](../../rubrics/gates.md)): a 60-minute written exam, a 20-minute oral exam covering Plato, Descartes, Gettier, and Singer, and a revised paper.

## Steps

Steps aren't timed: one step might take a single session or several. In every step the learner acts first. Outputs are saved where shown, and the Director adds the listed review items as each module ends.

### Module 0: starting point

**Step 0. Diagnostic** (Director, with the Assignment Designer). Not graded.

- A 20-minute written response to a short, unfamiliar argumentative passage: what is the author claiming, and why?
- A 10-minute spoken conversation about a philosophical question you care about.
- The results set your starting scores in `learner/record/dashboard.md`. If you've studied philosophy before, you can take the gate now instead.

### Module 1: arguments

**Step 1. Premises and conclusions.** Identify the premises and conclusion in six short passages. Then the Tutor explains indicator words and how arguments chain together. Save to `learner/assignments/introduction/step-01.md` (later steps follow the same pattern).

**Step 2. Validity and soundness.** Before any explanation, classify eight arguments as valid or invalid, and sound or unsound, giving reasons.

**Step 3. Necessary and sufficient conditions.** Test six proposed definitions (for example of "game" or "knowledge") with counterexamples.

**Step 4. Charitable reconstruction.** Reconstruct three short arguments in standard form, putting any premise you add in brackets ([format](../../templates/argument-reconstruction.md)). The Tutor checks them for charity, not just accuracy.

*Review items:* validity and soundness; necessary and sufficient conditions; the reconstruction format.

### Module 2: *Euthyphro*, definition and dilemma

**Step 5. First reading.** Read the whole dialogue without commentary. Mark each definition of piety Euthyphro offers and Socrates' objection to each. Write a précis ([format](../../templates/precis.md)) and save it to `learner/work/introduction/euthyphro-precis.md`.

**Step 6. Socratic tutorial** (Tutor). Reconstruct the dilemma at 10a–11b as numbered premises. Which premise would a defender of divine command theory reject, and at what cost?

**Step 7. Debate by voice** (Debate Partner). The Debate Partner defends the view that an act is right because God commands it, and you attack it. Then switch sides.

**Step 8. Secondary reading and objection.** Now read a scholarly overview of the dilemma (the third pass of the four-pass protocol), then write one objection to your own reconstruction and the strongest reply to it.

*Review items:* the dilemma in standard form; how a Socratic exchange of definitions and counterexamples works.

### Module 3: *Apology*, the examined life

**Step 9. Reading and précis.** Read the *Apology*, focusing on Socrates' account of his wisdom (20e–23c).

**Step 10. Tutorial** (Tutor). Reconstruct Socrates' argument that death is not something bad (40c–41c) and find its weakest premise.

**Step 11. Cumulative review** (Director). Mixed items from Modules 1 and 2, answered from memory.

### Essay 1

**Step 12.** Write, without AI help: "Reconstruct the Euthyphro dilemma and assess the strongest response available to a defender of divine command theory" (1,000–1,500 words). The Tutor returns a feedback memo ([format](../../templates/feedback-memo.md)); you revise; the Examiner scores the revision as practice. Save to `learner/work/introduction/essay-1.md`.

### Module 4: *Meno*, learning and knowledge

**Step 13.** Read the *Meno*. Reconstruct Meno's paradox (80d–e): how can you search for what you don't know?

**Step 14. Tutorial** (Tutor). The geometry demonstration (82b–85b): does it show that learning is recollection? Reconstruct the argument from the demonstration to recollection before discussing it.

**Step 15.** Knowledge and true opinion (97a–98b): explain Socrates' comparison with the statues of Daedalus in your own words. This sets up Gettier.

*Review items:* Meno's paradox; the difference between knowledge and true opinion.

### Module 5: Descartes, doubt and certainty

**Step 16.** Read Meditation I (AT VII 17–23). Reconstruct the dream argument and the evil demon argument separately. What does the second add to the first?

**Step 17.** Read Meditation II (AT VII 23–34). Reconstruct the cogito. Tutorial: does "I think" presuppose that there is an "I"?

**Step 18. Debate by voice** (Debate Partner): "You can know that you're not dreaming right now." Switch sides halfway.

*Review items:* the dream and demon arguments; the cogito.

### Module 6: Gettier, the analysis of knowledge

**Step 19.** Read Gettier's paper (three pages). Reconstruct the justified-true-belief analysis and both of his cases.

**Step 20. Counterexample lab** (Assignment Designer). Write your own Gettier case. Propose a fourth condition (for example, that the belief isn't inferred from anything false), then test it against a case the Assignment Designer gives you.

**Step 21. Essay 2.** "Explain Gettier's argument and assess the 'no false lemmas' response, using a case of your own" (1,000–1,500 words). Feedback memo, revision, practice score. Save to `learner/work/introduction/essay-2.md`.

*Review items:* the justified-true-belief analysis; the structure of a Gettier case.

### Module 7: Singer, an argument in ethics

**Step 22.** Read Singer's paper. Reconstruct his main argument, including his principle that we ought to prevent something bad when we can do so without sacrificing anything of comparable moral importance, and his example of the child drowning in a shallow pond.

**Step 23. Tutorial** (Tutor). Find the weakest premise. Consider the objections from demandingness, from distance, and from the fact that many others could help.

**Step 24. Debate by voice** (Debate Partner). Defend Singer's conclusion, then attack it.

**Step 25. Essay 3.** "Is Singer's principle too demanding? Reconstruct his argument and defend a verdict against the strongest objection" (1,000–1,500 words). Feedback memo, revision, practice score. Save to `learner/work/introduction/essay-3.md`.

*Review items:* Singer's argument in standard form; the demandingness objection.

### Module 8: gate

**Step 26. Gate paper.** Choose one of your three essays and revise it again to gate standard, with one more round of diagnosis-only feedback.

**Step 27. Gate** (Examiner). The written exam without AI help, the oral exam by voice, and the paper, against the [Foundations standards](../../rubrics/gates.md).

**Step 28. Reflection** (Director). What changed in your thinking during this unit, and which arguments changed it? The Director updates your record and schedules the confirmation check for about four weeks later.

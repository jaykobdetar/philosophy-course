# Elective: a non-European tradition

**Stage:** Advanced
**Requires:** [Introduction to Philosophy](introduction.md) (each option may add more)
**Status:** outline

## What this unit is for

Serious study of one philosophical tradition outside the European canon, read in its own terms rather than as a footnote to European philosophy.

## Scope benchmark

Pending, for each option: compare with 2–3 current syllabi and record them here.

## Options

Choose one. Before starting, the chosen option gets a full outline with its own anchor readings, and those readings get catalog entries.

- **Classical Chinese philosophy:** the *Analects*, *Mencius*, *Xunzi*, *Zhuangzi*, and the Mohists.
- **Indian philosophy:** the Upaniṣads, Nyāya epistemology, and Buddhist philosophy (no-self; Nāgārjuna).
- **Africana philosophy:** Du Bois, Fanon, Wiredu, Gyekye, and Menkiti on personhood.
- **Islamic philosophy:** Avicenna, al-Ghazālī's *The Incoherence of the Philosophers*, and Averroes.

## Mastery criteria

1. Reconstruct the tradition's central arguments in its own terms and vocabulary.
2. Explain the historical and textual context needed to read its main texts responsibly.
3. Compare one of its positions with a European counterpart without assimilating either to the other.
4. Assess a live debate within the tradition.

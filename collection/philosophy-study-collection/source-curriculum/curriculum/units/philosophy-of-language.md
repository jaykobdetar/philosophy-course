# Philosophy of Language

**Stage:** Core
**Requires:** [Logic I](logic-1.md)
**Status:** outline

## What this unit is for

How words refer and sentences mean, and how saying something differs from what is said: the canon of twentieth-century analytic philosophy.

## Anchor readings

- Gottlob Frege, "On Sense and Reference" (1892)
- Bertrand Russell, "On Denoting" (1905)
- P. F. Strawson, "On Referring" (1950)
- Saul Kripke, *Naming and Necessity*, Lectures I–II
- Hilary Putnam, "The Meaning of 'Meaning'" (1975)
- H. P. Grice, "Logic and Conversation" (1975)
- J. L. Austin, *How to Do Things with Words* (1962)
- Ludwig Wittgenstein, *Philosophical Investigations*, selections (section numbers)
- W. V. O. Quine, "Two Dogmas of Empiricism" (1951)

## Mastery criteria

1. Explain Frege's distinction between sense and reference and the puzzles it solves (informative identity statements; substitution in belief reports).
2. Reconstruct Russell's theory of descriptions, including his analysis of "The present King of France is bald", and Strawson's objections.
3. Reconstruct Kripke's modal, epistemic, and semantic arguments against descriptivism about names, and explain the causal-historical picture of reference.
4. Explain Putnam's Twin Earth argument for semantic externalism.
5. Distinguish what is said from what is implicated (Grice), and explain performative utterances and speech acts (Austin).
6. Explain Quine's attack on the analytic–synthetic distinction and, at an introductory level, Wittgenstein on meaning as use and on following a rule.

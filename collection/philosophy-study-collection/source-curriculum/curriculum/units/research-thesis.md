## What this unit is for

An independent research project, from question to defense, comparable in ambition (though not in institutional standing) to a senior thesis.

## Milestones

Each milestone is a small gate, run by the Examiner.

1. **Question and proposal:** a 2,000-word prospectus. Approved when the question is precise, philosophically significant, and answerable in 8,000–12,000 words.
2. **Literature review:** 1,500–2,000 words mapping the debate without taking sides, plus an annotated bibliography in which the Verifier has confirmed that every item exists and says what it's cited for.
3. **Full draft**, with structural feedback from the Tutor.
4. **Revision.**
5. **Presentation and defense:** a 20–30-minute presentation and a 30–45-minute oral defense, by voice.
6. **Final reflection:** where your views changed over the whole program, and which arguments changed them.

## Supervision

Help is minimal at this stage: the Tutor asks questions and gives Level 1 hints only. For the proposal, the Tutor tests whether the question is precise, significant, and manageable, and helps develop search terms for PhilPapers and background topics in the SEP. Sources are sorted into foundational works, major objections, recent contributions, and optional background, and the Verifier checks every one before it's used.

## Mastery criteria

1. Define a research question narrow enough to answer.
2. Find, assess, and use scholarly literature independently, through PhilPapers, SEP bibliographies, and journals.
3. Produce an 8,000–12,000-word thesis that makes a recognizable original argumentative contribution.
4. Revise it in response to criticism.
5. Defend it aloud without reading from a script.

## Gate

The Research & Thesis gate ([rubrics/gates.md](../../rubrics/gates.md)).

## Final portfolio

The Director assembles it at the end of the program:

- an early Introduction essay and its revision;
- representative logic work;
- a historical interpretation;
- a paper in ethics or political philosophy;
- a paper in epistemology, metaphysics, or philosophy of language;
- a paper in philosophy of mind or aesthetics;
- oral exam reports;
- the annotated bibliography;
- the thesis;
- the final reflection.

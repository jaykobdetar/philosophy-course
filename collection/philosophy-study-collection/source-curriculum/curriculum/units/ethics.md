# Ethics

**Stage:** Core
**Requires:** [Introduction to Philosophy](introduction.md)
**Status:** outline

## What this unit is for

The main theories of right action and good character, the metaethical question of what moral claims are, and applied argument.

## Anchor readings

- Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* I–II, with selections from X (Bekker numbers)
- Kant, *Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals* (Akademie volume 4)
- Mill, *Utilitarianism*, chapters 2 and 4
- Scanlon, *What We Owe to Each Other*, selections on contractualism
- Hume, *A Treatise of Human Nature* 3.1.1, on "is" and "ought"
- Moore, *Principia Ethica* §13 (the open question argument)
- Ayer, *Language, Truth and Logic*, chapter 6
- Mackie, *Ethics: Inventing Right and Wrong*, chapter 1
- Simone de Beauvoir, *The Ethics of Ambiguity*, selections
- Judith Jarvis Thomson, "A Defense of Abortion" (1971)

## Mastery criteria

1. Analyze a single case from consequentialist, deontological, virtue-based, and contractualist points of view, and explain where they diverge.
2. Explain Aristotle's function argument and his account of virtue as a mean.
3. Reconstruct Kant's formula of universal law and a standard objection to it.
4. Reconstruct Mill's "proof" of the principle of utility and assess the move from "desired" to "desirable".
5. Explain the open question argument, emotivism, and error theory, and defend a metaethical position against a serious objection.
6. Defend a position in applied ethics, such as a verdict on Thomson's violinist argument, against its strongest objection.

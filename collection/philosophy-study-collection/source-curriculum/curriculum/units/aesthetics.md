# Aesthetics

**Stage:** Core
**Requires:** [Early Modern Philosophy](early-modern.md)
**Status:** outline

## What this unit is for

Whether judgments of taste can be correct, what beauty is, and what makes something art.

## Anchor readings

- Hume, "Of the Standard of Taste" (1757)
- Kant, *Critique of the Power of Judgment*, "Analytic of the Beautiful", §§1–22 (Akademie volume 5)
- Morris Weitz, "The Role of Theory in Aesthetics" (1956)
- Arthur Danto, "The Artworld" (1964)
- Kendall Walton, "Categories of Art" (1970)
- Optional: Plato, *Republic* X, on imitation

## Mastery criteria

1. Reconstruct Hume's account of the standard of taste and assess whether it secures objectivity.
2. Explain Kant's four "moments" of the judgment of beauty: disinterestedness, universality without concepts, purposiveness without purpose, and necessity.
3. Reconstruct Weitz's argument that art can't be defined and Danto's account of the artworld, and evaluate a definition of art.
4. Explain Walton's categories of art and how they bear on which aesthetic properties a work has.
5. Apply an aesthetic theory to an unfamiliar work of art.

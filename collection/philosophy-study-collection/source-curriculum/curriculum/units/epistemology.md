# Epistemology

**Stage:** Core
**Requires:** [Logic I](logic-1.md), [Early Modern Philosophy](early-modern.md)
**Status:** outline

## What this unit is for

What knowledge and justification are, whether scepticism can be answered, and how knowledge depends on other people.

## Anchor readings

- G. E. Moore, "Proof of an External World" (1939)
- Edmund Gettier, "Is Justified True Belief Knowledge?" (1963), revisited from the Introduction
- Linda Zagzebski, "The Inescapability of Gettier Problems" (1994)
- Alvin Goldman, "What Is Justified Belief?" (1979)
- Laurence BonJour, "Externalist Theories of Empirical Knowledge" (1980)
- Keith DeRose, "Solving the Skeptical Problem" (1995)
- Miranda Fricker, *Epistemic Injustice* (2007), selections on testimonial and hermeneutical injustice

## Mastery criteria

1. Reconstruct a sceptical argument (from dreaming, an evil demon, or a brain in a vat) and assess Moore's response and the contextualist response.
2. Explain the justified-true-belief analysis, Gettier cases, and Zagzebski's argument that Gettier problems are inescapable.
3. Distinguish internalism from externalism about justification; reconstruct Goldman's reliabilism and BonJour's clairvoyance objection.
4. Explain testimonial and hermeneutical injustice, and why they are epistemic wrongs and not only ethical ones.
5. Find and assess the scholarly literature on an epistemological question independently.

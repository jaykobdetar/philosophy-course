# Kant & the 19th Century

**Stage:** Core
**Requires:** [Early Modern Philosophy](early-modern.md)
**Status:** outline

## What this unit is for

Kant's critical philosophy, his answer to both rationalism and empiricism, and the nineteenth-century responses that shaped later philosophy on both sides of the analytic–continental divide.

## Anchor readings

- Kant, *Prolegomena to Any Future Metaphysics*, as an entry point (Akademie volume 4)
- Kant, *Critique of Pure Reason*, selections: the B Preface, the Introduction, the Transcendental Aesthetic, and selections from the Transcendental Analytic (A/B pages)
- Hegel, *Phenomenology of Spirit*, selections: the Preface and "Lordship and Bondage" (paragraph numbers)
- Marx, "Estranged Labour" (*Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts of 1844*) and "Theses on Feuerbach" (1845)
- Kierkegaard, *Fear and Trembling*, Problema I
- Nietzsche, *On the Genealogy of Morality*, First Essay (essay and section)

## Mastery criteria

1. Explain Kant's question of how synthetic a priori judgments are possible, and his "Copernican" answer.
2. Reconstruct the Transcendental Aesthetic's argument that space and time are forms of intuition.
3. Explain how Kant responds to Hume on causation.
4. Reconstruct the lordship-and-bondage dialectic and its role in Hegel's account of self-consciousness.
5. Explain Marx's account of estranged labour and his criticism of Feuerbach.
6. Explain Kierkegaard's "teleological suspension of the ethical", and reconstruct and assess Nietzsche's genealogy of "good and bad" and "good and evil".

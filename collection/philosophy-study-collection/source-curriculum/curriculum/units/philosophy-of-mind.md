# Philosophy of Mind

**Stage:** Core
**Requires:** [Metaphysics](metaphysics.md)
**Status:** outline

## What this unit is for

How the mind relates to the body and brain, whether machines could think, and whether consciousness fits into a physical world.

## Anchor readings

- Descartes, Meditation VI (AT VII 71–90), and the Elisabeth correspondence, revisited
- Gilbert Ryle, *The Concept of Mind* (1949), chapter 1
- U. T. Place, "Is Consciousness a Brain Process?" (1956)
- J. J. C. Smart, "Sensations and Brain Processes" (1959)
- Hilary Putnam, "The Nature of Mental States" (1967)
- Alan Turing, "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" (1950)
- John Searle, "Minds, Brains, and Programs" (1980)
- Thomas Nagel, "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?" (1974)
- Frank Jackson, "Epiphenomenal Qualia" (1982)
- David Lewis, "What Experience Teaches" (1988)
- David Chalmers, "Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness" (1995)

## Mastery criteria

1. Reconstruct the main arguments for and against substance dualism, including the interaction problem.
2. Explain the identity theory and functionalism, and the multiple-realizability argument against the identity theory.
3. Reconstruct Turing's imitation game and Searle's Chinese Room argument, and assess the systems reply.
4. Reconstruct Jackson's knowledge argument and a physicalist reply, such as Lewis's ability hypothesis.
5. Explain the hard problem of consciousness and assess whether phenomenal consciousness is compatible with physicalism.

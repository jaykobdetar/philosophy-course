# Metaphysics

**Stage:** Core
**Requires:** [Logic I](logic-1.md)
**Status:** outline

## What this unit is for

What there is, what's possible and necessary, what makes a person the same over time, and whether we act freely.

## Anchor readings

- W. V. O. Quine, "On What There Is" (1948)
- David Lewis, *On the Plurality of Worlds* (1986), chapter 1, selections
- Saul Kripke, *Naming and Necessity*, Lecture III
- Leibniz, *Discourse on Metaphysics* §§8–13
- Locke, *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding* II.xxvii
- Derek Parfit, *Reasons and Persons*, Part III, selections
- Harry Frankfurt, "Alternate Possibilities and Moral Responsibility" (1969)
- Peter van Inwagen, "The Incompatibility of Free Will and Determinism" (1975)
- Optional: David Lewis, "Causation" (1973)

## Mastery criteria

1. Explain Quine's criterion of ontological commitment and apply it to a debate about numbers or properties.
2. Explain talk of possible worlds, and compare Lewis's modal realism with a rival view, reconstructing one argument for it and one against.
3. Explain Kripke's case for necessary a posteriori truths and essential properties.
4. Reconstruct and assess psychological-continuity views of personal identity (Locke, Parfit), including the problem of fission.
5. Reconstruct van Inwagen's consequence argument and Frankfurt's counterexample to the principle of alternate possibilities, and defend a position on free will.

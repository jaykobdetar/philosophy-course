# Ancient & Medieval Philosophy

**Stage:** Core
**Requires:** [Introduction to Philosophy](introduction.md)
**Status:** outline

## What this unit is for

The founding Greek arguments about reality, knowledge, the soul, and the good life, followed by a bridge through Hellenistic and medieval philosophy to the early modern period.

## Anchor readings

- Plato, *Republic*, selections: Books I–II (on justice) and the Divided Line and the Cave (509d–521b); *Phaedo*, selections on the soul's immortality (Stephanus pages)
- Aristotle, *Categories* 1–5; *Physics* II (the four causes); *De Anima* II.1–3 (Bekker numbers)
- Epicurus, *Letter to Menoeceus* (preserved in Diogenes Laertius, *Lives* X)
- Sextus Empiricus, *Outlines of Pyrrhonism* I, selections (book and section)
- Augustine, *Confessions* XI, on time (book, chapter, section)
- Anselm, *Proslogion* 2–4, with Gaunilo's reply, "On Behalf of the Fool"
- Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I, q. 2, a. 3 (the Five Ways)

## Mastery criteria

1. Explain Plato's theory of Forms and reconstruct one argument for it.
2. Reconstruct Plato's case that justice benefits the just person, and assess one objection.
3. Explain Aristotle's four causes and his account of the soul as the form of a living body, and contrast both with Plato.
4. Reconstruct one Hellenistic argument (Epicurus on death, or a Pyrrhonist mode) and assess it.
5. Reconstruct Anselm's ontological argument, Gaunilo's reply, and one of Aquinas's Five Ways.
6. Explain ancient and medieval views in their own terms before criticizing them.

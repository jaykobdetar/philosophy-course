# Logic I

**Stage:** Foundations
**Requires:** none
**Status:** outline

## What this unit is for

The formal tools used throughout philosophy: symbolizing arguments, testing validity, and constructing proofs. It can run alongside the Introduction.

## Anchor readings

- P. D. Magnus, Tim Button, Robert Trueman, and Richard Zach, *forall x: Calgary* (free; CC BY 4.0). The parts on key notions of logic, truth-functional logic (TFL), truth tables, natural deduction for TFL, first-order logic (FOL), interpretations, and natural deduction for FOL. The book's parts on modal logic and metatheory belong to Logic II, an elective option.
- The book's solutions booklet, for checking work after attempting it. The open-source Carnap system supports the book's notation and proof system and can be used for interactive exercises.

## Mastery criteria

1. Explain validity, soundness, consistency, and logical equivalence, and why validity depends on form.
2. Symbolize English sentences and arguments in TFL and in FOL with identity, noting what each symbolization leaves out.
3. Use truth tables to test for validity, equivalence, and consistency.
4. Construct natural deduction proofs in the book's system for TFL and FOL.
5. Construct an interpretation that shows an FOL argument is invalid.
6. Explain what the formal validity of a philosophical argument does and doesn't establish.

## Gate

The Logic I gate ([rubrics/gates.md](../../rubrics/gates.md)): problem sets, a written exam, a short oral, and a paper that formalizes an argument from a philosophical text.

# Materials catalog

Every reading, reference work, and resource the curriculum uses has an entry here. The Verifier maintains it.

**Status:** sourced on 29 September 2026. Every anchor reading has checked publication details and, where one exists, a legal free copy. Articles carry a DOI or JSTOR link where one was found. Two things weren't done: publisher links for books still in copyright (any scholarly edition that prints the standard reference numbers works), and a re-check of the "suggested" modern editions, which come from the original design report.

## Access types

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **stored** | A short item that may be redistributed, kept in [texts/](texts/README.md) with its license notice |
| **retrieve** | Fetched on demand by the retrieval tool (see [tools/retrieval](../tools/retrieval/README.md)) and never saved to the repository |
| **link** | A URL (and DOI where there is one), used when no tool applies |
| **learner** | Sourced by the learner: all books, plus articles without a legal free copy |

Items on Project Gutenberg, the Internet Archive, Wikisource, and the SEP can also be read through the retrieval tool, and PhilArchive records can be looked up with it once a free PhilPapers API key is set. Every entry keeps a link as a fallback where a legitimate one exists. **Stored (candidate)** marks a short text that could be stored once its status outside the US is confirmed; until then it's used by link.

## Rights labels

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **PD** | Public domain in the US and in countries with life-plus-70-year terms |
| **PD (US)** | Public domain in the US (Project Gutenberg's status for its texts); check local law elsewhere |
| **©** | In copyright: use a library, buy a copy, or use the link |
| Named licence | For example CC BY 4.0, as stated by the source |

A work is marked PD beyond the US only where the author's or translator's death date is known. In 2026, a work published before 1931 is in the public domain in the US; in life-plus-70 countries, a work is protected until 70 years after its author's or translator's death.

## Rules

- **Legitimate sources only.** Never link to or retrieve from pirate libraries.
- **Check before storing.** Mark an item stored only after its license or public-domain status has been confirmed, and record where the public-domain status applies.
- **Never commit restricted text.** Material from restricted sources, such as SEP entries or copyrighted articles, is read on demand and never saved here.
- **Keep retrieved files out of the repository.** If you use the retrieval tool, keep its download and document folders outside your copy of the curriculum.
- **Cite by standard reference numbers,** so any good edition works (see the table below). Learners record their own editions in `learner/editions.md`.
- **Free copies are often older translations** and may not print standard reference numbers. They're fine for reading; for graded work, quote a scholarly edition where you can.
- **Cite fixed SEP editions.** The SEP publishes fixed quarterly editions in its archives for citation; link the edition used.
- **The MIT License covers only this project's original content.** Everything listed here keeps its own license.

# Evidence and design rationale

The evidence snapshot is 30 September 2026. Requirements and web access may change. A dated institutional requirement supports what that institution requires; it does not dictate this curriculum. Empirical findings support bounded learning-design choices; they do not validate the entire program. The exact route, hours, word ranges, and readiness procedures are local design judgments.

## Institutional comparison

The comparison deliberately includes different architectures. Native units are retained; course credits, quarter units, and UK credits are not interchangeable. Some distribution requirements overlap with seminar requirements and must not be added twice.

| **Program** | **Verified design feature**                                                                                                                                                            | **Implication for this plan**                                                                              |
|-------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Harvard     | Eleven courses in the ordinary concentration, four distribution areas, two tutorials, and no ordinary-route thesis \[B01\]                                                             | Breadth plus repeated guided work can support a serious major without making a thesis universal            |
| Princeton   | Eight taught departmental courses plus junior independent work, a 10,000–15,000-word senior thesis, and a departmental examination \[B02–B03\]                                         | A small course count can conceal substantial separate research work                                        |
| Stanford    | Sixty quarter units, including early intensive writing, logic, science, value, theoretical philosophy, ancient and modern history, and a capstone \[B04\]                              | Early writing and science belong naturally in a broad common foundation                                    |
| Yale        | Twelve courses including prerequisites/senior work, three history courses, theoretical/value breadth, logic, advanced seminars, and a senior seminar or independent project \[B05\]    | Advanced seminar work and substantial revision can be an alternative to a fixed thesis format              |
| Cambridge   | Common first-year papers lead to later specialization; the final dissertation is optional, and supervision emphasizes repeated essays and discussion \[B06–B07\]                       | A beginner-safe foundation can lead to depth without studying every available subtopic                     |
| UCL         | A 360-credit single-subject BA with compulsory first-year foundations/study skills, then constrained choice; the dissertation is optional \[B08–B09\]                                  | A whole subject degree is much larger than a small US-style major and is not reproduced by matching titles |
| Edinburgh   | Four-year undergraduate honours route, early Greek/Islamic history, later specialization, and either an 8,000-word dissertation or two 4,000-word coursework dissertations \[B10–B12\] | Global historical material can be foundational; more than one capstone architecture is legitimate          |
| Toronto     | Separate seven-credit major and ten-credit specialist structures, with advanced-level study and no universal thesis requirement in the listed routes \[B13–B14\]                       | Major and specialist depth are distinct, and not all strong programs culminate in a thesis                 |

Workload conventions reinforce the need for honest pacing. Edinburgh’s first-year Logic 1 publishes 200 nominal hours for one course; Stanford ordinarily treats a unit as thirty total quarterly hours; UCL assigns ten notional hours per credit \[B11, B15–B16\]. These are institutional planning conventions, not measured forecasts for this learner. This plan’s 100–180-hour courses are locally bounded work packages and are not labelled equivalent to any particular institution’s modules.

The QAA’s 2025 Philosophy Subject Benchmark Statement provides a sector-authored reference for subject knowledge, intellectual/research/communication skills, plural philosophical methods, and increasing independence. It is not a prescribed syllabus or an accreditation rule for this plan \[B17\].

Actual syllabi supplied useful reality checks: Harvard’s 2024 proseminar concentrates on one substantial book with short responses and a long paper; UCL’s current study-skills module explicitly teaches exercises and essay writing; UC San Diego’s 2024 introduction uses revision and specifications; and its 2025 science course includes contemporary trust, bias, and uncertainty questions \[B09, B18, E14–E15\]. Archived syllabi illustrate teaching choices at their stated dates, not current universal reading lists.

## Pedagogical evidence used with limits

> • Writing and interpretation: philosophy-department guides and the APA’s statements emphasize precise questions, charitable interpretation, reasoned argument, discussion, and expression. They support the selected outcomes, while the detailed developmental ladder is this program’s design \[E01–E05\]
>
> • Argument visualization: Cullen and colleagues’ 2018 quasi-experimental study supports careful argument-analysis practice with feedback. It bundled intensive teaching and at least ten weekly hours of practice in a selective-college seminar. Its results cannot be promised for this five-to-seven-hour self-study program \[E06\]
>
> • Retrieval and spacing: Karpicke and Blunt’s science-text experiments and Cepeda and colleagues’ spacing study justify revisiting material and attempting recall before correction. They do not establish that flashcards alone teach philosophical interpretation or prescribe one fixed schedule \[E07–E08\]
>
> • Worked examples and revision: research on fading examples and writing feedback supports scaffolded practice and substantive revision, with important differences in population and subject matter. The particular paragraph-to-capstone sequence remains a local choice \[E09–E10\]
>
> • AI: different studies find different results depending on tutor design and setting. High-school mathematics and bounded undergraduate physics experiments do not validate AI assessment of a philosophy curriculum. Therefore assistance declarations, fresh tasks, source checks, and optional human review are central \[E11–E12\]
>
> • Global and methodological breadth: professional guidance supports serious engagement with diverse traditions and cautions against breadth without depth or treating traditions as ornamental additions. It does not require the exact Chinese/Africana choices made here \[E02, E13\]

## What changed from the earlier fifteen unit plan

The previous material supplied a useful introduction, roles, rubrics, and reading anchors, but fourteen units were still outlines. The new route replaces those placeholders with finite readings, ordered modules, writing tasks, assessment evidence, and explicit hours. It also changes the structure rather than preserving a number for its own sake.

> • Preserved: close primary reading, argument reconstruction, counterexamples, repeated writing and revision, oral explanation, source verification, and cumulative review
>
> • Added: beginner academic skills, required science, a sustained global tradition, Indian and Islamic/Jewish encounters in the common courses, explicit research training, access checks, and a fully specified first teaching sequence
>
> • Corrected: Republic’s missing justice answer, Kant’s missing causation locator, overbroad medieval and nineteenth-century claims, unsupported logic chapter boundaries, and confusion between a collected aid and the assigned primary text
>
> • Rebalanced: combined knowledge/reality and mind/language courses state their limits; aesthetics, social philosophy, and pragmatist/phenomenological work become real choices rather than every specialist area becoming compulsory
>
> • Replaced: rigid numerical AI mastery thresholds with criterion-level evidence; a mandatory long thesis with a bounded research capstone; and “always struggle first” with scaffolded practice followed by increasing independence
>
> • Disclosed: multi-year workload, unavailable editions, translation differences, limits of AI grading, uncertain transfer of educational findings, and the difference between self-study completion and external review

The research and review record at the end summarizes the completed critique rounds, model roles, substantive corrections, and limits. No number of review passes proves correctness; material problems must be checked against the relevant evidence.

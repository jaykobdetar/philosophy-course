# Research and review record

## What was checked

The curriculum was built from separate investigations of university requirements and actual syllabi, philosophy teaching and learning evidence, coverage/prerequisite alignment, and exact reading/edition/access requirements. Institutional facts, empirical findings, and local design choices were kept distinct. Eight programs supplied contrasting reference points; their names and requirements do not certify this plan.

The draft then received two rounds of full-manuscript AI critique. The second round used fresh instances and checked the revised text, including every option, capstone, assessment grid, and access appendix. Issues were tracked and corrected; agreement among models was not used as a substitute for source evidence.

## Limits that remain

No learner pilot or external faculty assessment has been conducted. Hours are adjustable planning estimates; matching them does not establish equivalence to a university course. Some required modern editions must be borrowed or otherwise obtained legally; their catalogue records are not proof that the learner currently has access. Bibliographic and representative passage checks do not amount to a page-by-page scholarly edition audit of every assigned book. Source access and current debates should be rechecked when later courses begin. AI feedback and this review process cannot award credits or validate a degree-level claim.

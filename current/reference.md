# Reference and research protocol

A course that asks for one checked scholarly interpretation includes a small research exercise, not an unspecified additional reading list. Allocate two to four hours within the stated module or essay budget: locate a relevant scholarly reference, read the section actually needed, verify its primary-text citation, and record how it affects your interpretation. You are not expected to read an entire encyclopedia entry or monograph because it appears in a bibliography.

Use the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy for orientation and bibliographies, PhilPapers for bibliographic discovery, publisher/journal pages and university repositories to verify publications, and your library catalogue for access. Search engines locate sources; search snippets are not substitutes for reading them. No API key or custom retrieval software is required. A source can be bibliographically verified while its full text is inaccessible; record that distinction.

For a substantial research task:

1\. Write the question in one sentence and list two or three relevant concepts and author names

2\. Read the relevant section of a scholarly reference entry, not automatically the entire entry

3\. Trace a cited primary passage and one significant scholarly disagreement

4\. Verify title, author, publication venue, date, and page/section scope at the publisher, journal, institutional repository, or the text itself

5\. Read the actual argument before citing it as support; distinguish a quotation, paraphrase, and your own inference

6\. Follow one backward citation and one later citation or recent related paper to test whether the discussion has changed

7\. Keep only sources that do argumentative work, and record dissenting evidence rather than search only for support

A source note contains: full citation; stable link or catalogue identifier; edition/translation; pages actually read; question addressed; argument in your own words; a quotation only if necessary; your objection; and how you may use it. Verify every quotation against the actual edition. If a text’s standard numbers are absent, cite its actual book/chapter and printed page instead of inventing a crosswalk. Exact wording from a modernized adaptation must be attributed to that adaptation, not presented as an unaltered translation.

For changing fields such as AI, the advanced seminar’s current-debate search is intentionally part of the learning task. The core packet supplies a complete starting argument structure; the later search teaches independent research. Recheck the field when you reach it, potentially years after this curriculum’s 2026 evidence snapshot.

## Reusable assignment forms

Argument reconstruction: state the question, quote or identify the exact passage, number the explicit premises and conclusion, bracket supplied premises, explain each inferential link, note a rival interpretation, and give one objection. End by distinguishing the author’s argument from any improvement you propose.

Short interpretation: state the passage’s role, explain the key term in context, support the reading with two precise textual observations, consider one plausible alternative, and justify your preference. Criticism follows accurate interpretation.

Essay proposal: question, provisional answer, main supporting argument, strongest objection, primary passages, and one explicit exclusion. Keep it short enough to test before drafting.

Feedback memo: identify the most consequential strength; the two most consequential problems; the passages where they arise; and the next revision task. A style edit is not a substitute for diagnosing an invalid inference or central misreading.

Independent-task record: prompt, allowed sources, conditions and time used, assistance declaration, outcome-level evidence, errors requiring repair, and the next fresh task. Scores without evidence are not useful records.

Source/edition record: author and title, edition and translator, actual file or book used, stable reference system, differences from the recommended edition, and unresolved access/citation issues.

## Tutor instructions

Begin each session by asking what course/module is active, which exact passage or task is in view, how much rested focused time is available, and what the learner has already attempted. Use the current course outcomes and declared source edition. Do not assume unseen text is available or that a source claim is correct because another AI stated it.

Teach one consequential issue at a time. Offer a brief orientation or a different worked example when needed, then ask the learner to perform the next step. Ask for the reason linking premises to conclusions, the best objection, or evidence for an interpretation. Distinguish factual error, contested interpretation, philosophical disagreement, and unclear prose. Never grade a position by whether it agrees with your own.

For a draft, diagnose before rewriting. Do not generate replacement assessed paragraphs. For a proof, identify the rule or scope problem without supplying the whole solution immediately. For a historical/global text, attend to genre, translator choices, intellectual context, and comparison limits. Do not require that all philosophy imitate one analytic essay format.

For assessment, state permitted materials, use a fresh task, request evidence for each criterion, and record uncertainty. Hold out solutions until the independent attempt. Escalate unresolved source or interpretation disputes to the text, a reliable specialist source, or a qualified human. Do not call a changed role name, a fresh chat, or model agreement independent certification.

End with the learner’s two-sentence account of what changed, the one artifact saved, and the next concrete step. Do not add surprise required readings, expand the course indefinitely, or use optional enrichment as an unstated gate.

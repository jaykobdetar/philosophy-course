# Course 4 Medieval philosophy and transmission

## Scope and core readings

100 hours; prerequisites Courses 1 and 3. This course studies selected arguments about time, intellect, revelation, causation, and existence across interconnected Latin, Arabic, and Jewish philosophical settings. It is a bridge with depth in a few problems, not a comprehensive medieval survey. Avoid narrating Islamic and Jewish thinkers merely as carriers of Greek ideas to Europe.

Core: Augustine, Confessions XI.14–31, Henry Chadwick translation, Oxford 1991; Anselm, Proslogion 2–4, Gaunilo’s complete On Behalf of the Fool, and Anselm’s reply sections 1–4, Thomas Williams translation, Proslogion with the Replies of Gaunilo and Anselm, Hackett 2001; Avicenna, “On the Soul,” complete excerpt in Muhammad Ali Khalidi, editor, Medieval Islamic Philosophical Writings, Cambridge 2005, pp. 27–58; al-Ghazali, The Rescuer from Error, the complete anthology excerpt, pp. 59–98, in the same anthology; Maimonides, Guide of the Perplexed I.50–60, Shlomo Pines translation, Chicago 1963; Aquinas, Summa Theologiae I, question 2, articles 1–3, and question 1, article 8, English Dominican Province translation. Read the editor’s introductions to the assigned Avicenna and al-Ghazali excerpts. The Avicenna and al-Ghazali assignments are complete anthology excerpts, not complete original treatises; the Avicenna selection comes from the Book of Salvation. Check the quoted page within the actual borrowed copy before citing it.

Outcomes: reconstruct distinct arguments for existence and distinguish their premises; explain the flying-person thought experiment without treating it as a complete proof of Cartesian dualism; interpret negative theology; compare reason/revelation relationships with contextual care; use a translation responsibly.

## Modules and assignments

**1. Context and transmission, 10 hours.** Read the anthology’s general introduction and source introductions. Map the languages, institutions, translation movements, and intellectual settings actually supported by these introductions. Label the scope as the Arabic/Islamic context supplied by this source; mark missing Latin/Jewish contextual details as questions rather than filling them from an AI’s recollection. Output: a sourced 500-word explanation of why a simple “faith versus reason” storyline is inadequate.

**2. Time and experience, 12 hours.** Augustine XI.14–31. Reconstruct the puzzle of past, present, and future. PC activity: distinguish a claim about temporal reality from a claim about measuring time. Write a 400-word objection and possible response.

**3. Anselm and Gaunilo, 14 hours.** Assigned Proslogion and replies. Produce parallel reconstructions, separating the original argument, parody, and response. Explain whether the parody targets the strongest reading. Do not simply label an argument “ontological” and assume that settles its validity.

**4. Avicenna on self and soul, 14 hours.** Assigned On the Soul excerpt. Describe the thought experiment’s assumptions, conclusion, and limits; compare Aristotle and Descartes cautiously. Write a 600-word interpretive exercise grounded in the actual translation.

**5. Al-Ghazali and philosophical inquiry, 12 hours.** Assigned anthology excerpt of Rescuer. Identify distinctions among mathematical, logical, physical, metaphysical, political, and ethical investigations where the text makes them. Write a 500-word analysis of an argument against philosophers, distinguishing autobiography, classification, and demonstration.

**6. Maimonides and Aquinas, 14 hours.** Guide I.50–60; Summa I q.1 a.8 and q.2 aa.1–3. Compare negative predication with one of Aquinas’s arguments. Map the objection–response form of a Summa article. Do not assimilate all theological arguments to one generic proof.

**7. Essay and revision, 18 hours.** Write 1,500–1,800 words: “What, exactly, does Avicenna’s thought experiment establish?”; “Does Gaunilo answer Anselm?”; or “Can negative theology make informative claims?” Include one competing interpretation and a justified textual decision.

**8. Assessment and delayed check, 6 hours.** Analyze a fresh assigned passage, compare two methods of argument, explain an unanticipated objection, and later reconstruct the essay’s key inference. Remediate contextual misattributions by returning to the source introduction and passage before repeating the task.

Optional extension: Averroes’ Decisive Treatise or further work in Islamic philosophy, Jewish philosophy, scholasticism, or medieval women’s thought. These require a separate reading plan and are not necessary for this bounded course.

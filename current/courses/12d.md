# Course 12D Social philosophy and applied justice

140 hours; prerequisites Courses 6–8 and preferably Course 11B. Core: Susan Moller Okin, Justice, Gender, and the Family, Basic Books 1989, chapters 1 and 8; bell hooks, Feminist Theory From Margin to Center, second edition, South End Press 2000, chapters 1–2; Iris Marion Young, “Five Faces of Oppression,” chapter 2 of Justice and the Politics of Difference, Princeton 1990; Elizabeth Anderson, “What Is the Point of Equality?”, Ethics 109, 1999, pp. 287–337; Peter Singer, Animal Liberation, 1975, chapter 1 “All Animals Are Equal,” in a clearly identified edition; Aldo Leopold, “The Land Ethic,” in A Sand County Almanac, 1949, complete; Charles Mills, “Ideal Theory as Ideology,” Hypatia 20, 2005, pp. 165–183. The two applied encounters are bounded tests of moral and political frameworks, not full courses in animal or environmental philosophy.

Outcomes: reconstruct a structural critique; distinguish distributive from relational concerns; analyze the public/private boundary; evaluate an extension of moral concern; use a concrete case without assuming away empirical complexity.

**1. Family and the scope of justice, 18 hours.** Okin 1 and 8. Identify the argument about the family and background assumptions of a theory of justice. Write a 700-word analysis of one claim.

**2. Feminist method and difference, 18 hours.** hooks 1–2. Reconstruct the critique of the movement and its audience; explain what follows for political method. Avoid treating any one author as all feminist philosophy.

**3. Structural injustice, 18 hours.** Young chapter 2. Apply two distinct forms of oppression to a documented case and explain the limits of the evidence. A fictional teaching case is acceptable if clearly labelled.

**4. Equality and relationships, 22 hours.** Anderson’s article. Map the objection to luck egalitarianism and the alternative. Write 800 words defending or challenging one inference with an original case.

**5. Beyond human institutions, 20 hours.** Singer’s chapter 1 and Leopold’s essay. Compare the unit of moral concern, the role of consequences, and conflicts within each approach. Do not make the approaches agree by removing their controversial commitments.

**6. Method and idealization, 12 hours.** Mills’s article. Audit an earlier case analysis for assumptions that conceal exclusion or historical conditions.

**7. Essay and revision, 24 hours.** Write 2,000–2,400 words on a single course dispute, including a documented or explicitly hypothetical case and the strongest objection. Separate empirical uncertainty from normative disagreement.

**8. Assessment and delayed check, 8 hours.** Analyze a changed case and interpret an unseen assigned passage. Readiness requires a defensible argument and careful scope, not adopting a prescribed political position.

# Course 12C Pragmatism phenomenology and existential inquiry

140 hours; prerequisites Courses 1, 5, and 9. This option gives sustained contact with methods that a purely analytic survey can miss. It is a focused comparison of inquiry and lived experience, not a complete history of “continental philosophy.”

Core: William James, Pragmatism, lectures II and VI, Harvard critical edition 1975 or complete 1907 text; John Dewey, “The Reflex Arc Concept in Psychology,” Psychological Review 3, 1896, pp. 357–370; Edmund Husserl, The Idea of Phenomenology, lectures I–II, Lee Hardy translation, Kluwer 1999; Maurice Merleau-Ponty, Phenomenology of Perception, Preface, Donald Landes translation, Routledge 2012; Sartre, Existentialism Is a Humanism, lecture text, Carol Macomber translation, Yale 2007; Simone de Beauvoir, The Ethics of Ambiguity, Part I, Bernard Frechtman translation, 1948; Kierkegaard, Fear and Trembling, Problema I, Alexander Jech translation, Hackett 2024. The collected Hollander anthology does not contain that required Kierkegaard section and cannot serve as its substitute.

Outcomes: explain a pragmatist method and a theory of truth without reducing them to expediency; describe phenomenological method and its limits; distinguish phenomenological description from empirical psychology; evaluate responsibility, freedom, and ambiguity; compare philosophical methods fairly.

**1. Pragmatist method, 18 hours.** James II and VI. Apply the pragmatic maxim to a carefully bounded dispute, then test a common caricature. Write 700 words on what James’s truth claim does and does not assert.

**2. Organism and environment, 14 hours.** Dewey’s article. Map the critique of a stimulus–response sequence and explain its philosophical significance without treating the article as contemporary neuroscience.

**3. Phenomenological reduction, 20 hours.** Husserl I–II. Write a 600-word explanation of the methodological move and a separate question about its justification. Keep description and argument visibly distinct.

**4. Embodied experience, 18 hours.** Merleau-Ponty Preface. Perform a short, safe description of ordinary perception, then distinguish your observation from claims attributed to the text. No simulation establishes a universal psychological law.

**5. Freedom and ambiguity, 22 hours.** Sartre’s lecture and Beauvoir Part I. Compare a precise claim about freedom, situation, or responsibility, with textual evidence and an objection.

**6. Faith and the ethical, 16 hours.** Kierkegaard Problema I and its editorial context. Explain the pseudonymous presentation and teleological suspension claim. Distinguish understanding the argument from endorsing its conclusion.

**7. Essay and revision, 24 hours.** Write 2,000–2,400 words on one methodological dispute or a bounded comparison between two assigned texts. Use genre-appropriate criteria: faithful description, interpretive evidence, and the inferential work done by the example.

**8. Assessment and delayed check, 8 hours.** Interpret a new passage and explain what would count as a successful objection within that method. Remedy caricatures by reconstructing the author’s question before comparing conclusions.

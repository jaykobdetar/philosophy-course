# Course 13C Social knowledge and justice

Prerequisite: Course 11B and Course 12D. Core: Charles Mills, “White Ignorance,” in Shannon Sullivan and Nancy Tuana, editors, Race and Epistemologies of Ignorance, SUNY 2007, pp. 13–38; Miranda Fricker, Epistemic Injustice chapters 1 and 7; revisit Young’s “Five Faces of Oppression” and Anderson’s “What Is the Point of Equality?”; add Kristie Dotson, “Tracking Epistemic Violence, Tracking Practices of Silencing,” Hypatia 26, 2011, pp. 236–257. Exact editions and legal reading routes are listed in the ledger.

Central question: when does a pattern of credibility, interpretation, or exclusion constitute an epistemic wrong, and what follows for institutions? Distinguish interpersonal and structural claims, diagnosis and remedy, and philosophical argument from empirical evidence about the case.

Default paper prompt: “Does one specified framework explain an exclusion case that another misses, and why?” Alternative: “Does a proposed remedy answer the identified epistemic wrong without creating a different injustice?” Use a documented case with restrained factual claims or a clearly hypothetical case. Do not make allegations about identifiable people as an exercise.

# Course 7 Knowledge and reality

## Scope and core readings

140 hours; prerequisites Course 1, the semantic and symbolization parts of Course 2, and Course 5. The combined course deliberately concentrates on knowledge, testimony, personal identity, and responsibility. It does not claim full coverage of epistemology and metaphysics. Modal ontology, grounding, time, and advanced skepticism are extension areas.

Read Gettier’s complete 1963 article again; Alvin Goldman, “What Is Justified Belief?”, in Pappas, Justification and Knowledge, 1979, pp. 1–23; Hume, Enquiry section X, Part I; Thomas Reid, An Inquiry into the Human Mind on the Principles of Common Sense, chapter VI, section 24, Derek Brookes critical edition, Edinburgh 1997; Miranda Fricker, Epistemic Injustice, Oxford 2007, chapter 1; Locke, Essay II.xxvii, complete; the Buddhist Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta, SN 22.59, complete, Bhikkhu Sujato translation, SuttaCentral ([<u>https://suttacentral.net/sn22.59/en/sujato</u>](https://suttacentral.net/sn22.59/en/sujato)); Derek Parfit, “Personal Identity,” Philosophical Review 80, 1971, pp. 3–27; Harry Frankfurt, “Alternate Possibilities and Moral Responsibility,” 1969, pp. 829–839; Peter van Inwagen, “The Incompatibility of Free Will and Determinism,” 1975, pp. 185–199. Required context is the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry “Buddha,” sections 1.b “Sources,” 3.b “The Four Noble Truths or Realities,” 3.c “Ontology of Suffering: the Five Aggregates,” and 3.d “Arguments for the Doctrine of Non-self” ([<u>https://iep.utm.edu/buddha/</u>](https://iep.utm.edu/buddha/)). Read these bounded sections within Module 5, not the whole entry; one early Buddhist discourse in a Theravāda/Pāli canonical collection is not a complete account of Buddhist or Indian philosophy or a direct stenographic transcript.

Outcomes: distinguish knowledge from true belief and evaluate a reliabilist proposal; compare competing testimony principles; explain an epistemic injustice; distinguish psychological continuity, identity, and the Buddhist analysis without conflating them; reconstruct a free-will argument; defend a focused position.

## Modules and assignments

**1. Knowledge and justification, 16 hours.** Gettier and Goldman. Create a map distinguishing an analysis of knowledge from an account of justified belief. Write 600 words evaluating one reliability condition using a new case.

**2. Testimony and trust, 16 hours.** Hume X Part I and Reid VI.24. Explain the disagreement over the grounds of trusting others, avoiding the claim that all testimony is about miracles. PC activity: vary source reliability, dependence among witnesses, and background evidence in a case matrix. State what the comparison does not decide.

**3. Epistemic injustice, 14 hours.** Fricker chapter 1. Reconstruct testimonial injustice and compare it with ordinary error or deception. Write a 600-word case analysis with a counterexample to an overbroad definition. Hermeneutical injustice is an optional later topic, not assessed from an unread chapter.

**4. Persons and continuity, 18 hours.** Locke II.xxvii and Parfit’s article. Map body, memory, psychological continuity, and numerical identity. Explain the role of fission without assuming that imaginative possibility alone settles metaphysical possibility.

**5. A different inquiry into self, 16 hours.** SN 22.59 and the four specified IEP “Buddha” sections. Identify the five aggregates, the argument’s structure, and the practical purpose of the discourse. Mark one claim directly supported by Sujato’s SN 22.59 and one stronger interpretive claim made by the IEP guide. The guide is a scholarly interpretation, not a neutral answer key; disagreement with it is not automatically a textual error. Write 700 words comparing a narrowly specified question with Locke while explaining why the projects are not interchangeable. Do not recast “not-self” automatically as a Western bundle theory.

**6. Freedom and responsibility, 22 hours.** Frankfurt and van Inwagen. Reconstruct the principle of alternate possibilities, a Frankfurt case, and the consequence argument. Diagram which assumptions differ, then write 600 words on whether an objection attacks determinism, alternate possibilities, or a transfer principle.

**7. Essay and revision, 28 hours.** Write 1,800–2,200 words on testimony, reliabilism, identity, or responsibility. Include one verified secondary interpretation and one serious rival argument. State clearly whether your paper is interpretive, conceptual, or normative.

**8. Assessment and delayed check, 10 hours.** Interpret a new assigned passage, construct a fresh counterexample, and answer an unscripted objection. If two positions are being conflated, make a question–premise–conclusion comparison before retesting. Retention is checked in science or the selected area course.

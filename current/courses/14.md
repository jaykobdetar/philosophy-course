# Course 14 Independent research capstone

180 focused hours; complete the common courses, global tradition, selected area, and advanced seminar, or document equivalent demonstrated prerequisites. Begin only when the research apprenticeship has produced a viable question and source access. The default product is a 5,000–7,000-word research paper, not a dissertation-length survey. It should make a modest, identifiable contribution: clarify a distinction, improve an objection, defend an interpretation, apply an argument to a carefully delimited new case, or show why a tempting comparison fails.

This local capstone length and workload are design choices. Universities use varied senior seminars, independent projects, essays, and theses. A longer thesis can be a valuable future project but is not automatically a better finish for this learner’s goals.

## Milestones

**1. Proposal and feasibility, 16 hours.** Write 800–1,000 words containing the question, significance, provisional claim, method, strongest anticipated objection, source needs, and exclusions. State why the question can be answered within the paper. The proposal is ready only when essential sources can actually be read.

**2. Literature and source dossier, 26 hours.** Build an annotated bibliography of the sources that genuinely bear on the question. A likely working set is eight to twelve relevant items, including sources already studied in the advanced seminar; relevance governs the count. Read the portions that bear on the question, not automatically eight to twelve whole books. Plan roughly forty to seventy pages of additional close reading in this phase, plus rechecking earlier notes and writing annotations. If the argument requires more new reading, enlarge the logged budget or narrow the question rather than pretend that a whole source has been read. For each, record a complete citation, legal access route, exact relevant pages/sections, the claim used, and whether you read the whole item or only a defined portion. Include foundational arguments, a serious competing view, and current scholarship where relevant. Verify every quotation.

**3. Argument plan and trial section, 20 hours.** Make a detailed outline and write a 1,000-word central section. Test the main inference before writing the entire paper. If it fails, revise the question rather than hide the failure in background exposition.

**4. Full first draft, 40 hours.** Write the complete paper in your own words. Separate exposition from defense, and make the contribution visible. Include citations and a provisional bibliography; do not postpone all sourcing to the end.

**5. Critical review, 16 hours.** Request a focused human review if available and chosen; otherwise arrange a skeptical source-bound review and label it provisional. Ask about the most serious interpretive error, the weakest inference, and the strongest objection. Write a response plan explaining which suggestions you will accept and why.

**6. Substantive revision, 32 hours.** Reorganize and rewrite where needed. Resolve citation issues, check every significant attribution, and remove claims beyond the evidence. Keep an explicit limitations section where appropriate. A revision memo explains the three most important changes.

**7. Presentation and defense, 12 hours.** Prepare a ten-to-fifteen-minute explanation and answer twenty-to-thirty minutes of unscripted questions or an equivalent accessible format. Include a challenge not rehearsed during drafting and an explanation of what would change your conclusion. Revise again if the defense exposes a substantive error.

**8. Final portfolio and delayed synthesis, 18 hours.** Submit the revised paper, bibliography, assistance declaration, revision memo, and selected earlier artifacts. Write a 1,000-word reflection comparing an early and late argument, identifying actual development and remaining gaps. After a pause, explain the project and apply its central distinction to a fresh case. These activities, including the final source audit, are within the 180-hour estimate.

## Final portfolio

Include the early baseline paragraph; Course 1 essay and revision; representative independent logic work; one historical interpretation; one ethics/political paper; one knowledge/science paper; one context-sensitive global-tradition paper, with translation analysis where relevant; the advanced seminar paper; the capstone; and concise records of independent tasks and dialogue. Select evidence rather than collect every note into an unreadable archive.

Use the shared criteria, with stronger expectations for research scope and handling alternatives. Every essential dimension must be independently competent. An unresolved central source error, invalid pivotal inference, inability to explain the paper, or unavailable essential evidence requires further work. A competent capstone may conclude that the initial claim was too strong. Recognizing a limit can be a substantive achievement.

If the project is too broad, narrow the question before adding hours. If the sources are inaccessible, change to a defensible adjacent question while preserving the learning goal, with the change documented. If a qualified reviewer is unavailable, finish the work and label the assessment honestly. Never claim publication-level originality, external certification, or equivalence to a particular university thesis without the evidence to support it.

## Optional longer thesis route

After this capstone, a learner who wants deeper specialist work may expand to 8,000–12,000 words with additional sources, external supervision if possible, and a new workload estimate based on actual capstone pace. This is a separate extension decision. It is not silently added to the required 180 hours and is not necessary for completing the recommended route.

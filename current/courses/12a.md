# Course 12A Mind and language

140 hours; prerequisites Courses 1, 2’s first-order language/semantics, 5, and 7. This is a bounded combined introduction, not two complete specialist courses.

Core: Frege, “On Sense and Reference,” complete, Max Black translation in Translations from the Philosophical Writings of Gottlob Frege, third edition, Blackwell 1980; Russell, “On Denoting,” Mind 14, 1905, pp. 479–493; Kripke, Naming and Necessity, Harvard 1980, Lecture I; Ryle, The Concept of Mind, 1949, chapter I; Putnam, “The Nature of Mental States,” 1967, complete, reprinted in Mind, Language and Reality, Cambridge 1975, pp. 429–440; Turing, “Computing Machinery and Intelligence,” Mind 59, 1950, pp. 433–460; Searle, “Minds, Brains, and Programs,” Behavioral and Brain Sciences 3, 1980, pp. 417–424, excluding the separate commentaries; Nagel, “What Is It Like to Be a Bat?”, 1974, pp. 435–450; Jackson, “Epiphenomenal Qualia,” 1982, pp. 127–136. The specified English Frege translation must be obtained; the collected German original is not an English reading substitute.

Outcomes: explain reference/sense and descriptions; assess a naming argument; distinguish behaviorist, identity, and functionalist strategies at introductory level; reconstruct machine-intelligence and consciousness arguments; identify the limits of a thought experiment.

**1. Meaning and reference, 20 hours.** Frege and Russell. Analyze an informative identity and a definite description. Produce a formalization with a prose account of what it clarifies and what it leaves unresolved.

**2. Names and necessity, 16 hours.** Kripke Lecture I. Distinguish rigid designation, epistemic certainty, and necessity. Write 700 words on one argument against a descriptive account; do not assess Lecture III claims that have not been assigned.

**3. Mind and explanation, 18 hours.** Ryle I and Putnam. Compare category-error and functionalist strategies. Construct an original multiple-realization example and explain why it matters.

**4. Intelligence and understanding, 20 hours.** Turing and Searle. Separate an operational test from a theory of consciousness. Reconstruct the Chinese Room and the systems reply as presented in the article. Write a 700-word objection and response.

**5. Experience and physicalism, 20 hours.** Nagel and Jackson. Identify the epistemic and metaphysical premises separately. Compare an ability/knowledge reply from a checked scholarly overview; do not present that overview as an assigned primary Lewis paper.

**6. Integrative workshop, 10 hours.** Select one connection between language, understanding, and consciousness. Produce a 1,000-word debate map with two rival views and one unresolved question. This is preparation for the essay, not an additional final paper.

**7. Essay and revision, 26 hours.** Write 2,000–2,400 words evaluating a single argument from the course. Keep interpretive fidelity separate from your proposed improvement. Use one verified secondary source and a new case.

**8. Assessment and delayed check, 10 hours.** Interpret a new passage, solve a new reference/description problem, and defend the paper against a changed case. If formalism obscures meaning, translate every symbol back into English before retesting.

# Course 13 Advanced seminar and research apprenticeship

160 hours. Complete Course 11 and one Course 12 option first. The packet-specific prerequisites determine intellectual readiness: Packet A needs Mind and Language, Packet C needs Social Philosophy, while Packet B builds on Chinese Philosophy and Ethics and accepts any completed area course. Choose one of the three defined seminar packets below. The default is AI Language and Consciousness after Mind and Language. Comparative Moral Cultivation follows the Chinese option; Social Knowledge and Justice follows the Africana option and Social Philosophy. Aesthetics or Pragmatism can serve as the selected area while the learner specializes further in the completed global tradition. Additional advanced specialisms are extensions, not promised unfinished course options.

This course supplies the research skills that the capstone assumes: constructing a manageable question, distinguishing an author’s argument from your improvement, locating and assessing scholarship, building a debate map, defending an interpretation, and revising a substantial paper. It is not merely an extra reading list.

## Seminar sequence for every packet

**1. Entry and question selection, 10 hours.** Explain two relevant arguments without notes; choose a packet; state three possible questions and narrow one. Read the core sources’ abstracts/introduction/conclusion for orientation. Output: a 400-word question note and an explicit list of scope exclusions.

**2. Primary-text and core-scholarship analysis, 44 hours.** Read the packet in full at its stated scope. Produce four 500-word reconstructions and two argument maps. Identify disagreements over definitions, evidence, inference, and purpose. These short tasks become material for the paper rather than separate polished publications.

**3. Research methods and current debate check, 22 hours.** Use the reference protocol below. Find four candidate scholarly sources, read their abstracts and introductions, then select two for full reading within a combined target of about forty to sixty pages. At least one should offer a serious challenge; at least one should be relatively recent at the time you take the course. If the search does not yield a defensible recent source, document that result instead of choosing a weak one to satisfy a date rule. Save citations, stable links, a short reason for selection, and notes distinguishing what was read from what was only indexed.

**4. Debate map and prospectus, 16 hours.** Write a 1,000-word map of three positions and a 600-word prospectus with a provisional thesis, objection, method, and feasible evidence base. The goal is a live question within the chosen literature, not proof that no one has ever had your idea.

**5. Seminar paper draft, 24 hours.** Write 2,500–3,500 words. Cite exact passages and explain the contribution of each secondary source. Mark uncertainties and unresolved objections rather than burying them. No AI-generated assessed prose.

**6. Critical response and revision, 24 hours.** Seek one substantial reader response if feasible; otherwise use a source-bound critical AI read and label its limits. Write a 500-word response to the strongest objection, revise the paper, and explain the main changes. A disagreeing review is not something to average away.

**7. Independent defense and transfer, 10 hours.** Give a ten-minute explanation and answer fifteen to twenty minutes of new questions, or use an accessible text equivalent. Complete an independent 800-word analysis of a fresh related case with sources visible but no live assistance.

**8. Capstone readiness portfolio, 10 hours.** Audit citations, select representative work, identify remaining weaknesses, and propose a capstone question. A later short transfer check is included. Remediate the weakest prerequisite before committing to the project.

Readiness for the capstone requires a manageable question, accurate source use, a defensible argument with a serious objection, evidence of independent performance, and a feasible access plan. A high word count or a large bibliography cannot compensate for a central error.

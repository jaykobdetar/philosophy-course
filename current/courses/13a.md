# Course 13A AI language and consciousness

Prerequisite: Course 12A. Core readings: revisit the exact Turing and Searle passages already assigned; Emily Bender, Timnit Gebru, Angelina McMillan-Major, and Shmargaret Shmitchell, “On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots: Can Language Models Be Too Big?”, FAccT 2021, pp. 610–623; David Chalmers, “Could a Large Language Model Be Conscious?”, Boston Review, 9 August 2023, complete essay based on the 2022 NeurIPS talk; Patrick Butlin and colleagues, Consciousness in Artificial Intelligence Insights from the Science of Consciousness, arXiv 2308.08708, version 3, 22 August 2023, sections 1, 2.1–2.5, and 4.1. Record the version and actual PDF pages read; this is a substantial technical selection of about forty report pages, not a brief article. Use the report’s Glossary for terms encountered, especially computational functionalism, access/phenomenal consciousness, recurrence, and global workspace. No programming or architecture implementation is required. During the forty-four-hour analysis phase, choose one theory contrast for close reconstruction rather than try to make every technical model the subject of your paper. If two initial reading sessions reveal unfamiliar terminology still preventing reconstruction, spend the next session on the report’s own definitions and diagrams and extend the logged module estimate if needed. These are sources for argument, not established answers about any particular current system.

Central question: what, if anything, do linguistic behavior, architecture, and functional organization justify about understanding or consciousness? Distinguish performance, meaning, understanding, access consciousness, phenomenal consciousness, and moral status. Do not infer one from another without an argument. Use Bender and colleagues for their actual claims about language-model risks and meaning, not as a comprehensive theory of consciousness.

Default paper prompt: “Does the argument I select establish a necessary condition for consciousness, or only a reason for uncertainty?” Alternative: “What would count as evidence against the systems reply in a specified model of understanding?” A tool’s fluent answer is an object of analysis, not empirical proof of its internal state. Any informal demonstration must be labelled illustrative, with no claim that a few prompts constitute a scientific experiment.
